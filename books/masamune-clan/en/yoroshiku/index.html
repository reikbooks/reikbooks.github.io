Yoroshiku

Book 10 of Masamune Clan Series

A stolen sacred artifact. A joint mission. A past he can’t escape.

When Sadi’s latest mission plunges him into a confrontation with his family’s dark secrets, can he fulfill his duty and find a way forward, before he’s lost in the currents of time?

L ately, everything felt too loud—even inside my own head.

I slammed my locker open, the clang echoing in the mostly empty break hall. A couple of teammates rushed to change, buzzing about tonight’s fireworks festival. Typical. The mission we’d just wrapped up had dragged on way too long.

Glaring at the single clean shirt left in my locker, I clicked my tongue and snatched the balled-up towel from the back instead. Laundry was overdue. Hell, everything was overdue. I should probably hit the market for new clothes, but right now, dragging myself back to that empty house felt worse than wearing a stinking, blood-stained uniform.

“You hitting up the festival, Sadi?” Kagawa asked, slamming his own locker shut across from mine. “Genta and I are grabbing ramen first.”

I shook my head, pulling the grimy towel over my shoulder. “Nah, you guys go. Shiro mentioned Elder Low wants me in the meeting room.”

Kagawa chuckled. “Busy as always, huh?”

“You know how it is,” I grumbled, rolling my eyes. “Ever since Elder Mei got sick, Elder Low’s been a total pain. Impossible to keep up. The work just keeps piling up.”

Kagawa’s grin faded into a sympathetic nod. “Yeah, no kidding. All thanks to that damn fake doctor, Katsu.”

Shirasu Mitsurugi, AKA Katsu. Ever since that bastard breached security a month and a half ago, sleep had become a luxury. Missions piled up, reports were demanded instantly, and downtime vanished. Just thinking about it made my temples throb again.

Kagawa clapped my shoulder. “Well, don’t let the old man work you to death. Catch you later.”

“Yeah, later.”

After he left, I headed for the showers, needing to scrub off the grime and sweat of the mission. I slumped down next to the tub, letting the hot water sluice over me. I scrubbed at my sticky skin, half-hoping the steam might slow the relentless spin cycle in my head.

The mission debrief I still hadn’t written. Elder Low’s increasing demands. The never-ending workload. The dusty, silent house. The mountain of dirty laundry and the empty food storage. Father’s logistics company, neglected and probably bleeding money… And underpinning it all, that sharp, piercing ache in my chest that shadowed me constantly—sleeping, curled up, running, even when I was ending an enemy’s life.

I took a shaky breath, testing the tightness. Still there. Shit, am I having a heart attack?

Dismissing the thought, I tossed down the brush and soap, rinsed off quickly, and finished the too-short shower. Outside, the distant pop and crackle of fireworks started. Glancing at my reflection in the misted mirror, I ran a hand through my damp hair. Still presentable enough, even uncombed. Didn’t exactly hurt my looks, I guess. I tightened the belt of my clean yukata and headed out.

Elder Low was waiting, posture rigid as ever. He gestured curtly to the opposite cushion and slid a mission scroll across the low table as soon as I sat.

Our next task: retrieve a sacred jewel box stolen from a high-ranking kingdom official’s residence. No solid leads, no witnesses, but the initial intel pointed towards Nobuga—an antique collector known for being slippery as an eel. He never got his own hands dirty, always using layers of henchmen who conveniently took the fall.

I scanned the intel summary and raised an eyebrow. Two weeks of the clan’s best trackers hitting dead ends. Then, they’d sent Tadashi. Of course. And in just two days, he had sniffed out a solid lead on Nobuga’s current location. Figures.

“So Tadashi’s waiting for me on Tora Island?” I asked, rolling the scroll shut. It seemed the elders were finally seeing sense, letting us work together again. After the Roroa mission, they’d demoted Tadashi and explicitly forbade us from joint operations.

I didn’t care about the joint missions—we could both handle solo work–but Tadashi’s demotion? Treating him like some green recruit? That pissed me off. Shiro took over his duties, and Tadashi got bumped down to Level C—same as the brats fresh out of the academy. I’d even seen him cleaning the damn toilets. What a waste of talent.

The elders wanted to make an example of him—discipline, they said. If even the clan leader’s son got sent to clean toilets, then no one was safe. Maybe it scared the lower ranks into obedience, but it was petty. And now? They didn’t hesitate to use his skills, even if, on paper, he was still scrubbing floors.

Elder Low waved a dismissive hand, interrupting my thoughts. “Tadashi has been assigned another task nearby. You will handle this alone.”

My jaw tightened slightly. Right. So much for seeing sense. “Understood.” I gave a curt nod and left the room, irritation simmering beneath the surface.

Fine. Solo it is.

I arrived on Tora Island around lunchtime. Nobuga was hosting a masquerade auction, offloading some of his collection. Perfect cover. I acquired a mask and blended in with the bidders.

I spent the afternoon scoping the place out. One building had unusually heavy security—likely the warehouse Nobuga was using for the stolen goods.

Night fell. Tailing Nobuga and his guards from the auction hall to the warehouse was simple. Once he slipped inside, I dealt with the four guards posted outside—quiet, efficient takedowns—and slipped through the door after him.

The warehouse felt eerily familiar, laid out just like Father’s warehouses down at the clan port: rows of tall iron shelves stacked high with labeled boxes and crates.

I melted into the shadows between the shelves, out of sight of the guards clustered near the back. I could faintly hear Nobuga giving instructions. One by one, the guards dispersed—easy pickings. I took them out silently.

With the last guard down, I stalked towards the lone figure remaining. Nobuga. He sat at a desk near the back wall, his back to me, writing something. He wore an ostentatious golden-yellow kimono.

Closing the distance silently, I lunged. One arm clamped over his mouth, the other pressed the razor-sharp tip of my kodachi against his throat. He stiffened, a muffled sound of surprise escaping him. He tried to surge upwards, but I had him pinned.

“Stay quiet if you value your life,” I hissed into his ear, wrenching his head back slightly.

Suddenly, the warehouse plunged into darkness. A flicker of movement to my right—I slashed instinctively with my kodachi, hitting empty air. Defenses down for a split second. A sharp sting bloomed on the left side of my neck.

Senbon.

My muscles locked. Nobuga slammed an elbow into my ribs, breaking my hold. My legs buckled.

I hit the floor, hard, utterly paralyzed. A low chuckle sounded from behind me as someone kicked my kodachi away, the blade skittering across the concrete. Nobuga scrambled to his desk, fumbled in a drawer, and produced the metallic clink of handcuffs. They snapped shut around my wrists, locking my hands behind my back.

Shit, shit, shit!

The lights flickered back on, blindingly bright. The paralytic agent kept me from even squinting. Nobuga knelt beside me, roughly shoved a gag into my mouth, and pressed my head to the cold floor. Then, impossibly, he bowed deeply towards the figure standing behind me.

“Intruder secured, Lord Nobuga,” the man I’d just subdued said, his voice laced with deference.

Wait… what?

My mind stalled. So the man we thought was Nobuga turned out to be just a lackey?

“Good work, Kitoru,” a voice replied from behind me.

I froze. Ice flooded my veins, and a steel band clamped around my chest. That voice… Shivers traced paths down my spine. I knew that voice.

He stepped over my prone body, kneeling beside the fake Nobuga, Kitoru. A hand tangled in my hair, yanking my head back brutally, forcing my gaze upwards.

“Well, well… what a pleasant surprise,” grinned a face I knew better than my own. My father. But not. The same sharp eyes, glinting with cold anger, bored into mine. Air refused to enter my lungs. My head throbbed violently.

No. Fucking. Way.

Father was dead. I’d watched the cremation flames consume his body. I’d carried his ashes to the clan cemetery myself. Panic clawed at my throat. I wanted to struggle, to scream, to move , but the paralysis held me fast. My eyes, locked open, stung and began to water.

“Don’t look so surprised,” the man—my father’s ghost, his double?—said, a low, humorless laugh escaping him.

His thumb brushed across my cheek, wiping away a traitorous tear, the touch sickeningly gentle against my skin.

“How have you been, Sadi?”

“ L ord Nobuga, the event’s about to start,” someone announced from the doorway.

The man wearing my father’s face clicked his tongue, releasing my hair. He stood, brushing off his kimono.

“What about the intruder, Lord?” Kitoru asked, still kneeling.

The voice— Nobuga’s voice, not Father’s—was cold, indifferent. “Cargo ship’s leaving soon. Pack him in a crate, weight it down, and dump him in the sea.”

My throat went dry. My mind spun, trying to reconcile the face with the order. Father… no. Why would he do this? If he—if Nobuga—wanted me dead, why not just finish it here?

Maybe he wants it slow and painful, a dark corner of my mind whispered.

No. That wasn’t Father. Father was dead. Ashes. This was Nobuga, the thief, the bastard who stole the jewel box. He just… he had Father’s face. That was all. It wasn’t him .

Nobuga swept out without another glance. Kitoru hauled me roughly across the floor towards a stack of empty wooden crates.

Then darkness swallowed me as the crate lid slammed shut. Sounds became muffled, distant. The paralyzing fear intensified, sharpened by the suffocating blackness. How could he know? This was Father’s favorite punishment—the isolation room, dark, cramped, silent. He knew I hated it, knew I’d rather take a whipping than be locked away to “reflect.”

In the absolute dark, absolute silence, every failure, every regret always clawed its way to the surface. Trapped. Buried alive. Hope drowned.

The crate swayed violently, then slammed down onto something hard. More thuds followed—other crates, stacking above, around me. My heart hammered against my ribs. Am I really going to die like this? Trapped in a box?

I strained against the paralysis, trying to fight the drug, desperate to move even an inch, but my muscles refused to obey. My head throbbed. Suffocating. Panic tightened its fist around my chest, stealing my breath.

Help.

Anyone…

Help me.

A sharp clarity pierced through the swirling panic.

((Sadi.))

I blinked, groaning against the gag. How long had I been out? Sensation was returning—pins and needles in my fingers. But trapped, folded up like this? Useless.

Was someone looking for me? I tried shouting, the sound barely a strangled noise against the gag. I threw my shoulder against the crate lid, a pathetic, restricted movement. What if I was buried too deep? What if no one could reach me? Panic surged again, choking me.

((Sadi, calm down.))

I froze, recognizing the voice echoing inside my own head.

((Tadashi?)) I frowned internally. Since when can he use telepathy?! For a split second, I was just stunned, then a faint awareness bloomed—a sense of Tadashi’s presence nearby. How did I know? How did this even work?

((Hello? Anybody there?)) I felt like an idiot, even just thinking it.

A flicker of amusement seemed to brush against my thoughts—Tadashi, probably smiling faintly. I mentally snorted. (( Oi! Stop smirking! I’m trapped in a goddamn crate!))

The mental ‘Tadashi’ clicked his tongue. (( I know that. Just be quiet for a minute, you’re giving me a headache.))

Oh. So he knew exactly where I was? Concentrating, I felt a strange sense of direction, like a compass needle pointing towards him. Huh. Useful, if we ever got teamed up again.

Wood scraped above me. A moment later, the lid was pried open, revealing Tadashi peering down, his face etched with concern. He reached in, quickly working the lock on the handcuffs. Freedom. I scrambled out, slumping against the side of the crate, rubbing my raw wrists. We were in the dark, swaying hold of a ship. The relentless rocking sent waves of nausea through me.

Tadashi looked like hell. Pale, clothes plastered to his skin, water dripping from his soaked hair. Did he swim out here?

“Came as soon as I heard you call,” he said, his voice slightly breathless. “Luckily, you weren’t too far out. Are you alright?”

“Besides being sore, numb, and generally pissed off? Peachy,” I grumbled, rolling my eyes. “What the hell was that? How did you hear me? How did you know where I was?”

Tadashi studied me for a beat, then shifted, looking uncomfortable. “I think… it’s the slave seal. When you were panicking, you must have accidentally activated some kind of connection.”

The seal. My stomach tightened. I remembered the incident a month and a half ago—helping Tadashi in the courtyard, then sudden, blinding agony radiating from the base of my spine. Just like when Father triggered the seal, only infinitely worse. I’d thought Father was killing me then, though I didn’t know why. I’d woken up in the infirmary, Tadashi beside me, Father’s corpse on the next bed.

After the funeral, Tadashi had explained. “When your father died, the seal was damaged… it nearly killed you. I couldn’t remove it—it’s permanent. I had to… take control. Stabilize it.”

Take control. The words still echoed, leaving a bitter taste. Did that make Tadashi my master now? Bound to him, just like I’d been bound to Father?

“I won’t use it,” Tadashi had insisted, seeing the look on my face. “I won’t force you. I’m just channeling energy to keep it stable. That’s all.”

I’d known he meant it. We’d become friends after the Roroa mission. I trusted him not to hurt me. But the fact remained: he held the leash now. I’d never be free of the damn thing. If Tadashi died, I died. Just another chain wrapped around my life.

Tch.

I swallowed hard, forcing the bitterness down. “Okay,” I said curtly, pushing myself to my feet. The ship lurched, and I braced myself against the crate. “So this connection… anything else I should know?”

Tadashi explained that when he’d repaired and bonded with the seal, some of our energy pathways must’ve linked. Neither he nor Anko, the seal expert, had known what side effects to expect—until now. Telepathy and tracking, apparently.

Well, that’s not entirely bad, is it? I drew a slow breath, trying to compartmentalize the whole ‘slave seal’ thing. I glanced at Tadashi, managing a weak smirk.

“Okay, but the important thing is, you can’t peek into my thoughts when I’m… uh… busy, right? With a girlfriend?”

Tadashi just glared, a look that clearly screamed ‘You are the stupidest person I know.’

I let out a short, humorless chuckle. Right. Priorities. I shook my head, the movement making the nausea worse. Time to focus.

“So,” I said, meeting Tadashi’s gaze. “Who exactly is Nobuga?”

W e went back and forth, comparing the few clues we had. I wasn’t sure I was ready to hear the answer, but Tadashi gave it anyway. Calm. Detached. Like he’d already run the numbers in his head. He had a point: if my father had merely faked his death, my slave seal probably wouldn’t have nearly self-destructed. So, we were working on the theory that Nobuga was my father’s brother.

It wasn’t entirely out of left field. Father never spoke about his family. The house I grew up in wasn’t ancestral; he’d bought it after marrying Mother. There were no family trees, no photos of his side—unlike Mother’s family albums, which Father had tossed out after the divorce. Growing up, Father was the center of my world; I’d practically turned myself inside out trying to earn his approval. He was all that mattered. So, even if Nobuga was his brother, it didn’t change anything. My mission was clear: retrieve the jewel box. Capturing Nobuga wasn’t part of the brief.

“He’s wanted for countless thefts. Are you really going to just let him go if you find him?” Tadashi asked as we reached Tora Island’s harbor, water still dripping from our soaked clothes after the swim from the cargo ship.

Honestly? I didn’t care about Nobuga himself. He’d ordered me thrown into the sea—any flicker of curiosity about some long-lost uncle was extinguished right there. He meant nothing to me. Whether he ended up in chains or vanished off the face of the earth wasn’t my problem.

But… I don’t know. Maybe he knew things. About Father. About why our family was erased. Maybe I could ask him what happened. Why Father kept his past a secret.

Maybe—just maybe—I had grandparents somewhere, or cousins I’d never met.

And maybe, someday… I could return home without feeling so alone.

I pushed the thought away. Too complicated. “He’s just a rotten thief,” I said, shrugging. “If the clan hasn’t ordered his capture, why make the extra work? My job is the box.” The implication was clear: Nobuga’s man, Kitoru, had likely taken the box, or knew where it was. Following him was the only lead we had.

Tadashi gave me a searching look but didn’t press. We slipped back towards the villa where the auction had been held. It was chaos. Overturned tables, spilled drinks staining the carpets, torn decorations—clearly evacuated in a hurry, tipped off somehow. We exchanged a look. A professional job.

“I’ll check the office; you take the warehouse,” Tadashi said. I nodded.

The warehouse where I’d been ambushed was now cavernously empty. Shelves scraped bare. Impressive, how fast they’d cleared out. Nobuga—or Kitoru acting for him—was efficient.

(( There’s no paper trail, but an atlas just fell out near the bookshelf .)) Tadashi’s voice echoed in my head, catching me mid-step as I turned away from the warehouse.

(( Anything useful? )) I asked, already moving, tracking his location with the link between us.

(( A few places are marked. Looks like some kind of travel route. ))

I crossed the garden and entered Nobuga’s temporary office. Tadashi stood near the bookshelf, holding open the atlas.

He pointed to the page without a word. I scanned the circled locations.

“Okay,” I said, tucking the atlas under my arm. “Looks like we have a trail.”

Tadashi followed me out. I stopped at the gate and turned, raising an eyebrow. “Where do you think you’re going? Don’t you have your own mission?”

He hesitated, looking momentarily flustered, like a fish caught on a line. He then shrugged. “Ah… well. I need a vacation.”

I stared at him. “Seriously? That’s the best you’ve got?” It was possibly the lamest excuse I’d ever heard.

Tadashi winced. “My mission’s practically finished. It wasn’t… critical.”

“Right,” I drawled. “The kind of ‘not critical’ mission the elders pull you off regular duties for? Like, say, toilet cleaning level?”

He bit back a laugh and rolled his eyes. “You know I can’t discuss details. Point is, I don’t need to report back immediately.”

“Wow.” I shook my head. “Elder Low would whip you for saying that out loud.”

Tadashi snorted, snatched the atlas from under my arm, and started walking. I trailed after him.

“I’m just saying, in case you hit your head and forgot—we’re not supposed to work together. The elders hate it when someone as ‘important’ as you prioritizes anything—or anyone—over the clan.”

“I’m not the heir anymore,” he said with a shrug. “Since I was demoted and they had me cleaning toilets, I think they’ve made it clear I’m not that important.”

I looked at him for a moment longer, still skeptical.

“They won’t know I’m with you,” he added casually. “Unless someone tells them.”

I didn’t say anything, but a small smile crept onto my lips. He wasn’t saying he was worried about me facing my maybe-uncle alone, but it was written all over his face. And maybe… maybe I was glad he was sticking around. My head felt less like a pressure cooker with him nearby.

“Want to stop for ramen?” I asked, falling into step beside him. It was easier than saying thank you.

Tadashi snorted. “Only if they have honey-grilled shrimp topping. No chili.”

I laughed. “Deal.”

Our first lead took us to a smoky drinking den where someone matching Kitoru’s description—posing as Nobuga—had been seen. The nervous waiter confirmed Kitoru had arranged a meet-up for tonight at the night market near Sakurazuka Shrine.

We staked out the bustling market, splitting up. No sign of him.

“Maybe he’s late?” Tadashi mused, suggesting we check out a dragon dance performance happening nearby.

“What if he shows while we’re gone?”

“Relax. I’ve got eyes watching the main paths. We’ll check in.”

The dragon dance was impressive, twin serpents of silk and bamboo weaving through the cheering crowd. It was packed; Tadashi nearly got flattened by someone’s oversized backpack, making me snort with laughter. We wandered through the bazaar stalls afterwards.

I picked up a small, carved paperweight for Elder Mei, hoping it might cheer her up. Tadashi bought a delicate flower hairpin.

“For Shiro,” he mumbled, asking if I could give it to her later. Apparently, she was still freezing him out.

I found myself looking at a simple woven bracelet with a sunflower pattern.

“For your girlfriend?” Tadashi asked, appearing beside me.

I scrunched up my nose and shook my head. “Thought maybe… for my sister. Or Mother,” I murmured, voicing one of the noises in my head—surprising myself.

I rarely talked about them, especially not since… everything. But with Tadashi, who already knew most of it anyway, the words slipped out. The festive atmosphere, the brief reprieve from the chase… it lowered my guard. The knot in my chest loosened fractionally.

I looked at the crowd and took a deep breath. It had been a month and a half since Father was killed. He’d divorced Mother years ago, but still… shouldn’t she know her ex-husband died? And… should I move closer to her?

I could live on my own. I didn’t want to burden her. But if I were nearby, maybe I could visit her. Keep her safe. Still, I had big responsibilities in the clan.

Would the elders even let me move?

Tadashi waited patiently. I cleared my throat, pointing at the stall. “Stuff’s cheap here. Should I get them something?”

“Ah.” Tadashi nodded, scanning the wares. He picked out a light blue bracelet similar to the one I held and a small knitted purse. “Your sister likes this color blue, remember? And your mother might like this.” He smiled faintly. Right. He’d spent time working at their house before. He’d knew their tastes.

“Do you… want to stop by their place? After this?” Tadashi asked carefully. “It’s not far off the route Nobuga seems to be taking.”

I was surprised, but the idea settled warmly in my chest. “Yeah. Maybe. We’ll see.”

I bought us both cotton candy. We ate it watching kids light sparklers, the brief moment of normalcy feeling strange. Then we split up again to check in with our informants.

Tadashi’s voice came through minutes later. (( Kitoru showed at the dango stall. Waited, but his contact was a no-show.))

((Still there?)) I asked, already moving towards the stall.

((Gone. Left a message with the stall keeper—my informant. Said if a man in a white robe came looking, tell him to meet at the next stop.))

I frowned. White robe? Mitsurugi clan colors. A cold dread settled in my stomach. This wasn’t just about a stolen jewel box anymore. If the Mitsurugi were involved… something bad was going down.

The chase was back on. We pursued Kitoru relentlessly, but the man was slippery. He favoured crowded spots—waterfalls, scenic viewpoints, amusement parks, bustling city districts, even a high-stakes casino—changing meet-up locations constantly. It felt less like a mission and more like a bizarre, high-speed tour of every tourist trap in the region.

Days blurred together. Itaishami Waterfall, Tattun Ropeway, Uorohime Tower, Daisuku Amusement Park, Junjuku District, Leeshan Casino… the list went on. Tadashi got his ‘vacation,’ alright.

Four days in and nearly out of money, I stopped at a clan safe house, wired for more funds, and submitted an official request to have Tadashi formally assigned to my mission. He kept glancing around like he expected to be caught—we both knew exactly what kind of punishment waited if he didn’t check in.

I wasn’t stupid. The elders wouldn’t have sent him out of town unless his original mission was important. He’d been gone five days without contact—far too long by Tadashi’s standards. He must’ve already been sanctioned.

Tadashi took half a day to file a written report for whatever mission he’d dropped, sent it through a secure channel, then came back looking only slightly less anxious.

The next day, our luck finally turned. We tracked Kitoru to a secluded residence—presumably one of Nobuga’s hideouts.

Tadashi, ever resourceful, managed to infiltrate the house disguised as a temporary servant, mapping the layout. I spent hours observing from the treeline, timing guard patrols. Planning the infiltration was meticulous, but finding Nobuga and Kitoru inside wasn’t as difficult as I’d imagined.

The problem was, after all this, we still hadn’t found the cursed jewel box.
