S omething was off.

I looked up, staring at the imposing nameplate on Sadi’s front gate. The silence behind the black wood planks felt heavier than usual, and the growing unease in my chest told me this wasn’t going to be just another Saturday.

Sadi and I were tasked with checking, recounting, and recording the items in one of our clan’s warehouses. Sadi’s father, Mr. Akito, was overseeing us. I wiped my damp palms on my kimono.

For some reason, facing Mr. Akito always made me nervous. I already knew Sadi disliked me, but I wasn’t sure about his father. Mr. Akito was always cold towards me, even though he never criticized me publicly.

I took a deep breath, trying to center myself. It’ll be fine. Everything will be alright . I knocked and waited, but there was no answer.

“Mr. Akito?” I called, knocking again.

Still no one opened the door. Should I just wait here? I stood by the gate, nodding or bowing to those who passed. I hadn’t misheard, had I? Elder Yun had told me to meet Mr. Akito at 6:00 a.m. sharp.

But… why is it taking so long? Is Mr. Akito out?

My legs were aching when I finally decided to sit on the steps in front of the gate. Just as I was about to, I spotted the corner of a piece of paper sticking out from under the door.

Is this for me? I picked it up.

To: The Idiot

From: Sadi

Why are you at my house? Are you offering to mow the lawn?? Today we’re working at the clan warehouse at the docks. Father is waiting for you at his office before 6:30.

I bet you just assumed he’d meet you at home. You should’ve asked and confirmed the meeting location beforehand, idiot.

Oh, no! 6:30?!

How long had I been waiting here? I started running.

Mr. Akito owned a shipping container and warehousing business. He rented some of his warehouses at the docks to our clan. The docks were located at the northern edge of the city. A ten-minute ride on horseback, but a thirty-minute walk.

I ran as fast as I could. My chest ached, and I was gasping for breath by the time I reached Mr. Akito’s office.

Mr. Akito was talking to someone. He turned when I arrived and excused himself.

“Good morning, Mr. Akito,” I greeted, bowing respectfully.

Mr. Akito narrowed his eyes at me but didn’t reply. He simply walked past me into the next room. I followed and closed the door. Mr. Akito picked up two notebooks from his desk. I recognized one of them as my activity report.

“Masada needs to know you’re an hour late,” he said, making a note in my report. “I can’t speak for his parenting, but Sadi is always thirty minutes early for any appointment, especially with someone deserving of respect.”

Heat flooded my cheeks. I opened my mouth to apologize, but Mr. Akito cut me off.

“You don’t value my time, Tadashi. Do you know how many important meetings I had to reschedule or cancel because of you?”

“I’m sorry, Mr. Akito, I—”

“I have a busy day. You and Sadi just get to work. I’ll check on you later,” Mr. Akito said sharply. “Find Sadi in the next warehouse and give him this book. He knows what to do.”

He rose from his desk and handed me both notebooks.

“Do your job properly. Don’t disappoint me again,” he added. “I’ll leave your punishment for your tardiness to Masada.”

Oh… I hugged the books tightly. People might think I’d get lighter punishments because I was the clan leader’s son. But that wasn’t true. Father didn’t want to be seen as playing favorites. If I made a mistake, he’d usually punish me more severely than the other clan officials.

Ignoring the tightness in my chest, I bowed. “Yes, Mr. Akito.”

I trudged along the small path to the next warehouse. Sadi greeted me there with a smirk. I trailed silently behind him as we navigated the rows of warehouses, finally reaching a shadowy dead-end alley. Sadi ran his hand along the upper right wall until he found a hidden lever. An unsuspecting door swung open. Moss obscured the lever and number plate, making them almost impossible to spot. Sadi stepped inside.

The room was spacious and cool. The ceiling was high. Sunlight streamed in from a row of large windows high up, just below the roof. Cardboard boxes were neatly stacked on storage shelves that almost reached the ceiling. In the corner, there was a small office cubicle and a rest area, complete with a tiny toilet.

Sadi went to the desk and pulled a thick book from the bookcase beside it.

“Here you go,” Sadi called, handing it to me. “This is the inventory log. Your job is to count every single item in these boxes and write the number down in this book and on the card attached to each box.”

I nodded.

Wait a minute…

“All of this is my job? What are you doing?”

Sadi grinned. “Isn’t it obvious? My job is to fill in for my father and write your activity report.”

What?! I glared at Sadi.

“I can’t possibly count all of this by myself. There are…” I looked around the room. Two hundred? Five hundred? A thousand?? “Hundreds of boxes! How long is this going to take?”

Sadi shrugged. “That’s why you need to start now. It’s your fault we’ve already wasted an hour. And because of you, I had to move boxes into the warehouse this morning before even having breakfast. Now hurry up. Or do you want me to write ‘work incomplete’ in your activity report?”

I forced myself to stay quiet. My activity report was already full of red marks. I’d made a lot of mistakes this month. I had to complete this task successfully.

Sadi then opened my report. The first page was a brief profile about me. “Tadashi Masamune, hmm… Wow, it’s your birthday today?”

I frowned. My birthday? Oh, I’d forgotten… My family rarely celebrated it. I had wondered why Mother was baking a cake this morning. She had also told me to come home early, by lunchtime at the latest.

“I can’t stay here long. I’ll do half, and you do the other half,” I said, crossing my arms.

“Nope. You still owe me, remember? You promised to do whatever I said. Don’t forget, your little brother would’ve drowned without my help.”

Oh, no… why did he have to bring that up? My stomach clenched every time I remembered Raito vanishing during the festival. But Sadi was right. Raito was saved because Sadi had helped me. I had also made a promise to him.

I clenched my fists.

“You should be grateful I’m only asking you to do this, and not something embarrassing or dangerous,” Sadi continued. “Now, start with the row at the far end. Hurry up, before I write down that from 7:30 to 8:30, you were just sitting around relaxing.”

I had no choice but to comply. I started working immediately. The first box I opened contained first-aid kits. There were fifteen of them. I wrote it down in the book and on the card attached to the box. It only took a few moments, but the problem was, I didn’t know how many boxes were in this room.

I let out a soft groan, a mixture of frustration and a bleak amusement at my own bad luck.

There was no way I could finish by lunchtime. I had some tea before leaving, but Mother hadn’t packed me a lunch today, probably because she wanted me to eat at home. Normally, the person overseeing my activity would be responsible for providing me with a meal, but Sadi wasn’t reliable… he hadn’t even eaten himself.

Looks like I’m skipping lunch today.

I had just finished checking one row of shelves when Sadi stood up.

“I’m going to get some food. I’ll be back later this afternoon,” he said.

He’s just leaving me here?

Sadi sneered. “Why the long face? Scared?”

I shook my head quickly.

“Don’t let anything go missing or break. You don’t have any money, so you wouldn’t be able to replace it,” Sadi warned before he left. He pulled the lever outside the warehouse. I realized too late, but Sadi had locked me inside.

I raced to the door, banging on it. “Sadi! Don’t lock it!” I yelled, but he ignored me.

There had to be a lever inside, but I hadn’t been paying attention when Sadi opened and closed the door. Now, I couldn’t find it anywhere.

I looked around. The room was still bright with sunlight, but what would happen when it got dark?

Aah… I hurried back to the shelves, determined to finish my work as quickly as possible.

When I finally finished, my hands, legs, and shoulders were aching. There was no clock in the warehouse, so I didn’t know what time it was. It was starting to get dark.

Why hasn’t Sadi come back? Surely he hasn’t forgotten I’m still in here?

I paced anxiously. Mother must be waiting for me at home. I hadn’t contacted her all afternoon; she must be worried. I had to find a way out of here. I scanned my surroundings. There was only one door, the one I had entered through. I looked at the large windows near the ceiling. Just glass with wooden frames, no bars. Below the windows was a narrow catwalk for cleaning access.

I could slip out through a window, but how could I get up there? There were ladders attached to each shelving unit for reaching the top shelves, but they were built into the frame and couldn’t be detached. I also didn’t have any tools or weapons with me, just a single kunai.

Wait… there seemed to be a supply of rope in one of the boxes.

I found the shelf I was looking for, in the handyman supplies section. Nails, hammers, rope, paint cans, brushes, and other things. I grabbed a coil of rope and threw it up towards the metal railing of the catwalk. I wrapped the other end around my hand and slowly climbed.

Just as I neared the top, a shadow suddenly appeared behind the window. The sudden movement startled me, and I lost my grip. I fell onto the top shelf, landing in the gap between some boxes, narrowly avoiding tumbling to the floor. At the same moment, the window above shattered.

I froze, clinging to the top shelf’s railing. Across from me, someone climbed in through the window. No, three people.

Our clan warehouse was being robbed.

“ H urry,” a gruff voice whispered.

I pressed myself against the shelf, holding my breath. I hoped they didn’t see me.

From behind a large box, I watched the three of them rappel down.

“Are you sure she stashed it here, Otone?” another man asked, giving a small cough.

“I don’t know, Moto, but I caught a glimpse of her last night before she got away,” replied Otone, his voice raspy. “She disappeared at the end of that dead-end alley. This warehouse is suspicious too, isn’t it? We searched it over and over, but there was no door. What do you think, Mr. Kishu?”

Kishu, the gruff-voiced man, murmured in agreement. “This warehouse is likely their secret stash. I’ve already broken through multiple protective seals, but there’s one more in here I can’t crack.”

“Let me check around the room,” Moto said.

Oh, no! I quickly pulled the rope I had used, which was still slung over the railing. A moment later, Moto walked past my hiding place, without looking up.

“The room’s empty, boss,” Moto finally reported.

“If the intruder isn’t here, she’s probably already taken the document. However, there’s still a slight chance she hid it here,” Kishu, the leader of the thieves, said. “Alright, let’s make sure. Split up and check the contents of these boxes one by one, starting with the shelves at that end.”

“This is a wild goose chase,” Otone grumbled.

“Just do it, and stop complaining,” Kishu snapped.

“But it’s still early evening…is this really okay, boss? What if someone comes?” Moto asked.

“Well, just kill them!” Kishu replied with a chuckle.

My heart leaped into my throat when Otone started approaching my hiding place. Not yet, he hadn’t reached me, but he was only three shelves away.

I wiped my sweaty palms. What was I supposed to do? If I stayed quiet, they would eventually reach me and find me. If I tried to escape, I had already pulled the rope back. Even if I could throw it back up without them noticing, I couldn’t climb it quickly. They would definitely catch me.

“What kind of thing are we looking for, anyway?” Otone asked as he rummaged through a box. “The boxes in this section are all filled with stationery.”

Otone moved away, returning to Kishu. I let out a slow, shaky breath.

“I’ve scanned the seals in this area. It seems like none of the objects in these boxes are sealed with spiritual power,” Kishu said.

“Well, just look for anything that could have a stack of papers slipped inside. If there were any books or parchment scrolls in that stationery section, the document might be hidden in there.”

Otone nodded and went back to the stationery shelves.

“Boss, there’s an office cubicle at the end of the room,” Moto said. “I’ll check there.”

“Fine,” Kishu replied.

Moto then approached the office area, which was right in front of the shelves where I was hiding, only a few steps away. I froze. If I tried to make a run for it, would I survive?

I knew the elders planned to train me in more advanced techniques. Even so, I was still a beginner; my training had only just begun. There were many techniques I hadn’t mastered yet.

I wasn’t sure I could face these thieves, especially three against one. If I recklessly challenged them, it would be suicide.

My mind raced, desperately searching for an escape, but I was trapped.

Aah… what am I going to do?

This is it. I’m dead.

Suddenly, I heard the sound of something tearing.

“Why did you rip the lantern, boss?” Otone asked, stopping his search.

“Well… you never know, it might have been hidden inside the lantern,” Kishu said. “I couldn’t figure out how to open the top, so I just tore it.”

That lantern… the one that costs more than my weekly allowance? I swallowed, my stomach churning with fresh anxiety.

Sadi’s warning echoed in my mind. These items weren’t ours; they belonged to the clan. And the clan treasurer was notoriously meticulous, hating any kind of waste. I was doomed if he found out I was involved.

If I managed to get out of here safely, how could I replace the damaged items? I didn’t know how much allowance Father had been saving for me.

I shifted uneasily, every muscle screaming to move, but fear kept me frozen.

“Boss, I haven’t found anything yet, but there’s something strange here,” Moto said. “According to the inventory log, there was a stocktaking today.”

My heart pounded. Moto had found the inventory log I had been filling out.

“Oh… yeah, that’s right, someone was recounting the stock in the warehouse today?” Otone asked. “There are markings on the outer boxes too. Do you think they’re still around here?”

I squeezed my eyes shut. Oh, God… are they going to check this warehouse again?

Moto’s footsteps echoed back and forth around the office cubicle. He opened and closed the door.

“The bathroom’s empty. No hiding places around here. I also checked the gaps between the empty boxes at the back of the warehouse. No one’s there, so it looks like the warehouse worker has left,” Moto reported. “But I think it’s best we come back tonight, boss. What if the stocktaking isn’t finished and the worker comes back?”

Kishu was silent for a moment. “If the worker comes back, they’ll see the things we’ve damaged. They’ll tighten security in this place, so it’s better if—”

The click of a lever and the heavy scrape of a door suddenly came from the entrance. The three thieves instantly ran to the back of the warehouse, hiding behind a stack of empty boxes. Someone entered. My stomach dropped.

That had to be Sadi. The warehouse door opening meant I could escape! But… what if the thieves caught him? What if they killed him?!

“Tadashi!” Sadi called. “How’s the work going? Finished yet?”

I shrank back. I was in the middle of the furthest row of shelves. If I suddenly jumped down, ran to the front, and dragged Sadi out of there, could we be faster than the three thieves? I reached into the box nearest to me. It contained hammers. I grabbed one.

“My father couldn’t come—” Sadi stopped, as if just realizing I wasn’t there. “Hey, where’s that idiot?”

One of the thieves hiding near me slipped out. Moto. He watched Sadi, who had started searching for me between the shelves. Moto was carrying a kodachi. I gripped the hammer tighter. My heart pounded in my chest. I tried to control my breathing.

“Tadashi, don’t mess around,” Sadi called, approaching the shelves where I was hiding. “Your father was looking for you—”

Moto passed beneath me. I jumped down and swung the hammer at his head. He yelled, throwing me off his shoulder. I scrambled up, bumping into Sadi, and dragged him towards the door.

I was lucky they hadn’t expected me. But as we neared the door, a volley of throwing knives whizzed towards us. One of them grazed my upper arm.

“AAH!” I stumbled.

Sadi instantly pulled me up, yanked out the knife, and shoved me outside. The thieves roared angrily and chased after us. I ran as fast as I could, following Sadi, who knew the area better.

“Who were those guys?!” Sadi gasped, struggling to breathe.

“I don’t know!” I shouted. “They broke in through the window and were searching for something in the warehouse.”

Sadi led me towards an intersection. “Father’s still at the office. I’ll get him. Turn right and hide, okay?!”

I nodded. We split up at the intersection. The road led to Hanbo, a rough part of town. I ran towards the small market there, hoping to disappear into the crowd. But the sun had already set. The shops were starting to close; there weren’t many people around.

I gasped for breath. Where could I hide?

I glanced behind me. Otone was still tailing me. I couldn’t believe it. Why was he still after me? Shouldn’t thieves run away when they’re discovered? Was it because I had injured one of them? Or because I had overheard them searching for something? But I didn’t even know their full names, and their faces were unclear. Was it simply because they didn’t want any witnesses?

I looked around. My gaze landed on a clinic. That clinic… yes! I remembered when Elder Yun had shown me the locations of our clan’s safe houses on the map.

There should be a house around here that we could use to take refuge from pursuers. Like the other clan warehouses and buildings, it was hidden and protected by multiple seals. Ordinary people wouldn’t be able to penetrate them.

Wait, but how did those thieves get into the warehouse?

Hold on… oh yeah, their boss said he had scanned the seals in the warehouse. So Kishu was a seal expert. Otone clearly couldn’t break seals. I had to find that safe house, and fast.

I scanned for the landmarks I remembered from the map, spotting a narrow alley to the left of the clinic. I hurried down it, searching for the right house number, finally finding a dilapidated, boarded-up house.

I unlocked the layers of spiritual seals, pried open the door bar with the hammer I was carrying, and slipped inside. I resealed the protective wards so Otone couldn’t follow me.

Inside, the house was pitch black. I felt my way along the walls. There were no other doors. Then I found a back door. As soon as I stepped out of the house, I found myself in front of a small shrine lit by flickering candles.

Oh… the wooden house was just a camouflage. So this shrine is the real safe house?

I entered the shrine and hid behind a pillar. As I stopped, I realized the throwing knife wound on my arm was still bleeding. It hurt. I winced. Was the cut deep?

I was about to remove my haori to use as a bandage when suddenly someone twisted my shoulder from behind and slammed my back against the pillar. Agonizing pain shot through my arm as the attacker pinned it against the pillar.

“Be quiet or die,” the person hissed, pressing the tip of a kunai against my throat.

I blinked, trying to focus in the flickering candlelight. My attacker was shrouded in shadow.

“Wait…Tadashi?” a familiar voice exclaimed. I frowned, trying to place it.

“What are you doing here?!” The person released me.

I cradled my throbbing arm. Their face came into view as they stepped back.

Oh…

“Anko?” I whispered.

Anko was my cousin, though we weren’t particularly close. She never visited, and I mostly knew her as a teacher at the academy – the same academy I attended. I might even have her as an instructor next semester.

Anko was usually busy at the academy, rarely seen at the clan hall. But she’d been given a mission a few days ago.

“Don’t tell me Uncle Masada asked you to tail me,” Anko said, sounding annoyed. “This is dangerous work. If he doubted my abilities, he should have chosen someone else for this mission.”

I shook my head quickly, trying to reassure her.

“It’s not like that. I’m hiding here because I was being chased by thieves,” I replied.

Anko stared at me, speechless.

“Huh? What do you mean? Why were thieves chasing you ?”

I quickly explained about the thieves. Anko rubbed her face wearily.

“You said the boss, the seal expert, was named Kishu?”

I nodded.

Anko sighed. “How much do you know about my mission?”

I often sat in on clan meetings with my father. Not every mission was discussed, but the elders had mentioned Anko’s assignment yesterday.

“Elder Yun just said you were retrieving documents related to the Kurasai case,” I said, trying to remember. “I don’t know any of the details.”

“It’s better that way. The point is, those thieves who broke into the warehouse were after me ,” Anko explained. “They’re working for the enemy in Kurasai.”

She walked to the window and peered outside. The candlelight illuminated the small, walled garden surrounding the shrine, its boundaries formed by the backs of neighboring houses.

“So, yesterday I managed to get some of the documents, but Kishu caught me scanning his room,” Anko continued. “I managed to escape, even though I was injured. Last night, I stopped by the warehouse to grab a first-aid kit and some supplies, then hid here.”

“You’re injured?” I asked, looking at Anko more closely.

She shrugged. “I’ve taken care of most of it. Just a lingering pain in my lower back.”

That explained her limp and wince.

“Why didn’t you return to headquarters?” I asked.

“My mission isn’t over,” Anko said, shaking her head. “There are still documents I need to retrieve – documents Kishu most likely sealed and took. I have to get them while they’re distracted. If I go back now, they’ll escape.”

I leaned back against the pillar. Kishu’s relentless pursuit all the way here meant those documents must be incredibly important. Shouldn’t she deliver them to headquarters first?

Otone, locked out of the safe house, would have returned to the warehouse to report to Kishu. Kishu and Moto were probably already on their way back.

I needed to convince Anko to escape before they arrived. With her injury, I didn’t think she could handle all three of them if they found us here. Even if she agreed, she probably wouldn’t get far.

“What if I take the documents to headquarters?” I suggested. “I can also inform the elders that you need assistance.”

Anko considered this for a moment, then smiled.

“That’s not a bad idea.”

Anko then unclasped the choker she was wearing and handed it to me.

“Take this to headquarters. Don’t lose it, okay?”

I examined the choker, sensing the powerful, intricate seal woven into its design. My fingers traced the patterns, deciphering the hidden message within. It referred to someone blood-related to the seal’s creator.

The message indicated the seal could only be opened by the highest authority. I frowned.

“So, the document you retrieved is sealed in here, and only Father can open it?” I asked to confirm.

Anko stared at me in surprise. “You can read it?”

I felt my cheeks flush. I could sense seals and decipher their hidden messages – a skill I hadn’t yet revealed to my father, though my instructor at the academy seemed aware of it.

“A little,” I mumbled.

“Interesting…” Anko said, her eyes gleaming with a newfound respect. “Go now. Secure the docu—”

Anko gasped, her face draining of color.

“They’re here,” she hissed. “Quick, hide!”

I raced up the steps to the shrine’s loft, where three open doorways led to different rooms. I chose the far right, a storage room with a high ceiling and a large statue. Spotting a small alcove at the statue’s base, I squeezed inside.

Footsteps ascended the stairs, then moved across the loft, approaching my hiding place.

I held my breath. The person entered and circled the room. They peered behind the statue. My heart hammered against my ribs. Don’t look here. Don’t look here, I pleaded silently. The person then left. I closed my eyes, slightly relieved.

The sounds of a struggle drifted up from below, followed by Anko’s cry of pain. Then, silence. Terror clenched my chest. Had they killed her?

“Moto!” Kishu yelled from downstairs. “What’s the situation upstairs?”

“Clear, boss,” Moto replied.

“Did you find the document?” Kishu asked. “I searched the woman, but I found nothing. Otone’s checking the rooms downstairs.”

“There’s a bedroom, a small library, and a storage room up here. I’ll look around,” Moto answered.

“Check the library first,” Kishu ordered. “I’ll interrogate this intruder.”

“Okay, boss!” Moto’s footsteps moved towards the next room.

I had to get out. Crawling out of the alcove, I crouched and scanned the room. Crates and boxes filled the space, leaving only one way out.

My gaze drifted upwards.

High above the statue, a small window was barred with decaying wooden slats. I checked my pockets – the hammer was still there. But breaking through those bars would alert Moto.

What could I do?

A sickening thud echoed from below, followed by another scream from Anko. The sounds continued, a brutal symphony that made my blood run cold. I couldn’t bear to hear it, but I was too terrified to intervene.

I had to get out. I had to get help.

I peeked behind me to make sure Moto wasn’t standing outside the storage room. Okay, clear. My gaze fell upon a crate.

A signal flare!

The crate’s label confirmed it. If I lit the flare, nearby clan members would see it. They could rescue Anko. Snatching a flare and striker from the crate, I tucked them into my kimono and started climbing.

Okay… here goes nothing.

I swung the hammer, splintering the wood. I yanked the broken slats free just as Moto’s footsteps pounded towards the room.

“You!” he roared.

Splinters tore into my arm, but I pushed myself through the opening. Unfortunately, Moto grabbed my leg before I could escape.

I kicked back hard, connecting squarely with Moto’s face. My foot seemed to sink into his eye socket.

“Argh!” Moto recoiled, releasing his grip. I scrambled out of the window. It was too small for Moto to follow. Quickly lighting the flare, I tossed it onto the roof and ran, searching for a way down as red smoke billowed into the night sky.

My leg throbbed with each stride, but Moto was already on my heels. I sprinted across the garden and into a narrow, winding alleyway, the flickering lanterns of the main street a tantalizing few hundred meters ahead. But it wasn’t far enough.

Moto caught me.

We tumbled to the ground, a tangle of limbs. I kicked and thrashed, but he grabbed my injured arm.

“AAh!” I cried out, doubling over in pain. Moto slammed me to the ground and twisted my arm behind my back. I gasped. A searing pain shot through my arm and shoulder, blurring my vision with tears.

“You again! You must be working with that woman!” Moto snarled. “Hand over the document!”

“I don’t know—”

Ugh!

Moto slammed my head against the ground. He released my arm and kicked me repeatedly in the stomach before I could even draw a breath. Agonizing pain ripped through me. My vision swam.

My lungs burned, my chest constricted. I was on the verge of blacking out when a shout came from the main street.

“Hey! Let him go!”

Could that be Mr. Akito?

I tried to focus, but my vision swam. Moto hauled me to my feet, sending another jolt of pain through my injured arm.

“Back off!” Moto shouted. He wrapped his arm, the one holding the kodachi, around my neck, choking me. “Or I’ll kill him!”

I struggled in vain, gasping for air. Mr. Akito halted. Moto’s free hand rifled through my pockets, snatching Anko’s choker.

“Hah! What’s this, you little liar” he hissed at me. He shoved and kicked me from behind, then turned to run. But Sadi was waiting. He slashed Moto’s arm with his sword.

“Argh!” Moto groaned. The choker slipped from his hand. I lunged for the choker, scooping it up before it could be trampled underfoot. I rolled away just as Mr. Akito reached us, his katana flashing in a deadly arc that ended Moto’s life.

“Are there others?” Mr. Akito snapped. I nodded.

“Inside the shrine,” I gasped. “With Anko.”

“Sadi!” Mr. Akito barked. “Take him to the main hall and wait there.”

“Yes, Father!” Sadi replied, helping me to my feet. Mr. Akito turned and ran towards the shrine.

Sadi grumbled, shoving me forward. “Move it, dead weight! Before more of them show up.”

At the clan’s main hall, I was immediately ushered to the infirmary, Sadi trailing behind. Clan officials swarmed him, demanding answers.

Sadi gave his account of the attack, conveniently omitting the part where he’d locked me in the warehouse. He claimed the thieves broke in while we were working, forcing us to hide and then escape.

The doctor bandaged my arm. Aside from the knife wound, I had only scrapes, bruises, and a sore stomach. He was about to leave when Mr. Akito entered.

“Anko needs you in the next room,” he directed the doctor, who hurried out with the nurses. Dismissing Sadi with a curt nod, Mr. Akito closed the door behind him. Then, my father entered.

“Anko and I apprehended the enemy. These are the documents we recovered,” Mr. Akito said, handing my father several sheets of paper. Father nodded, scanning them briefly. I pulled Anko’s choker from my pocket and offered it to him.

“The remaining documents are in here,” I explained, offering the choker. Father’s expression was unreadable as he took it, his fingers quickly working the seal to retrieve the document inside.

“Do you see what I mean, Masada?” Mr. Akito asked Father, a small smile playing on his lips.

Confusion furrowed my brow.

“Your son is a security risk,” Mr. Akito continued, his gaze cold. “He knows far too much. He wouldn’t last five minutes under interrogation. If I hadn’t intervened, he’d have given everything away.”

Stunned, I stared at Mr. Akito.

“But I didn’t—”

My father raised a hand, silencing me.

“That’s an exaggeration, Akito,” Father said. “The speech seal is reserved for ninja undertaking clan missions.”

“But they’re never privy to sensitive information beyond their assigned missions,” Mr. Akito countered. “They don’t attend clan meetings, mission briefings, security debriefings… Masada, your son even sits in on discussions of clan finances!”

Mr. Akito glared at my father. “What if the enemy captured him and forced him to reveal the identities of our elite forces, or the locations of our safe houses? This is about clan security, Masada. This is for the good of the clan—”

“Akito,” my father interrupted firmly. “This isn’t the time or the place. And this isn’t my decision alone.”

“Fine. We’ll discuss this in your office,” Mr. Akito said, his face flushed with anger. “I’ll convene a meeting with the clan officials and elders. If you still fail to grasp the seriousness of this situation, we’ll put it to a vote.”

Mr. Akito then left the room.

Father approached me.

“How are you feeling, Tadashi?” he asked.

The painkiller hadn’t fully kicked in yet. My arm throbbed, my stomach ached, and my body was a mass of bruises. But I didn’t want my father to see how much pain I was in.

“I’m fine, Father,” I replied softly.

“Your mother was worried, but I sent word to reassure her.”

Guilt stabbed at me. I’d promised my mother I’d be home for lunch. I’d failed her.

I clenched my fists.

“I’m sorry I didn’t come home for lunch,” I mumbled, staring at my hands. “I hadn’t finished.”

He nodded. “Stay here. I have to take care of something.”

“Yes, Father,” I replied, a knot of unease tightening in my stomach.

What was happening? Why wasn’t he sending me home? Did it have something to do with that special meeting? Was that why he was keeping me here?

A cold dread coiled in my stomach, growing until I couldn’t contain it any longer. “Father?” I blurted out just as he reached the door. “What did Mr. Akito mean?”

My father hesitated, his gaze searching mine. He seemed to be wrestling with something internally. “We’ll talk later, Tadashi,” he said finally, his voice gentle.

I watched the door close, my head pounding. Mr. Akito had twisted the truth, making me seem more helpless than I’d been. I hadn’t been able to fight Moto, that was true. But I hadn’t revealed anything.

Anxiety gnawed at me as I waited. At 9:00 p.m., the infirmary door opened, admitting my father, Mr. Akito, and a procession of clan elders. The small room suddenly felt suffocating. Their gazes were heavy with disapproval, some bordering on outright hostility.

What had I done wrong now? I lowered my head, shame burning in my chest. Was their anger solely because of Moto? Had they all assembled to witness my punishment?

I clutched the blanket, searching my father’s face for answers. He pulled up a chair and sat beside me.

“Tadashi,” my father began, his voice heavy. “The clan security council has decided. I’m placing the speech seal on you.”

The speech seal? The words caught in my dry throat. “What… what does that mean?” I croaked.

I knew little about seals, but my instructor had warned us about the adverse effects of placing them on the body.

“We’re concerned the enemy might capture you and force you to divulge clan secrets.”

“But I would never—”

“I know, son. But the enemy has many ways of making people talk. This seal guarantees you won’t be able to speak about clan secrets to anyone, whether you want to or not.”

I swallowed, a wave of regret washed over me.

I’m sorry, Father. I’ll train harder. I’ll be stronger. Please…

“Do you understand, Tadashi?” my father asked, his voice gentle but firm.

Help me, Father…

My tongue felt thick and clumsy. I could only manage a small nod, my unspoken questions swirling within me. The elders’ watchful gazes felt like a physical weight.

My father’s hand rested on my shoulder, gently guiding me back down onto the bed. Terror choked me, but I obeyed, my gaze fixed on his face. He closed his eyes briefly, then took my hand, his grip surprisingly tight.

Then, he applied the seal on me.

We arrived home at 10:00 p.m. My mother and Shiro were waiting in the living room, Raito asleep in Shiro’s lap.

“Tadashi! Are you alright?” my mother exclaimed, rising to her feet.

I nodded.

“Mother…” I began, my voice choked with emotion.

My mother rushed to embrace me.

“Have you eaten?” she asked. “We haven’t cut your birthday cake yet.”

I was starving, hadn’t eaten since breakfast. But the lingering nausea from the seal, combined with the ache in my bruised stomach, made the thought of food unappealing.

“Let Tadashi rest, Makoto,” Father said. “We’ll cut the cake tomorrow.”

I nodded. My father excused himself, and my mother sent us upstairs.

“Can you take Raito to his room, Tadashi? I want to tidy up the dining room.”

I winced. My arm throbbed. I didn’t want to refuse my mother, but what if I dropped him?

“I’ll take him, Mother,” Shiro offered, scooping Raito into her arms and heading upstairs. I followed her to Raito’s room.

“Thanks, Shiro,” I said as she tucked Raito in.

“What’s wrong, Tadashi?” Shiro asked, looking at me with concern. “What did you have to do today?”

The speech seal clamped down, a sharp pain lancing through my head. My activities today were forbidden topics—a cruel test devised by the elders.

Don’t speak. Don’t speak. Don’t speak… DON’T SPEAK…

My head throbbed, my mouth dry and bitter. I swallowed hard, forcing the words out. “Nothing special. Just… a normal task.”

The seal pulsed, a searing rebuke, demanding silence.

Pain stabbed behind my eyes. I fussed with Raito’s blanket, trying to hide my grimace and control my ragged breathing. Dizziness washed over me. My father had said I’d get used to the seal, but right now, the pain made it hard to even think.

“Tadashi?” Shiro’s hand reached for my bandaged arm. I flinched, a jolt of pain radiating through me.

“Sorry, Shiro,” I choked out, each word a searing brand. The pain was almost unbearable. “Just… tired.”

Oh God… it hurts so much.

I blinked repeatedly, fighting the black spots clouding my vision. I didn’t want to faint in front of Shiro, but I was too overwhelmed to speak. I couldn’t bring myself to end the conversation and send her away. She hadn’t done anything wrong, she didn’t deserve this. I just hoped she’d leave it at that and stop asking more questions.

“Get some rest,” Shiro finally said. “Everything will be alright tomorrow.”

I nodded. Shiro then left me alone.

Gradually, the pain in my head subsided. I didn’t know how long I’d been in Raito’s room. I sat there, focusing on my breathing, watching my little brother sleep.

Raito’s small hand found my thumb, pulling it to his mouth. I smiled, gently removing it and kissing his forehead.

Tomorrow was a new day. As long as I had my family, I would be able to face anything.

I stroked Raito’s head. He smiled as if in agreement.

I had to be strong.

Tomorrow had to be better.
