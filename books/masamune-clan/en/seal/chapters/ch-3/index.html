Chapter 3

I blinked, trying to focus in the flickering candlelight. My attacker was shrouded in shadow.

“Wait…Tadashi?” a familiar voice exclaimed. I frowned, trying to place it.

“What are you doing here?!” The person released me.

I cradled my throbbing arm. Their face came into view as they stepped back.

Oh…

“Anko?” I whispered.

Anko was my cousin, though we weren’t particularly close. She never visited, and I mostly knew her as a teacher at the academy – the same academy I attended. I might even have her as an instructor next semester.

Anko was usually busy at the academy, rarely seen at the clan hall. But she’d been given a mission a few days ago.

“Don’t tell me Uncle Masada asked you to tail me,” Anko said, sounding annoyed. “This is dangerous work. If he doubted my abilities, he should have chosen someone else for this mission.”

I shook my head quickly, trying to reassure her.

“It’s not like that. I’m hiding here because I was being chased by thieves,” I replied.

Anko stared at me, speechless.

“Huh? What do you mean? Why were thieves chasing you ?”

I quickly explained about the thieves. Anko rubbed her face wearily.

“You said the boss, the seal expert, was named Kishu?”

I nodded.

Anko sighed. “How much do you know about my mission?”

I often sat in on clan meetings with my father. Not every mission was discussed, but the elders had mentioned Anko’s assignment yesterday.

“Elder Yun just said you were retrieving documents related to the Kurasai case,” I said, trying to remember. “I don’t know any of the details.”

“It’s better that way. The point is, those thieves who broke into the warehouse were after me ,” Anko explained. “They’re working for the enemy in Kurasai.”

She walked to the window and peered outside. The candlelight illuminated the small, walled garden surrounding the shrine, its boundaries formed by the backs of neighboring houses.

“So, yesterday I managed to get some of the documents, but Kishu caught me scanning his room,” Anko continued. “I managed to escape, even though I was injured. Last night, I stopped by the warehouse to grab a first-aid kit and some supplies, then hid here.”

“You’re injured?” I asked, looking at Anko more closely.

She shrugged. “I’ve taken care of most of it. Just a lingering pain in my lower back.”

That explained her limp and wince.

“Why didn’t you return to headquarters?” I asked.

“My mission isn’t over,” Anko said, shaking her head. “There are still documents I need to retrieve – documents Kishu most likely sealed and took. I have to get them while they’re distracted. If I go back now, they’ll escape.”

I leaned back against the pillar. Kishu’s relentless pursuit all the way here meant those documents must be incredibly important. Shouldn’t she deliver them to headquarters first?

Otone, locked out of the safe house, would have returned to the warehouse to report to Kishu. Kishu and Moto were probably already on their way back.

I needed to convince Anko to escape before they arrived. With her injury, I didn’t think she could handle all three of them if they found us here. Even if she agreed, she probably wouldn’t get far.

“What if I take the documents to headquarters?” I suggested. “I can also inform the elders that you need assistance.”

Anko considered this for a moment, then smiled.

“That’s not a bad idea.”

Anko then unclasped the choker she was wearing and handed it to me.

“Take this to headquarters. Don’t lose it, okay?”

I examined the choker, sensing the powerful, intricate seal woven into its design. My fingers traced the patterns, deciphering the hidden message within. It referred to someone blood-related to the seal’s creator.

The message indicated the seal could only be opened by the highest authority. I frowned.

“So, the document you retrieved is sealed in here, and only Father can open it?” I asked to confirm.

Anko stared at me in surprise. “You can read it?”

I felt my cheeks flush. I could sense seals and decipher their hidden messages – a skill I hadn’t yet revealed to my father, though my instructor at the academy seemed aware of it.

“A little,” I mumbled.

“Interesting…” Anko said, her eyes gleaming with a newfound respect. “Go now. Secure the docu—”

Anko gasped, her face draining of color.

“They’re here,” she hissed. “Quick, hide!”

I raced up the steps to the shrine’s loft, where three open doorways led to different rooms. I chose the far right, a storage room with a high ceiling and a large statue. Spotting a small alcove at the statue’s base, I squeezed inside.

Footsteps ascended the stairs, then moved across the loft, approaching my hiding place.

I held my breath. The person entered and circled the room. They peered behind the statue. My heart hammered against my ribs. Don’t look here. Don’t look here, I pleaded silently. The person then left. I closed my eyes, slightly relieved.

The sounds of a struggle drifted up from below, followed by Anko’s cry of pain. Then, silence. Terror clenched my chest. Had they killed her?

“Moto!” Kishu yelled from downstairs. “What’s the situation upstairs?”

“Clear, boss,” Moto replied.

“Did you find the document?” Kishu asked. “I searched the woman, but I found nothing. Otone’s checking the rooms downstairs.”

“There’s a bedroom, a small library, and a storage room up here. I’ll look around,” Moto answered.

“Check the library first,” Kishu ordered. “I’ll interrogate this intruder.”

“Okay, boss!” Moto’s footsteps moved towards the next room.

I had to get out. Crawling out of the alcove, I crouched and scanned the room. Crates and boxes filled the space, leaving only one way out.

My gaze drifted upwards.

High above the statue, a small window was barred with decaying wooden slats. I checked my pockets – the hammer was still there. But breaking through those bars would alert Moto.

What could I do?

A sickening thud echoed from below, followed by another scream from Anko. The sounds continued, a brutal symphony that made my blood run cold. I couldn’t bear to hear it, but I was too terrified to intervene.

I had to get out. I had to get help.

I peeked behind me to make sure Moto wasn’t standing outside the storage room. Okay, clear. My gaze fell upon a crate.

A signal flare!

The crate’s label confirmed it. If I lit the flare, nearby clan members would see it. They could rescue Anko. Snatching a flare and striker from the crate, I tucked them into my kimono and started climbing.

Okay… here goes nothing.

I swung the hammer, splintering the wood. I yanked the broken slats free just as Moto’s footsteps pounded towards the room.

“You!” he roared.

Splinters tore into my arm, but I pushed myself through the opening. Unfortunately, Moto grabbed my leg before I could escape.
