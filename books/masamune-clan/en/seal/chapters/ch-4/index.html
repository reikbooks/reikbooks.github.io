Chapter 4

I kicked back hard, connecting squarely with Moto’s face. My foot seemed to sink into his eye socket.

“Argh!” Moto recoiled, releasing his grip. I scrambled out of the window. It was too small for Moto to follow. Quickly lighting the flare, I tossed it onto the roof and ran, searching for a way down as red smoke billowed into the night sky.

My leg throbbed with each stride, but Moto was already on my heels. I sprinted across the garden and into a narrow, winding alleyway, the flickering lanterns of the main street a tantalizing few hundred meters ahead. But it wasn’t far enough.

Moto caught me.

We tumbled to the ground, a tangle of limbs. I kicked and thrashed, but he grabbed my injured arm.

“AAh!” I cried out, doubling over in pain. Moto slammed me to the ground and twisted my arm behind my back. I gasped. A searing pain shot through my arm and shoulder, blurring my vision with tears.

“You again! You must be working with that woman!” Moto snarled. “Hand over the document!”

“I don’t know—”

Ugh!

Moto slammed my head against the ground. He released my arm and kicked me repeatedly in the stomach before I could even draw a breath. Agonizing pain ripped through me. My vision swam.

My lungs burned, my chest constricted. I was on the verge of blacking out when a shout came from the main street.

“Hey! Let him go!”

Could that be Mr. Akito?

I tried to focus, but my vision swam. Moto hauled me to my feet, sending another jolt of pain through my injured arm.

“Back off!” Moto shouted. He wrapped his arm, the one holding the kodachi, around my neck, choking me. “Or I’ll kill him!”

I struggled in vain, gasping for air. Mr. Akito halted. Moto’s free hand rifled through my pockets, snatching Anko’s choker.

“Hah! What’s this, you little liar” he hissed at me. He shoved and kicked me from behind, then turned to run. But Sadi was waiting. He slashed Moto’s arm with his sword.

“Argh!” Moto groaned. The choker slipped from his hand. I lunged for the choker, scooping it up before it could be trampled underfoot. I rolled away just as Mr. Akito reached us, his katana flashing in a deadly arc that ended Moto’s life.

“Are there others?” Mr. Akito snapped. I nodded.

“Inside the shrine,” I gasped. “With Anko.”

“Sadi!” Mr. Akito barked. “Take him to the main hall and wait there.”

“Yes, Father!” Sadi replied, helping me to my feet. Mr. Akito turned and ran towards the shrine.

Sadi grumbled, shoving me forward. “Move it, dead weight! Before more of them show up.”

At the clan’s main hall, I was immediately ushered to the infirmary, Sadi trailing behind. Clan officials swarmed him, demanding answers.

Sadi gave his account of the attack, conveniently omitting the part where he’d locked me in the warehouse. He claimed the thieves broke in while we were working, forcing us to hide and then escape.

The doctor bandaged my arm. Aside from the knife wound, I had only scrapes, bruises, and a sore stomach. He was about to leave when Mr. Akito entered.

“Anko needs you in the next room,” he directed the doctor, who hurried out with the nurses. Dismissing Sadi with a curt nod, Mr. Akito closed the door behind him. Then, my father entered.

“Anko and I apprehended the enemy. These are the documents we recovered,” Mr. Akito said, handing my father several sheets of paper. Father nodded, scanning them briefly. I pulled Anko’s choker from my pocket and offered it to him.

“The remaining documents are in here,” I explained, offering the choker. Father’s expression was unreadable as he took it, his fingers quickly working the seal to retrieve the document inside.

“Do you see what I mean, Masada?” Mr. Akito asked Father, a small smile playing on his lips.

Confusion furrowed my brow.

“Your son is a security risk,” Mr. Akito continued, his gaze cold. “He knows far too much. He wouldn’t last five minutes under interrogation. If I hadn’t intervened, he’d have given everything away.”

Stunned, I stared at Mr. Akito.

“But I didn’t—”

My father raised a hand, silencing me.

“That’s an exaggeration, Akito,” Father said. “The speech seal is reserved for ninja undertaking clan missions.”

“But they’re never privy to sensitive information beyond their assigned missions,” Mr. Akito countered. “They don’t attend clan meetings, mission briefings, security debriefings… Masada, your son even sits in on discussions of clan finances!”

Mr. Akito glared at my father. “What if the enemy captured him and forced him to reveal the identities of our elite forces, or the locations of our safe houses? This is about clan security, Masada. This is for the good of the clan—”

“Akito,” my father interrupted firmly. “This isn’t the time or the place. And this isn’t my decision alone.”

“Fine. We’ll discuss this in your office,” Mr. Akito said, his face flushed with anger. “I’ll convene a meeting with the clan officials and elders. If you still fail to grasp the seriousness of this situation, we’ll put it to a vote.”

Mr. Akito then left the room.

Father approached me.

“How are you feeling, Tadashi?” he asked.

The painkiller hadn’t fully kicked in yet. My arm throbbed, my stomach ached, and my body was a mass of bruises. But I didn’t want my father to see how much pain I was in.

“I’m fine, Father,” I replied softly.

“Your mother was worried, but I sent word to reassure her.”

Guilt stabbed at me. I’d promised my mother I’d be home for lunch. I’d failed her.

I clenched my fists.

“I’m sorry I didn’t come home for lunch,” I mumbled, staring at my hands. “I hadn’t finished.”

He nodded. “Stay here. I have to take care of something.”

“Yes, Father,” I replied, a knot of unease tightening in my stomach.

What was happening? Why wasn’t he sending me home? Did it have something to do with that special meeting? Was that why he was keeping me here?

A cold dread coiled in my stomach, growing until I couldn’t contain it any longer. “Father?” I blurted out just as he reached the door. “What did Mr. Akito mean?”

My father hesitated, his gaze searching mine. He seemed to be wrestling with something internally. “We’ll talk later, Tadashi,” he said finally, his voice gentle.

I watched the door close, my head pounding. Mr. Akito had twisted the truth, making me seem more helpless than I’d been. I hadn’t been able to fight Moto, that was true. But I hadn’t revealed anything.

Anxiety gnawed at me as I waited. At 9:00 p.m., the infirmary door opened, admitting my father, Mr. Akito, and a procession of clan elders. The small room suddenly felt suffocating. Their gazes were heavy with disapproval, some bordering on outright hostility.

What had I done wrong now? I lowered my head, shame burning in my chest. Was their anger solely because of Moto? Had they all assembled to witness my punishment?

I clutched the blanket, searching my father’s face for answers. He pulled up a chair and sat beside me.

“Tadashi,” my father began, his voice heavy. “The clan security council has decided. I’m placing the speech seal on you.”

The speech seal? The words caught in my dry throat. “What… what does that mean?” I croaked.

I knew little about seals, but my instructor had warned us about the adverse effects of placing them on the body.

“We’re concerned the enemy might capture you and force you to divulge clan secrets.”

“But I would never—”

“I know, son. But the enemy has many ways of making people talk. This seal guarantees you won’t be able to speak about clan secrets to anyone, whether you want to or not.”

I swallowed, a wave of regret washed over me.

I’m sorry, Father. I’ll train harder. I’ll be stronger. Please…

“Do you understand, Tadashi?” my father asked, his voice gentle but firm.

Help me, Father…

My tongue felt thick and clumsy. I could only manage a small nod, my unspoken questions swirling within me. The elders’ watchful gazes felt like a physical weight.

My father’s hand rested on my shoulder, gently guiding me back down onto the bed. Terror choked me, but I obeyed, my gaze fixed on his face. He closed his eyes briefly, then took my hand, his grip surprisingly tight.

Then, he applied the seal on me.

We arrived home at 10:00 p.m. My mother and Shiro were waiting in the living room, Raito asleep in Shiro’s lap.

“Tadashi! Are you alright?” my mother exclaimed, rising to her feet.

I nodded.

“Mother…” I began, my voice choked with emotion.

My mother rushed to embrace me.

“Have you eaten?” she asked. “We haven’t cut your birthday cake yet.”

I was starving, hadn’t eaten since breakfast. But the lingering nausea from the seal, combined with the ache in my bruised stomach, made the thought of food unappealing.

“Let Tadashi rest, Makoto,” Father said. “We’ll cut the cake tomorrow.”

I nodded. My father excused himself, and my mother sent us upstairs.

“Can you take Raito to his room, Tadashi? I want to tidy up the dining room.”

I winced. My arm throbbed. I didn’t want to refuse my mother, but what if I dropped him?

“I’ll take him, Mother,” Shiro offered, scooping Raito into her arms and heading upstairs. I followed her to Raito’s room.

“Thanks, Shiro,” I said as she tucked Raito in.

“What’s wrong, Tadashi?” Shiro asked, looking at me with concern. “What did you have to do today?”

The speech seal clamped down, a sharp pain lancing through my head. My activities today were forbidden topics—a cruel test devised by the elders.

Don’t speak. Don’t speak. Don’t speak… DON’T SPEAK…

My head throbbed, my mouth dry and bitter. I swallowed hard, forcing the words out. “Nothing special. Just… a normal task.”

The seal pulsed, a searing rebuke, demanding silence.

Pain stabbed behind my eyes. I fussed with Raito’s blanket, trying to hide my grimace and control my ragged breathing. Dizziness washed over me. My father had said I’d get used to the seal, but right now, the pain made it hard to even think.

“Tadashi?” Shiro’s hand reached for my bandaged arm. I flinched, a jolt of pain radiating through me.

“Sorry, Shiro,” I choked out, each word a searing brand. The pain was almost unbearable. “Just… tired.”

Oh God… it hurts so much.

I blinked repeatedly, fighting the black spots clouding my vision. I didn’t want to faint in front of Shiro, but I was too overwhelmed to speak. I couldn’t bring myself to end the conversation and send her away. She hadn’t done anything wrong, she didn’t deserve this. I just hoped she’d leave it at that and stop asking more questions.

“Get some rest,” Shiro finally said. “Everything will be alright tomorrow.”

I nodded. Shiro then left me alone.

Gradually, the pain in my head subsided. I didn’t know how long I’d been in Raito’s room. I sat there, focusing on my breathing, watching my little brother sleep.

Raito’s small hand found my thumb, pulling it to his mouth. I smiled, gently removing it and kissing his forehead.

Tomorrow was a new day. As long as I had my family, I would be able to face anything.

I stroked Raito’s head. He smiled as if in agreement.

I had to be strong.

Tomorrow had to be better.
