Chapter 2

“ H urry,” a gruff voice whispered.

I pressed myself against the shelf, holding my breath. I hoped they didn’t see me.

From behind a large box, I watched the three of them rappel down.

“Are you sure she stashed it here, Otone?” another man asked, giving a small cough.

“I don’t know, Moto, but I caught a glimpse of her last night before she got away,” replied Otone, his voice raspy. “She disappeared at the end of that dead-end alley. This warehouse is suspicious too, isn’t it? We searched it over and over, but there was no door. What do you think, Mr. Kishu?”

Kishu, the gruff-voiced man, murmured in agreement. “This warehouse is likely their secret stash. I’ve already broken through multiple protective seals, but there’s one more in here I can’t crack.”

“Let me check around the room,” Moto said.

Oh, no! I quickly pulled the rope I had used, which was still slung over the railing. A moment later, Moto walked past my hiding place, without looking up.

“The room’s empty, boss,” Moto finally reported.

“If the intruder isn’t here, she’s probably already taken the document. However, there’s still a slight chance she hid it here,” Kishu, the leader of the thieves, said. “Alright, let’s make sure. Split up and check the contents of these boxes one by one, starting with the shelves at that end.”

“This is a wild goose chase,” Otone grumbled.

“Just do it, and stop complaining,” Kishu snapped.

“But it’s still early evening…is this really okay, boss? What if someone comes?” Moto asked.

“Well, just kill them!” Kishu replied with a chuckle.

My heart leaped into my throat when Otone started approaching my hiding place. Not yet, he hadn’t reached me, but he was only three shelves away.

I wiped my sweaty palms. What was I supposed to do? If I stayed quiet, they would eventually reach me and find me. If I tried to escape, I had already pulled the rope back. Even if I could throw it back up without them noticing, I couldn’t climb it quickly. They would definitely catch me.

“What kind of thing are we looking for, anyway?” Otone asked as he rummaged through a box. “The boxes in this section are all filled with stationery.”

Otone moved away, returning to Kishu. I let out a slow, shaky breath.

“I’ve scanned the seals in this area. It seems like none of the objects in these boxes are sealed with spiritual power,” Kishu said.

“Well, just look for anything that could have a stack of papers slipped inside. If there were any books or parchment scrolls in that stationery section, the document might be hidden in there.”

Otone nodded and went back to the stationery shelves.

“Boss, there’s an office cubicle at the end of the room,” Moto said. “I’ll check there.”

“Fine,” Kishu replied.

Moto then approached the office area, which was right in front of the shelves where I was hiding, only a few steps away. I froze. If I tried to make a run for it, would I survive?

I knew the elders planned to train me in more advanced techniques. Even so, I was still a beginner; my training had only just begun. There were many techniques I hadn’t mastered yet.

I wasn’t sure I could face these thieves, especially three against one. If I recklessly challenged them, it would be suicide.

My mind raced, desperately searching for an escape, but I was trapped.

Aah… what am I going to do?

This is it. I’m dead.

Suddenly, I heard the sound of something tearing.

“Why did you rip the lantern, boss?” Otone asked, stopping his search.

“Well… you never know, it might have been hidden inside the lantern,” Kishu said. “I couldn’t figure out how to open the top, so I just tore it.”

That lantern… the one that costs more than my weekly allowance? I swallowed, my stomach churning with fresh anxiety.

Sadi’s warning echoed in my mind. These items weren’t ours; they belonged to the clan. And the clan treasurer was notoriously meticulous, hating any kind of waste. I was doomed if he found out I was involved.

If I managed to get out of here safely, how could I replace the damaged items? I didn’t know how much allowance Father had been saving for me.

I shifted uneasily, every muscle screaming to move, but fear kept me frozen.

“Boss, I haven’t found anything yet, but there’s something strange here,” Moto said. “According to the inventory log, there was a stocktaking today.”

My heart pounded. Moto had found the inventory log I had been filling out.

“Oh… yeah, that’s right, someone was recounting the stock in the warehouse today?” Otone asked. “There are markings on the outer boxes too. Do you think they’re still around here?”

I squeezed my eyes shut. Oh, God… are they going to check this warehouse again?

Moto’s footsteps echoed back and forth around the office cubicle. He opened and closed the door.

“The bathroom’s empty. No hiding places around here. I also checked the gaps between the empty boxes at the back of the warehouse. No one’s there, so it looks like the warehouse worker has left,” Moto reported. “But I think it’s best we come back tonight, boss. What if the stocktaking isn’t finished and the worker comes back?”

Kishu was silent for a moment. “If the worker comes back, they’ll see the things we’ve damaged. They’ll tighten security in this place, so it’s better if—”

The click of a lever and the heavy scrape of a door suddenly came from the entrance. The three thieves instantly ran to the back of the warehouse, hiding behind a stack of empty boxes. Someone entered. My stomach dropped.

That had to be Sadi. The warehouse door opening meant I could escape! But… what if the thieves caught him? What if they killed him?!

“Tadashi!” Sadi called. “How’s the work going? Finished yet?”

I shrank back. I was in the middle of the furthest row of shelves. If I suddenly jumped down, ran to the front, and dragged Sadi out of there, could we be faster than the three thieves? I reached into the box nearest to me. It contained hammers. I grabbed one.

“My father couldn’t come—” Sadi stopped, as if just realizing I wasn’t there. “Hey, where’s that idiot?”

One of the thieves hiding near me slipped out. Moto. He watched Sadi, who had started searching for me between the shelves. Moto was carrying a kodachi. I gripped the hammer tighter. My heart pounded in my chest. I tried to control my breathing.

“Tadashi, don’t mess around,” Sadi called, approaching the shelves where I was hiding. “Your father was looking for you—”

Moto passed beneath me. I jumped down and swung the hammer at his head. He yelled, throwing me off his shoulder. I scrambled up, bumping into Sadi, and dragged him towards the door.

I was lucky they hadn’t expected me. But as we neared the door, a volley of throwing knives whizzed towards us. One of them grazed my upper arm.

“AAH!” I stumbled.

Sadi instantly pulled me up, yanked out the knife, and shoved me outside. The thieves roared angrily and chased after us. I ran as fast as I could, following Sadi, who knew the area better.

“Who were those guys?!” Sadi gasped, struggling to breathe.

“I don’t know!” I shouted. “They broke in through the window and were searching for something in the warehouse.”

Sadi led me towards an intersection. “Father’s still at the office. I’ll get him. Turn right and hide, okay?!”

I nodded. We split up at the intersection. The road led to Hanbo, a rough part of town. I ran towards the small market there, hoping to disappear into the crowd. But the sun had already set. The shops were starting to close; there weren’t many people around.

I gasped for breath. Where could I hide?

I glanced behind me. Otone was still tailing me. I couldn’t believe it. Why was he still after me? Shouldn’t thieves run away when they’re discovered? Was it because I had injured one of them? Or because I had overheard them searching for something? But I didn’t even know their full names, and their faces were unclear. Was it simply because they didn’t want any witnesses?

I looked around. My gaze landed on a clinic. That clinic… yes! I remembered when Elder Yun had shown me the locations of our clan’s safe houses on the map.

There should be a house around here that we could use to take refuge from pursuers. Like the other clan warehouses and buildings, it was hidden and protected by multiple seals. Ordinary people wouldn’t be able to penetrate them.

Wait, but how did those thieves get into the warehouse?

Hold on… oh yeah, their boss said he had scanned the seals in the warehouse. So Kishu was a seal expert. Otone clearly couldn’t break seals. I had to find that safe house, and fast.

I scanned for the landmarks I remembered from the map, spotting a narrow alley to the left of the clinic. I hurried down it, searching for the right house number, finally finding a dilapidated, boarded-up house.

I unlocked the layers of spiritual seals, pried open the door bar with the hammer I was carrying, and slipped inside. I resealed the protective wards so Otone couldn’t follow me.

Inside, the house was pitch black. I felt my way along the walls. There were no other doors. Then I found a back door. As soon as I stepped out of the house, I found myself in front of a small shrine lit by flickering candles.

Oh… the wooden house was just a camouflage. So this shrine is the real safe house?

I entered the shrine and hid behind a pillar. As I stopped, I realized the throwing knife wound on my arm was still bleeding. It hurt. I winced. Was the cut deep?

I was about to remove my haori to use as a bandage when suddenly someone twisted my shoulder from behind and slammed my back against the pillar. Agonizing pain shot through my arm as the attacker pinned it against the pillar.

“Be quiet or die,” the person hissed, pressing the tip of a kunai against my throat.
