Twin Blades

Book 5 of Masamune Clan Series

Undercover mission. Ruthless ambitions. The things you’re willing to sacrifice.

Sadi’s faced impossible missions before, but this time, he’s up against more than a corrupt drug ring. Sent to clear the name of a clan official’s daughter, he thinks he’s ready for the task—until his rival Tadashi lands squarely in the crossfire. Now, forced to make an unthinkable choice, Sadi must decide between loyalty to his clan’s demands or saving the only person who sees past his hardened façade.

In the Tsuki Kingdom, where loyalty is a double-edged sword, can Sadi pay the price?

B AM!

My father hurled me across the room, sending me crashing into the kotatsu table in his room at the clan’s safe house. Pain exploded in my head. I swiped at the blood trickling from my temple and scrambled to my feet. Too slow. His boot connected with my ribs, driving the air from my lungs.

Damn it! Was this just because I chose a bad time to show my face?

He yanked my hair, hard. I clawed at his wrist, but he just tightened his grip. My vision blurred.

“Two months, and you’ve still got nothing?” Father snarled, his voice ringing in my ears. “Are you useless or something? Think this is a joke?”

I gritted my teeth, forcing back a scream. My lungs burned with each ragged gasp. Gone before sunrise, back after midnight, no time to even think about the case. A joke? Does he think I pull intel out of thin air? This isn’t some hack-and-slash mission where I can just waltz in and out. My blood was boiling.

I wrenched free of his grip, a move that sent a jolt of agony radiating from the seal at the base of my spine.

He released my hair, but his boot slammed into the back of my head. Pain throbbed behind my eyes. I scrambled back to the corner, putting as much distance between us as I could. I glanced at the locked door and windows, a hysterical laugh barely escaping my lips. Not nearly far enough. In this cramped room, I was still within arm’s reach.

Father was not done. “Akane’s parents are breathing down my neck. The cops won’t even let her out for a hefty bribe.”

Akane, the clan official’s loudmouth daughter, had been caught with 2 kilos of weed stuffed in a suitcase. She claimed she was just holding it for someone and had no idea what was inside, but the cops weren’t buying it. If it was just about busting Akane out of jail, no problem. But if she’s actually guilty, I could bend over backward until I’m six feet under, and I still wouldn’t find any evidence to clear her name. Right?

So, my job now was to dig up that evidence, or better yet, nab Mio, the one who set Akane up.

“Are you even listening, Sadi?” Father hissed, his voice like a viper’s. He loomed over me, casting me in shadow. Every muscle in my body screamed in protest. No way to attack. No way to escape. Trapped like a rat, as always. If only it weren’t for this damn seal, this cursed brand that kept me from fighting back, from leaving him for good. Bastard. What kind of father brands his own son like a damn animal?

I nodded sharply, anticipating another outburst. My body straightened involuntarily.

“Yes, Father,” I said, keeping my voice steady. Good. My gut twisted every time I had to act like the obedient son in front of him, so I forced my thoughts back to Akane’s case.

I hadn’t found the guy Akane mentioned, but there was definitely a dealer named Mio operating here. It meant Akane was just unlucky enough to be the one Mio decided to use.

The problem was, Mio was a ghost. Hardly ever showed his face. There was another link in the chain, though – Akane’s roommate. Girl was cute, definitely my type. Misao. I’d been working on her for a month. Finally got her to agree to a date yesterday.

“I just need a bit more time, old man,” I muttered, forcing the words out. Ugh. It wasn’t always like this. Now the slightest thing could set him off.

Father snorted and stepped back. The pressure crushing my chest eased. I clenched my fists, forcing myself to take slow, measured breaths, fighting the urge to bolt for the door.

“One more week, Sadi,” Father said, his voice cold. “I can’t keep cleaning up your messes. You understand what’s at stake, don’t you?”

My jaw clenched. I had to get out of there before he changed his mind. I nodded curtly and pushed myself to my feet.

My head still throbbing, I made my way back to my lodgings in Seikagi, the town where Akane’s dorm was. The old man’s words echoed in my skull the entire journey.

He always managed to twist things around, make me believe everything was my fault. My fault that Mom left him. My fault he didn’t have enough backing in the clan, which meant I wasn’t chosen as the successor.

My fault that I couldn’t complete the jobs he assigned me, the ones meant to boost his image with the other clan elders. My fault that I screwed up his schemes, his little power plays.

My fault, my fault, always my damn fault.

And the worst part was, for every screw-up, he made me believe I deserved the punishment.

I gritted my teeth, the scenery blurring past as I finally reached Seikagi. My hands shook as I unlocked the door and slammed it shut behind me. I headed straight for the bathroom, stopping in front of the mirror.

It had been a while since the old man had given me an ultimatum like this. This job had to be done perfectly, without a hitch, or I’d be paying the price. The last time he’d chained me up in the basement was when I’d dared to see Mom.

The old man had forbidden me from seeing Mom since their divorce fourteen years ago. For that long, I had no idea where she and Shizuka had gone… until Tadashi spilled the beans.

Ugh! I cranked the tap and splashed water on my face, trying to wash away the thought of that idiot. Tadashi. The guy could piss me off even more than the old man could. I didn’t want to end up putting my fist through the mirror just because his face popped into my head.

Two days later, a carrier pigeon arrived just as I was heading out.

Need backup at Keniiwari, ASAP. – Taka.

An urgent call for backup. Written using one of Tadashi’s aliases, but no one knew I was undercover here except the old man.

Hmm… Father was one of the mission supervisors. To make communication easier, missions far from HQ usually had a command center in a nearby city. Conveniently, he was supposedly away on a business trip. Did this mean he was overseeing Tadashi’s mission from afar?

I crumpled the message and shoved it into my pocket.

So, the old man had actually let that idiot ask for my help? What was he playing at? This was just going to throw a wrench in my plans. I’d just booked a villa by the beach yesterday, a two-hour drive from here. Next week, I was planning to take Misao there for a “romantic getaway”, get closer to her, and pry more information out of her. Today, we were supposed to go to the Lantern Festival on the beach – a chance to scope out the villa, too.

“What’s up, Sando?” Misao asked.

“Ah, sorry, Misao. Looks like we’ll have to reschedule our little scouting mission. Something came up, a family emergency,” I said, pressing a quick kiss to her forehead. “We’ll go out when I get back, alright?”

“Aw, but the festival’s ending soon. Tonight’s the last night,” Misao whined, her lip jutting out.

“Hey, hey, don’t worry, sweetheart.” I gave her a reassuring smile, stroking her cheek. “I’ll make it up to you. I promise.”

“Can’t you just go tomorrow morning?” Misao pleaded, her voice still laced with disappointment.

Hah! Honestly, I couldn’t care less about going, but the old man must have had a reason for sending me to bail out that idiot Tadashi. I sighed and shook my head.

“I don’t know what kind of mess my brother’s gotten himself into this time, but he wouldn’t contact me unless it was a life-or-death situation,” I grumbled.

After ditching Misao, I swung by a warehouse on the edge of town to pick up some gear and weaponry. Inside the secure drop box, I found a message from the old man, written in our clan’s cipher. It contained a short rundown of Tadashi’s mission.

One of the clan’s farms had reported some strange happenings. For the past few months, almost every night, they’d been finding livestock dead, ripped apart like something had gone to town on them, but only the heart and eyes were missing.

At first, they thought it was some kind of ritualistic killing. But then they found animal tracks leading into the forest. The thing was, if it was a wild animal, why would it leave the rest of the carcass untouched?

Tadashi had found the creature responsible. It looked like a wolf, but bigger, much bigger. He’d managed to kill one, but apparently, there was a whole pack of them out there. After Tadashi offed one of them, the pack retaliated, surrounding the farm last night. Looks like the idiot couldn’t handle them on his own.

Seriously? I thought the old man would be thrilled if that idiot failed his mission.

Hmm… but maybe Father was worried someone would question his judgment for not sending backup sooner.

I sighed. Great. Looks like I was playing clean-up crew for that idiot again.

I reached the farm before midnight. The air was crisp and cold. An eerie silence hung over the place, broken only by the wind whispering through the nearby fields.

Was that idiot dead already? I unsheathed my swords. The farm gate was padlocked from the outside, the farmhouse next to it dark and silent. I moved cautiously, following a trail of blood.

A sheep lay sprawled on the ground, its belly ripped open. Not far from it, a wolf with grayish-white fur lay motionless.

Damn. The idiot wasn’t exaggerating about the size of these wolves. Three times the size of a normal wolf, almost as big as a bear. This was no ordinary wolf. This was a monster.

Deep slashes crisscrossed the wolf’s body, blood oozing from a gaping wound in its chest. It looked like it had taken a direct hit to the heart. X-shaped slashes marked both its eyes.

I circled the farm, finding two more of the monstrous wolves lying dead.

I followed the blood trail again, this time along a path that snaked into the forest. Human footprints marked the soft earth. My grip tightened on my kodachi.

Movement flickered behind the trees. I flattened myself against a thick trunk.

A monstrous wolf emerged from the shadows. Its eyes glowed yellow, one of them marred by a red scratch. Its body was littered with wounds. Its breath came in ragged gasps as it growled, its gaze fixed on me.

I wasn’t about to wait for it to make the first move.

Three slashes, two swift stabs.

I lunged, my kodachi slicing through the air, but the beast only roared in fury. I leaped back, narrowly avoiding its razor-sharp claws.

Damn it.

This thing… was tougher… than it looked!

One of the wolf’s paws slammed into me, sending me flying backward into a tree.

It lunged!

I reacted instantly, plunging my kodachi into its heart and rolling away. My blade scraped against bone as I yanked it free.

A guttural roar ripped from the beast’s throat before it collapsed, its body going still.

I sucked in a deep breath, approaching the wolf cautiously. My sword hilt was slick with blood. I nudged the monster with my boot. It didn’t budge.

Good. It was finally dead.

Answering howls echoed through the forest, cutting through the night. I froze, my blood running cold.

More? How many more were there? I sprinted through the trees, desperately searching for a way out of the forest.

They were on my tail.

I reacted instinctively, ducking as a dark shape launched itself at me.

Dodge. Slash. Stab.

A searing pain shot through my arm as claws raked across it, but my stab found its mark. The monster convulsed and then went limp.

Three more wolves emerged from the darkness, growling menacingly, surrounding me. One of them towered over me, its size and demeanor marking it as the alpha.

Damn it!

The wolf in front of me snarled, baring its fangs. I sidestepped its lunge and thrust my kodachi at the closest target.

THUD

My blade found its mark, sinking into the creature’s eye. The beast yelped in pain, its head jerking back. I didn’t hesitate, driving my blade into its other eye.

A blur of fur and teeth launched itself at me. I stumbled back, losing my grip on one of my kodachi. I lashed out with my foot, catching it in the head and blinding it, but the beast managed to tackle me to the ground. I thrashed, my remaining kodachi a blur as I stabbed wildly.

One of my wild stabs must have found its heart, because the monster collapsed on top of me, its weight crushing the air from my lungs. My hand was pinned beneath it, my kodachi still buried in its thick hide.

Then its blinded companion lunged, claws extended, aiming for my face.

A flash of steel.

The beast crumpled to the ground, a tanto protruding from its chest.

Tadashi landed gracefully on one knee beside me, like a gymnast completing a flawless routine. His eyes were sharp, his body coiled tight, ready for anything. He yanked his tanto free and sprang to his feet, just as the alpha wolf launched its attack.

I kicked the dead wolf off me and rolled away, putting some distance between myself and the alpha’s deadly claws.

I had to give the idiot credit, though. Tadashi wielded his katana and tanto like they were extensions of his own limbs. He moved like a whirlwind, a blur of steel as he attacked and evaded, but the fight was clearly one-sided. The alpha wolf was far faster and more agile than its packmates, while Tadashi… looked like he was hurt. His movements were sluggish, and he was barely managing to avoid the wolf’s slashing claws.

I scrambled for my dropped kodachi and lunged forward, just as the alpha wolf pounced on Tadashi. Tadashi hit the ground hard, the monster’s fangs sinking into his shoulder.

Now or never!

I drove my blade deep into the beast’s flank. The alpha wolf staggered, its paw lashing out at me. Its chest was wide open. I ducked under its claw and plunged my other kodachi into its heart.

A final, enraged roar tore from the alpha’s throat before it collapsed beside Tadashi.

For a moment, we just froze there, the silence broken only by our ragged breathing. Tadashi pushed himself up, his hand shaking as he reached for his fallen katana. His jaw was clenched, his gaze distant. He seemed lost in thought, completely ignoring me.

“Don’t mention it,” I said, my voice dripping with sarcasm.

Tadashi blinked, momentarily stunned, then turned away, muttering a barely audible “thanks.”

Argh! What a prick!

I let out a frustrated sigh. Arrogant bastard, acting like I wasn’t even there. Always sucking up to the elders and clan officials, hanging out with them, pretending to be the perfect, obedient little soldier.

I raked my gaze over him, taking in the damage.

Up close, Tadashi was a wreck. His hair was a tangled mess, his cheeks bruised and bloodied, his lip split. His clothes were torn to shreds, and deep scratches crisscrossed his exposed skin. He limped painfully, favoring his right leg – probably a bite from one of those damn wolves.

“Don’t worry, I’m not going to sprout fur and fangs anytime soon,” he said, his voice flat.

I snorted. Werewolf? Seriously? The idiot’s jokes were enough to make me want to throttle him.

“I think those things are dark creatures, summoned by the Mitsurugi Clan, but I can’t prove it. I haven’t been able to find the summoner either.” Tadashi mused.

My brow furrowed.

“Then why didn’t you just use the Sword of Light technique?” I scoffed, crossing my arms. “Would’ve fried those dark creatures, no problem.”

Of course, that technique needed a ridiculous amount of energy. More than I’d want to admit not having. But Tadashi? He had power to spare—it was his forte.

Tadashi paused, considering, then slowly shook his head. “I tried yesterday, but I nearly ended up as wolf chow. Somehow, the monsters mess with my power flow. Any technique that uses it just fizzles out when I get close.”

After patching ourselves up and changing clothes, I was ready to head back, but Tadashi stopped me.

“Your father wants us to report to him directly, as soon as we’re done here,” Tadashi said, his voice weary. He wiped the sweat from his brow, then grimaced as he hefted his gear bag onto his shoulder. I recognized the look on his face. It was the same look he’d had during that hellish training with Elder Low on that remote island a couple of years ago, when we’d been relentlessly hunted by the clan’s elite and forced to rely on each other to survive.

Bone-deep exhaustion and a gnawing ache that just wouldn’t quit.

I wasn’t feeling so great myself. My head throbbed, and the claw marks on my skin burned like hellfire. What if they got infected?

“Maybe I should get these looked at by a doctor,” I said.

“It’s the middle of the night. You won’t find a doctor awake at this hour.” Tadashi studied me for a moment, then asked, “Any broken bones or bad bruises?”

I shook my head. “No. Why do you ask?”

He pushed up his sleeve, revealing three thin scratches near his elbow.

“Four hours ago, these were deep gashes,” he said.

Huh… seriously? I checked my own wounds and my jaw dropped.

The pain was intensifying, but most of the claw marks were practically gone. Only faint red lines remained, already fading. They’d probably be completely gone by morning. How the hell was I supposed to explain this to a doctor?

Those wolf creatures must have had some kind of venom in their claws and teeth. If they were dark creatures, like Tadashi suspected, I doubted any doctor would have a cure. I settled for popping a painkiller and hoping for the best.

By the time I reached the old man’s place four hours later, my body felt like it was being consumed by flames. Every jolt and jostle sent a fresh wave of agony through my skull. And to make matters worse, the idiot had passed out. Damn it all!

I hauled Tadashi into the old man’s room and dumped him on the floor. The old man simply stood there, arms crossed, observing him with a cold detachment.

“When he comes to, make sure he writes a report about his little adventure,” Father said dismissively. “Now get him out of my sight.”

“I don’t need him,” I protested. “I can handle my mission on my own. He’ll just slow me down.”

Father’s eyes narrowed, and he took a step towards me. I instinctively backed away.

“This is a golden opportunity for your mission, Sadi,” Father hissed, his grip tightening on my arm.

“Tadashi is in a lot of pain. He passed out from it. All you have to do is buy some drugs, pretend it’s for his pain management. Once you’re a customer, the dealer won’t hesitate to show you where he operates.”

A cruel smirk twisted the old man’s lips.

“Simple, isn’t it? Just make sure Tadashi gets hooked on the drugs.”

I rode with one arm wrapped around Tadashi, keeping him from sliding off the horse. Only forty-five minutes to Seikagi, but with an unconscious dead weight slumped against me, it was a struggle to stay balanced. It took all my concentration, and the old man’s order to get Tadashi addicted was making my head pound even harder.

From the day I was born, Father had groomed me to be the next clan leader. But in the preliminary test, Shiro, Tadashi’s younger sister, had somehow managed to snag the top spot. I came in second, while Tadashi trailed behind in third.

When Shiro turned down the position, it should have been mine by default, right? But no, the elders and clan officials decided to hold a vote instead.

The candidates were me and Tadashi, of course. And somehow, that idiot had managed to garner more support than me. So, he got the position.

Father was furious, of course, but he never let it show. Since then, he’d become fixated on finding ways to undermine Tadashi, discredit him, or even get rid of him altogether.

And me? I’d been following the old man’s orders like a loyal dog.

Partly because I had no idea what else to do with my life, and partly because it gave me a way to vent my frustrations.

A chance to get back at that arrogant prick? Sure, why not?

Tadashi was the reason Father was always on my case, always finding fault with my work. He was constantly comparing us, telling me I needed to be more like Tadashi.

Sometimes I wondered if the old man wanted to kill Tadashi or make him his new favorite son.

Father’s order this time was actually starting to scare me. He’d punish me if I disobeyed, but getting Tadashi hooked on drugs was way beyond the line. If Tadashi’s family found out, they’d have my head.

Besides, I had no clue how to get the idiot addicted in the first place.

If he was still out cold, maybe I could just inject him. But then what? Was I supposed to keep knocking him out every time I wanted to give him a dose?

Argh! That idiot was nothing but a walking headache.

I got back to the lodgings just as the sun was rising. Tadashi was already awake, his face pale, his skin radiating heat. He sat quietly on the tatami mat in the living room, arms folded on the kotatsu table.

Was he expecting me to wait on him hand and foot?

“Get your own damn drink if you’re thirsty,” I snapped, heading for the kitchen.

“Sorry for the trouble,” Tadashi rasped, his voice rough.

“You’re Taka here, Sando’s younger brother,” I said, making sure he understood the cover story. We’d used this brother act before, but I wanted to make sure he remembered who he was supposed to be. “There’s only one bedroom—it’s mine. If you need to rest, just lie down in the living room.”

I could have used some sleep, but the fever and the throbbing in my joints made it impossible to relax. I rummaged through the medicine cabinet and popped a couple of painkillers.

“Father wants a report,” I said. “And he insists you should join me on the mission. Apparently, he thinks I could use the help.”

Tadashi watched me for a moment, then shrugged as if it was no big deal.

Ugh. What a pain.

“What do you need help with?” Tadashi asked, his voice slow and deliberate. He winced, rubbing his temple, clearly fighting through the pain to focus.

I sat down across from him and laid out the situation. Akane, a student at the academy in Seikagi, shared a dorm with Misao. Apparently, Akane was about to travel when Mio, Misao’s friend, asked her to deliver a bag to someone at her destination. But when Akane was about to board the ship, she was busted because the bag was full of weed.

Since Akane knew Mio through Misao, I decided to get closer to Misao. In her dorm, I saw a photo of her with two guys. She said it was her older brother, Roroa, and that Mio was one of his underlings. I did some digging and found out that Roroa was the one running the drug operation, supplying Mio, who was just a small-time dealer.

Once I found Roroa’s base of operations, I planned to look for their records – customer names, transaction details, delivery schedules, the whole shebang. They had to be keeping track of who they were using as mules, where they were going, how much they were carrying, and how they planned to get the goods back. That kind of information could be used to clear Akane’s name.

I wasn’t sure what Misao’s role was in all of this. She never went anywhere suspicious, just back and forth from her dorm to her job. I was planning to buy some drugs from her and then tail her, see if she contacted Mio or went to pick up more supplies from their headquarters.

But things were still new with Misao. I hadn’t had a chance to bring it up yet. If I pushed too hard, too fast, she might get spooked and dump me. But “Taka” here was a wild card. Misao didn’t know him. I could just say he was an addict, couldn’t I?

“So, here’s the thing. I need you to buy some drugs for me,” I said, cutting to the chase.

Screw it. I didn’t have the energy for elaborate schemes. Instead of trying to come up with some convoluted plan to get Tadashi hooked, I might as well just be blunt.

Tadashi blinked, his chin resting on his hand, his fingers drumming a light rhythm on the table.

“Just buying them?” he asked cautiously.

I met his gaze, my jaw clenched. “Buying and using them.”

Tadashi’s face went still, his expression unreadable. He turned towards the window, his gaze fixed on the rising sun.

“Is there really no other way?” he asked quietly.

Of course there was another way. I could try to work my way into Misao’s good graces, manipulate her into making a deal, and then convince her to take me to their headquarters. But that would take time. Too much time. I only had five days left. Frankly, if the old man actually cared about the mission, he wouldn’t have given me such a ridiculous order. It was obvious he was just using this as an excuse to screw Tadashi over.

I wasn’t about to explain any of that to Tadashi. I simply stared back, refusing to answer.

“Your father put you up to this, didn’t he?” he asked, his voice quiet. He must have been remembering that time three years ago when he’d stayed at my place. He’d seen the old man lose his temper and lay into me. He’d seen the seal, too, since he was the one who patched me up afterward. The memory, sharp and humiliating, sent a wave of anger surging through me.

I slammed my fist on the table, the force of it rattling the teacups. “I don’t need your damn pity, understand?!”

Tadashi flinched, his eyes widening in surprise. “I wasn’t—”

“And don’t you dare drag my father’s name into this!” I snarled. That was one of Father’s cardinal rules. If I messed up and made him look bad, he’d make me regret the day I was born.

Damn it!

Why did I have to grovel like this to Tadashi, of all people? It was humiliating. If I didn’t need his help so damn badly, I would have punched his smug face in.

I took a deep breath, trying to reign in my temper. My chest ached with suppressed fury.

How much longer could I take this?

Even if it meant swallowing my pride, I had no choice but to obey the old man.

Father had made damn sure that the enslavement seal on my body was permanent. He controlled my every move, and he would continue to do so until he died or transferred control of the seal to someone else. As Anko’s student, Tadashi knew all about seals. He knew exactly what that damn thing meant for me.

Tadashi let out a slow, sharp breath, then buried his face in his hands.

“Sadi, I’m afraid that I …” His voice trailed off, leaving the sentence unfinished. The silence stretched between us, heavy and suffocating.

“Alright,” Tadashi said finally, straightening up. His eyes, though filled with weariness, showed a hint of steel as he met my gaze. “But I have one condition.”

My brow furrowed.

“What is it?”

Tadashi didn’t answer. He placed his clenched fist over his heart, then brought it down sharply, placing it in my open palm.

Yoroshiku.

It was an ancient warrior’s oath, a pledge of loyalty and service, a gesture of respect that had all but vanished. They didn’t teach it at the academy anymore. The new generation of ninja wouldn’t even know what it meant.

Elder Mei had taught it to Tadashi because he was the heir apparent. As the second in line, I’d been taught all the same things, all the ancient customs and traditions.

A faint smile touched Tadashi’s lips as he slowly rose to his feet and walked towards the bookshelf.

“I need some paper for that report,” he said, effectively dismissing me.

I remained rooted to the spot, staring at my empty palm, the weight of his unspoken promise pressing down on me.

(My life is in your hands. I trust you to watch my back.)

A loud banging on the door jolted me awake.

“Sando!”

Misao. What the hell was she doing here at this hour? Ugh! What time was it anyway? My head still swam with the remnants of sleep.

“Sando, open up! I know you’re in there!”

I threw the blankets off and stumbled towards the door. Tadashi was still curled up on the floor in the living room, facing the wall. I ignored him and yanked the door open.

“What’s going on, Misao?” I croaked, blinking against the harsh glare of the morning sun.

“Why aren’t you dressed?” Misao hissed, pushing past me into the lodgings and slamming the door shut. I looked down. Shit, I was only in my boxers. Not that Misao seemed to mind. The way she blushed and her eyes kept flicking down to my chest told me all I needed to know. I grinned, enjoying the reaction.

“Your boss came looking for you at my shop. Why aren’t you at work?” Misao demanded.

Shit! I groaned inwardly.

This cover identity was getting harder to maintain. My “boss” was a real piece of work, some low-level thug who’d gotten me a gig as a security guard at the mayor’s office.

“Sorry. He didn’t give you any trouble, did he? I overslept. Rough night, you know?”

Misao glanced towards the living room. “Who’s that?” she asked, her brow furrowing.

I rolled my eyes. “My brother, Taka. Uh, excuse me for a moment. Nature calls.”

I was just finishing up in the bathroom when Misao’s voice, laced with panic, called out from the living room.

“Sando! Get in here! Quick!”

I rushed into the living room. Tadashi’s body was rigid, his eyes rolled back in his head, his limbs jerking and twitching uncontrollably. He was having a seizure. I started towards him, but Misao grabbed my arm, stopping me.

“Don’t touch him,” she said urgently.

“But—”

“There’s nothing you can do. He’s on the floor, he can’t hurt himself,” Misao explained quickly.

“So, we just wait it out?” I asked.

Misao nodded. “I’ve seen this happen a lot… back home…”

I frowned, feigning confusion. Misao’s eyes widened slightly, realizing her slip-up.

“Uh… I mean… never mind. Forget I said anything.”

When the seizure finally passed, I cautiously approached Tadashi, who lay on his side, his breathing ragged, his clothes drenched in sweat. His gaze was unfocused, distant. A knot of unease twisted in my gut. Was this real, or was he faking it?

I waved my hand in front of his face. He blinked slowly, his eyes struggling to focus.

“Hey, Taka,” I said softly, making sure he remembered his role. “Can you see me?”

Tadashi frowned, his brow furrowed in confusion.

“Sando?” he croaked, his voice weak and raspy.

Misao appeared beside me, handing me a glass of water.

“Try to get him to drink something,” she said gently.

I helped Tadashi sit up, holding the glass to his lips. “Here, take a sip.” He managed a few sips, then turned away, gagging. He clamped a hand over his mouth, his eyes squeezed shut.

I placed a hand on his forehead. It was burning up. His entire body trembled uncontrollably.

Tadashi slumped against me, his hands gripping my shirt, his hair plastered to his forehead with sweat.

“Hey… it’s okay. I’ve got you,” I murmured, trying to sound reassuring.

Tadashi buried his face against my shoulder, his body wracked with tremors.

“Hurts…” he whimpered, his voice barely audible.

I guided him back to lie down on the tatami and brushed the damp hair away from his forehead. Within minutes, he drifted back to sleep, but even in sleep, his body twitched and trembled.

“Misao,” I said, turning to her, my voice laced with concern, “you said you’ve seen this before?”

Misao nodded slowly.

“I don’t know what to do,” I pleaded, playing the worried brother role to perfection. “What’s wrong with him?”

Misao studied Tadashi for a moment, her brow furrowed in thought.

“Has your brother ever been injured? Like, seriously injured?” she asked carefully.

I nodded, weaving a story on the spot. “He took a bad fall a while back. Hiking with some friends, he went tumbling down a ravine. Broke a few ribs, bruised his spine pretty badly. The doctor gave him some painkillers for it.”

“Do you know what kind of painkillers he was on?” Misao asked, her voice soft.

I shook my head. “I don’t remember the name. I haven’t lived with him and the old man for a while now. I just visited him, checked in on him, and then went back home.”

Misao knelt beside Tadashi and gently lifted his arm, examining the inside of his forearm. She frowned, shaking her head.

“I’m not sure… but I have a suspicion. You really should take him to a doctor.”

I pressed my lips together, thinking. How could I steer the conversation towards her brother’s business?

“Ever since I moved out, he’s… gotten mixed up with some bad people. I don’t know what he’s done, but the cops are after him,” I said, my voice low and confidential. “They probably wouldn’t check the hospitals, but if they find him getting treatment here…” I let the sentence trail off, letting her fill in the blanks.

Tadashi moaned in pain, his body curling up and trembling.

This was it. My chance.

“Please, Misao, if you know anything…” I pleaded, my voice thick with fake concern. “He was barely conscious when I found him yesterday. He was in so much pain, but I couldn’t figure out why. There weren’t any visible injuries. Maybe it’s something from that old injury, maybe he just needs his medication.”

Misao hesitated, biting her lip.

“Sando…” she began, her voice hesitant. “I’m not sure, but… there’s a chance your brother is addicted to something.”

I feigned shock. “You mean those painkillers the doctor gave him?”

Misao shrugged. “It’s possible.”

“But would a doctor really prescribe something addictive?” I pressed.

“Maybe it got worse, and he started taking more than he was supposed to,” Misao said quietly. “You know some of those painkillers are pretty strong, right? They have opiates in them.”

I gasped, my eyes widening in feigned horror. “You’re saying my brother is a drug addict?”

Misao sighed heavily and walked over to the window, staring out at the bustling street below.

“I… I’ve seen it firsthand. Growing up… Let’s just say I’ve been around addicts. I’ve seen what happens when they go through withdrawal. It’s different for everyone, depends on the drug. Some of them, yeah, they have seizures.”

“But… if he’s having seizures because of withdrawal, doesn’t that mean he’s trying to quit? I shouldn’t give him any more drugs, right?” I asked, my voice carefully neutral.

Misao turned back to me, her expression troubled. “There’s no way to know for sure why he’s withdrawing. Maybe he’s still using, but just ran out of money. Or maybe, like you said, he’s trying to quit cold turkey, which is never a good idea. Some drugs, you can’t just stop taking them. You have to wean yourself off them slowly.”

I rubbed my face, feigning exhaustion and worry. “I just want him to be okay. I don’t want him to suffer. Please, Misao, can you help me? Do you know what kind of drug he might be using? Where could I get some?”

Misao was quiet for a long moment, then she let out a slow, weary sigh. “How much money do you have?”

There it was.

She’d taken the bait.

Time to reel her in.

I handed over the money, and Misao disappeared. I changed Tadashi’s sweat-soaked clothes and left him curled up on the floor, his breaths coming in short, shallow gasps.

It was real. He wasn’t faking it. Whatever venom those wolf creatures carried in their bites was a hell of a lot worse than the stuff in their claws. Tadashi was in agony, and I was using it, twisting it to my advantage, all for the sake of a mission. I didn’t know if Tadashi had agreed to this out of pity, or some misguided sense of duty to the clan. But addiction wasn’t a joke. It wasn’t something you just bounced back from.

Addicts would do anything for their next hit. Steal, lie, cheat, even hurt the people they loved. Some of them overdosed. Some of them just gave up, couldn’t take the pain anymore.

I sat at the kitchen table, my gaze fixed on Tadashi’s slumped form in the living room. He was carrying the weight of my mission, my burden, even though he knew how heavy it was. Guilt, a sharp, twisting knot, tightened in my chest, squeezing the air from my lungs.

Would he ever recover from this?

M isao returned with a small vial filled with a murky liquid. I’d never seen anything like it before. “This is the good stuff,” she said, her voice low. “The latest, strongest blend.”

Misao injected the drug into Tadashi’s arm. Less than five minutes later, the tremors that had been wracking his body ceased. His breathing slowed, becoming deep and even, as if he were drifting off to sleep. But then his eyes fluttered open, their gaze distant and unfocused.

I sat on the tatami beside him.

“Taka?” I said softly.

Tadashi turned his head towards me, a hazy question in his eyes. A strange sense of peace seemed to have settled over him.

“Yeah?”

“How are you feeling?” I asked, my voice barely a whisper.

Tadashi did something I’d rarely seen him do. He smiled. A genuine, unguarded smile that reached his eyes. A sharp pang of guilt stabbed at my conscience. I’d never seen him look so at peace, not even when his father had praised him in front of the entire clan for completing his graduation mission with honors.

“Good,” he murmured, his voice soft and content.

“No pain?” I asked, my voice still hushed.

He shook his head slowly, that blissful smile still plastered on his face. I placed my hand on his forehead. He was still feverish, but the heat didn’t seem as intense. I should have grabbed a thermometer to be sure.

“What happened last night? You just… collapsed,” I said, feigning concern.

Tadashi’s brow furrowed as he tried to recall the events of the previous night. He was quiet for a long moment, then slowly shook his head. “I don’t remember.”

I shot Misao a questioning look. She just shrugged, her expression unreadable. “Don’t press him,” she said quietly. “He’s probably having trouble focusing. Stick to simple questions. Yes or no answers.”

Right.

“Think you can sit up?” I asked.

“Sure,” Tadashi replied easily.

He sat up, leaning back against the wall, and then, as if suddenly realizing she was there, he turned to Misao. I introduced them, and Tadashi greeted her with a charming smile, even complimenting her on her beauty. Misao blushed, obviously flattered.

Misao excused herself, saying she needed to get back to her shop. She reminded me to contact her in eight hours.

That afternoon was surreal. Tadashi was… different. Like a completely different person. He was in an incredibly good mood. Smiling, laughing, completely carefree.

He acknowledged everything I told him, nodding and remembering details about my father, his father, the clan issues, but he didn’t seem bothered by any of it. It was like none of it mattered, like it was all just background noise.

The way he talked, the confidence in his voice, it was like he thought he could take on the world. It worried me a bit, and I felt obligated to keep an eye on him.

When the drug wore off, the pain came back with a vengeance. I contacted Misao, buying more of the drug, keeping Tadashi high and pain-free throughout the day.

My fever had broken on Wednesday, three days after the wolf attack. I figured Tadashi would have recovered from the venom around the same time, but it seemed the drug had already taken hold.

Misao hadn’t been exaggerating. This stuff was potent. And Tadashi was hooked.

That night, sleep evaded me. I lay awake, staring at the ceiling, wondering how long I would have to keep up this charade with Misao. She seemed to be warming up to Tadashi, though. Each time I saw them talking, laughing, I was reminded of that silent oath, his fist pressed against my palm.

The idiot… he’d always had a way of messing with my life.

Thanks to the old man’s warped view of the world, I’d always thought that being clan leader was a cushy gig. Just sit back, enjoy the perks, and bask in everyone’s admiration.

The old man always said that being clan leader would make my life easier. Now I was starting to see through his lies.

As the backup, I learned the same things Tadashi did, trained the same way he did. I didn’t have to sit through all the boring clan meetings, but the secretary kept me updated on everything that was going on.

I didn’t have as much responsibility, but I had to be ready to step in at a moment’s notice. And when it came to martial arts, no one in the clan trained harder than the two of us.

If anyone understood what it was like to live under the old man’s thumb, it was Tadashi. We were both trapped in the same cage.

I guess I’d always seen him as competition, not the enemy Father wanted me to believe he was.

Now that Tadashi was spiraling down this dark path… I couldn’t stand by and do nothing. He’d trusted me to help him, hadn’t he?

So, on Thursday afternoon, I stopped giving him the drug. The withdrawal symptoms hit him hard. The tremors returned, worse than before. Nausea, vomiting, a runny nose, headaches, he was sensitive to light and sound, drenched in cold sweat. He even had another seizure.

He begged me for another dose, pleaded, then yelled, even cursed. I stared at him, stunned. Tadashi was the most composed, well-mannered person in the entire clan. As the heir, he’d been drilled on etiquette and decorum since he was a kid. I’d never seen him lose control, never heard him raise his voice, let alone use that kind of language.

He even tried to make a run for it, but I tackled him, wrestled him onto the bed. We struggled, a tangle of limbs and desperate gasps. Weakened as he was, he didn’t stand a chance against me. Finally, his fight drained away, leaving him trembling in the corner, hugging his knees to his chest.

I had to work that night.

“Don’t do anything stupid,” I said, my voice hard, unconsciously mimicking the old man’s tone of command.

“Don’t yell, don’t answer the door if anyone knocks. I’m locking you in. You can use the bathroom. And don’t even think about trying to escape through the window, got it?”

There wasn’t a window in my room, but there was a small ventilation shaft in the bathroom. It would be a tight squeeze even for a kid, but desperation could make people do crazy things.

“Tadashi, did you hear me?” I snapped, frustrated by his silence. “Do I need to tie you up and gag you? Do you want to spend the night on the bathroom floor?”

He slowly lifted his head, his eyes bloodshot, his gaze vacant.

“Don’t,” he rasped, his voice raw and broken.

I’d cleared the room of anything he could use to hurt himself—weapons, anything sharp or breakable. I’d even taken down the mirror in the bathroom and stripped the bed of its sheets. It should have been enough, but I couldn’t shake the feeling that I was forgetting something, that something bad was going to happen.

I crouched down in front of him, meeting his gaze.

Damn it. I wasn’t good at this whole comforting thing. Why did everything have to be so damn messy? Maybe this was karma for all the times Tadashi had looked after me, patched me up. Now it was my turn to take care of him.

“I’ll be back after work,” I said, the words feeling clumsy and inadequate. “You don’t have to go through this alone. You’ll be fine. You don’t need that stuff.”

My chest tightened as Tadashi shook his head, silent tears tracking down his cheeks.

Damn it. What the hell was I supposed to do?

Tadashi mumbled something, his voice barely audible. I leaned closer, straining to hear.

“Sadi, I can’t take it anymore… I can’t… I can’t stop. I can’t control it.”

“Don’t give me that crap!” I snarled, grabbing his shirt and shoving him back against the wall.

“Listen up, you idiot,” I growled, my face inches from his. “Don’t tell me you don’t remember why I punched you after you passed Elder Zuko’s class.”

Elder Zuko’s class was a special kind of hell reserved for ninja who were about to embark on suicide missions. Interrogation and Torture 101, they called it. (Yeah, yeah, there were 201 and 301 levels, too.) Tadashi and I had to endure it before we went on that insane mission to retrieve the royal bracelet from the Koga clan.

“That sadistic bastard broke my leg, poisoned me, and made me puke up everything I ate for two weeks straight. You think he went easy on you? You aced his class, and I failed. So don’t tell me you can’t handle a little withdrawal.”

Tadashi didn’t even try to fight back. He just sat there, eyes closed, absorbing the verbal blows. His voice, when he finally spoke, was rough with unshed tears.

“It’s not about the pain, Sadi,” he said, his voice a broken whisper. “It’s… it’s the feeling. The feeling of… perfection. Of everything being… right.”

As I listened, it hit me—Tadashi could withstand the pain; he was used to it. But happiness? In our messed-up lives as ninjas, burdened with overwhelming responsibilities, happiness felt like a distant dream, something we longed for but hardly recognized. I got that. So what truly broke him was that he had tasted that fleeting contentment, and now it was being taken away. Losing that moment of peace was the real agony.

“So what if things aren’t perfect?” I shot back. “This clan, they own us, Tadashi. They always have, and they always will. They’ll keep demanding the impossible, pushing us to our limits. You’re not perfect, you’re going to screw up, and they’re going to punish you for it. That’s just how it is.”

Tadashi looked at me, his eyes filled with a mixture of hurt and despair. I ignored it. This was for his own good. He needed a wake-up call, even if it was a painful one.

“This is the life we chose, Tadashi. Don’t be a coward. Don’t hide from it. That drug, it’s a worse master than the clan ever will be. Trust me, I’ve seen what it does to a friend. You have to choose. Who do you want to be a slave to? The clan? Or a needle?”

Tadashi turned away, burying his face in his hands. “You don’t understand,” he choked out. “The clan… they won’t… they won’t have me anymore, Sadi.”

“Damn it, Tadashi!” I released him, pushing away from the wall. I ran a hand through my hair, frustration and helplessness warring within me.

“I met Mom and Shizuka a while back,” I said quietly, shifting gears.

Tadashi fell silent, watching me with a wary curiosity.

“They’re still at the same place you told me about,” I said quietly. Four years ago, when Tadashi had been punished and forced to serve the clan members, he’d spent some time at my mom’s place. She’d remarried and moved outside the city.

“You know, Father always treated me like I was a disappointment. But Mom and Shizuka, they didn’t care about any of that.”

They accepted me, no judgment, no expectations. I’d only stayed for a short visit, but it meant the world to me. For days afterward, I felt this… warmth, this sense of belonging that I’d never felt before. It kept me sane during the old man’s wrath.

I turned back to Tadashi, searching his face for a reaction.

“You messed up, yeah, but the clan took you back after they were done punishing you, right? I don’t know about your father, but I think your brother and sister, they want you to come home. Even if you’re not… yourself. It’s not going to be easy, but they’re waiting for you.”

Time to play my trump card.

“If your mother were still alive,” I said softly, “she’d be waiting for you, too. You know what I mean? Even if you screw up, even if the clan turns its back on you, it’s not the end. You’re still here, Tadashi. You still have a life to live. You can fix this. Don’t throw it all away on drugs. You can’t give up. Not when there are people out there who care about you.”

Tadashi stared at me, speechless. It was like I’d slapped him across the face.

Good. That was all I had.

He’d always been a family man, and I knew how much his mother had meant to him, even after all these years.

The stunned look on his face slowly melted away, replaced by a flicker of something else… hope? Regret? He covered his eyes with trembling hands, took a shuddering breath, and then nodded.

About damn time.

“Listen, I’m already late for work,” I said, my voice flat, emotionless. “If you want to have a group hug and braid each other’s hair, we can do that later.”

Tadashi choked back a sob, then let out a shaky laugh. I snorted, rolling my eyes.

“If you puke, clean it up. I’m out of here.”

Tadashi nodded weakly.

“Stay put, alright?”

“Alright.”

I locked the bedroom door, pocketed the key, and headed out.

I figured he’d be okay. I was wrong. When I got back to the lodgings, the front door was unlocked. The bedroom door hung open, a silent accusation. Tadashi was gone. I checked the stash of money I kept in a small box under the mattress. Empty.

I checked the closet. Empty. His clothes, his bag, everything was gone. Even his toiletries were missing from the bathroom. A headache blossomed behind my eyes, and my pulse quickened. The idiot had bolted.

Damn it! Where the hell had he gone?

There were no signs of a break-in. Someone had to have let him out. And since I hadn’t brought anyone else here, and the only other person with a key was Misao…

He’d taken all his belongings, which meant he hadn’t been forced to leave. But why would he go with Misao? Where would they even go?

Misao knew I’d cut him off. The only explanation that made sense was that she’d felt sorry for him and taken him to their headquarters. This could actually work to my advantage. I just needed to figure out where they’d gone.

I scanned the room. It was tidy, nothing out of place. I checked the bathroom. Same story. Except for a bar of soap that had fallen near the drain.

I picked it up, about to put it back in the dish, when something caught my eye. Scratched into the soap, barely visible, were three letters.

AKI.

My heart skipped a beat. Was this a message? From Tadashi?

I paced the small bathroom, my mind racing. AKI… what the hell did it mean? My father? Aki was one of the old man’s aliases. But why would Tadashi leave a message about him? Did he want me to go see him?

I grabbed a map of the area. There was nothing around the clan’s safe house except empty fields. I traced my finger along the road, fifteen minutes from his location, until I found it—Tasekomi Valley.

An abandoned textile factory. The company had gone bankrupt years ago, leaving the building to rot. Perfect. The kind of place a drug operation would thrive in. I swung by the warehouse, grabbed some extra weapons, and headed out.

I didn’t bother seeing the old man. No point in telling him that Tadashi had taken off. I just left a message saying I was checking out a possible drug den in Tasekomi and that I might need backup.

My plan was simple. Get into the factory, grab Mio, find their records, and get out. With Tadashi, if he was still there. And still alive.

Night had fallen by the time I reached the factory. It looked deserted from the outside, but appearances could be deceiving. The building was a massive, windowless structure, shrouded in darkness. No guards at the gate. I hesitated, a flicker of unease in my gut, but decided to scout the perimeter before making my move.

A sliver of moon hung in the sky, casting an eerie glow over the overgrown yard. The factory was surrounded by high, moss-covered walls, a ring of ancient pines standing sentinel beyond the gates. The building itself sat at the bottom of a sloping valley, tucked away in shadow. A faint light spilled from a small window at the back of the factory. Someone was home.

I was about to make my way to the back when a prickling sensation crawled up my spine. I froze. Someone—no, something—was watching me. A faint panting, a low growl, barely audible in the stillness of the night.

A twig snapped underfoot, and a sudden gust of wind rustled the leaves in the pines. Two glowing yellow eyes pierced the darkness, watching me from behind a thick trunk. And then I saw it—a flash of white teeth in a silent snarl.

That explained the lack of guards.

The wolf creature was a blur of motion, lightning fast. I leaped aside, swinging my katana just as it lunged. The blade connected with its flank, a sickening thud echoing through the night.

The wolf snarled, a guttural sound of fury, and charged again.

For a few heart-stopping moments, we were locked in a deadly dance—lunge, parry, dodge, strike. Then I saw my chance. My blade flashed out, finding its mark, burying itself deep in the creature’s chest.

The wolf shuddered, collapsing to the ground, its glowing eyes dimming, staring up at me with a vacant intensity. A shiver ran down my spine. How the hell was Roroa controlling these things?

I didn’t think he was a Mitsurugi, but he could be paying them off to secure the area. Or maybe this whole operation was a Mitsurugi front, run by outsiders to keep their hands clean.

I slipped through the broken gate into the factory’s backyard. A window on the back porch was boarded up, strips of wood nailed across it to keep out unwanted visitors. The faint glow of an oil lamp flickered from behind the makeshift barrier.

I peered through a crack in the boards. A toilet. The stench of stale urine, vomit, and something unidentifiable (and decidedly unpleasant) hit me like a physical blow. There was no back door. I’d have to go through the window.

I followed a narrow hallway, the air thick with the cloying sweetness of incense and something sharper, chemical. The hallway forked, and I could hear muffled voices coming from a large room to the right, lit by the flickering glow of oil lamps.

I peered inside. The room was filled with people, slumped in chairs, sprawled on the floor, lost in a drug-induced haze. Some were unconscious, others groaning, laughing, talking to themselves. A dozen or so guards kept watch, their faces hard, their eyes watchful. But no sign of Mio.

I turned left, following the hallway as it curved, leading to a corridor with polished wooden floors and shoji screens lining the walls. Muffled voices drifted from behind the closed doors. I moved quickly, past what looked like a kitchen, a dining room, a meeting room, even a guard barracks. At the end of the corridor, a narrow staircase led upwards.

The second floor was eerily silent. The doors here were solid wood, no paper screens. From downstairs, a sudden clamor of voices erupted, breaking the stillness.

I ran down the hallway, turning a corner. A small door, locked. I pulled out a length of wire, quickly picking the lock. Inside, expensive antiques and luxurious furnishings hinted at the wealth and power of the room’s owner. This had to be Roroa’s office.

The desk was clear, but I ran my hand underneath, feeling for a hidden mechanism. There. A small button. I pressed it, and a secret drawer slid open at the bottom of the desk. It was filled with ledgers, records of transactions, shipments, everything.

Jackpot.

Footsteps. Coming closer.

I slammed the drawer shut and glanced around. A door on the opposite wall led to a small library. Towering bookshelves lined the walls, creating a labyrinth of narrow aisles that opened into a central atrium. A shoji screen stretched across the far end of the room.

A harsh voice barked from behind the screen. “Bring him in!” The shoji slid open. I flattened myself against a bookshelf, hiding in the shadows, watching through a gap in the books.

A guard entered, followed by a man I recognized as Roroa. And then Misao.

And then my blood ran cold.

Two more guards followed, dragging a limp figure between them. Tadashi. His hands were bound behind his back, his head lolling against his chest. They tossed him to the floor like a sack of rice.
