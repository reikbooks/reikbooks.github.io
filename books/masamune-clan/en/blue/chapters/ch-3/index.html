Chapter 3

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

“An assasin?!”

I squeezed Shiro’s hand tighter and looked around. My class was ahead of us, with only a few stragglers behind.

“What do you mean, Shiro?” I whispered. “How do you know?”

Shiro pressed her lips together, refusing to answer. Her face was pale. Was she serious? She couldn’t be joking about something like this. Shiro had incredibly sharp instincts; several times, she’d known what I wanted before I’d even spoken. But…

“We have to cancel this trip,” she said, her voice strained. “Everyone needs to get out of this forest.”

I frowned.

“Shiro, I can’t just tell Mother or the teachers something like this without any explanation,” I said. “They’ll have questions. They might think I’m playing a prank.”

“Would you rather someone die than risk embarrassing yourself?” Shiro’s voice was sharp.

Heat flooded my cheeks. “No! Of course not. I don’t want anyone to get hurt. It’s just… they can’t dismiss everyone based on a child’s claim without proof—”

“Do they have to wait until someone is attacked?”

I opened my mouth to argue, then hesitated. “They need something concrete. Overhearing a plan, seeing something suspicious…”

“They’ll listen to you , Tadashi. I’m just a kid. If you tell them, they’ll believe you. Just say you overheard something in the bushes.”

“It’s not that simple. What if they ask what I heard? If they investigate and find out I lied, it would disgrace our clan.”

Shiro clicked her tongue and stamped her foot in frustration. “Why are you worried about the clan right now?! This is an emergency! If someone dies, it’s on your head.”

Shiro released my hand and strode ahead. My stomach twisted.

“Shiro, wait…”

“I’m going to ask Mother to take us home,” she called over her shoulder. “It’s up to you to save everyone else. If anything happens, it’s your responsibility.”

I bit my lip, my mind racing. What was I supposed to do?

“M-maybe I should tell Anko to keep a sharper eye out,” I stammered.

Shiro shrugged and quickened her pace. I hurried after her.

“Where are they?” I finally asked, even though I still didn’t know what I would do. I couldn’t possibly handle this alone.

“I don’t know,” she said. “I can only sense their intent, not just anger or the desire to harm, but a cold-blooded intent to kill.”

I glanced at the people around us: an elderly woman with a young girl, three older academy students, a mustached man with a boy from my class, a young man dressed in black, the beginner class teacher, and the school medic—a young man with sandy blond hair and dark eyes.

“Do you think they’re among us?” I asked, eyeing the man in black. He seemed out of place. He didn’t look like a student. Was he chaperoning a younger sibling?

“Depends who the target is,” Shiro replied, her gaze fixed ahead. “But they’ll likely try to isolate them.”

I considered her words. Who would be targeted on a school field trip? A student? Surely not… A teacher? No, it would be easier to attack them elsewhere, another time. Too many witnesses here, though it might be possible if they were alone. Were the parents the targets? Some who were usually hard to reach might be here today. Could there be multiple targets? Gathering them at the same event would certainly make it easier.

But who was important enough to be targeted here ?

Elder Han’s words from last week suddenly echoed in my mind: A leader is the spearhead of their people. Even off the battlefield, the clan leader is the enemy’s first target. Be prepared to be attacked from all sides—physically, or through scrutiny of your every action. You, and your family.

A chilling thought surfaced. Was it possible…? No, I pushed it away.

We caught up to Mother, and Shiro put her plan into action. She pretended to trip, crying out and clutching her ankle.

“I think I sprained it!” she whimpered. “I want to go home.”

“But we haven’t even reached the halfway point,” Mother said, glancing at me. “It would be a shame for Tadashi to miss the rest of the trip.”

I shook my head. “It’s alright, Mother. I’m happy we got to walk together this far.”

“Alright,” Mother said, relief evident in her voice. “I’ll let your teacher know. Take Shiro to the medic. He should have some ointment for the swelling.”

I nodded and helped Shiro, who was putting on a convincing limp, find the medic—a tall young man at the back of the group.

“What’s the matter?” he greeted us, his smile friendly. I explained about Shiro’s ankle, and he helped her sit on a fallen log. He knelt to examine her ankle, and Shiro suddenly yelped.

“Oh! I need to pee!” she exclaimed, hopping on one foot. She grabbed my arm. I stared at her, bewildered.

“Ow… come on! There’s a river over there.”

Shiro pulled me along. Before we reached the river, she abruptly veered off behind some tall bushes, yanking me hard. I stumbled against her.

“Tadashi, the medic is the assasin!” she hissed. “Run! He’s after us!”

Before I could question her, Shiro was sprinting through the undergrowth. I followed, leaping over roots and dodging branches. I glanced back. I couldn’t see him, but I could hear the pursuit closing in.

We’re not going to make it.

“Shiro, I’ll distract him! Find Mother!”

Shiro nodded and sped up. I stopped, my hand trembling as I pulled out a shuriken. Just buy her some time…

The bushes rustled. I threw the shuriken, then ran in the opposite direction.

The man followed me. Good.

Wait… does that mean I’m the target?

I ducked under a low-hanging branch, squeezing through a narrow gap between two trees, hoping he couldn’t follow. But the forest thinned out, and I found myself on the riverbank. In the distance, I saw Shiro almost reaching Mother. They were alone.

Something whizzed past my ear.

I dropped to the ground. A nunchaku whistled through the air where my head had been a moment before. He slammed the chain down again, and I rolled, narrowly avoiding the weighted ends. The blows came fast and furious, giving me no chance to counter.

I spotted an opening, dodged, and used the kata Elder Low had drilled into me, thrusting my fist into his ribs. But I wasn’t fast enough. He stumbled back, but quickly recovered, grabbing my wrist, twisting it, and slamming me to the ground. My arm bent at an agonizing angle.

“Aargh!”

Pain exploded in my arm. I kicked out, connecting with his jaw, and he recoiled, releasing his grip. I scrambled to my feet, cradling my injured arm.

I heard someone shout my name.

Mother?

The momentary distraction was all it took. The whir of the nunchaku and the sickening thud against the back of my head sent me spiraling into darkness.
