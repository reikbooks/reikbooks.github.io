Chapter 4

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

Pain.

I gasped, blinking against the throbbing in my head. Every movement sent jolts of pain through me. My right arm throbbed, a searing agony. Slowly, fragmented memories surfaced: the riverbank, Mother and my siblings across from me, the assasin striking from behind.

He caught me.

My heart hammered against my ribs. If he was an assasin, as Shiro claimed, why was I still alive? A kidnapper, perhaps? Or was I not the target after all? But I was a witness… he should have eliminated me.

My mouth tasted of blood. I gritted my teeth, pushing down the fear that threatened to consume me, and assessed my situation.

A black cloth blindfolded me. I was propped against a tree, ankles and knees bound tightly. My wrists were tied behind the trunk.

The gurgling of the river, the scent of damp earth… I was still near the riverbank.

Where was he? Silence hung heavy in the air. Had he left? But why? Or was he close, watching?

I have to escape.

Carefully, I tested the ropes, trying to loosen them. Too tight. I persisted, the rough fibers chafing against my skin until my wrists were raw. Inch by agonizing inch, I managed to shift my hands. Pain shot up my injured arm.

I bit back a groan. Stay quiet. He mustn’t know.

With my wrists in a slightly better position, I fumbled with my left hand, pressing two fingers against my right, forming the hand seal to release a concealed shuriken. Slowly, I began to saw at the ropes.

A heavy boot stomped down on my hand, driving the shuriken into my flesh.

A cry of pain ripped from my throat.

The man snatched the shuriken. A stinging slap sent my head reeling.

“Trying to escape so soon?” he asked, his voice chillingly calm and quiet, like a teacher explaining a simple lesson.

“W-what do you w-want—”

Another slap cut me off. Blood filled my mouth. I heard fabric tear as he ripped a strip of cloth and gagged me. I could still speak, but my words were muffled.

“I want you to call your mother,” he whispered, his breath hot against my ear. “After she’s dead, I’ll deal with of your siblings. You’ll be last.”

My family… He’s going to kill them all!

I struggled, but he choked me, his thumb pressing against my throat, tracing an invisible symbol on my skin. A moment later, the seal activated. He left me, though likely still close.

The chilling realization of his plan hit me like a physical blow. The seal! It locked my body. My chest tightened, as if an invisible hand were squeezing the air from my lungs. The ropes constricted, burning hot against my skin. Sharp pain pierced my flesh, intensifying rapidly until it was unbearable.

The seal held me frozen in torrents of pain.

I wanted to scream, but if Mother heard me… she would come. Straight into his trap.

A choked groan escaped me. The pain was excruciating, like hot knives peeling my skin layer by layer. Tears streamed down my face, soaking the blindfold. My body trembled, held rigid by the paralysis seal.

I bit down hard on the gag. Don’t make a sound. Mother can’t come here. Shiro and Raito…

The burning knives twisted, digging deeper. Agony tore through me.

Aaaaah!

I gasped, trembling. God, p-please…

Be quiet, be quiet, be quiet—

Mother…

Be quiet. Mother will be hurt because of you.

Help me…

BE QUIET!

Mother…

BE QUIET, TADASHI!

I wept. Tears streamed down my face.

S-sorry…

I couldn’t hold it back any longer.

“AAAH! AAAAAHH!! AAAAAAAHHH!!!”

Screams ripped from my throat, raw and ragged. The gag was useless, like trying to silence a dying animal.

Tadashi, stop! You’re luring her here!

A sliver of rational thought pierced the agony, but it was no match for the overwhelming pain. Everything blurred. Pain, blood, and the suffocating darkness that refused to claim me.

I heard a faint call, not far away.

“Tadashi?”

Terror, like a bucket of ice water, poured over me.

Mother…

“DON’T COME HERE!” I choked out, the words garbled and weak. “MOTHER, GO AWAY!”

Footsteps approached. Mother… She heard me. I tried to shake my head, but my body refused to obey.

“Tadashi!”

My heart clenched. A whimper escaped my lips. No…

“Please, Mother…” I sobbed, knowing it was futile. Please, just leave me.

A sharp jolt of pain as someone touched my arm. Mother screamed. A thud.

What’s happening?

“You!” Mother’s voice was raw with fury. The sounds of a struggle – metal clashing against metal, a sickening thud. Then an explosion of sound, a shriek, and silence.

Mother!

Gasping breaths approached. Who—

The gag was ripped from my mouth.

“Tadashi… oh, my darling—”

The assasin… is he dead?

Mother frantically untied the blindfold, then the ropes. The paralysis seal released as my bonds were cut.

“Are you alright?”

My voice was gone. I nodded, raising a trembling hand to wipe my tears, swallowing the blood that coated my throat. I forced the words out.

“He… he wanted to kill us all, Mother.”

A groan came from a few meters away. The assasin’s prone form stirred.

I flinched. Mother gripped my arm.

“Listen to me, Tadashi,” she said, her voice strained. I looked at her, and my blood ran cold. Crimson stained her clothes at her stomach.

Mother …

“Go find Shiro and Raito. Get help. Understand?” Her voice was urgent. “Go!”

Leave her? Alone with him? I should stay and fight!

“But Mother—”

She shook me, her eyes fierce. “Listen! He might have accomplices. Your siblings are in danger. You have to protect them. I’ll handle this.”

The man was getting to his feet.

Mother pulled me close, her arms tight around me, and kissed my forehead. “Whatever happens, don’t give up. Be strong, Tadashi,” she whispered, her forehead pressed against mine. I clutched her clothes, unable to breathe, my chest aching.

Then she smiled, tears in her eyes.

“Look after your siblings, Tadashi. I love you.”

A volley of throwing knives whizzed towards us. Mother deflected some; the rest thudded into the surrounding trees.

“Run!” she hissed.

Through blurred vision, I stumbled away, my injured arm screaming in protest. I roughly wiped my cheeks, my throat raw with unshed tears. I have to be strong. I didn’t want to leave her, but my parents had charged me with protecting my siblings.

Mother was holding him off, buying me time.

If anything happens, it’s your responsibility. Shiro’s words rang in my ears.

I swallowed, my raw throat convulsing.

This is my fault.

I found Shiro carrying a sleeping Raito. She had already found help – Anko and two other teachers.

Anko saw me and rushed over. “Where’s your mother?” she demanded, her voice sharp with worry.

“Near the river… west of here,” I gasped, pointing.

Anko barked orders at my homeroom teacher to take us home, then she and the others raced towards the river.

My teacher took Raito from Shiro and led the way. Shiro kept glancing back at me, her brow furrowed, her lips moving silently as if she wanted to speak, but our teacher’s presence held her back. I kept my gaze fixed on the ground.

The sun blazed high in a cloudless, blue sky. Its brilliance a stark contrast to the storm of fear churning inside me. The river flowed serenely, its gentle ripples a cruel mockery to the chaos I carried in my heart.

I cast one last glance over my shoulder, my feet rooted for a fleeting moment, then turned and trudged home. The weight of Mother’s gaze seared into my memory. Her smile lingered—gentle, encouraging, and devastatingly knowing.

It was as if she understood… as if she knew I would carry this regret for the rest of my life.

But fate is merciless, and I couldn’t turn back time.

On that hushed, quiet afternoon, Mother left us forever.
