Epilogue B - Shirasu Mitsurugi

If Mother saw this, she’d kill me.

Of course, she’d have to take a number—Tadashi’s father and siblings would be first in line. Anko and Sadi too. Honestly? The Masamune elders. Maybe everyone who’d ever known Tadashi’s kindness would sign up.

Forget them—even Ikaru and I wanted me dead.

The only one who didn’t—ironically—was Tadashi himself.

What other reason was there to endure this hell, if not to save me from Ikaru? Me. Shiro. Father.

He wanted to save us all—so he took the full brunt of Ikaru’s attention.

That bastard Ikaru was like a child with a new toy—determined to bend it to his will.

Tadashi and I agreed that to avoid suspicion, he had to resist Ikaru at first. Who gets captured by the enemy and surrenders without even being interrogated?

But there would be no interrogation here. Ikaru had no interest in extracting information—Tadashi couldn’t speak anyway, bound by the speech seal. The perfect arrangement.

This was merely Ikaru’s sadistic game. Torture after torture, all to force a single phrase from Tadashi’s lips: Lord Ikaru . To break him until he knelt willingly, obeyed without the Seal of Obedience, without chains—just the ruin of his own pride.

Of course Tadashi refused.

Watching the kid carry out our secret mission made me uneasy. Tadashi played his part so convincingly, I worried his mind might actually fracture. He needed to “break,” of course—to surrender completely so Ikaru would trust him enough to reveal his weakness.

But he didn’t break under Ikaru’s hands. Broken bones, yes—but his spirit? Never.

He surrendered to me .

That day, Ikaru ordered me to torture Tadashi. The bastard watched with a smug grin as I obeyed. I tried to treat it like any other job—to detach myself, to ignore Ikaru’s taunts—but it wasn’t that simple. He knew exactly how to twist the knife, how to make sure both Tadashi and I felt every second of it.

I focused solely on ending the session as quickly as possible. Like peeling an onion, I stripped away Tadashi’s defenses layer by layer. I knew the exact moment his walls crumbled. He sobbed softly in my arms, his pleading voice cutting deep.

Ikaru laughed, his proud gaze fixed on me. “Perfect, Shirasu,” he praised, his smile so infuriating I wanted to tear it from his face. “Now get out and guard the door. I’ll call you when I need you.”

Ikaru signaled for me to kneel. He seized my hair and forced my head down. Gritting my teeth, I reached out and pressed my lips to the hem of his robe. When he finally released me, I rose on shaking legs. Never had I felt so degraded, so utterly defiled. And never had I wanted so desperately to kill Ikaru—and myself—as I did in that moment.

Who knows how long I stood there, frozen before the door, fists clenched, my thoughts raging—forced to listen helplessly to Ikaru’s movements just beyond.

When Ikaru finally finished, I stepped inside and stared blankly at my brother’s unconscious form sprawled across the floor. Blood pooled beneath his severely wounded body.

My head pounded. My eyes burned, my face was on fire. Gasping for air, I knelt to heal Tadashi before staggering to my feet. I rushed down the hall to the bathroom, flung the door open, and retched into the toilet.

The pale light of dawn crept through the window as I sat staring at the empty sky. No drink in my hand, no food in sight, not even a blanket to ward off the chill that made me shiver. My brother fared worse—he always did.

I’d warned Ikaru to take breaks between sessions, but who could stop that psychopath? At this rate, Tadashi might die before our mission succeeded. I wasn’t sure which outcome was worse—Tadashi dying before he tried to kill Ikaru, or after.

What if this mission succeeded, but Tadashi paid with his life?

The sky remained silent beneath my accusing stare.

I couldn’t pinpoint the exact moment I began seeing Tadashi as my younger brother—when his well-being started mattering to me, when that protective instinct first stirred. All I knew was the regret that followed.

I regretted bringing him to this godforsaken place. I had no idea how Mother thought Tadashi could help me escape.

I squeezed my eyes shut, but Tadashi’s whimpers still echoed in my mind.

Mother… is this what you wanted when you chose to bring him into this world?

After that, Tadashi followed Ikaru’s every word without question—without hesitation. Even when ordered to torture me. He showed neither pleasure nor remorse, just silent obedience. It worried me. Did he still remember our secret mission? I never got the chance to ask.

Until finally, one afternoon—for the first time since locking Tadashi away—Ikaru left the room, leaving Tadashi alone with me.

I quickly checked Tadashi’s condition. He remained semi-conscious, his body still twitching from the fading pulses of my healing energy.

“Hey,” I murmured, checking his pulse before gently wiping the blood from his face. Tadashi blinked slowly, struggling to focus his eyes on me. “Ikaru’s out. You hanging in there?”

“Ibuprofen. Please,” he rasped. I grabbed the medicine kit and administered a strong dose of painkiller, then helped him drink. Tadashi stayed quiet, shivering as he struggled to steady his uneven breaths.

“I saw something,” Tadashi said, pointing toward the door at the far end of the room. “Ikaru went in there several times—for an hour or two each time. But once he forgot to close the door. That’s when I saw him… disappear.”

I frowned. The cramped room was filled with torture devices. What had Tadashi meant—was there a secret door hidden somewhere? I searched every inch of the space, running my hands along the walls for any lever or mechanism, but found nothing. It was just a dead-end storage closet.

Tadashi and I exchanged a silent glance.

The answer only came to us the next time Ikaru left.

“I think I know where Ikaru vanished,” Tadashi whispered, tightening his grip on my arm. “He went to the realm of darkness.”

Ah. Right—why hadn’t I thought of that before?

I opened the door to the small room and used my Doboro’s power to slip into the darkness between dimensions.

The realm of darkness overlapped with the real world—like opening a closet and discovering unexpected space inside. The door in that room led me to an altar.

Dark creatures encircled the altar, their energy-draining tendrils snaking outward.

That was where Ikaru made his offerings and paid the creatures bound to him by contract.

The Mitsurugi Clan had long forged pacts with creatures of darkness. We offered what they desired, and in return, they granted our wishes.

Take my Doboro, for example. It wielded both electrical and teleportation abilities. I could tap into its power, but each use came at a cost—a jolt of electricity coursing through my body. The stronger the ability I used, the more intense the shock. Of course it hurt. That pain was exactly what the Doboro craved. To them, I was nothing more than a master providing their next meal.

Ikaru, as clan head, forged many contracts. His mind control ability stemmed from these pacts—a power passed down through generations from the previous leader, along with the Seal of Obedience. With it, he could control us partially or entirely.

To fulfill his contracts, Ikaru had to regularly sacrifice his energy to the dark creatures. That was his weakness—the perfect moment to strike. He’d been hiding here all along.

Tadashi and I seized our next chance to put the plan into action. Once Ikaru vanished into that room, I guided Tadashi into the darkness dimension and handed him the weapon I’d procured from Nobuga—a blade that should’ve been lethal.

But when Tadashi drove the Kusanagi into Ikaru’s prone form on the altar, the bastard woke up instead.

His eyes gleamed in the darkness. As Tadashi’s blade shattered, Ikaru struck with a bone-crushing blow. I gaped at the broken blade in disbelief. How could the Kusanagi break? Unless… it had been a fake all along.

No time to curse Nobuga. Ikaru’s grip crushed my throat, cracked my jaw—no air for screams. He flung me down and triggered the Seal of Obedience. Fire raced up my spine—locking my limbs, stealing my voice. Then he seized Tadashi by the hair and slammed him onto the altar.

“Shirasu, you’ve truly disappointed me,” Ikaru said, his voice calm as he traced a seal around the altar. “Because of that, I’ll bestow the Seal of Obedience upon your brother. I should’ve done this long ago, but I wanted to see him submit willingly. Now, you’ve left me no choice.”

Ikaru chuckled darkly. “You and your brother will pay for this—for everything.”

Through the searing pain, I watched as Ikaru pressed the Seal of Obedience against Tadashi’s skin.

I wanted to look away. To block out Tadashi’s screams. But I couldn’t.

We’d failed—utterly.

And it was entirely my fault.

After Ikaru caught Tadashi and me red-handed, he dragged us to the clan base on Mount Kazan. What followed was pure hell. I thought I’d endured the worst, but Ikaru’s cruelty surpassed even my darkest imagination.

Trapped in the underground prison, I’d lost all sense of time. Too long—that much was certain. Each breath felt like it might be my last.

Back then, I didn’t know Seishiro was helping Shiro secure the real Kusanagi sword. When Shiro finally reached our clan’s stronghold, Ikaru released me—only to take control of both my body and Seishiro’s to intercept Shiro, Sadi, and Anko.

It was the first time I fought with Ikaru fully in control. My thoughts were clouded; I couldn’t focus on my own will. All that remained was the single purpose of eliminating the clan’s enemies.

Like a crazed puppet on invisible strings, I attacked Anko without mercy—sweet, gentle Anko, who still wore the necklace I’d given her. With every blow, my heart splintered as if being torn apart.

Anko fought well—she even managed to wound me and crack a few ribs—but her strength wasn’t enough to withstand all my attacks.

She was hurt—because of me.

I wanted to stop, to say something—anything—to her.

Sorry.

Help.

Anko…

I love you.

I’d do anything to stop these attacks, even for a moment, but I was powerless. All I could do was stare blankly at Anko. A desperate part of me hoped she’d break through my defenses and end this torment once and for all.

Then Shiro managed to kill Ikaru.

Ikaru was dead.

The invisible strings binding me under Ikaru’s Seal of Obedience snapped without warning. Seishiro and I felt it simultaneously—right in the middle of battle. My knees hit the ground as overwhelming pain wracked my body. Beside me, Seishiro collapsed, unconscious. But Anko and Sadi didn’t understand. Before we could react, Sadi’s attack struck, leaving Seishiro bleeding and broken.

Ugh.

Anko kicked me hard in the ribs. I tried to crawl, but blood filled my throat. Again and again, her kicks landed. I couldn’t see her face—was she still holding a grudge over my betrayal? I understood. Hell, I wanted to kick myself too. But she needed to understand it was over. Ikaru was dead. I had no fight left in me, least of all for her.

“Stop, Anko!” I begged. She didn’t listen. The sword plunged into my gut, and I screamed as the blade tore through me. Drenched in blood, I curled into myself, instinctively healing the wound—only for the agony to return, leaving me whimpering and shaking. My strength was fading fast.

Anko stood before me, studying me intently. I met her gaze, willing her to see that Ikaru’s hold over me was broken.

“Listen—Shiro has killed Ikaru—” I began, but my words were cut off as explosions rocked the hall below. The ground trembled, then split apart as the clan base collapsed into itself, devoured by molten fire.

Damn it all! I never thought Ikaru’s death would bring this whole place crashing down.

I teleported to Seishiro’s side and jolted him with a healing wave. His wounds were so severe that my energy drained rapidly, nearly depleting entirely.

Ugh. I staggered, my vision blurring as I fought the urge to collapse into darkness.

No. I couldn’t faint now. I grabbed Seishiro’s arm and teleported beside Tadashi—just in time, as he was about to slide into the lava chasm.

Damn it!

I grabbed Tadashi and teleported again—this time to Seishiro’s safe house. Too far. Agony seared through my body as I collapsed onto the bedroom floor. My skull cracked against the wall, darkness swallowing my vision. Gritting my teeth, thoughts scrambled, I dragged myself to Tadashi’s side. His face was ghostly pale. Stomach wound. Bleeding out. Fading pulse. He was dying.

Gods.

I drew a deep breath and focused, mustering the last of my strength. I had to stay strong—this was for my brother.

He saved me. Now it was my turn to save him.

I summoned my healing power, gripping Tadashi’s hand with all my strength. The surge of energy made me stagger, but I clung on desperately. My life force drained away mercilessly. My chest constricted—pain flared, breath fled. The metallic tang of blood flooded my mouth. My limbs turned leaden, my skin ice-cold. Then darkness swallowed me whole.

Somehow, I was still alive. I blinked groggily at Seishiro as consciousness returned. He’d changed my clothes—I lay tucked into bed, warm and comfortable. Beside me, Tadashi slept on, undisturbed.

“Sei?” I murmured, my voice thick with concern as I searched his face for any sign of how he was doing.

“We’re safe now. Get some rest,” Seishiro murmured, his voice steady and reassuring. “I’ll keep watch over you both.”

When I woke again, I felt better. Seishiro had made soup and insisted I drink some bitter herbal brew. After checking Tadashi’s condition, I treated the remaining minor wounds. He was stable but still weak, so I set up an IV and catheter before letting him rest.

For a month afterward, Seishiro and I handled Ikaru’s remaining lackeys and clan members who still stirred up trouble. No one dared claim Ikaru’s power—as the clan’s official heir, perhaps they were waiting for my return.

I kept tabs on news of the Masamune clan. Shiro reported her success—Ikaru dead, the Mitsurugi clan destroyed—to the King. Though Masada was released from detention, the financial sanctions against our clan remained. Afterward, Shiro threw herself into managing the clan’s affairs. To no one’s surprise, she proved remarkably capable.

I once watched her from a distance when Shiro visited Tadashi’s grave. She still wore that necklace—Mother’s sacred pendant from her days as guardian of Jikan Temple. Apparently, Mother had given it to Tadashi, who then passed it to Shiro. I never liked how it changed hands, but I trusted Shiro to care for it.

I had no idea how Anko was really doing. She carried on teaching at the academy without complaint, business as usual. Part of me wanted to talk to her—to apologize face-to-face—but I couldn’t muster the courage to approach her. And though I longed to rekindle what we’d had, I wasn’t ready to put Anko first. Not yet. Tadashi still needed me.

Life slowly returned to normal—except for Tadashi lying unconscious in his bed.

He still hadn’t woken up, despite my healing all his wounds. His body functioned normally, yet every attempt to rouse him failed. He lay motionless, eyes closed, as peaceful as if he were merely asleep.

Six months slipped by.

I still cared for Tadashi and helped Seishiro build his plantation business. Whenever I stayed home too long looking gloomy, Sei would invite me out. We’d walk for miles before turning back. Once, he even accompanied me on a pilgrimage to Mother’s grave. I prayed there for hours, begging her forgiveness with all my heart.

The peaceful atmosphere I lived in every day sometimes felt suffocating. I longed for Tadashi to wake up—to apologize, to atone for everything I’d done wrong. But Tadashi never stirred. Sometimes, as I sat by his bedside watching him, I wished I could lie down beside him and never wake up again.

Perhaps this was simply my punishment. I had to survive and endure whatever came my way—who else would look after Tadashi if I were gone?

That morning, Sei invited me for another walk. We strolled along the beach—I found myself wishing I could bring Tadashi here someday. The bright sunlight lifted my spirits a little. After sharing lunch together, Sei and I ambled home at a leisurely pace.

I should have walked faster. That afternoon when I arrived home, I found my father sitting beside Tadashi.
