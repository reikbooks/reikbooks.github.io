Epilogue B - Shirasu Mitsurugi

If Tadashi hadn’t gotten Riku—Ikaru’s concubine’s son—caught by the authorities, Ikaru wouldn’t be in such a rage.

I shrugged on my jacket and grabbed my tanto. Every movement sent sharp jolts of pain—Ikaru’s latest parting gift. The older he grew, the more childish he became. Wiping blood from the corner of my lip, I turned to Seishiro.

His disapproving gaze said everything. He was worried, but he couldn’t ask me to back down—not in front of the clan, not after Ikaru had explicitly ordered me to lead. We held each other’s stare for a long moment.

Seishiro merely arched a brow. I rolled my eyes and signaled the advance. As the clan’s official heir, I couldn’t refuse orders just because Ikaru had decided to make an example of me—again. Ours was probably the only clan where the leader tortured his successor daily. Not that anyone dared mention it. No one wanted to die by Ikaru’s hand—or mine, or Seishiro’s.

Seishiro snorted, stubbing out his cigarette before falling into step behind me. That was his way of saying he’d watch my back on the mission.

Good, because my thoughts were scattered.

The image clung to me: Tadashi’s face in the bulletin before Ikaru tore it apart. With Riku’s capture, Tadashi’s photo had become the clan’s headline news.

When I killed Makoto, Tadashi was only seven. Now, at fifteen, the sight of him nearly stole my breath. He looked so much like Mother—if Chiyo had been a man. Father’s influence was there too, blending seamlessly. No one would recognize the so-called “illegitimate child” rumored to have died at birth, some outcast’s son from a forgotten village. But just as I saw our parents in him, Ikaru must have been shaken by that resemblance too.

Then again, Ikaru shouldn’t have lost control—he saw Chiyo’s child every day. Me. Maybe he’d grown used to my face, or maybe I simply didn’t resemble her enough. One thing was certain: my nature was nothing like hers. As for Tadashi? I had no idea.

Hah. Maybe I should keep an eye on Tadashi, just in case. He’d already stirred up trouble and caught Ikaru’s attention—which meant, indirectly, Tadashi had landed on my radar too as Ikaru’s bodyguard.

Yeah, that sounded like a plan. I didn’t need to babysit Tadashi myself—I could always tap the clan’s informants.

Funny how the thought of tailing a kid actually gave me a little extra motivation. Then again, when had my life ever been normal?

The Masamune clan successfully eliminated all infiltrators from their compound. We lost our most valuable asset—Toru Ishikawa, our linchpin for extracting information from that clan.

Ikaru was busy disciplining every soldier involved in the failed mission to steal the King’s artifact bracelet. Naturally, he started with me. Even though I wasn’t directly part of the operation, the responsibility for its failure still fell on me.

I buried myself in the mission report. Thankfully, the writer was one of our most thorough operatives. He’d painstakingly compiled intelligence from multiple sources, piecing together a complete picture even when our forces hadn’t witnessed events firsthand.

Hmm… so the Masamune clan sent Tadashi to infiltrate the Koga clan as a slave. Not exactly a job for an inexperienced kid. Somehow, Tadashi still managed to steal the bracelet from the Koga stronghold—though not without trouble. You know how those guards are—they get a little too enthusiastic about torturing their slaves. Toru himself took a crack at Tadashi while he was there—that guy was downright unhinged—but Tadashi flipped the script. Killed Toru, snatched the bracelet, and got out.

Course, it wasn’t a clean escape. Took an arrow, then got dragged straight to Oda Ishikawa—the Ishikawa clan’s resident lunatic. Even branded him with the Fuun mark. Real piece of work, that one.

Oh.

Hah. Big news. That brand couldn’t be removed—no way, no how. The Masamune heir had a slave mark on him. Heh. I’d love to see Masada and his clan elders’ faces when they found out.

I clicked my tongue. One thing was clear now—Tadashi wasn’t just some spoiled brat. The kid had spine.

Tadashi settled the dispute over the marijuana fields and wiped out Seishiro’s wolf pack. To make matters worse, he dismantled Roroa’s entire drug operation—one of our most valuable business partners.

I scratched my head, unable to explain the strange feeling welling up inside me. Part of me wanted to shout that the kid who attacked our clan was actually my brother—but of course, I wasn’t that reckless. Ikaru didn’t even know I’d uncovered Masada’s identity. And if I showed support for an enemy clan? Ikaru would have my head on a platter.

But that wasn’t what really mattered. The bigger issue was… Tadashi had used Roroa’s drugs on that mission. Our patented substances were some of the strongest on the market, and yet Tadashi had beaten his addiction?

I bit my lip. This was serious. Our entire organization was banned from using that drug—its grip was too powerful. We only dealt it; we never touched the stuff ourselves. If Tadashi had kicked the habit, his body and willpower were far stronger than I’d ever imagined.

“Why do you look like you’re about to laugh?” Seishiro muttered between curses that night. I kept him company while he drank, drowning his frustration over his business being forcibly shut down. Ikaru had beaten him badly—a rare humiliation for someone as meticulous as Seishiro.

“It’s nothing,” I said, picking up a glass from the table and taking a sip. “Just thinking about some girl.”

Seishiro snorted and resumed his grumbling.

I stared at the glass in my hand. Truth was, I couldn’t even remember the face of the girl who’d delivered Mother’s diary. I was just seeing that night’s events differently now.

Mother’s diary had opened new possibilities. Maybe—just maybe—her wish to free me from Ikaru could come true someday.

Maybe I could dare to hope.

Maybe he could actually help me.

Tadashi.

The brother I’d refused to acknowledge until now.

Ikaru sent me to infiltrate the Masamune clan.

It made sense. As the clan head’s direct descendant, I’d pass any blood-based detection shield effortlessly. Not that Ikaru mentioned that—he wanted to keep Father’s identity hidden from me. He just said he needed someone reliable, not a weakling like Toru Ishikawa.

I entered as Katsu Masamune. The irony wasn’t lost on me—infiltrating under my own name. Well, maybe it was just a code name Mother gave me, but it worked well enough.

Fabricating documents and orchestrating events to make the Masamune clan seek me out as their general physician took time, but I had patience. Ikaru hadn’t set a deadline. I was ready to wait years if needed. Besides, my medical training made the role easy enough to play.

But then I met Anko.

Sweet, sharp, beautiful—a girl with both brains and brawn. Her eyes were keen, scrutinizing, like she could see right through me. Not as Katsu, not as Shirasu the lackey of Ikaru, but as the Shirasu who’d once won his parents’ hearts. She pulled the old me back to the surface—the version who spoke from the heart.

My first mistake was falling for her.

Twenty-nine years alive, and for the first time, I understood what it meant to be drunk on love.

It haunted me every time I manipulated her. I’d lie awake at night, restless, imagining what could’ve been if we ran away together—left our stifling clans behind.

Anko made me believe happiness and peace weren’t just distant dreams.

Still, I always crushed that fantasy… because Ikaru would hunt me to the ends of the earth just to destroy whatever life I managed to build.

I refused to imagine what that bastard would do to Anko if we ever got caught.

So that was that. Anko could only ever be seasoning for this mission. It burned like swallowing embers every damn day… but never mind. Better this way.

I kept my distance from Tadashi and Shiro. They might recognize me, even after nine years since Makoto’s death. Especially Shiro—that kid seemed to have a sixth sense. Masada wasn’t a problem; he was too busy to even cross my path.

I kept my head down—following orders, never making waves. That gave me the freedom to watch Tadashi.

But seeing him up close… he wasn’t what I’d expected.

Sure, he had Mother’s face, her kindness too. And I had to admit, the kid was tough. After being stripped of his position as clan heir, the pressure should’ve crushed him. But it didn’t. He just kept working, steady as ever.

What really surprised me was Tadashi’s relationship with Masada. I’d assumed he was Father’s golden child, wrapped in warmth and favor. Not even close. Masada was cold, harsh—like Tadashi barely registered to him.

Masada ignored him.

No, worse—Masada treated him like he didn’t exist.

How many times had Tadashi dragged himself back from missions, bleeding, needing care? Masada never showed. Only Raito ever visited, doted on by Tadashi like the little prince he was.

Beyond Raito? Tadashi had no one. Maybe Sadi and Shiro, if they weren’t too busy.

I gathered all the information on Tadashi. Fortunately, they’d maintained complete records of his activities and missions from the moment he became clan heir until his demotion.

The documents contained thorough performance evaluations of Tadashi, annotated with comments from trainers, mentors, the elders—and occasionally Masada. They read like clinical observation notes on a lab specimen, meticulously filed away in the restricted section of the main hall’s library. Not that it posed any difficulty for me—I just had to teleport in.

The truth was, Tadashi had always been the elders’ puppet. Not like me, forced to serve Ikaru in every conceivable way, but close enough.

Still, that didn’t make him some naive, honest kid. Tadashi had his secrets—chief among them, his drug use. A glaring omission from his mission reports.

Which meant he was working with Sadi. Maybe their relationship mirrored mine and Seishiro’s—though I hadn’t shown Seishiro all my cards yet.

The idea of asking Tadashi for help no longer seemed impossible. But whether he’d trust me—or even agree—remained uncertain.

Not that it mattered.

Ikaru’s impatience grew, his plan to destroy the Masamune clan taking shape. Good. That meant I had an offer Tadashi couldn’t refuse.
