Shiro Masamune

It was close to midnight when Sadi and I got back to Anko’s house. She was still up, heating soup for a late supper.

“Still think Seishiro’s a good guy?” Sadi asked pointedly as he made tea. I kept scribbling in my notebook, barely looking up.

“Okay, he’s not good—but he was trying to send us a message,” I said, certain of it.

“What message? Those were just garbled symbols!” Sadi shouted from the kitchen.

“What symbols?” Anko asked, bringing the soup to the table. She’d missed the main hall meeting—tied up with academy business. I showed her my notebook. Her eyes widened as she stared at the symbols, stunned.

“Here’s a summoning circle,” Anko said, picking up the Mitsurugi clan’s book and flipping to a marked page. The illustration showed a hexagram inside a ring of arcane symbols. “It needs sacrifices to activate. These circles can do all sorts of things—communication, teleportation, opening portals, even calling dark creatures.”

Sadi set the tea down and leaned over Anko’s shoulder, studying the book. “What do these ten symbols mean?”

Anko frowned. “I’m not sure. They look like an activation sequence.”

Sadi sank into his seat and took a slow sip. “He’s planning to summon something, isn’t he? Dark creatures to attack the clan.” He paused. “We should prepare. Any second now the sky above the compound could rip open, and we’ll be swarmed by bloodsucking caterpillars… or worse.”

I drummed my fingers, shaking my head. “I doubt it. If Seishiro wanted to communicate, he’d do it directly—no ritual. And if he meant to summon creatures, why wait? This feels like a teleportation circle. A portal. Almost like he’s offering us a direct path to the Mitsurugi base.”

Sadi’s jaw dropped. “A shortcut? Why would he give us a shortcut?”

“Think about it. Why kill our agents in that pattern? It was a signal. He helped us get the Kusanagi sword, and now he’s practically shouting for us to bring it to him.”

“But why not just say it? Leave a note, an address—anything would’ve been simpler.”

“Maybe his clan is watching him and forbids revealing the base’s location. Or maybe the Seal of Obedience prevents it. How would I know?” I shrugged.

Sadi clicked his tongue. “You’re jumping to conclusions. Why build a portal at all? What if it’s a trap?”

“A trap? After all this? He’s gone to great lengths to help us. He needs something, Sadi. Something urgent.”

But what was so urgent they needed our help? What couldn’t Seishiro or Shirasu do themselves?

I froze as the pieces fell into place. They couldn’t kill Ikaru—the Seal of Obedience prevented it. And the reason why they needed my help now was because the plan with Tadashi had failed .

My thoughts tumbled as I reconstructed it: Shirasu paid Akira for the Jomon stone and the Kusanagi sword, not knowing Akira gave him fakes. He likely intended Tadashi to wield them. When Tadashi tried—and failed—to kill Ikaru with the counterfeit weapons, Ikaru must have flown into a rage and turned on both Shirasu and Tadashi.

Realizing the disaster, Seishiro desperately searched for the real sword. Maybe Ikaru ordered him to find and destroy it. While scouting Sumugari Temple, Seishiro spotted me. He knew I wanted the sword to kill Ikaru. So he helped me get the real one and took my replica—which he shattered during his fight with Sadi—so he could report back that the sword had been destroyed.

After that, Seishiro hastily crafted his “message” with the bodies he left behind—a clear summons urging me to hurry. He even provided our transport: that summoning-circle portal.

“Seishiro’s sending a distress signal. He needs our help to rescue Tadashi and Shirasu!” I announced, jumping to my feet and grabbing my jacket and weapon.

“Hey, wait. Where do you think you’re going, Shiro?” Sadi grabbed my arm.

“We have to hurry! Tadashi could be dying!” I shouted, heart hammering. “Anko, can you activate the circle?”

“Hold on, sweetheart. Slow down. Are you really going alone? We don’t know how many Mitsurugi are waiting. We should alert the clan and get backup before we charge in.”

“Fine. You handle it. Catch up later. I’m going now.”

“Seriously? I haven’t even eaten!” Sadi stood and grabbed his jacket. “Are you opening the portal—what, right here?”

Anko shook her head. “We don’t know if it works both ways. What if enemies come through? My house would be ruined. Let’s use the forest behind Shiro’s place.”

“Okay, I’ll meet you there,” Sadi said.

Anko gathered her equipment and the Mitsurugi book as we hurried to my house. Raito’s bedroom light was still on. He peered out when I opened the yard gate.

“Shiro?”

I waved and headed to the backyard. Raito hurried downstairs and followed me out. “Where are you going?”

“The Mitsurugi stronghold. Tadashi’s inside.”

“What? I’m coming!” Raito blurted.

I shook my head at once. “Anko’s opening a portal to their base. Sadi and reinforcements will follow. You stay here on guard, make sure no enemies slip through. And prep medical supplies in case anyone needs them.”

Raito looked ready to argue, but my glare shut him up. With a reluctant nod and a mutter, he fell in line. We followed Anko into the dense woods behind the house. The canopy swallowed most of the moonlight, leaving only a dim silver glow over a small clearing at its center.

Anko planted a power stone and set a reinforcement seal over it. Standing, she cast a protective barrier around the stone. Energy tendrils snaked outward, forming a wide circle with the stone at its heart. Consulting the Mitsurugi book, she activated a series of seals in swift succession.

Threads of light wove together into two intersecting stars. As the hexagram took shape, the circle began to hum, its surface shimmering like disturbed mercury. Static crawled across my skin.

“Shiro, the symbols,” Anko said, voice tight with strain. I thrust my notebook into her hand. She traced the sequence across the energy mirror’s surface, and as the last line connected, the glowing glyphs went dark.

The energy inside the mirror spun faster, darkening with each rotation. Thunder boomed from its depths. A fierce gust howled through the trees, bending their crowns. I lifted a hand to sweep my bangs aside.

The hum grew louder. Dark tendrils slithered within as strange symbols bloomed across the surface.

Anko scanned the pages, brow furrowed.

“Shiro, we’ve got trouble!” her shout was swallowed by the wind.

“What is it?”

“This circle demands a sacrifice.”

“Huh? Didn’t Seishiro already sacrifice ten people?”

“Yes, but there’s one thing missing: Seishiro’s blood—the key to open the portal.”

“What do you mean? Weren’t you the one who opened it?”

“Yes, but since Seishiro killed the sacrifices, he’s considered the one opening the portal. This mirror wants his blood—or a relative’s.”

The black energy circle swelled. Anko’s protective seal shuddered.

“Can’t you just close it?” I asked, pulse racing.

“No. The ritual’s already in motion.”

The ground trembled as the swirling black energy threatened to implode, edging toward a void. Without hesitation, Anko drew a kunai across her palm. Blood welled as she pressed her hand to the dark mirror. The energy roared—and the air split with a sharp, concussive crack.

A portal shimmered open, revealing an endless void beyond. Just past the threshold stood a towering white torii gate. Its sacred rope was knotted above a weathered nameplate: Yaminomon Temple.

The portal didn’t lead to the world of darkness—it opened a shortcut to the volcanic Mount Kazan region, far south of our kingdom. Beyond those peaks lay the Yami Kingdom.

I met Anko’s eyes—an unspoken question passing between us.

Raito approached and wrapped a bandage around her hand. She stayed silent, clearly shaken. I could feel her confusion, her worry. I understood. She was wondering if Seishiro was truly her blood relative.

I’d sensed faint traces of concern and affection from Seishiro when he sent wolves to guard Anko at Sumugari Temple. I hadn’t dwelled on it then, but now I was almost certain—Seishiro was likely Anko’s half-brother, sharing a mother or a father. Anko glanced at me, then looked away. She must have realized it too.

“So this is the Mitsurugi clan’s hideout?” Raito asked, tightening the last bandage.

I shrugged and pointed to the narrow path behind the temple gate, where candles burned with an eerie black-gold glow. “Anko and I will take this path. When Sadi arrives, tell him to follow, but spread the troops around the perimeter. We don’t want everyone converging here.” Raito nodded.

Anko unsheathed her bladed fans as I tightened my grip on my chain sickle. A jolt of static prickled my skin as I stepped through the portal. The mountain air was crisp and arid—utterly still, without a whisper of wind. Before us, a narrow path wound down the rocky slope and disappeared into encroaching darkness.

The moon vanished behind thick clouds as the black candles guttered with an eerie light. I crept forward, cautious with every step. At the end of the path, a fog-draped field of jagged stones spread out. Beyond it yawned a cave mouth, flanked by two crimson torches burning like demonic eyes.

I exchanged a glance with Anko and nodded. We’d barely taken three steps onto the misty field when the sound of labored breathing reached us. Something was dragging itself forward, panting heavily, emerging from the darkness beyond the fog.

They stepped into view—grotesque, demonic beasts hungry for blood. They looked like twisted bears with spiked tails, bodies like failed experiments. Matted fur and ragged flesh hung from their frames. Crimson eyes glowed over jaws bristling with razor teeth.

I struck without hesitation. My kusarigama’s chain sang through the air, coiling around the nearest beast’s throat. A sharp yank slammed it to the ground. As it groaned and lurched forward, I leapt and slashed its neck with the sickle at the chain’s end. In one motion, I jerked the chain with my left hand, sending the spiked weight hurtling toward the next monster. It caught the chain and yanked hard—but I flipped backward, my blade finding its arm midair.

Its shriek roused the pack. I severed its head and leapt clear, dodging raking claws. Then everything blurred.

The monsters roared, slashing at anything in reach. Their hulking frames made their movements predictable—but I’d forgotten their tails. A spiked appendage whipped across my arm, sending a searing jolt through flesh.

I dodged, parried, countered with a kick. My chain sickle spun through a deadly rhythm—spin, pull, slash—before I sprang back. Behind me, Anko carved through them with her fans. Flashes erupted from her protective and burning seals, pulsing in the dark as we fought.

SLASH!

A claw grazed my cheek, missing by a hair. I staggered back, swinging wide—the sickle bit into the bellies of the closest beasts. Planting my left palm on the ground, I kicked a lunging monster square in the chest.

“Ugh!” A tail coiled around my waist and hurled me down. I slashed at its base and rolled as claws tore at the earth where my face had been.

They kept coming—an endless tide. Gasping, I swung again. One creature slammed into me as I dodged another, sending me sprawling. I looked up just in time to see a third beast lunging for my head. My arm shot up to block, but I already knew it was too late.

AARGH!
