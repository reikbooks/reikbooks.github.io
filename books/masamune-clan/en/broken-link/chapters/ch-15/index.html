Epilogue A - Tadashi Masamune

Two and a half months ago.

Previously on Scarlet Steel…

I t was close to midnight when I finished my mission report in the main hall. “I’m home,” I murmured, easing the back door shut behind me. The terrace lamp cast a weak glow through the window, stretching long shadows across the dim hallway. The kitchen was silent, hollow.

Strange. Raito’s not back yet?

A scrap of paper on the kitchen table caught my eye—edges curled, scrawled over in Raito’s unmistakable messy handwriting:

“Gone three days with Toya—rush job. Raided your piggy bank, sorry! Broke T_T Will pay you back after the gig. Swear. Not lying.”

Hmph. That kid.

I crumpled the note and tossed it into the trash.

The sink was a temple of dirty plates and glasses, and a mountain of laundry slumped out of the basket—both undoubtably Raito’s doing. Gods… how many times did I have to tell him?

How did Shiro always manage to get through to him?

One word from Shiro, and Raito snapped to attention. With me? All I ever got was that infuriatingly sheepish grin. I’d once tried to teach him a lesson by letting the laundry pile up—he retaliated by raiding my closet the next morning.

Ugh.

With a sigh, I rolled up my sleeves and tackled the dishes. The laundry and the rest of the cleaning could wait. Between missions outside clan territory, I barely had time to breathe, let alone keep house.

After a quick wash, I headed for my room. But as I stepped into the living area, a prickle ran down my spine. I froze, checking the detector shield I’d set up. Still intact. Only family could pass through. It should be secure.

Yet something was wrong.

A faint voice drifted through the house. I was in my under-kimono, unarmed. My gaze darted to my bag on the dining table; I edged back and slipped my fingers around my tanto. Pressed flat to the wall, I followed the sound.

It was coming from Father’s study. The door was slightly ajar. I bit my lip. Father never left that door open. And he hadn’t lived here for months—I’d locked it myself the day he and Shiro moved out.

And that sound…?

My throat tightened. Singing. A lullaby—Mother’s lullaby. The one she hummed me to sleep with.

But how could anyone be inside?

I gripped the tanto and crept closer. The humming grew clearer—then cut off the moment I reached the doorway.

“I’ve waited a long time for you, Tadashi.”

A soft voice. A shadowed figure stood by the window, backlit by the oil lamp, swallowing its light.

“Who are you?” My voice came out hoarse. “How did you get in here?”

He chuckled.

“The usual. Picked the lock.” He stepped forward, out of the glare.

I lunged, blade flashing for his chest—but before steel met flesh, the house’s detector shield flared. A dark shape materialized beside me, slamming me against the wall as crackling tendrils coiled around my limbs.

Doboro.

I twisted to sever its hold, but the angle was wrong. A razor-tipped tentacle lashed my arm, knocking the tanto from my hand. Its coils tightened at my throat like a vise.

“Ugh—”

Air fled my lungs. My vision pulsed dark at the edges.

Shirasu—the creature’s master—picked up my fallen sword and studied me with a faint smile. The Doboro loosened just enough to let me gasp.

I formed a seal without hesitation.

Ember .

The octopus shrieked in my ear—but instead of releasing me, it shocked me again, white-hot pain ripping through my nerves.

“Is this how they taught you to greet guests?” Shirasu tsked.

Then he drove the tanto into my stomach—once, twice, again—before ripping it free.

The monster forced me to my knees. I thrashed, but Shirasu crushed my skull under his boot until my vision swam. Blood seeped across the tatami. My thoughts scattered like embers.

“W-what do you want?” My throat was raw. I needed to stall—to find an opening—

AGH!

Fresh agony as Shirasu twisted the blade deeper. I bit back a scream, reduced to shuddering breaths.

“Relax, Tadashi.” Shirasu’s voice was almost gentle. “You won’t die today. But I can stab you and heal you as many times as I please—so mind your tone.” He leaned down, breath hot on my ear.

“ Now… let’s talk. ”

The story continues…

Before the searing pain could fade, Shirasu grabbed my hair and yanked me upright. I stayed on my knees, bound, the world tilting. Bile burned my throat; I ground my teeth to keep it down.

A-ah… my head…

Shirasu’s outline blurred. No matter how I blinked, the darkness wouldn’t lift.

“Are you done acting up, or can we talk now?”

His voice ebbed like surf. He wanted to talk?

“Talk about what?” It came out as a rasp.

“Masada.”

I drew a shallow breath. About Father? I swallowed the copper taste of blood.

“What do you mean?” My voice was barely audible as the dark fringe of my vision receded.

Shirasu snorted.

“Come on, you’re not that dense. How did Masada react when he heard my name? Shirasu Mitsurugi. He won’t be coming home anymore. He knows I can break in here whenever I want—especially with no patrols.”

Maybe I was too slow, because irritation flickered across his face. “Think, Tadashi. Your detector shield didn’t activate when I walked in.”

I stared.

“I—I don’t understand—argh!”

The Doboro shocked me again. This time it didn’t stop until tears streamed down my face and my breath shattered into ragged gasps. I fought to think through the pain.

The detector shield doesn’t trigger for me or my siblings—only for those with Father’s DNA. His real children.

Shirasu could pass through without setting it off.

Oh. My eyes widened.

“You… you’re his son? ” The words scraped out of me.

Shirasu’s lips curled. “He won’t acknowledge me to the elders. The proof? Your identity is hidden too, isn’t it?”

“W-what?”

My identity? What was he talking about?

Shirasu smirked.

“Has Masada ever treated you the same as your siblings, Tadashi? Can you honestly say he loves you? Haven’t you always felt like an outsider?”

I clenched my jaw. “That doesn’t mean—”

He chuckled darkly and crushed his hand against my jaw.

White-hot pain exploded.

“Before becoming Head of the Masamune Clan, Masada Masamune infiltrated the Mitsurugi Clan. He married my mother, then abandoned us when the mission ended. Later, he married Makoto, who agreed to take you and raise you as her own,” Shirasu said, eyes pinning me.

“Y-you’re lying—”

AAH! Another jolt tore through me.

“You’re not Makoto’s child by blood, Tadashi. Your real mother was Mitsurugi. She died right after you were born. Masada only married her for the mission. He never loved her. His favoritism has been obvious, hasn’t it? Always coddling Shiro and Raito—his children with Makoto. You were the child he never wanted. He regrets your very existence. He even made you serve your own siblings.”

“That’s not—”

“You can’t deny it. Masada ordered you to watch over Shiro and Raito. Their needs always come before yours. He doesn’t care how you’re doing—whether you starve, fall ill, get hurt, or die . As long as they’re safe, nothing else matters .”

“You don’t know anything—”

“Do I need to spell it out? I’ve memorized every secret document the clan has on you. I know what’s written—and what’s not.” Shirasu sneered.

“Years ago, when you were a child, you lost track of Raito at the Children’s Day Festival. He nearly drowned. Masada was furious, wasn’t he? Even though he should have thanked you for saving Raito. He refused to see the truth—he only cared about your mistake. Tell me—did he beat you when you got home?”

“Father never—”

“Ah. So he locked you up instead. In your room? No—too lenient. He denied you supper and shut you in the shed, didn’t he?” Triumph laced his smile.

I bit my lip, my stomach knotting. That was the first time I’d truly feared Father.

“How long did he keep you in the dark, Tadashi? A day? Two? You were seven. Is that how a loving father behaves? You begged that night. Pleaded for release, for forgiveness. He wouldn’t answer. You still have nightmares about it, don’t you?

“When you abandoned the clan heir evaluation to rescue Shiro from that child abduction ring… he let the elders punish you severely. They whipped you three days straight while Shiro walked free. Fair?

“Shiro’s safety was all that mattered. Your suffering was irrelevant. Even after isolation—after they sent you on that impossible mission—he didn’t lift a finger.

“A year and a half. Homeless. Starving. Silence. Not a word from your own father. Not a sign he even saw you. And you still call that love? ”

“ Shut up! ” I snapped, eyes squeezed shut against the rushing memories.

I’d known for years that Father treated me differently. That he loved Shiro and Raito more. I’d known it as a child. I never complained—even when it stung.

But hearing it laid bare—

Gods .

Had Father hated me from the start? Just because I was Mitsurugi?

Bile rose in my throat.

No .

I couldn’t— wouldn’t —play into Shirasu’s game.

This was all lies. Manipulation. Poison meant to break me.

I forced my eyes open and glared at him.

“What’s between Father and me is none of your concern,” I said, voice low and ragged. “If that’s all you came for, then leave. Torment me all you want—I won’t believe you.”

Shirasu studied me, unreadable. Then the Doboro’s grip fully released. I slumped against the wall and wiped blood from my lip.

He stepped back, arms folded, smirk fading to ice.

“Fine. Maybe this will convince you.” He tossed an envelope at my feet. “Consider it proof of good faith—information even your best spies couldn’t get.”

He leaned in, voice dropping.

“Listen carefully, Tadashi. Ikaru Mitsurugi is coming for your family. All of you. His plan is already in motion. He won’t settle for killing you—he wants the Masamune Clan erased from history.”

A beat.

“I know the details. If you actually want to save them, I’m offering to work together. Think it over. I’ll be back for your answer.”

He flicked my tanto across the floor—just out of reach.

“One more thing.” His smile turned razor-thin. “Keep this between us. If Ikaru learns I warned you, he’ll kill me. And you’ll lose your only ally inside the Mitsurugi Clan.”

He stroked his Doboro before dismissing it. The detector shield, which had been blaring when the flying octopus was around, fell silent. It was a chilling confirmation of his identity.

Shirasu… was really Father’s son.

Which meant—

He was my older brother.

“Why?” The word tore out of me. “Why betray Ikaru now? After serving him all these years—what’s in it for you? ”

Shirasu paused. Slowly, he turned back, hands slipping into his pockets.

“You know what a Slavery Seal is, don’t you?” His voice was eerily calm. “As a seal specialist, you understand its evil. But your clan’s records won’t tell you its true origin. It comes from the Mitsurugi Seal of Obedience—a dark art.”

His smirk vanished.

“Every child with Mitsurugi blood receives it from the clan head. Me. Mother. All of us. We’re all Ikaru’s puppets.” His fingers twitched, as if fighting invisible chains. “You can’t imagine what he’s done to us. What he did to her .”

His gaze burned into mine.

“I’m asking you because you’re her son. My blood. The only Mitsurugi descendant Ikaru hasn’t enslaved—the only one who can still kill him.”

Silence. Then, softly:

“Don’t you want to avenge your own mother? ”

I stared at him, speechless. Instinct screamed to reject his words, but my mind was blank.

“If I’m Mitsurugi by blood, what’s stopping Ikaru from branding me with the Seal of Obedience and enslaving me too?” I forced myself to focus. “If he controls me, I’ll be just like you—unable to kill him.”

Shirasu went quiet, then stepped closer. Escape was impossible. “There’s something Ikaru didn’t anticipate,” he said. “The seal won’t fully control you—not when you’ve already mastered a Slavery Seal.”

I froze. What?

“Right now, you control Sadi’s Slavery Seal, don’t you?” he murmured. My blood ran cold.

“How do you know—”

“I’ve watched your clan for years, Tadashi. I know Akito’s secret. I killed him—have you forgotten?” He let that settle. “I saw the seal’s power fade when he died. Sadi should have died with him. He’s alive because you were there. You took control.”

Shirasu’s voice stayed steady. “When someone masters a Slavery Seal, they gain partial immunity to Ikaru’s Seal of Obedience. It won’t free you entirely—his grip is too strong. But it gives you a chance. A way to resist.”

He went on, “When you regain control from Ikaru, don’t strike immediately. Wait. Let him think you’re still under. Only attack when he’s completely off guard. Ikaru doesn’t know about Akito’s secret or that you took his seal. He has no idea we’ve found his weakness.”

I bit my lip, struggling to process it. Shirasu wanted to work with me? The idea was absurd. His plan was vague at best. With odds this slim, could I really pull it off?

“I’ll sweeten the deal,” Shirasu said, crouching to meet my eyes. “You want answers about the Slavery Seal. The Mitsurugi Book of Dark Arts has them all. Help me, and I’ll tell you where it’s hidden.”

He straightened, expression hardening. “The book is rare—most think it was destroyed after the war. But a few copies survived. Only high-ranking Mitsurugi know their locations.” A faint smirk touched his lips. “I won’t fetch it for you—that’s your trial. Difficult, yes. But based on what I’ve seen, I’d bet on you.”

I swallowed. The offer was too tempting. Shirasu watched me with icy eyes, then rose.

“Do you understand what I’m offering? You’re trying to break Sadi’s Slavery Seal. The answer is in that book. Sadi will be free—truly free. He won’t die if you do.”

His gaze held mine. “That’s what’s been weighing on you, isn’t it?”

Without waiting for a response, Shirasu vanished in a blink.

I exhaled slowly and touched my stomach—no pain, no wounds. He’d healed me. He really hadn’t come to kill me.

I stared at the letter on the floor, its edge stained by pooling blood. I snatched it up, feeling the familiar pressure of a blood-relation seal—the same design that inspired the detector shields I’d installed throughout the house.

“Only the writer’s second child can break this seal.”

Yet when my fingers brushed it, the wax softened. My breath hitched as it parted.

Hands trembling, I unfolded the letter. The handwriting jolted me—I’d know it anywhere.

This was Chiyo Mitsurugi’s writing.
