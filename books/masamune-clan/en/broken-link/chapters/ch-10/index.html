Brother

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

Shiro Masamune

My arm would’ve been ripped open if someone hadn’t grabbed the bear-like monster by the scruff and taken its head off in a single, clean stroke.

Sadi!

He kicked another monster aside, clearing space for me to get up. Twin swords flashed as he lunged, carving through throats.

Ah… I should’ve used Kusanagi.

I slid my chain sickle back into place and drew the sword. Its golden-white glow warmed my palm. One fluid swing, and the monster in front of me split cleanly. Its dying shriek cut short as Kusanagi’s light reduced it to swirling ash.

Wow. Okay then.

I slashed through the rest. Each one vanished the instant Kusanagi touched them. It ended fast. At the cave mouth, I leaned against the wall, panting, fingers tight around Kusanagi’s hilt as I steadied my breath.

“You okay?” Sadi asked, sheathing one sword while drawing his trident.

I nodded, pushing past the sting of a hundred cuts and bruises. After a steadying breath, I motioned Sadi forward. Anko snapped her bladed fans shut and fell in behind us.

The ceiling rose as we descended the rocky path, opening into a cavern so vast the roof vanished into shadow. Flickering red torches lit the space—one of the cave system’s many atriums. Across the chamber, two dark passages branched deeper underground. We’d barely reached the center when two figures stepped out from the tunnels ahead.

Shirasu and Seishiro.

Their blank stares locked onto us, eyes glowing white. A thick aura of killing intent bled off them, but beneath it, fear prickled through—sharp enough to pierce my emotional shield and leave a bitter taste of salt in my mouth.

I clenched my jaw and glanced at Anko. She gave a tight nod. Shirasu and Seishiro—under Ikaru’s control.

Anko flicked her fans open. “Hello, big brother Sei,” she murmured.

Seishiro’s shock cracked through my defenses, sending a jolt of worry through me. Anko turned to Shirasu and smiled.

“Hey, Shirasu,” she said, tipping her fan to lift the necklace at her throat. “Still got your little present.”

Shock, joy, longing, hope, affection, worry, fear, hatred, rage, despair —Shirasu’s emotions tangled into chaos, slamming against my shield like shattering glass. Anko struck without waiting. Her fans met Shirasu’s nunchaku with a sharp clash.

He squeezed the handle. Twin blades snapped from the ends with a metallic rasp as he slashed at her. Her fans snapped shut, whirled, and coiled around the chain, wrenching the nunchaku from his grip. Both weapons spun away. Anko snorted and drew a short sword. Shirasu mirrored her instantly.

Seishiro lunged without warning, iron claws slashing for me. Instinctively, I raised Kusanagi to parry.

Ugh—his strength. I stumbled back. He retracted the claws and hacked a massive boomerang at my throat. I barely dodged, but he didn’t let up—slash after slash, claws and blade raining down.

CLANG! Sadi’s trident caught Seishiro’s claws. He twisted, entangling them, then drove a brutal kick into Seishiro’s chest. Undeterred, Seishiro swung his boomerang again. Sadi barely deflected with his off-hand sword. Steel shrieked against steel as they strained against each other, neither giving an inch. Across the atrium, Shirasu and Anko spun through their own deadly dance.

“Shiro, find Tadashi now! I’ll hold Seishiro!” Sadi shouted, blocking with all his might.

I nodded and sprinted toward the branching tunnels. Left or right?

Argh—

Wait. Shirasu came from the right. I veered and ran down the crimson-lit passage.

The corridor sloped downward, narrowing as it went. What began wide enough for a dozen people tightened to barely two arm spans. At the far end, a chasm split the floor lengthwise, its edge lined by a steep staircase clinging to the wall. The steps descended into a row of shadowed alcoves, their darkness broken only by the distant drip of water.

I tightened my grip on Kusanagi, scanning. Torchlight flickered, shadows jittering across the stone. Some alcoves gaped empty; others were fitted with rusted bars and chains. One niche held a jumble of bones. Another, corpses in varying stages of decay.

Ugh.

I forced down the tightness in my chest and kept going. The passage opened into a vast hall flanked by high mezzanines. Opposite the entrance stood a small barred room.

My breath hitched.

Tadashi stood there.

I moved fast. He knelt with his hands and neck chained to the wall, ankles shackled to weighted iron balls. His head hung low, a black blindfold over his eyes. His once-white inner kimono clung filthy and torn, soaked with blood.

“Brother,” I whispered, voice catching. He didn’t move. “Tadashi!”

He flinched, then slowly raised his head, trembling. Fear and panic radiated from him, sharp enough to pierce my shield. He must have thought Ikaru was the one who called his name.

Jaw tight, I grabbed the heavy chain securing the cell door. A massive padlock dangled from it. I fished a wire from my pouch and worked it until the lock clicked. I yanked the chain free and slipped inside.

Tadashi tried to prostrate himself, but the chains held. He bowed as deeply as he could, shoulders straining, shaking with fear. Fury burned hot in my chest. How dare Ikaru treat my brother like this.

“I’m here,” I whispered, brushing my fingers to his shoulder. He didn’t seem to hear—just flinched and trembled, eyes empty. A sharp pain shot from my fingertips where they met his skin, running through my veins like live electricity.

I staggered back. My emotional shield buckled under the onslaught—searing agony, gnawing hunger, biting cold, clawing fear, blind panic, PAIN beyond words. I yanked my hand away, blinking through tears as I fought to steady myself.

Focus, Shiro. Those aren’t your feelings. You’re fine. Be quick.

I drew a shaky breath and tightened my hold on Kusanagi. Drawing power from the Jomon stone, I steadied, raised the blade, and cut the chain binding his neck.

CLANG. It snapped. I severed the restraints on his wrists and ankles in quick succession. Tadashi crumpled forward. Ignoring the searing pain bleeding off him, I caught him and pulled him close.

Up close, I heard his whimpers, his ragged breaths. I stroked his back—and froze when my palm came away sticky with blood.

“Shh. It’s me, Shiro. I’m here. Just breathe. You’re okay.”

I eased back and cupped his chin, lifting his face. Bruises mottled his jaw; dried blood crusted his split lips. Carefully, I loosened the knot of the blindfold.

My breath hitched into ragged bursts. His eyes were swollen nearly shut, irises bloodshot and raw, like he’d been beaten until even sight hurt. He blinked blindly, struggling to see.

Aah . Oh gods.

My chest burned with a dull, aching fire. I swallowed bile, fists clenched.

Ikaru… I’ll kill you.

I turned away, then moved to support Tadashi. “Let’s get out of here,” I whispered, voice shaking. I reached deep into my empathy, shoring up my shield, shutting out my own feelings.

We were halfway across the hall when movement flashed behind us. Instinct took over. I ducked and shoved Tadashi down. His hand struck Kusanagi with a sharp crack, sending the blade skittering across the floor, just out of reach.

I swore and lunged for it—but a shadow seized my arm and yanked me off balance.

The devil creature was a nightmare—long hair shrouding its face, jagged teeth jutting from a gaping maw, white eyes burning with madness. It lunged for my throat, but the moment its fingers brushed Tadashi’s necklace, it recoiled.

The power stone in the pendant flared, casting golden-white light. The thing shrieked and crumbled into ash. I staggered upright just as a swarm of shadowy figures poured from the dark corners—shifting masses of black smoke flickering between solid and ethereal. I searched frantically for Kusanagi—nothing.

Ugh! I whipped out my chain sickle and struck. The fiend dissolved like smoke, slipping effortlessly aside. My chain clanged against stone—no hit, no snare. I swung again. My blade met only air.

The creatures circled, shrieking with glee. Then they merged into one massive, grinning shadow that loomed over me, its foul breath washing across my face.

I gasped as my throat tightened, squeezed by invisible hands. My emotional walls shattered, my mental defenses collapsing like brittle glass.

“AAAH!”

Agony ripped through my chest, razor-sharp and all-consuming. I hit the floor and curled in on myself as terror clawed at my throat. Bile surged; my vision blurred; my lungs refused air. Darkness swarmed my mind, a whirl of fractured memories and nightmares flashing too fast to grasp.

Tadashi’s memories crashed into me like a waterfall eating stone—relentless, chaotic. No order, no sense, just jagged shards of his life stabbing into me, tearing me apart.

The electric jolt of pain didn’t fade. Tears streamed from the stabbing agony in my mind. How had Tadashi endured this?

Then the memory of Ikaru torturing him hit me like a fist.

Oh gods.

I opened my mouth and screamed, raw and endless, until the copper tang of blood filled my throat. Pain— white-hot, blinding —racked me. Thought vanished as my muscles seized. My hands clawed at my chest, desperate to rip out the torment, and my fingers grazed the cool metal of my pendant.

The power stone pulsed warmth through me, banishing the nightmares. I groaned, rolled onto my side, and retched until there was nothing left.

Someone grabbed my hair and yanked me upright, forcing me to my knees. My heart lurched.

Tadashi .

His eyes burned white, like Seishiro’s and Shirasu’s. A crooked smirk twisted his bloody mouth.

Brother …

My stomach tied itself in knots.

“You’re finally here. How are you, Shiro?” Tadashi asked, slow and gentle—the same tone he always used when greeting me. My chest ached with longing.

I clung to his wrist, staring up at him, tears and snot streaking my face. I couldn’t look away. My heart and mind waged war.

Let go, Shiro. He’s not your brother. Ikaru’s controlling him. Strike now.

I couldn’t. Tadashi was hurt. Ikaru had tortured him. I couldn’t make him suffer more. I froze, split down the middle.

Tadashi clicked his tongue and drove his foot into my chest. I slammed into the wall and slumped, coughing.

“Father and Mother’s golden child. Top scorer in the heir selection. The elders’ star pupil. Beloved by brothers, colleagues—everyone. Ambitious. Driven. Ruthless. Never made a mistake. Never faced consequences,” he murmured, that bitter smile twisting his battered face.

I froze. Doubt coiled in my chest as I met the hatred blazing in his eyes. He looked like a stranger and exactly like himself. Was Ikaru really controlling him? Or had this darkness always been there, waiting?

“You’re so lucky, Shiro. Life’s been safe and easy for you, sheltered from pain. You got everything good while I got nothing. I envy you. God, how I hate you.”

He kicked me again. I groaned, fighting the swell of dizziness. I’d felt flickers of his envy before, little moments here and there. I’d said things that hurt him—sometimes on purpose, sometimes not. But he never said a word.

Is this what you’ve been wanting to say all along, brother?

Blinking, I spotted my kusarigama nearby. I turned slowly back to him. His flat stare met mine, then he snorted and gestured impatiently.

“Go on. Take it. You’ll need it,” he said, amusement in his voice. I didn’t need telling twice. I snatched up the chain sickle and rose to face him.

My chest ached. I didn’t know if I could fight Tadashi—or if this was even Tadashi anymore.

“Get out of his head!” I shouted at Ikaru. “Fight me yourself! Leave my brother out of it.”

Tadashi chuckled. “What, scared of the truth? Any idea how much I’ve got stored up here that I never said?” He tapped his temple with two fingers.

“Too much, Shiro. Far too much,” he murmured, gaze drifting. Then he looked at me, eyes locking onto mine with a soft smile.

“You caused me so much pain, Shiro. I could only bear it, bury it so deep…until I often wished I were dead.”
