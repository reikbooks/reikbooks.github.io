Calling

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

Shiro Masamune

“You want to contact Seishiro?!” Sadi stared at me like I’d suggested inviting the King over for juice boxes and hopscotch. We were having breakfast at Anko’s—she’d made incredible tuna onigiri, so good Sadi was already on his third.

I leaned against the terrace railing, watching him spring to his feet. “It’s logical, isn’t it? I need to know if he’s truly with us or if this is all a game.”

Sadi’s face tightened. “What if he’s still evil?”

“I thought it through last night. Remember Tadashi’s letter? He put a tracking seal on Shirasu while he was asleep . Isn’t that strange?” I stood and began pacing.

“Tadashi was with Shirasu somewhere safe enough for him to sleep—probably Shirasu’s home. Could you sleep with your enemy nearby? No. That means Shirasu didn’t see Tadashi as a threat. He trusted him. And Tadashi was the same. He could have killed Shirasu in his sleep, but he didn’t. Instead of going to Father for help, he followed Shirasu’s plan. That means he trusted Shirasu too.”

“Not necessarily,” Sadi countered. “Planting a tracking seal without Shirasu noticing says a lot.”

“But that’s Tadashi, isn’t it? Always working in the background, leaving out crucial details. With the drug operation, he did the same to protect you. I think he still trusted Shirasu. Maybe he believed Shirasu was good at heart, forced into evil because Ikaru had him under a Seal of Obedience. Who knows? What’s clear is that Tadashi left me those clues to protect me. He was cornered, Sadi. He told me he had no backup. If anything went wrong, he wanted me ready to face Ikaru.”

Silence fell between us.

“So you’re saying Seishiro might be good too—just forced into it because of Ikaru’s seal?” Sadi asked, disbelieving.

“Maybe. I don’t know—that’s what I’m trying to figure out. Say Tadashi agreed to work with them to save me. Why trust them so easily? If they were truly evil, how could he believe them? Unless… they were forced into doing bad things.”

Sadi planted his hands on his hips. “Did you even see how Shirasu and Seishiro acted during the New Year attack? They didn’t look forced. I haven’t fought Shirasu, but Seishiro… if you’d ever faced him, you’d see the madness in his eyes. He’s wild and untamed, like those wolves he keeps.”

“But he helped us get the Kusanagi last night,” I said. “Those wolf demons obey him—they didn’t touch me, Anko, or even you. They actually shielded us from the temple guards. That means he can’t be purely wicked. He must have his reasons for helping us. That’s why I need to speak with him.”

“You’re forgetting something.” Sadi shook his head. “Last night, he came at me on purpose—first with his boomerang, then his iron claws. When I drew my second sword, he suddenly pulled the Kusanagi. My heart nearly stopped—I thought he’d stolen it. Then it shattered, and he ran.”

“That was the replica he took from me,” I said. “His attack was a diversion to mislead the guards. Maybe he pretended to take the Kusanagi from you to draw all the attention.”

“Why are you defending him?” Sadi grumbled, rolling his eyes. “Fine, say Seishiro isn’t that bad. Do you even know where he lives? How will you reach him? And what exactly do you plan to ask? You think he’ll just hand over honest answers?”

I stopped pacing and leaned on the railing again, taking a breath. I didn’t have a plan for getting information out of Seishiro—maybe I’d try my empathic ability, like I had with Nobuga.

As for where to find him… I’d thought of retracing our steps to where we first met. Seishiro had run a child trafficking ring. Maybe I could cross the Heinna Forest, catch the train across the Doori River… but then what? Where would I get off? I could jump off at each stop and ask around. That region had to be his territory.

“Just don’t ask me to track his drug operations again, got it? With Roroa’s business down, I hope that bastard Seishiro crashes and burns too,” Sadi muttered, dropping into his seat. He snatched the last rice ball.

Anko glanced up from the Mitsurugi book and snorted. “Don’t forget the dishes!”

Sadi grinned but took his plate to the kitchen.

I turned to Anko for her perspective. “What do you make of Shirasu and Seishiro?”

Anko met my eyes, finger marking her page. “You know I can’t answer fairly, Shiro. I dated Shirasu. I searched for darkness and found only light. His betrayal felt like a fall from grace. I felt foolish. But Tadashi’s letter… it shifted everything. It made me wonder if my instincts had been right all along. If there’s something beneath the surface I missed.” A beat. “People will dismiss me because of my past with Shirasu. But Tadashi? He sees through illusions.”

She sighed, a small sound. “Tadashi always sees the best, even in those who only ever offer him thorns. Look at Sadi. A year ago, who would’ve believed they’d be friends?”

I mulled that over until a sharp knock interrupted us. Anko opened the door. Kagawa stood there.

“Elder Mei wants you in the main hall immediately—mission report,” Kagawa announced. I wiped my mouth, nodded, and stood.

Sadi hurried out of the kitchen, drying his hands and tossing the damp towel on the table. “Ugh. I just filed a report the day before yesterday. We only just got back.”

Kagawa rolled his eyes. “Yeah, yeah. Tell Elder Mei yourself.”

Sadi slung an arm around Kagawa’s shoulders as they headed out. “What’s the latest from the main hall?”

“Everyone’s stressed,” Kagawa muttered. “Genta’s wasting away—down five kilos already.”

Sadi chuckled and kept up the chatter.

I looked back. Anko closed the Mitsurugi book and sealed it for storage. She grabbed her jacket and locked the door behind us.

“Hey, wait up—I forgot something,” Sadi called to Kagawa. He pivoted back toward us, voice dropping to a whisper. “I left Tadashi out of yesterday’s report. I only said we were investigating weapons against Ikaru. Are you telling Elder Mei about him?”

I frowned. Elder Mei knowing about Tadashi’s link to Shirasu was… unacceptable.

“No,” I said. “Tell them I found the clues in one of Elder Yun’s books.”

Sadi snorted, a slow grin spreading. He held out his hand. “Welcome to the Tadashi Protection Club. Membership’s been stagnant for ages.”

Anko laughed. I swatted Sadi’s hand away and jabbed him in the ribs.

“Ow! Easy, Sweetie,” he protested.

I ignored him and fell into step beside Anko.

Ugh. I took a breath, bracing myself. Kagawa hadn’t exaggerated—the main hall was a pressure cooker. The air was heavy with the elders’ and our colleagues’ unease, their anxiety pressing against my mind.

Elder Mei listened as I explained the Jomon stone and the Kusanagi. When I showed her both and slotted the stone into the sword, the blade pulsed with white light—warm and surprisingly light in my hands. Elder Mei nodded.

“Good work, Shiro. I was concerned about the break-ins at the Yasakani and Sumugari temples.” She exhaled softly. “It’s… convenient that the Mitsurugi will be blamed. Our reputation is safe, at least. The King will expect a swift resolution. Now that we have the weapon, how do we find their stronghold?”

I spread a map of the kingdom across the table. “First we mark all known locations where they operate or have done business. Then we hit each site, looking for someone we can question. We prioritize by recency, scale, and type of activity.”

We discussed personnel and assignments. Our intel team was already stretched, taking extra missions just to make minimum payments on our debt to the King. The conversation slid into finances and other pressing clan matters.

By afternoon, I finally sank into my office chair. Sadi slipped in with an onigiri and a glass of iced sweet tea—a late lunch.

“A few days away and the work’s already a mountain,” he said, collapsing and draining the tea in one long gulp. “What do you think of Elder Mei’s plan to pull academy kids into intel?”

“Good if they’re trained,” I said, closing my eyes. “A disaster if they’ve never seen the field.”

“Exactly. What if they blow a tail and lose the target? More trouble than they’re worth.”

I shrugged and tossed the thick clan registry onto his desk. “You handle the names. You’ve been in the field longer; you know their records. Start forming teams and assigning leads.”

Sadi groaned, flipping the registry open with a thud. “You should’ve given this to Anko. She teaches them. And I’m not taking any C-levels into the field—especially not these academy brats.”

I clicked my tongue, remembering my sleepless days trying to break Tadashi’s record. “Fine. Contact Anko. Have her send her top picks.”

Sadi snorted and scribbled a note for Anko. “You want the small fry too?”

“Who?”

“Your little brother.”

“Raito?” I shrugged. “Bring him. He’s done recon, hasn’t he?”

Sadi snorted. “Yeah, right. All he does is date. Did you know—”

The office door burst open. Kagawa stood there, ashen. “Emergency meeting—now. It’s urgent.”

Sadi and I sprang up and rushed out. Elder Mei and Elder Low were already in the meeting room.

“A report just came in. Our agents in several clan safe houses have been murdered,” Elder Mei said tightly. She passed the report to us. Ten safe houses across different cities had been hit. The victims were torn apart—hearts and eyes ripped out. Symbols were carved into the backs of their hands.

“Seishiro,” Sadi snarled. “His wolf monsters did this.”

I studied the estimated times of death. The first murder happened last night, an hour after we secured the Kusanagi. Each killing followed exactly two hours apart. After fleeing Sumugari Temple, Seishiro had been busy hunting. My hands clenched.

“Sadi, mark the locations on the map. Include the timeline and symbols.”

He marked the sites. When he finished, we stood in silence, staring at the map.

The ten points formed a hexagram centered on our clan complex. The symbols carved into the victims’ hands were just lines in varying shapes—meaning unknown.
