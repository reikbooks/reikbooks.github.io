Live and Be Happy

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

Shiro Masamune

I stared at him, breath hitching, chest tightening. Tadashi smirked. “Go on, Shiro. Ask me anything. I’ll spill all my darkest secrets… just for you, my dear little sister.” His chuckle scraped like a blade.

My fists clenched, heat flooding my face. I knew Ikaru was manipulating me, but looking into Tadashi’s eyes and hearing his voice made trust feel inevitable.

Then the truth hit me, cold and sharp: my brother would never speak like this. Beneath Ikaru’s forced bravado, I could feel the real Tadashi struggling. He was terrified. Ashamed. Some of Ikaru’s words were braided with truth, but the rest was poison. Tadashi didn’t want me to misunderstand. He was fighting Ikaru inside his own mind. I snapped my head in a sharp refusal.

“Enough. What do you want, Ikaru? Give up. This base is surrounded,” I said, bluffing through the dread coiling in my gut. I still couldn’t find Ikaru’s true form. My grip tightened on my weapon. Tadashi and I circled, neither willing to strike first.

“Tch. Must you always talk to your elders like that?” Tadashi said, stopping to face me fully. “I’ve treated you with nothing but respect. I never laid a hand on you or Raito. Yet you—how many times have you struck me or humiliated me in front of others?” His gaze hardened. “You think I enjoy being shamed?”

I didn’t see him move. One heartbeat he was still; the next he was in front of me, his hand cracking across my face. The blow sent me reeling. My cheek burned, my lip split, and blackness swam at the edges of my vision.

Flashes hit me— Tadashi looking down, his face flushed after I’d slapped him. Tadashi trembling as I shouted him down before the clan. Tadashi clenching his fists, bowing stiffly as he excused himself. He hadn’t said a word, but the shame and pain back then had been raw, relentless.

I’d known, and I’d ignored it. Brother… I’d been so cruel . A small sound escaped me. Guilt knotted my stomach, smashed my guard to pieces. Ikaru’s words cut deeper, draining what strength I had left. I couldn’t face Tadashi like this. Not now.

Get up, Shiro. Defeat Ikaru. It’s the only way to save him. Be strong.

I squeezed my eyes shut for a beat, forcing myself to breathe. Then I pushed to my feet. Tadashi glared, his eyes lit with venom.

“You know what kills me? You act like you have the right to tear me down. Calling me incompetent, when you’re the one who abandoned your duty as heir. I’ve spent years cleaning up your messes,” Tadashi muttered. “I’m done being your servant—”

I whipped my sickle at his throat. He slipped back, seized my wrist, and slammed me to the floor. I caught myself on an elbow and kicked at his chest. He blocked, hammered my wrist until the chain-sickle clattered away, then pressed in a blur of strikes.

I struggled for air. Every block and parry sent fresh pain through me. I hadn’t expected this kind of strength—not from someone injured. Was it Ikaru’s power?

When Tadashi was still at the academy, I used to beg him to spar. He always made time on weekends—bare-handed or with whatever weapon I wanted. Back then, I always had the edge.

“Did you know how hard it was to hold back? I didn’t want to hurt you,” Tadashi said, barely winded as I gasped. He brushed aside my attacks without effort.

“Ugh. Pathetic. You’re weak. Want a real challenge, little sister?”

He shattered my guard and drove a fist into my ribs. Pain detonated through me. I twisted away, and his knee crashed into my gut. I hit the floor on my knees. He grabbed my hair and looked down at me, smirking.

“There. That’s better. On your knees.” He shoved my head down. “Good girl. Now, let’s teach you some manners.”

The Kusanagi sword shimmered into his hand. So he’d taken it. Hidden it. He pressed the cold blade to my throat—then went still. Silent. Anger, fear, and dread twisted tight in my chest. His emotions. The urge to fight burned through my veins. Pressure pounded in my skull.

I understood. Tadashi was pushing back—everything he had—against Ikaru. My fingers closed around the hand pinning me as I lifted my gaze, hope breaking through.

“Tadashi… please.” My voice shook. Love, shame, self-loathing poured off him in unending waves. He wanted to help—God, he wanted to—but he couldn’t.

Blood seeped from the corner of his eye. His smirk froze.

“We’re going to have fun, Shiro. The Master craves your screams,” he whispered, voice wrong and hollow.

No . Tadashi’s resolve surged through me like a shock.

He shuddered. His hands fell away as he clutched his head, trying to claw Ikaru out. The Kusanagi clattered to the floor. Tadashi whimpered in pain.

“Ahh… S-Shiro…” he breathed.

I snatched the sword and staggered back. A flicker of satisfaction from Tadashi—then his agony tore through me . White-hot. All-consuming. I choked, stumbling, eyes locked on him.

Ikaru had activated the Seal of Obedience.

Tremors racked him as blood trailed from his eye. He went still, then turned to me slowly. White flashed through his gaze, and a vicious smile curled his lips.

Ikaru had taken him back. A black sword appeared in Tadashi’s hand. He attacked without hesitation.

I brought the Kusanagi up and met him. No more words. He unleashed strikes from every angle. I barely held my ground. Each blow hit like a hammer, too fast to fully block. My only chance was to match his speed, to steal and mirror him mid-fight.

He didn’t relent. His smile turned crooked, his eyes gleaming an unnatural white. Each block sent pain radiating through my chest.

Tadashi would never try to kill me. He was the gentlest soul—the one who held me when I was sick and scared, who watched over me. I didn’t want to hurt him. But to save him, I couldn’t lose.

I forced my breathing steady and surged forward. He faltered, thrown off by my sudden change. His movements staggered. Ikaru was pushing his injured body past its limits. I caught his blade, saw the opening.

I lunged. My blade sliced his arm. Blood spilled. His grimace twisted my chest, but I couldn’t hesitate. Wounds heal. If Ikaru kept him, I’d lose my brother forever.

I’m sorry. Hold on. I’ll save you.

Ikaru seemed to feed on the pain. His attacks grew wilder, but Tadashi’s body couldn’t keep up. Another opening.

Three feints. I aimed for his stomach. He blocked, countered immediately, forced me back. I slipped aside, reset, thrust for his ribs—half certain he’d parry.

He didn’t.

The white in his eyes blinked out. Tadashi seized control at the last possible moment. His dark eyes focused on me, clear. He didn’t move to defend. Didn’t even flinch. He just looked at me with a sad, gentle smile, love tightening around me like a vise.

Shiro …

The Kusanagi sank into him.

He grabbed the blade, anchoring it, making sure it landed true, keeping me from tearing it free. White light seared through every part of him Ikaru held.

My brother screamed as static tore through his nerves. He collapsed, curling in on himself, trembling. Agony wracked him as the Seal of Obedience shattered. I felt it too, dropping to my knees beside him. Ikaru’s grip finally broke.

I gulped air in ragged drafts. My vision cleared. Tadashi lay whimpering. I shook his shoulder.

“Tadashi! Tadashi!”

He swallowed, braced, and pulled the sword free. White-hot pain swallowed him whole. His body convulsed as blood gushed from the wound.

I cradled his head, fighting tears. He turned toward me, blinking, trying to focus. His lips parted. Blood spilled instead of words.

“Brother, please.” His emotions crashed through me—relief, pride, tenderness. Love without end. But underneath it all was fear. He was scared.

He took my hand and traced words onto my palm.

Ikaru lied. Don’t trust him.

I bit my lip, overwhelmed. Even now, he worried about me more than himself.

“Everything you said under Ikaru’s control? I know,” I whispered.

He shook his head, agitation knotting his features. Guilt and regret surged through him, a storm he couldn’t hold back.

What was he trying to say? Apologize? He’d promised he wouldn’t. I squeezed his hand.

“Don’t worry. I’m okay. You didn’t hurt me. It wasn’t your fault. No more apologies, all right? I already forgave you,” I breathed into his ear. “I love you, Tadashi. Can you hear me?”

He froze. He heard. Tears cut slow tracks down his cheeks. Joy poured off him, unrestrained. Gently, he drew my hand to him, tracing words against my palm with trembling fingers.

You are stronger.

My breath caught.

Live, Shiro.

He pressed a soft kiss to the back of my fingers and guided my hand to his chest. Love and hope broke over me, relentless and bright.

Live free and be happy.

Under the crimson torchlight in the volcano’s heart, with love swelling in him, my brother smiled for the last time. Then he closed his eyes, his fingers slipped from mine.

“No! Brother!” I shook him, desperate. “Tadashi! Wake up—please—don’t leave me!”

A soft tsk sounded behind me.

“Well, well. Never thought you’d stoop to killing your own brother, Shiro.”

I stood and spun. A powerfully built man in his middle years strode into the hall. His long white hair fell to his waist. He wore a black military uniform trimmed with gold beneath a calf-length leather coat, moving like a general surveying a field he already owned. His expression was ice as he looked down his nose at me.

“You’re Ikaru?!” I snapped, forcing my fear down. I needed to get back to Tadashi, but every instinct screamed that this man was lethal. I couldn’t face him unprepared.

He looked me over with clinical disdain. “So this is Masada’s cherished child?”

A sly smile touched his lips.

“A little insolent. That can be fixed. In the end, they all learn to obey—even your brother.”

My fingers tightened on the Kusanagi’s hilt. “Enough.”

I stepped in and struck. Ikaru raised a black sword with lazy grace, one-handed, his left arm resting behind his back. I aimed for his chest and pressed hard, but he read every move. My blade cut only air. Strikes that should have landed slipped past him like smoke. I feinted, misdirected, poured everything into speed and angles—he ghosted through them without effort.

Why couldn’t I touch him? I bit my lip, hesitated, stepped back. Amusement flickered in his eyes.

“Who taught you the sword, Shiro? Do you truly not know the basics?” he clicked his tongue.

Heat rose to my face. Were my attacks that transparent? His style was nothing like Tadashi’s. I needed time to read it. Remembering one of Tadashi’s lessons, I shifted my grip and attacked again.

Ikaru slid aside with ease. “Pathetic. So desperate you’re stealing your brother’s moves?”

He lunged—three rapid slashes. I staggered, unable to block or counter.

“You call yourself heir with skills like that? Six months of training, perhaps?” He laughed, then left an opening on purpose and blocked my response with insulting ease.

Sweat stung my eyes; the Kusanagi grew slick in my hand. A cold knot tightened in my gut. He wasn’t even trying, and I was already at my limit.

“Understand now?” Ikaru smirked. “You think you can defeat me? Tadashi couldn’t—and he’s your finest swordsman. Your brother’s been on missions since he was eight. You? You stayed home and studied. Tell me I’m wrong.”

He stepped in, cutting off my retreat. I attacked, teeth clenched. He parried with whip-quick precision, the impact numbing my arm.

So it was true. Tadashi had always held back. I hadn’t been ready. Humiliation burned through me. Why didn’t he let me lose? Why didn’t he force me to grow?

CLANG! His blade flashed. I barely got mine up, and the force nearly tore it from my hand.

Stop blaming him, Shiro. Your failures are yours. Focus.

Two quick slashes shattered my guard. I hauled the Kusanagi up to block the next strike, but my timing was off. With a smirk, Ikaru twisted his blade at the last instant and thrust straight for my throat.
