Your Letter

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

Shiro Masamune

Sadi and I headed to Anko’s house. He shot me a doubtful look when I said I wanted her to examine his seal.

“I can’t sense Tadashi at all, Shiro. It’s been wrong ever since he broke it. Like waking up and realizing a limb is gone.”

“It’s worth a shot, isn’t it?”

“It’s past midnight. What if Anko’s asleep?”

“Then why were you knocking on my door in the middle of the night?”

“I wanted to check on you. When you weren’t at the rest house, I got worried. I imagined you’d fallen off a cliff or something.”

I stared at him. “That’s the dumbest thing I’ve heard all day.”

“Hey, don’t blame me. Tadashi told me to keep an eye on you. You looked awful earlier—I didn’t want you doing anything… drastic.”

Gods . “You think I’d kill myself? I haven’t even managed to kill Shirasu yet. How am I supposed to wipe out that damned clan if I’m dead?”

The front door swung open. “Take your lovers’ quarrel somewhere else,” Anko said flatly. “You’re waking the street.”

She gave us both a cutting look. She wore a loose t-shirt and shorts, a towel over her damp hair—fresh from the shower—and held a steaming bowl of ramen.

“Ah, the radiant Anko! How did you know I was starving?” Sadi reached for the bowl. Anko rapped him on the head with her bladed fan.

“Ow! Keep that thing away from my skull,” Sadi grumbled, rubbing the spot.

Anko rolled her eyes and ushered us in. Sadi sat and explained how Tadashi had broken his seal. I added that the severance might not be complete—that Sadi could still have some way to track him. We needed Anko to confirm. She studied me for a moment, then motioned for Sadi to lie on the futon.

“Want to observe, Shiro?” Anko asked. “Activate a basic tracking seal.”

I did.

Anko’s hands flashed through a series of signs.

Protect.

Stabilize.

Release.

Sadi’s energy tendrils stirred, faintly glowing as they wove into intricate, orderly knots. Anko examined the delicate pathways, then formed new seals. Gray tendrils appeared beneath the normal flow—complex, branching, studded with nodes. But they were dormant. No current. No pulse. No life.

“Ah,” Anko murmured, intrigued. Her fingers traced the gray pathways until they met a solid black barrier, cutting the foreign threads off from Sadi’s own energy.

“What’s the black barrier?” I asked.

“I’ve never seen anything like it,” Anko said. “This must be how Tadashi broke the seal. Clever work. He isolated the nodes that control Sadi’s tendrils and locked them behind this barrier. Then he rerouted the pathways so the body’s energy could flow normally again.”

She pointed at a gray sac at the base, a smaller black barrier sealed inside. “This was the slavery seal’s power source. Tadashi used to channel his energy here—but it’s empty now. The barrier blocks their mental link.”

She glanced at me, then rolled her eyes when I just blinked. “The point is: Tadashi disabled the slavery seal and severed their connection with this barrier. I just don’t know what it is—or how he made it.”

Anko finished the examination and woke Sadi.

“So he can’t track Tadashi anymore? Is there another way?” I asked, sitting when Anko gestured. She told Sadi to make me some ramen.

“If I’d placed a tracking seal on Tadashi, or if he was carrying something that had one, maybe. But I never did.”

Sadi returned with a bowl and handed it to me. “Can’t you track him through his belongings?” he asked Anko. “He left me a letter.”

Anko asked for the letter, and we read it together. I flipped the paper over, frowning at the envelope.

“Hey, the seal on this envelope matches the carving on my necklace,” I murmured.

“Where? Let me see,” Anko said.

I took off my necklace and gave it to her. The moment her fingers brushed the power stone, she went still. She studied it, then compared it to the seal on Sadi’s letter. Slowly, she laid the necklace over the envelope on the table and touched the stone again.

Protection.

Activate energy.

Open.

Energy pulsed in the air above the pendant, its sides etched with intricate carvings. Anko traced the glowing lines and formed a series of unfamiliar seals. Her fingers shaped a precise symbol, channeling energy into the pathways. Currents from her hands wove with the pendant’s light, twisting and locking together in a shimmering dance.

She gave a faint smile and pressed her palm to the stone once more.

Reveal .

The stone flared, casting a glow over the paper beneath. Black tendrils unfurled across the surface, revealing a thick book hidden under Sadi’s letter. We stared.

Sadi picked up the book and wiped the dusty cover, streaked with dark red like dried blood. The embossed title sent a shiver through me.

The Mitsurugi Clan’s Book of Dark Arts.

“Gods,” Anko whispered.

Sadi didn’t look surprised. “Tadashi said he needed this to free me from the slavery seal,” he said, turning brittle pages.

“Tadashi used dark arts to break your seal?” Anko asked. Sadi’s pained expression was answer enough. She shook her head. “That explains the black barrier—it must have come from this.”

“Hey, there’s a letter,” Sadi said, suddenly alert. A single page lay before us, with two envelopes tucked into it—the very page that explained the slavery seal. Anko plucked them free and held them up, revealing Tadashi’s familiar handwriting. One was addressed to Masada Masamune, the other to Shiro Masamune.

“These are protected with a blood seal,” Anko murmured. Her gaze shifted to me. “Tadashi must have left us something important.”

I stared at the envelope addressed to Father. He was confined in the palace—no visitors allowed. How could I deliver it to him, let alone uncover the secrets inside?

My hands trembled as I reached for the second envelope, the one with my name. I traced the letters, hesitating before I broke the seal.

What had Tadashi left for me? When had he written this? Why hide it? I would never have found it—never known his final words—if I hadn’t worn the necklace he gave me.

“This necklace is precious to me, Shiro. I hope you’ll wear it.” Tadashi’s hopeful smile played on repeat in my mind.

Ah, brother…

I bit my lip, sank into a chair, and unfolded the letter. It was long. Drawing a shaky breath, I began to read.

Shiro,

After finishing that mission with Anko last November, Shirasu came to my house to warn me. The Mitsurugi clan is coming for us. They plan to strike during the New Year Festival.

Their leader, Ikaru, wields dark arts and commands an army of dark creatures. He masters the Mitsurugi’s Seal of Obedience, which lets him control minds.

Ikaru has a vendetta against Father. He was the one who ordered Shirasu to kill Mother. Now he wants to take you, to break Father by hurting you. He won’t stop until our family is dead and our clan destroyed. Shirasu offered to help me fight him, and I agreed.

I wanted to tell you or Father, but Shirasu forbade it. He didn’t want our clan interfering with his plans, and I wasn’t in a position to argue.

Here’s what I know: Shirasu intends to capture me during the New Year mission so I can get close to Ikaru and strike when he’s vulnerable. I don’t know where their base is, but I managed to place a tracking seal on Shirasu’s weapon while he slept. The seal’s design and activator are included in the letter for Father. Don’t go after him alone, Shiro. I can’t tell Father, but you can. Please ask for his help.

Shirasu kept me in the dark about the details of the Mitsurugi attack, so I’ll have to improvise. I may not be able to stop the chaos, but I’ll do everything in my power to protect Princess Kaguya and Prince Yoru. And I promise I’ll protect you too, Shiro.

If Shirasu’s plan fails, you’ll have to face Ikaru. He must have a weakness, no matter how twisted his schemes are. I hope this book holds the answer, but I only just got my hands on it. Maybe you’ll find something useful where I couldn’t.

Shiro… by the time you read this, I’ll probably be gone. The odds of surviving this mission are slim. I have no one to call for help, no backup if things go wrong—but I’ve made my peace with that.

All that matters is keeping Father, you, and Raito safe.

I am forever grateful for you, Shiro.

Thank you for watching over Raito since we were children, for keeping our home and family together. Your cooking reminds me of Mother. Thank you for helping me study and for staying through my struggles. Even without control over your empath power, you never left my side. You’re incredibly strong. I owe you for all your sacrifices.

Thank you for shouldering the clan’s duties, for guiding me through missions, for caring for Father. He always wanted you as his heir. He knew you’d surpass me. You have surpassed me. He’s so proud of you.

I’m proud of you too, Shiro. Always. My brilliant little sister—smarter, kinder, steadier than I could ever be. You’re the kindest person I know, and I will always cherish your kindness.

I love you, Shiro. You know that, right?

I’m not good with words, and nothing I write can truly express this. You and Raito… you’re the light of my life, guiding stars in the dark. You are my reason for living. I’d do anything for you. I’d give anything.

I know I don’t have much… but if there’s anything you want, Shiro, it’s yours. Anytime. Take it all if you wish.

Shiro… I betrayed your trust. The regret is a weight I carry. I want to apologize, but I know how you feel about apologies. I hurt you, and your anger is justified. Don’t feel obligated to forgive me—not now, not ever—if you don’t want to.

After our last conversation, your expression haunted me. The strain around your eyes, the tightness in your jaw… I should have seen it sooner.

I was selfish to demand forgiveness when you needed space. I only made things harder, and I’m ashamed. So I won’t burden you with apologies anymore.

Hate me if you need to, Shiro. If the hurt lingers, don’t forgive me. I won’t ask you to. You’re free to move on, to heal, without giving me absolution. This is my burden to bear, and I will carry it.

Shiro, there’s so much I want to say, but the words won’t come. Time is running out, so I just hope you’ll understand. All this time, you were my only support. The only one who truly knew me. I had no one else.

You accepted me exactly as I am, Shiro. You gave me the joy of being an older brother. If only you knew how lucky I feel, how deeply grateful I am.

Thank you for everything, Shiro.

Take care of yourself. You’ll always be in my prayers.

Your loving brother,

Tadashi.
