Struggle

Epilogue A - Tadashi Masamune

Four months ago, not long after I was stripped of my position as Clan Heir, Elder Mei sent me on a royal mission. Fishermen had found an artifact called the Celestial Pearl, but the Mitsurugi Clan stole it—rumor said they meant to use it in a ritual. The King ordered our clan to retrieve the pearl and stop them.

I seized the Celestial Pearl mid-ritual, but the artifact hurled me back through time. A temple guardian named Chiyo Mitsurugi saved me. She treated me like her own son, and I came to see her as a mother. What I didn’t know then was the truth—she really was my birth mother.

That night, I lay awake in the dark, turning it over and over in my mind.

I couldn’t pinpoint when Chiyo realized I was her son. But one thing was certain—the moment I revealed who I truly was, she must have pieced everything together.

Ikaru had planned to use the Celestial Pearl to rewrite the past and kill me. But I had already sabotaged his ritual. From that point on, Chiyo must have known exactly what had to be done. As guardian of Jikan Temple and keeper of the Celestial Pearl, she had to make sure the time loop closed cleanly.

Which meant ensuring every event unfolded exactly as it had before.

Since I was raised in the Masamune Clan, Chiyo entrusted her two-week-old baby—me—to Father. The date she handed me over matched the birth date in the clan archives.

She hid the Celestial Pearl, her sacred charge, where fishermen would find it seventeen years later.

Then she sent me back to the future—paying for it with her life.

Chiyo’s face floated up in my mind as she looked at me for the last time. Her gentle smile held me like I was the best thing that had ever happened to her. My chest ached with suffocating tightness.

Mother …

That night, I curled up on my futon, crushed beneath the weight of the two women I’d loved most.

What kind of son lets both his mothers die? Makoto and Chiyo died because of me. Because of my mistakes.

I killed them.

Gods …

I don’t deserve to live.

My dearest Tadashi,

I hope this letter finds you well.

I’m sorry I kept the truth from you—and sorrier still that you had to discover it this way. My first son, taken by the Mitsurugi Clan, is named Shirasu Mitsurugi. He is your biological older brother. His father, like yours, is Masada Masamune.

You are my second son—the baby you once held in your arms. It wasn’t strange—it was fate. You descend from temple guardians with the power to traverse time. When you traveled to the past, you had already stepped into the time loop that shaped your destiny.

Do you remember? Before we parted, I entrusted you—still an infant—to a midwife, a dear friend. She was the one who delivered you to Masada. His wife, Makoto, was pregnant, but her child would be lost during birth. You took that child’s place; Makoto raised you as her own. No one else knows—not even the clan elders.

When I left you with the midwife, I also left a diary for Shirasu, to be given to him after your fifteenth birthday. From that day on, he would watch you. From your own account, I gathered he wouldn’t interfere until you completed the Celestial Pearl mission.

What happened next, I don’t know. Whether he chose to reveal himself to you or let you remain unaware—that decision was his alone.

If he did reach out, then he was meant to deliver this letter to you.

Tadashi, I know Shirasu killed Makoto. Your pain must run deep. I have no right to ask your forgiveness, nor will I burden you by asking you to stand with him against Ikaru.

All I ask is that you acknowledge him as your brother—whether you hate him or not. That is enough.

May you both find peace on the paths you’ve chosen.

Forgive me. May the gods watch over you.

With all my love,

Chiyo Mitsurugi

I sank into the futon, exhausted. Every muscle ached. Three sleepless nights had followed three endless days of work, with Shirasu’s words spinning in my head.

Ikaru hurt Mother. I want him dead. Don’t you want to honor your own birth mother?

My breath shuddered.

Haven’t I been a devoted son all this time, Mother?

I threw an arm over my face, pressing against my burning eyes.

Years spent caring for my siblings, years spent obeying Father’s every command… none of it was enough. There was always some flaw to find, some failing to lament. What more did he want?

Maybe only Ikaru’s death would finally prove my devotion. Maybe then Father would see me —not as an inconvenience, as Shirasu once suggested, but as a true son.

Shirasu…

Having an older brother unsettled me. He had killed Makoto—the woman who raised me, though not by blood. The loss permanently scarred me, a hollow ache in my chest.

I still had nightmares about her death, even after all these years. They came on the worst nights. In my dreams, Shirasu tortured me for hours. Sometimes he made me watch while he hurt Mother. Sometimes I was the one holding the knife. I’d wake gasping, chest tight, bile rising in my throat.

I wanted revenge. I wanted Shirasu dead. Three days ago, that hunger still burned in me.

But now, knowing the truth—that Shirasu had been Ikaru’s puppet since childhood, as powerless as Sadi under his father’s command… I couldn’t hate him the same way.

Gods… what was I supposed to do?

If I still wished my own brother dead, did that make me a monster?

But if I didn’t despise the man who murdered the woman who raised me, did that make me a faithless son?

I didn’t know anymore.

I think… I didn’t want Shirasu dead now. I just wanted nothing to do with him. To walk away and never look back.

But Ikaru still threatened my family. Shirasu wouldn’t reveal his clan leader’s plans unless I joined him. And I needed that knowledge to free Sadi from the slavery seal.

I drew a slow breath.

What choice did I have, really? Tell Father? But how would he react?

He’d rage if he learned Shirasu had come to me—and that I’d let him go.

Shirasu returned in the dead of night.

I’d been back from the main hall barely fifteen minutes. Raito was already asleep, sprawled across the futon as if he’d collapsed mid-study. I gathered his scattered books, tucked them beside him, and pulled the blanket over his shoulders. He murmured something happy, his lips curling into a smile. I huffed a quiet laugh.

“You spoil him too much.”

I shot to my feet, spinning toward the voice, hands instinctively reaching for a weapon that wasn’t there. Shirasu leaned against the far wall of Raito’s room, arms crossed. I said nothing, shifting to shield Raito from view.

“We need to talk. Here or your room?”

I closed my eyes briefly. The last time he’d wanted to “talk,” the floor of Father’s study had been slick with my blood. And I didn’t want Raito waking to find us together.

“My room.”

I held the door for Shirasu, forcing my breath to steady. The silence inside felt brittle, stretched taut.

He scowled, taking in the cramped space. “This is it? They stuck you in a closet?”

I pressed my lips together. “Skip the pleasantries.”

“A glorified storage room. That bastard always played favorites.”

The insult landed a beat late. “Don’t you dare speak of Father like that.”

“Why are you still defending him?”

“Shirasu.” My voice dropped, a warning. “Enough. What do you want?”

His cold gaze held mine. “Have you read Mother’s letter?”

I nodded.

“And?” he asked, his tone softening slightly. “What did she say?”

“She apologized for leaving. She asked me to accept you… as my brother.”

Shirasu exhaled—disappointment, relief, I couldn’t tell. “Have you considered my offer? What’s your decision? Don’t drag this out. If you refuse, I won’t ask again.”

I looked away, the weight of it pressing down. Did I even have a choice? Both my mothers were gone, a consequence of my failings. If I ignored Shirasu’s warning, Ikaru would destroy what remained of my family.

All because I couldn’t bring myself to work with my brother.

I clenched a fist in my hakama. This was pointless. We both knew the answer.

“I’m considering it,” I muttered. “I just don’t know what you expect from me.”

Shirasu shrugged.

“I’ll tell you what you need to know once you agree. Until then, follow my orders.”

“Got any ideas? You really think I can take on Ikaru?”

Shirasu scoffed. “Listen carefully. You won’t face Ikaru head-on, got it? For now, we play along with his plan—then we turn on him.”

I must have still looked unsure, because Shirasu rolled his eyes. “Ikaru’s first move? He’ll attack and kidnap Shiro during the New Year festival.”

“What?! Why Shiro—”

“She’s the clan heir now. Ikaru will use her to humiliate Father. You can guess what a man like him would do to a woman.” His gaze sharpened. “Your job is to protect Shiro at the festival. Then I’ll ‘capture’ you.”

I frowned. “Why me? Why not kill Ikaru during the attack?”

“Ikaru operates from the shadows—he won’t show himself. He’ll send Seishiro and me to do his dirty work. If you want him dead, you have to get close.”

Shirasu leaned in. “After I take you, you’ll be brought to our base. That’s your chance. Find his weakness. Wait for the right moment—when his guard drops—then strike.”

“If it’s just about finding a weakness, why can’t you do it? You’re already near him.”

“He’s paranoid. No one in the clan knows his weakness, not even me. And even if I did, the Seal of Obedience binds me. It has to be an outsider.”

A cold smile touched his lips. “Ikaru has a personal vendetta against Masada. Capturing you? That’ll thrill him. You’re perfect. I’ll help where I can, Tadashi—but he can’t know we’re working together.”

It sounded simple—pretend to be captured, then stab Ikaru in the back when his guard was down. Only one problem: Shirasu hadn’t mentioned what came after.

After capture came torture.

I had no backup plan, no clan support waiting in the wings. Even if I discovered Ikaru’s weakness, I doubted I could kill him. More likely, I’d be the one bleeding out at his feet.

I drew a steadying breath, sorting through the chaos in my head. Silence stretched between us. Shirasu’s gaze burned into me. Brother or not, Chiyo’s words as she held baby me echoed in my memory:

I’ll name him after you. I want him to love his older brother as you love your siblings.

She wanted this—she wanted her fractured family whole again. And me? I just wanted to honor my mother’s dying wish.

I lowered my eyes, swallowing my fear.

“I’ll follow your plan,” I said at last. “But I have one condition.”

Shirasu narrowed his eyes.

“You’re in no position to make demands,” he snapped. “This is for your benefit. I’ve already given you plenty of free information. What more do you want? Listen, I don’t care if your sister and clan survive. I want revenge for what Ikaru did to Mother. Even if it fails, so what? Mother’s gone—revenge won’t bring her back. I’m offering you a chance to prove yourself a devoted son. You should be thanking me, not making requests.”

His sharp, accusing stare bored into me, as if to remind me—Chiyo died after giving birth to me. And the truth was, it was my fault. She spent nearly all her strength sending me back to the present. Because of me, she fell gravely ill. Because of me, she was gone.

I was the one who killed my mother.

My face burned. I couldn’t breathe.

“I… I understand,” I whispered, forcing down the sting in my throat. “But I’m not asking for much. I just… want to see Mother’s grave. To pay my respects.”

Shirasu went still, caught off guard. For a long moment, he studied me, expression unreadable. Then, finally, he nodded.

“You have a mission tomorrow. Get your leave approved first. Once that’s settled, I’ll pick you up. We’re going into Mitsurugi territory, so be ready.”

Before I could say another word, he was gone.
