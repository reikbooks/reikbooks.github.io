Break In

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

Shiro Masamune

Nobuga’s base sat beyond the city limits, straddling the border between the Tsuki and Kawa kingdoms. After a full day’s ride, Sadi and I reached the compound under cover of darkness. We split up to scout the two imposing three-story houses that made up the stronghold. I took the first; Sadi took the second.

“Well?” I asked when we regrouped.

“Third floor,” Sadi said. “I got in through a roof vent earlier. Six guards on that level, two posted outside his door.”

“Can you get back through the vent? I’ll watch from the balcony.”

He nodded. We scaled the building and took our positions. Through the window, I spotted a man in a golden kimono writing at his desk. Nobuga. Fifteen minutes later, Sadi slipped silently from a vent above the bookshelf and crept up behind him.

“Busy?” Sadi pressed a kunai to Nobuga’s throat. The man froze, his face tightening. “I shouldn’t have let you go last time.”

Nobuga smirked. “Hello, Sadi. Feels familiar, doesn’t it?”

He tapped a hidden switch. A blur cut past Sadi, but Sadi had already flattened himself. Senbon needles thudded into the opposite wall.

Before he could recover, two bodyguards lunged from behind. As Sadi parried, Nobuga snatched up a stack of documents and bolted.

He shoved open the balcony door—and stepped straight into my strike. He crumpled. The guards burst in, blades drawn. Sadi and I moved as one, dropping them quickly. Sadi slammed the door while I dragged Nobuga into a chair and tied him down. A quick search turned up nothing.

Sadi slapped him awake. “Hey. Rise and shine. Your house is on fire.”

Nobuga jolted, blinking, paling as he realized he’d been captured.

“Where’s the Jomon stone?” Sadi asked, not bothering with preamble. “Don’t play games. You took it, didn’t you? That jewel box held a fake.”

Nobuga chuckled. “Only figured that out now? It’s been nearly a month.”

Sadi hit him. Nobuga’s lip split, blood pattering onto the floor.

“I won’t ask again,” Sadi said, drawing his kunai. He drove the blade into the back of Nobuga’s hand and dragged it across the flesh. Nobuga screamed.

“I already sold it to the Mitsurugi clan!” he spat.

Sadi cursed and raised his hand to strike again, but I stopped him. Nobuga sneered.

“Out on a mission with your girlfriend, Sadi?”

Sadi growled, but I lifted a hand to silence him. I stepped closer. A chill bloomed in my chest, a discordant note against the steady rhythm of Nobuga’s heartbeat. It wasn’t a lie exactly, but something carefully constructed—a truth bent just out of alignment.

I drew a slow breath. My teacher had once taught me an empathic technique I rarely used because it drained me. I touched Nobuga’s chin and pushed raw fear into his mind. The effect was immediate. His body went rigid. His eyes widened. Sweat beaded across his brow as panic seeped into him, searching for an escape that wasn’t there. Nausea rose in me; I clenched my jaw.

“Tell me everything, from the beginning,” I murmured, stoking the fear. “Do not leave anything out.”

Nobuga whimpered, swaying. At last, he began to talk.

Shirasu Mitsurugi had hired him to steal both the Jomon stone and the Kusanagi sword. Nobuga had stolen the stone from Yasakani Temple and crafted two replicas, giving one to Shirasu and slipping the other into the jewel box Tadashi recovered.

“Where’s the real Jomon stone?” I asked.

“I kept it,” Nobuga stammered. “It’s worth a fortune to collectors.”

“And the Kusanagi sword?”

He shook his head. “Sumugari Temple is impenetrable. I only had reference images, so I made two replicas and gave one to Shirasu. The real sword is still there.”

“Why does this slime love making fakes?” Sadi muttered.

I told Nobuga to show us where he’d stashed the stone. He revealed a hidden safe in the room. He unlocked it, and Sadi retrieved the relic.

“What about the sword?” Sadi asked, holding up the replica.

“Take it,” I said. “I know what to do with it.”

Sadi handed me the Jomon stone and the replica Kusanagi. The gem pulsed warm in my palm, its energy almost tangible. I slid it into my pendant, then stowed the blade.

Sadi knocked Nobuga out, and we slipped outside.

“Don’t forget to call the authorities.” I closed my eyes, steadying my breath against the drain of the technique. A tremor ran through my hands—small, but I felt it.

Sadi noticed. “What’s wrong, Shiro?”

I shook my head.

“Want a lift? I can carry you bridal style,” he said with a grin.

I glared, stomped his foot, and strode off.

“Hey! I was just asking! Shiro, wait up!”

Anko had been right.

Sumugari Temple stood encased beneath a massive dome of energy. From a distance, the barrier shimmered in the sunlight. As we drew closer, static crawled over my skin, lifting the hairs on my arms.

The temple path was empty. No visitors. Only royal guards and Fuma clan ninja patrolled in shifts. The shield rippled each time someone passed through.

“No wonder Nobuga couldn’t break in. How did the Fuma clan build a shield this big?” Sadi winced. “They should’ve used this during the New Year Festival. We ought to get one for the main hall too.”

“The shield’s anchored with power stones at multiple points,” Anko said. “It can’t be moved like the one we used for the Royal Family yesterday. And the temple’s royal property—they’ve got the budget. Our clan doesn’t.”

“So what now?” Sadi looked at me. “You still don’t know what’s inside.”

I wrinkled my nose. Improvising wasn’t ideal, but I’d have to trust my instincts. “No choice. We stick to the plan.”

Simple: Sadi would create a diversion while Anko breached the shield. I’d slip in, grab the real sword, and swap it with our fake. If things went south, they’d think the theft had failed and wouldn’t chase far.

Sadi snorted, tugging a mask over his face. “You owe me ten bowls of ramen after this, sweetheart.”

I rolled my eyes as he dashed off. Anko stepped toward a power stone and formed signs with her hands, waiting for Sadi’s signal. Moments later, the shield flashed crimson—Sadi forcing his way through.

Anko moved, weaving a rapid series of signs, then pressed a glowing seal onto the stone. The shield flickered and sagged. Energy surged back, trying to reform, but Anko was faster, sealing each anchor in turn.

“Go, Shiro!” she barked, voice tight.

I sprinted in, Nobuga’s replica Kusanagi in hand.

Guards formed ranks before the temple, weapons ready. A commander shouted orders, sending troops after Sadi and leaving gaps in their lines.

I took a narrow path, vaulted over crates, and scrambled for the roof. As my feet hit the tiles, something streaked toward me. Instinct took over. I swung in a defensive arc. My blade caught—locked between iron claws.

Seishiro Mitsurugi grinned beside me, eyes glowing wolf-yellow. “Let me borrow that sword, sweetheart.”

He wrenched the replica free. I skidded to a halt, but he was already ducking low and breaking off toward the north side, angling for Sadi. A pack of wolf-creatures surged behind him, cutting off the guards.

Seishiro glanced left and signaled. Two wolves peeled away, charging Anko. Chaos erupted as Fuma ninja joined the fray, some breaking off to pursue Seishiro.

I froze for a heartbeat, torn between helping Anko and chasing him.

Let him go. Get inside now—while they’re distracted.

I crashed through a temple window and landed hard. A spiral stair led down to a vast chamber where the sword rested in a display case. Another barrier shimmered across the room.

I stumbled into it. The seal flared—fire licking my skin. I bit back a cry, smashed the display glass, and wrenched the case open.

The door burst inward. Two guards charged, but my fingers had already closed around the Kusanagi’s hilt. They struck in unison.

I dodged, ducked, and slashed at one guard’s midsection. Steel rang. I leaped back, deflecting his counter. For long, ragged heartbeats, I could only parry his relentless attacks.

CLANG. Kusanagi shattered his sword. Before the shards hit the floor, I channeled power into my next swing. The second blade snapped—then reinforcements flooded the chamber. I cut through them and spun for the stairs. A volley of shuriken clattered across the steps, blocking my escape.

I veered, sprinted for the roof, each breath searing my chest. Pressing a hand to my ribs, I found the pendant under my palm grow warm. A brilliant white light erupted, washing over me, easing the sting of the barrier’s flames.

I was stunned. The Jomon stone had healing properties .

I sprinted toward Anko. Guards swarmed her—but it wasn’t them that stopped me short. Two wolf-like creatures tore into anyone who pursued.

I slammed into two of Anko’s attackers, sending them sprawling. She stared, startled, then seized my arm and pulled me toward the perimeter. The creatures kept pace, a whirling storm of teeth and claws behind us.

As Anko left her post, the seals unraveled. The dome shuddered, then rose again, sealing the temple.

A distant wolf howl echoed. The beasts following us turned, loping away as if summoned. Anko and I collapsed, gasping, the silence almost loud.

“What were those?” I asked.

Anko shook her head. “I don’t know. They turned on the guards and helped me. Did you get the sword?”

I was about to answer when Sadi burst in. “Hey! You won’t believe who I just ran into!”

“Seishiro,” I whispered.

“Yeah! He came at me—and then his wolves turned on the guards and the Fuma ninja! Then he called them off and bolted.”

I bit my lip, struggling to make sense of it.

Tadashi had chosen to work with Shirasu , even knowing Shirasu killed Mother. Did he not resent him? Why agree to his terms? I still couldn’t understand it, but the choice was made. All I could do was trust Tadashi had his reasons.

Now Seishiro had helped us get the Kusanagi—which made no sense. Shirasu and Seishiro were our enemies. Why act like allies? Were they planning a coup against Ikaru?

How would that help our clan? Tadashi should be dismantling the Mitsurugi, not putting Shirasu and Seishiro in power. Could they reform the clan? What if they were worse than Ikaru?

What if they were lying—using Tadashi to remove Ikaru, only to betray him after?

I clenched my fists. Too many unknowns. I needed the truth. My gut said Father knew something. Tadashi’s letter to him might hold answers, but with Father unreachable, I couldn’t open it yet.

“Let’s head back,” I said at last. “We’ll figure it out at home.”

We slipped back into the shadows.
