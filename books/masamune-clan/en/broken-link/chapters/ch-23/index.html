Epilogue C - Masada Masamune

The room stayed quiet. Masada studied his glass, still nearly full. He’d only taken a small sip—for appearances. Drowning his troubles in drink was tempting, but those days were behind him. He’d trained his body to resist such weaknesses.

Besides, Shirasu would refill it the moment it emptied. The young man had already drained three glasses and now watched Masada with sharp, calculating eyes.

Masada didn’t bring up their past—his departure, Chiyo, or Tadashi’s birth. He knew Shirasu saw his actions as unforgivable.

Still, if the boy had grown stronger because of it and was fine now, there was no point dredging it up. Shirasu wouldn’t forgive him anyway—no need for apologies.

To Masada, being a responsible father meant leaving Chiyo enough money to survive when he walked away—nothing more.

“Will you lift the speech seal from Tadashi?”

Masada raised a brow.

“Only temporarily—just long enough to repair his tangled energy pathways. Once that’s done, I’ll put it back.”

Shirasu glared at him like he was dirt under his shoe.

“What are you talking about? Do you even know what happened to him? Why would you put that cursed seal back?”

“As an agent of the Masamune clan, Tadashi must bear the speech seal. As for what happened to him—I have my suspicions.”

Shirasu shook his head. “Whatever you’re imagining, Ikaru did a hundred times worse.”

Masada crossed his arms, unimpressed.

“I’ve carried out missions like Tadashi’s. I even infiltrated the Mitsurugi clan myself. I know how they operate. You don’t need to explain Ikaru to me. I knew him before I met Chiyo—before you were born,” Masada said, voice cold. “What he did to you and Tadashi is over. You failed to kill him, but he’s dead now. He can’t hurt you anymore.”

Shirasu slammed his glass down.

“You… you really…” He trailed off, shaking his head, face burning crimson. “Tadashi didn’t do this to kill Ikaru. Don’t you get it? He did all of it for you. For Shiro. Even for me. There was nothing in it for him. He didn’t have to—but he loves you, so he—”

“I already know,” Masada cut in. “What he did was wrong—no question. He thought sacrificing himself would protect his family, but his actions hurt the entire clan. If he wants to lead, he needs to put the clan first. Always.”

“He wept in my arms.”

Masada fell silent. Shirasu’s voice went flat and quiet as he turned to stare out the window.

“He believed you didn’t love him because he kept falling short of your expectations,” Shirasu said. “Ikaru destroyed him—body and soul. And even broken, Tadashi never forgot you. He couldn’t bear your shame, so he begged me. ‘Don’t tell Father,’ he said. ‘Just kill me instead.’”

Masada froze for a moment.

“I don’t see the point of this conversation. What you’re talking about is in the past,” he said slowly. “Remember—you were the one who forbade Tadashi from telling me about your collaboration. I couldn’t help him. I couldn’t stop him from surrendering to Ikaru. What else do you want? Are you projecting? Or do you really expect me to apologize for not caring about your fate the way I cared about Tadashi’s?”

For a moment, Shirasu just gaped at him, mouth opening and closing soundlessly.

“Wow. Just… wow. Never saw that coming. Guess you really don’t have any regrets. Should’ve known better.” He shook his head, exhaled sharply, glared at the ceiling, and refilled his glass. He knocked it back in one go.

“Masada, you’re a real bastard,” Shirasu spat. “To hell with your damned clan. You’re no father of mine. Go rot in hell.”

Masada simply stared at his eldest son. Did Shirasu truly think his opinion mattered? Not a chance.

“If you’re done, I’ll wake Tadashi and take him home. He has work to do.”

Shirasu shot to his feet. “Like hell you will! Have you lost your mind? He hasn’t recovered. As his doctor, I won’t allow it.”

Masada watched him with a faint smile.

“His wounds are healed, aren’t they?”

“He’ll likely develop psychiatric disorders.”

“I’ll alter the speech seal. He won’t remember a thing about Ikaru’s torture—”

“You don’t need to recall trauma to suffer terror, panic attacks, or hallucinations. Victims often suppress memories as a defense—even without your memory trick. But their bodies remember what their minds forget. He needs medication just to function,” Shirasu said, the patient tone of a teacher with a slow student.

“But you seem fine now, after suffering under Ikaru for so long?”

Shirasu laughed bitterly. “Don’t pretend to care, you bastard. You don’t know a damn thing about me. I’ve dealt with Ikaru my entire life.”

His voice sharpened. “Tadashi needs time to heal—mentally. He needs someone who understands what he’s been through, someone who accepts him completely. Not a heartless bastard like you who only wants to use him.”

His glare hardened. “And he’s been in a coma for months. His body’s weak. He needs therapy before anything else. Fighting? He might not even be able to sit up or walk yet. Face it—you won’t be using him on any mission anytime soon.”

Masada stood and stepped closer to the bed. “Then let’s ask him what he wants—stay here for a while or return to the clan. If he just needs rest, he can do that there.”

“He can’t relax at home. You stuck him in that tiny converted storage closet. It’s cramped, stuffy, barely livable. He has no one to ask for help. And instead of resting, he’ll feel like he has to clean the whole damn house.”

“I never told him to go home. I’ll have him hospitalized—he can be treated there. If you want to be his doctor, relocate to the clan complex. Use whatever disguise you like; your secret’s safe with me.”

Shirasu flushed again. He stayed silent, but his glare bored into Masada’s back. Without acknowledging him, Masada took a seat beside Tadashi.

“Why are you so determined to take him? Are you doing this just to punish me?” Shirasu’s voice dropped low. “You already have Shiro and Raito. You don’t need Tadashi—you barely acknowledge he exists.”

“Tadashi isn’t yours to claim—this isn’t a battle over a possession. Why are you so dependent on him? I remember how you resented Chiyo and me for having another child. You hated him.”

Shirasu closed his eyes, took a breath, then sat on Tadashi’s left.

“That was at first,” he murmured, taking Tadashi’s hand. “Before I really knew him. We went through hell together. We grew close. He risked himself to save me from Ikaru. How could I hate him?”

Masada turned his head.

“Are you angry that I’ll erase his memories of your bond? Afraid he’ll go back to hating you?”

Shirasu paused, then shook his head.

“I don’t know exactly when it changed, but during that reckless mission of mine, he stopped hating me. Even if his memories are taken, I’ll remember everything. I’ll hold them for him. And if he forgets me… I’ll just make new memories with him.”

“Gods.” Masada chuckled softly. “So dramatic. You two really take after Chiyo.”

“Shut up. Don’t you dare say Mother’s name with that filthy mouth.”

Masada offered a faint smile and turned to Tadashi. With a swift motion, he activated a protective seal, opened the energy pathways, and examined the speech seal.

“Don’t send him home,” Shirasu pleaded, his soft urgency nearly breaking Masada’s focus. “He’s given everything to your clan. By now, they already think he’s dead. No one will come looking if he doesn’t return. Just let him go. Leave him be. Give him that—one small reward for years of service. Have you no mercy left for your own son?”

Masada simply raised an eyebrow. He found the blockage and removed the speech seal, extinguishing it. Tadashi’s entire energy network flared to life. Masada worked quickly to realign the pathways.

Then he restored the speech seal, burying Tadashi’s memories of Ikaru deep beneath the surface. With a gentle nudge, he brought his son back to consciousness.

Tadashi drew a sharp breath, then opened his eyes. He blinked, brow furrowed, studying Masada.

Shirasu gently tugged the hand Tadashi held, turning him. The boy almost jumped, his gaze flicking nervously between them like a mouse cornered by two snakes.

“F-Father?”

“How are you feeling, Tadashi?” Masada asked softly.

Tadashi flushed under his gaze. His brow knit tighter before he answered.

“I’m fine, but…” His voice was hoarse. He glanced at Shirasu. “D-did you summon Shirasu to the main hall?”

Shirasu glanced at Masada, who only shrugged.

“We’re not in the main hall. What’s the last thing you remember?”

Tadashi hesitated. He opened his mouth, but no sound came. Shirasu handed him a glass and helped him drink. Tadashi closed his eyes briefly and whispered thanks. When he looked back at Masada, he’d gone pale.

“After getting Shiro to safety during the New Year mission, Shirasu stabbed me,” he said carefully, watching Masada. “Were we captured by the King’s troops?”

Masada shook his head. “After you passed out, Shirasu got you to Ikaru’s base. That was seven months ago. We’re outside the city now.”

Tadashi reeled.

“Ikaru… what about him? And Shiro—is she all right?” He pressed a hand to his temple, wincing. “Why can’t I remember anything, Father?”

“Shiro managed to kill Ikaru. Shirasu saved and treated you, but you fell into a coma. I woke you and sealed your memories of the time you spent with Ikaru,” Masada said. “I didn’t realize you’d been unconscious all this time. I only learned the truth when I came looking for you here. Your older brother has been caring for you.”

Tadashi’s pupils dilated when Masada acknowledged Shirasu’s identity.

Ah. Masada almost clicked his tongue. He should have chosen his words more carefully.

“You and Shirasu… have you made amends?” Tadashi asked softly.

Shirasu scoffed. “We can at least agree on one thing—Masada’s the biggest bastard alive.”

Masada shot him a look, then shook his head. Shirasu waved him off.

“Move your ass, bastard. My patient comes first.”

Masada released Tadashi’s hand and stepped back. Shirasu grabbed a stethoscope and began a methodical exam.

“Listen, Tadashi. Masada’s got a rotten plan. He needs you to do something,” Shirasu murmured, eyes fixed on him. “He’ll try to guilt you. Don’t fall for it. You’ve sacrificed enough. As far as the clan’s concerned, you died in the line of duty. They already held your funeral. You don’t owe them anything. Masada’s just using you.”

Masada watched them. Tadashi shot him a glance, then looked back to Shirasu.

“You’re still sick—you can’t travel yet. Masada won’t care. He never truly cared about you. He and your siblings treated you like a servant. Don’t go back. You only suffer there. You’re my brother now. Stay with me.”

Tadashi flushed, lowered his gaze, and gripped the blanket in his lap, as if afraid to speak in Masada’s presence.

Shirasu snorted, then turned to Masada and waved him away. “Out. And no eavesdropping. I need to talk to my brother.”

Masada finally rose and left the room.

Outside, Seishiro leaned against the wall by the door. Masada stopped before him.

“You killed my wolves,” Seishiro said, lighting a cigarette.

Masada shook his head. “If you’d been here to invite me in, I wouldn’t have had to.”

Seishiro scoffed. “You sent Anko to the Mitsurugi base too. You didn’t even bother to watch her properly.”

Masada studied him for a long moment. As Ken Masamune’s illegitimate son and Hojo Mitsurugi’s nephew, Seishiro carried secrets only Masada knew. Before Masada left Shirasu and Chiyo, he’d struck a bargain—Seishiro would protect Shirasu, and in return, Masada would watch over Anko, Seishiro’s half-sister.

“The King ordered Anko, Sadi, and Shiro to your clan’s base. They were the Masamune agents involved in the New Year mission,” Masada said. “Besides, Anko’s fully recovered now—same as Shirasu.”

Seishiro took a drag and smirked. “Fine. Then we’re even.”

Masada regarded him quietly.

“Are you leaving Shirasu after this?”

Seishiro chuckled. “No. I already think of him as a brother. I just need to make sure he can take proper care of Anko.”

“Ah… so they’ll get back together?” Masada asked, interest piqued. He needed to know what it meant for Anko—and her standing in the clan.

“I don’t know. The timing’s not right yet. Shirasu and I still have things to do. We’ll see.”

Masada nodded and offered a faint smile.

“Thank you. You’re a good brother.”
