Understanding

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

Shiro Masamune

“Shiro?”

I jolted awake in an unfamiliar room, disoriented. A figure stood in the doorway. The light hit my eyes and my body moved on instinct—I lunged. My heart hammered. Had I been captured? How could I have been so careless, falling asleep—

“Whoa, hey! It’s me, Sadi!”

I slammed him against the wall. He winced but didn’t fight back, hands raised to show he was unarmed.

Then it all snapped into place—memories locking together like puzzle pieces. The New Year Festival. Shirasu. Tadashi … dying to protect me. I let go of Sadi’s collar with a rough jerk and stepped back, swallowing hard against the nausea.

No. No. Tadashi isn’t dead.

He can’t be. Not until I see his body with my own eyes.

“You fell asleep. I got worried and came to check on you,” Sadi said gently, like he was explaining something simple to a child. I glanced at Tadashi’s futon—rumpled, the blanket crumpled on the floor like a discarded thought.

“Go wash up. I’ll make some tea, okay?” he offered.

I just nodded.

While Sadi headed downstairs, I folded the blanket. My gaze lingered on Tadashi’s memory box beside the futon. I opened it again. The necklace with the white stone pendant still peeked out from the top—Tadashi’s final gift, the one I’d refused.

“This necklace means the world to me. I’d be honored if you’d wear it, Shiro.” His voice had been full of hope.

I saw him again, bowing his head, taking the necklace back with trembling hands. He’d offered me his most precious treasure, and I’d cast it aside.

“Ah… sorry if it’s not to your taste, Shiro,” he’d mumbled, like it was his fault for not choosing better.

A sharp pain speared through my chest. I fastened the necklace and slid the box back into the wardrobe.

After washing my face, I joined Sadi on the porch. Night had fallen. The moon glowed pale under a sky full of stars. I sipped the hot tea he’d made. Its mild sweetness warmed my mouth and settled my empty stomach.

Sadi drew a slow breath. “Elder Mei mentioned Tadashi’s funeral rites—”

“He’s not dead,” I cut in.

“Huh?”

“I never saw his body. So he can’t be dead.”

Sadi studied me for a long moment, choosing his words with care. “Shiro, your brother was badly wounded. He lost too much blood. You saw the scene. There’s no way he could’ve survived.”

I shook my head. “Then why take his body? If he was really dead, why not leave him there? What use would his corpse be to Shirasu?”

Sadi opened his mouth, then shut it again.

“I know the kingdom won’t investigate. Our clan won’t dig that deep. But that one detail keeps nagging at me,” I said.

“Who knows what goes on in a mind like Shirasu’s?” Sadi muttered. “Maybe he kept it as a trophy. Or cut it up and sent it back to scare the clan. I can’t predict your father, but I know it would wreck you.”

A wave of nausea rolled through me. The thought had crossed my mind, but I hadn’t expected him to say it out loud.

“Sorry,” Sadi said quickly. “What I mean is—Shirasu is targeting you, Shiro. He’s probably already planned how to exploit your weaknesses and fracture us.”

I snorted. “He already won. Didn’t you hear the King? Years of unpaid labor—our reputation ruined. Everyone will treat us like dirt—other clans, the public. People are already blaming us, calling us incompetent.

“We’re a small clan, not the Fuma. They think they can walk over us. If society shuns our trade, we’ll go broke. Even without more attacks from Shirasu, we’ll collapse.”

Sadi grimaced. “Ugh. I hate when you’re right.”

I dragged us back to what mattered. “Listen. There’s another reason Shirasu took Tadashi’s body. What if Tadashi isn’t dead? What if he’s a prisoner at the Mitsurugi base—held on their orders or by Shirasu himself? They could be interrogating him right now. Using him to plan an attack.”

Sadi still looked unconvinced. “Shiro, I know it’s hard. But the evidence is clear. That blood trail… no one survives losing that much. And his Star Shield faded. You know what that means.”

I bit my lip. “What if he deactivated it—on purpose?”

Sadi’s eyes widened. He started to protest, but I pushed on. “He had a clear view of me from the hill. He knew I was almost safe. When the Fuma charged, he dropped the shield. He didn’t have the strength to sustain it any longer. Think about it—he’d held three Star Shields for over ten minutes. I can’t imagine the agony he must’ve been in.”

Liar .

As an empath, I’d felt every inch of Tadashi’s suffering. I just chose to ignore it, building walls to shield myself from his truth. I let the silence stretch while Sadi processed.

“So you’re saying he lowered his shield to gather strength against Shirasu, but it didn’t matter because Shirasu killed him anyway?”

I shook my head. “He didn’t die. How many times do I have to say it? You’re missing something, Sadi. Shirasu is a doctor. If Tadashi lost that much blood, Shirasu would have been the one to give him a transfusion—”

“Listen to yourself. How much could he even lose? Why drain him just to put it back?”

“How should I know? Maybe the Mitsurugi run a hospital and blood bank. And Shirasu has healing powers. Anko’s mission report said he healed her stab wound.”

Sadi went still, then swore under his breath and scrubbed a hand through his hair. “So Tadashi’s alive and needs our help? You know they won’t get anything out of interrogating him, right? The speech seal will stop him.”

I covered my face with shaking hands. “They might not know that, Sadi. They’ll keep torturing him, hoping he’ll give up something about our clan.”

Sadi drained his glass and slammed it down. “Damn it! If I’d known this would happen, I never would’ve let Tadashi break my seal.”

I frowned. “What seal?”

Color crept up Sadi’s neck. He scratched at the back of it, cursing under his breath, weighing his words.

“A long time ago, my father put a slavery seal on me,” he muttered. “Tadashi knew. He found out when he stayed at my house. When my father died, the seal would’ve killed me too. Tadashi intervened—he took control of it and saved my life. Anko helped with the transfer.”

I went silent. Akito had branded his own son? He’d died months ago during Shirasu’s infiltration.

And then I remembered—Tadashi setting up shields in the main hall with Sadi and Anko. When we questioned everyone about Akito’s death, I’d sensed they were hiding something. I’d had no proof. So this was it.

“Tadashi said there was a glitch during the transfer,” Sadi continued. “Side effect—we ended up with a telepathic link. We could sense each other’s thoughts and track each other’s locations.”

He took a breath. “Tadashi promised he’d find a way to break the seal. Honestly, I didn’t think he’d manage it. But he did.”

We sat quietly for a while. I stared into my nearly empty teacup, feeling just as hollow. “Sadi,” I said at last, “I need to ask you something. Please be honest.”

“Uh… you’re making me nervous. Just ask.”

I closed my eyes, bracing for the truth Tadashi had refused to give me.

“What happened after your mission to take down that drug lord?” I looked him dead in the eye. “What were you and Tadashi really doing that kept you from coming straight home?”

That was the root of everything. If Tadashi had returned immediately, the elders wouldn’t have panicked or guessed. There’d have been no power struggle, no chaos. I wouldn’t have been named heir. I wouldn’t have felt that sting of betrayal. Tadashi and I… we might still be side by side.

Sadi looked at me with pity, like he already knew I couldn’t handle the answer.

“Just tell me. I can take it,” I said through my teeth.

He cleared his throat, hesitant.

“I rented a villa and locked Tadashi inside,” he said finally. “He couldn’t come back. I made sure of it.”

What?

“Don’t get angry yet. I locked him up because he was going through withdrawal.”

“What?!”

Sadi dragged a hand over his face and let out a low groan. “On that mission, Tadashi got hooked on drugs. On purpose. I gave them to him. He took them willingly.”

I shot him a razor look. “Say that again.”

He inhaled slowly. “It started with my father. You know how obsessed he was with getting rid of Tadashi so I could become heir? When Tadashi fell ill from that wolf-thing bite, my father saw his chance.”

“Getting him addicted was his brilliant idea?”

Sadi ruffled his hair in frustration. “You have no idea how stressed I was when he ordered me. He controlled my seal—I had no choice. I’m terrible at schemes, so I just told Tadashi straight: we needed him to buy drugs from the dealer to infiltrate their base and wrap the mission fast.”

I groaned. I could already see the ending.

“So Tadashi agreed, you finished the mission, but he ended up addicted, and you forced him through two weeks of cold-turkey rehab?” I asked.

“That’s right. I couldn’t write that in the mission report. Either way, I was screwed. In the end, Tadashi took the blame for me.”

My fists clenched. “Do you know what they did to him in isolation for those two weeks?”

Sadi swallowed. “Don’t tell me. I already feel sick about it. Honestly, I’m amazed he kept his sanity.”

I pressed my hands to my face. If only Tadashi had told me the truth—I’d still have been angry, but at least I’d have understood.

He wouldn’t have had to beg for forgiveness, I would have given it willingly.

Would you really?

You’re only saying that to make yourself feel better, Shiro. Deep down, you knew he didn’t betray you—not on purpose. He was cornered and made a choice he thought was right in the moment. He never meant to hurt you.

He loves you, Shiro. He’d do anything for you. You refused his apology because you needed someone to blame for being named heir. You couldn’t stand suffering alone, so you made sure he suffered with you.

Ten years ago, you did the same—blamed him for Mother’s death. You remember his face. How he didn’t fight back when you hit him. He never denied it. He truly believed it was his fault. He still does.

You haven’t changed, Shiro. You still make your brother suffer. You’re still that selfish little brat.

Now face the consequences. How many days has he been missing? If he wasn’t dead on New Year’s Day, maybe today’s the day he met his end.

It’s too late for forgiveness. Too late for apologies. Your brother is gone.

He’s really gone for good.

“Shiro. Shiro!”

A hand shook my shoulder.

Sadi.

“Focus. Listen to me. Breathe. Slow and steady. That’s it.”

I wiped away tears, choking on the sobs clawing up my throat.

Oh, brother…

Another childhood memory surfaced, sharp as glass.

“It’s okay, Shiro,” Tadashi whispered, tracing gentle circles on my back. “I love you.”

Sadi draped his jacket over my shoulders and squeezed my hand. “You okay?”

No. Of course not.

I nodded anyway, forcing my thoughts into line. This couldn’t go on—I needed to act.

An idea struck. I shot to my feet, grabbed my jacket, and caught Sadi’s hand, pulling him after me.
