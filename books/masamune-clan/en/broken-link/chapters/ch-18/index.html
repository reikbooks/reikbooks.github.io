Epilogue B - Shirasu Mitsurugi

Two and a half years ago.

Ikaru granted me a few days off—a reward for my last mission’s success. “Be back by midnight Sunday,” he ordered, grabbing a fistful of my hair. An unnecessary gesture, but one he always used to assert dominance. I nodded quickly, still kneeling, my eyes fixed on the marble floor beneath his feet. “Thank you, Lord Ikaru,” I murmured, leaning forward to kiss the hem of his robe. With a dismissive wave, he turned away. I didn’t linger. Seishiro was guarding the door, as it turned out. I shot him a glare as he took a casual drag from his cigarette, then grabbed my jacket off the table where I’d left it. “Where you headed?” he asked, exhaling smoke with practiced ease. “Want company?” “The usual place,” I snapped, yanking on my jacket. “Alone.” Seishiro’s gaze flicked to the open buttons of my white satin shirt. I frowned—oh. The bruise on my neck. My fingers brushed the tender skin at my throat, and I channeled a weak pulse of healing energy into it. Just enough to fade the mark. My reserves were already drained from patching up the rest of Ikaru’s handiwork. Maybe I should just call it a night. I fastened the cuffs but left the top of my shirt open, collar dipping to mid-chest. Whatever. Too late to care now. If exhaustion won out, I could always crash in one of the nightclub’s rented rooms. Or faceplant into its swimming pool. Whichever ended things quicker. A quiet sigh escaped me. “Don’t die,” Seishiro offered, like he’d plucked the thought straight from my skull. I flipped him off and slammed the door behind me. Damn it. Why wouldn’t my brain just shut up? I stared at the nightclub ceiling, still unable to fill the gaping hole tearing through my chest—a black void growing larger, more unbearable by the second. Is this all life is? Just pain and fleeting pleasure, meaningless and hopeless? If not for Mother begging me to stay, I’d have checked out long ago. Why is fate so cruel? Why do some get to live so fully? How can they be happy and at peace while I— A sharp knock rattled the door. Shit. Couldn’t I get five damn minutes of peace? Always something. With a grumble, I dragged a hand through my hair and yanked the door open. A club girl stood there smirking, thrusting a brown-paper-wrapped box at me. I frowned. A book? “Someone left this for you,” she said, voice flat. The package felt heavy in my hands—plain, unmarked, layered with seals that shimmered under my touch. The outermost one cracked open at my fingertips, revealing a name inside: Katsu Masamune. “Wait—who gave you this?” I grabbed her wrist. She shrugged. “Some chick dropped it off. Said it was yours.” “What’d she look like?” “Short black hair, I guess? Plain face. Didn’t really look.” The girl rolled her eyes. “Thought she was an ex or something.” An ex? I didn’t do serious. And who even knew I frequented this place? No name on the package, just that strange inscription. Katsu Masamune. Who the hell was that? After the girl left, I closed the door and sank onto the bed. My fingers traced the seals on the package before methodically peeling them away. The wrapping paper tore open to reveal a brown leather diary—Chiyo Mitsurugi’s name embossed on the cover in my mother’s unmistakable script. My breath caught. Between the first pages lay two sealed white envelopes, each labeled like the diary—one for Tadashi, the other for Katsu. The wax seal on Katsu’s letter crumbled under my trembling fingers. With a deepening frown, I began to read my mother’s words.

Shirasu, By the time you read this, I will be gone. I pray you are safe and well. Forgive me for leaving you, for failing to shield you from Ikaru. There is so much I long to say, but I dare not—not while you remain under his watch. This diary holds the answers you seek, but let me tell you now of your younger brother. I know you despise your father for abandoning us. That hatred will only deepen when you learn I bore him another child. Yes, Shirasu—you have a brother. His name is Tadashi. Your father has raised him all these years. Twelve years separate you. I do not yet know how to free you from Ikaru, but if you choose to seek out Tadashi, perhaps together you can find a way. Please, my child, do not hate him. With all my love, Chiyo Mitsurugi.

I returned to Mother’s house—a four-hour journey from Ikaru’s residence, one of our clan’s hubs in the city. I arrived just before dawn and spent the next two hours cleaning: sweeping, mopping, wiping down windows and tables, cutting the overgrown grass, laying out the tatami mats. When I finished, I slumped in the dining room. Morning sunlight spilled across the small wooden table where Mother’s diary lay. I didn’t know if I wanted to read it. Well, that wasn’t true. I would read it eventually. I just didn’t want to face what was inside—not if it confirmed my suspicions. All that nonsense about a brother and Father. Father. Hah. The bastard who made Mother suffer. I’d stopped idolizing him the moment he abandoned us. Looking back, I couldn’t believe how desperately I’d craved his approval. He cared for me—I knew that much—but only as far as molding me into a worthy heir for the clan trials. I’d broken myself trying to meet his expectations, yet pride never once flickered in his eyes. Things improved slightly when the elders named me heir, but then he left—right when we needed him most. Mother… shattered. Yes, that was the word. Then Ikaru picked me up like discarded trash. He took every grudge he held against Father and poured it into me. I dragged a hand down my face. Those years with him were hell, but at least Ikaru was easy to please—as long as I obeyed. And now Mother expected me to forgive the man who’d stranded me here? Yeah, right. But what about this brother business? I picked up the other envelope—still sealed. A snort escaped me when I read the name. Mother always did have an absurd sense of humor. Who names their kid after food? Unless—wait. My eyes widened. Katsu. Father’s favorite breaded cutlet. Tadashi. Mother’s favorite soup. Gods. I laughed until my sides ached, wiping tears from the corners of my eyes. Even now, she still knew how to make me laugh—even if it felt bitterly hysterical. With a sigh, I tossed the envelope back onto the table. Katsu Masamune. So did that mean Father was actually from the Masamune clan? Pfft. One of the very clans we were supposed to avoid on assignments. A bunch of meddlesome know-it-alls. Not as powerful as the Fuma, sure, but like a bird’s eye chili—small but packing a punch. Their clan head… what was his name again? Mada? No. Masada. Wait—Masada Masamune. The man whose wife I’d killed ten years ago. Makoto Masamune. That mission had been a disaster. My orders were to eliminate Masada’s wife and children. Ikaru had cackled like a madman when he learned I’d taken out Makoto, but he never let me forget that I’d failed the primary objective. I only got the wife. The kids were still out there. And Ikaru didn’t tolerate failure. He made sure I’d remember that lesson for the rest of my life. I’d done my research, even found the perfect opening during some field trip for Masada’s brats. If I remembered right, his kids were named— I blinked. Tadashi. Shiro. Raito. Tadashi Masamune. Heir to the Masamune clan. Hellfire! My fingers twitched toward the envelope, itching to tear it open. But breaking the seal would just reduce it to ashes—and I’d never know what was inside. Damn it. So Mother meant that Tadashi? My brother? I grabbed fistfuls of my hair, frustration boiling over. Wasn’t that just a code name? My real name wasn’t Katsu either. How common was Tadashi, really? Maybe it was just some other poor bastard with the same name. But how could he be my brother? Tadashi was Masada’s son, the clan head’s heir. Had Mother left him on Masada’s doorstep, a discarded babe from a forgotten tale? Impossible. She wouldn’t. Or… she’d given him to Masada? But how could she even know Masada Masamune? She’d lived her entire life within the village, never venturing beyond our borders. Unless— Masada Masamune was actually my father. Damn it all. What kind of twisted game was this? I needed answers. Now. I grabbed Mother’s diary and flipped open the first page. The faint scent of jasmine—her favorite—drifted up. The garden used to overflow with those flowers. Now everything lay dead and neglected. I blinked hard against the sting in my eyes. Not now, Shirasu. Shaking my head, I started to read.

The late afternoon sun cast long shadows as I drew a slow breath, my forehead still pressed against the cool table. The faint floral scent of the paper filled my lungs again, doing little to ease the lightheadedness that clung to me. Hunger gnawed at my stomach with sharp insistence, but exhaustion weighed heavier than my need for food. How long had it been since I’d eaten? Time stretched endlessly whenever I served Ikaru—each day blurring into the next without end. The library first, then the market. A simple enough plan. Dinner would come after. Pushing myself upright, I smoothed the diary’s pages before securing it with practiced hands, reapplying the seals before tucking it safely inside my jacket. A splash of cold water on my face, a steadying breath, and I stepped out into the fading light. The village library was small, but it housed the entire archive of clan bulletins. In a place this tiny, no one bothered indexing the articles—they were simply bound by date, making any search a tedious affair. You needed the exact date to find what you were looking for. Fortunately, Mother had noted dates in her diary. The real question was how long they kept these bulletins. Fifteen years… they might’ve already been discarded. It took me fifteen minutes to find the one I needed—tucked away on a corner shelf, already marked for disposal. Lucky it was still there. I pulled it out. A faded photograph of Masada Masamune’s appointment as clan head. The details were blurred, but slowly, the image sharpened, merging with the hazy memory of my father’s face. I had to admit, he was a master of disguise. No wonder almost no one recognized him. This photo was where the whole mess began. The moment Ikaru realized the man who stole Chiyo from him was actually a Masamune infiltrator—one who then rose to lead their clan, mocking Ikaru’s ignorance from afar. No wonder his hatred for the Masamune ran so deep. With a sigh, I carefully reshelved the disturbed archives. Honestly, I couldn’t fathom why Mother thought my brother could help me escape Ikaru’s grasp. Sure, he was an outsider, beyond Ikaru’s direct control as clan head. But the kid was just a snot-nosed brat. What could he possibly do? And asking Father for help? Never. That traitorous bastard cared nothing for me or Mother—only his precious reputation. I’d sooner help Ikaru kill him. As for my brother? Hah. Probably some spoiled brat, coddled with love, clueless about real pain. I didn’t know him, owed him nothing, and sure as hell didn’t care. Killing Makoto—the woman Tadashi believed was his birth mother—wasn’t personal. Just business. And if Ikaru gave the order? I’d kill Tadashi too, without hesitation. That night, I skipped shopping and cooking altogether. Instead, I headed back to the city, determined to lose myself in taverns and nightclubs. I drank until the world blurred, desperate to wash away the image seared into my mind—a child born from my mother’s womb, raised on my father’s love. Living free—never shackled by a bastard like Ikaru. I wasn’t jealous. Really. My chest just ached—maybe trying to outdo the hollow pit in my stomach that hadn’t been filled in gods knew how long. No amount of liquor could numb it. Eventually, good old Seishiro hauled my half-conscious ass back to base and dumped me at Ikaru’s feet. The clock struck twice. Past midnight already? I’d missed Ikaru’s deadline. I bit my lip hard, stifling the hysterical laugh clawing its way up my throat. Ikaru didn’t speak. He just tore open my shirt, forced me to kneel, hands braced against the wall. The sharp whistle of his belt slicing through the air was the only warning before the first lash landed. And that was just the beginning.
