Weapons

Shiro Masamune

I woke with puffy eyes, my cheeks still damp. Sunlight poured through the latticed window, flooding the room. I bolted upright, clutching the blanket in the unfamiliar room.

“You’re awake?” Anko’s soft voice reached me. She stood by the wardrobe mirror, fastening a necklace with a white tulip pendant. Fresh from her bath, she was still dressing. “You slept soundly. I hope you’re feeling better.”

I nodded, slowly piecing together last night. Something inside me had shattered when I read Tadashi’s letter. I cried until my chest burned and my breath tore in ragged gasps. Anko held me, brought me to her room, tucked me in, and stroked my hair until I drifted off.

“I had Sadi scout the palace prison’s security so you can sneak in later to visit your father. You should search Elder Yun’s library for anything on the Mitsurugi clan records. I’ll study their Dark Arts book in the meantime,” Anko said.

I agreed with a nod.

After breakfast, I went to Elder Yun, and he revealed the entrance to his hidden library. It was concealed on a back terrace where the forest nearly swallowed the stone. A stairway led down into a secret library buried beneath the earth.

I moved through towering shelves packed with volumes, the air thick with the scent of old paper. In a dim alcove, I gathered several books and settled into a corner. The weight of History of the Ninja Clan Conflicts felt heavy with promise. I opened its brittle pages and listened to the hush of secrets.

Fifty years had passed since the war, but its beginning remained clear. Rival clans exposed the Mitsurugi’s ambition: to seize the Tsuki Kingdom using forbidden magic and creatures summoned from a dark dimension.

The Fuma Clan countered the Mitsurugi’s expansion. When the Mitsurugi struck neutral, allied clans, the Fuma absorbed them for protection, forging an alliance of light magic practitioners. The Mitsurugi did the same. Both sides hunted information relentlessly. Spies became commonplace.

The war turned when a spy within the Mitsurugi delivered critical intelligence to the Fuma. They swiftly crushed the Mitsurugi’s strongest territory and launched coordinated strikes.

The Mitsurugi were routed and scattered, but they thirsted for vengeance. They hunted the leaders of the Light Magic Alliance, including my grandfather, Daze Masamune, who fell to their blades.

Ken Masamune—my father’s older brother and Anko’s father—should have inherited leadership. But the Mitsurugi nearly annihilated Daze Masamune’s bloodline, leaving only my father and Anko alive. Father, away on a mission, and Anko, visiting her grandmother, were spared.

The Masamune elders recalled Father and named him clan leader. In the final chaos, Hojo Mitsurugi was assassinated, his inner circle purged, and their dark sorcery destroyed.

I picked up another book and leafed through it. This one went deeper into the realm of darkness, describing a parallel dimension that existed in the cracks of our reality.

It fed on human hatred and fear, growing stronger with each dark emotion. Only complex rituals and forbidden incantations could open portals to this shadowed plane.

It teemed with ancient creatures born from humanity’s deepest evils. Only those who wielded negative energy and mastered dark arts could command them, through sinister spells, dark seals, and blood sacrifices.

The bond between master and creature was an exchange: power for a price.

The book cataloged creatures that had crossed into our world, and I studied the entries closely. Shirasu’s flying octopuses—electric and teleporting—were called Doboro. Seishiro’s wolf monsters were known as Okami. Many others remained unnamed, their abilities undocumented.

I grabbed the next book. After hours in the library, I finally found something useful.

The Tsuki Kingdom safeguarded two sacred artifacts of light magic capable of severing and destroying power gained through dark arts. The first was Jomon, a white power stone kept in Yasakani Temple. Shaped like a comma—a magatama—it pulsed with immense cosmic energy that countered corruption. It was sealed in a jewel box and watched by one of the King’s most trusted priests.

The second artifact was the Kusanagi sword—a sacred blade that could wound any dark creature and sever a master’s control.

To fight darkness, the sword drew power from Jomon. Its hilt held a slot crafted specifically for the stone. The Kusanagi rested in Sumugari Temple, fiercely guarded by the Fuma clan.

Legend claimed this very blade felled Hojo Mitsurugi.

Aha. This was exactly what I needed.

I finished my research and rushed to Anko’s, where Sadi had already arrived. Anko hadn’t found anything crucial in the Mitsurugi book yet and needed more time. I shared my discovery.

“So we have to steal both the Jomon Stone and the Kusanagi Sword from the King?” Sadi asked, worried. “What if we’re caught? What else will the King do to our clan?”

I snorted. I’d been wrestling with the same fear, but there was no other way. “We can’t afford to get caught.”

“Wait… what if we told the King the truth—that we need the artifacts to fight the Mitsurugi?”

I clicked my tongue. “He already thinks we’re incompetent for shattering the Mirai. Why would he trust us with the kingdom’s remaining artifacts?”

“Ugh, you’re right. So which one first?”

Anko shook her head. “The Kusanagi can wait. The Fuma seal masters at Sumugari Temple will be hard to bypass. You should scout it first.”

“I’ll handle that,” Sadi said, then briefed us on the palace prison. “I don’t think you’ll see your father yet, Shiro. He’s in maximum-security isolation—no contact, not even with guards. Every six months, the palace temple holds a purification ritual for prisoners. You might sneak in as a temple maiden then, but you’d have to wait half a year.”

I closed my eyes and nodded. “We can’t wait for my father’s instructions. We move forward with what we know. I’ll go to Yasakani Temple first. The Kusanagi is useless without the stone.”

They agreed.

That afternoon, I headed to Yasakani Temple. Smiling monks greeted me, and after a quick prayer, I followed one into an underground chamber.

A labyrinth stretched out before me, mossy walls rising under the glow of faint, bioluminescent vines. A heavy silence pooled in the shadows. Knife in hand, I slipped into the maze.

At the center, eight doors stood etched with ancient symbols. I checked each for a seal’s aura, finding a powerful pulse behind the seventh.

I focused my energy, tracing the intricate symbol carved into the door. A soft click, and it swung open. Inside, a jewel box rested on a slender pedestal. I lifted it carefully.

I had barely exhaled when a click echoed from a pillar. Instinct took over. I flipped back as a hail of arrows shredded the spot where I’d stood.

Iron bars slammed down, sealing the doorway. I hurled myself into a roll and slid clear as they clanged shut. Distant bells erupted. Clutching the jewel box, I darted through the labyrinth, weaving between pillars and ducking behind walls to evade the charging guards.

I reached Anko’s as evening fell. Sadi was already there.

“Hey, this is the jewel box Tadashi and I stole from Nobuga!” Sadi exclaimed, examining it.

Anko snatched it, her face darkening. “Was this already opened?” she asked, pointing to a broken seal and pry marks.

Sadi stared, speechless.

“Tadashi took the box from Kitoru, but we were rushed, and I was focused on the seal-breaking ritual. I didn’t examine it closely and returned it to the clan right away,” he said.

Anko opened the box, revealing neatly arranged jewelry. I spotted a white magatama, but after a careful check, Anko dismissed it as an ordinary power stone—its energy faint and unremarkable.

“This is a replica,” she said. “Maybe the monks only verified the stone’s presence and didn’t test its energy.”

Sadi cursed. “Was the real stone stolen after we returned the box?”

“You reported the Mitsurugi clan was after it,” I said. “Could they have intercepted it on the way to Yasakani?”

Sadi shook his head. “The guards and priests reported no attacks or disturbances. Unless there was a Mitsurugi infiltrator among them.”

I bit my lip. Possible.

“But why would the Mitsurugi want this box? That stone counters dark arts, right?” Sadi scratched his head.

“To destroy it,” Anko said. “Maybe Ikaru realized it threatened him and tried to eliminate it.”

Silence fell. If the Jomon stone was gone, our chances against the Mitsurugi plummeted.

“Would anyone else want the stone?” I pressed. “Maybe it was already fake when you first got the box.”

Sadi opened his mouth, then hesitated, his expression hardening.

“That damned scoundrel. Nobuga must’ve taken it first.” He shot to his feet, grabbed his gloves, and headed for the door. “No wonder Kitoru gave up the location so easily. They had nothing to lose if we took it. And I let them walk away afterward.”

I frowned. “Your report said they escaped.”

Sadi winced, flushing. “A minor detail.”

“Sadi,” I growled. Why did Sadi and Tadashi always keep secrets?

“I let him go on one condition, okay? Tadashi needed Nobuga’s help to break my seal.”

“Why?”

Sadi shrugged. “Because Nobuga is actually my father’s twin. His real name is Akira. Tadashi said the ritual needed Akira’s blood.”

My head throbbed. Neither of them had mentioned this in their reports. No wonder the elders forbade Sadi and Tadashi from working together.

“Whatever. The important thing is finding them,” Sadi said, grabbing his jacket and nodding to Anko.

I followed. “Are they still at the same place? Nobuga’s slippery. He’s probably fled.”

Sadi scoffed. “Relax. I got their base location and network sites last time. We hit them now.”
