Crossroads

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

T he flow of time deposited Tadashi in a familiar room—Chiyo’s house, shrouded in deep night. He watched as his father, Masada, stood motionless over Shirasu’s sleeping form. Though only eight, Shirasu already showed remarkable promise. Pride swelled fiercely in Masada’s heart, but it was swiftly eclipsed by a cold ache.

The time had come. He had been summoned back to his own clan.

Masada had never forgotten his duty; he had simply prayed for the chance to secure his family’s freedom from the Mitsurugi Clan before the recall came. But the political landscape had imploded into devastating war. His entire immediate family—Daze, Ken—had been killed. The Masamune Clan was now calling him home to lead.

He felt no lust for power. Surely someone from Daze’s rival faction craved the position, but they too must have perished, or the elders deemed them unfit. Masada had carefully avoided politics, yet his formidable reputation as a successful merchant and diplomat made him the only viable candidate left. He possessed the strategic mind and charisma the shattered clan needed to survive. The elders had laid the burden upon him, and he could not refuse.

Accepting this mantle meant the swift, surgical amputation of his old life. The responsibilities of a clan leader were absolute. His every breath would be scrutinized; his past, relentlessly dissected for weaknesses. If he brought Chiyo and Shirasu with him, they would become his most catastrophic vulnerability. No Masamune would accept a wife and son from the very clan that had massacred so many of their own. His family would be tormented, and Masada’s leadership doomed before it began.

He had to leave them behind.

The decision tore through him like a blade.

He shut his eyes, wrestling his feelings into submission. He had provided what he could. Chiyo had enough savings to live comfortably. Tonight he would eliminate Hojo, the current clan head of the Mitsurugi, neutralizing a direct threat to Shirasu’s future. With Hojo gone, Shirasu should ascend to clan leadership. However, his son was still a child; the elders would likely appoint a temporary regent—perhaps Ikaru, or another political opportunist.

He silently prayed for his wife and son’s safety. Masada knelt beside the boy and gently stroked Shirasu’s hair. The child smiled faintly in his sleep, a portrait of innocence. Masada placed a finely crafted short sword, an heirloom from his merchant days, beside the bed.

Forgive me, son. I pray you grow into a strong, independent man who can protect your mother.

He activated the subtle energy lines within Shirasu, reinforcing his son’s mental defenses—a rare, hidden skill Masada possessed. He had the ability to wipe himself from their minds entirely, but he rejected the finality of it. Let them remember him as their father. If they grew to hate him for the abandonment, it was a price he would pay. Their safety was the only currency that mattered.

His work was done. Masada rose, turned, and walked away, his back impossibly straight, his heart left in ruins on the floor.

Tadashi bit his lip until he tasted blood, wiping his wet cheeks as he watched his father retreat. A devastating fracture tore through Masada’s soul, an agonizing resolve that seared itself into Tadashi—the bitter price of a man forced to sever his deepest ties upon the altar of duty.

Three Years Later

Masada sat in a quiet tavern on the edge of town, a secluded spot he reserved for discreet meetings. He was waiting for a business contact, but the figure who approached his table was the last person he expected to see.

Chiyo.

He was stunned. His wife was even more beautiful than he remembered. Her eyes glistened with unshed tears. Masada’s throat constricted. How had she found him? How long had she known his identity? Was she here to demand his return?

“Chiyo,” he whispered, his voice catching.

She didn’t throw accusations. Instead, she collapsed against him, her arms wrapping around his neck. Her body shook with silent sobs as she whispered his real name and the alias he wore like armor. Masada closed his eyes and breathed deeply. He kissed her forehead and stroked her hair. He knew he was endangering them both, but he couldn’t pull away. He quickly escorted her to a private room at a nearby inn.

Inside, Chiyo managed a fragile calm. “I learned who you were months after you left,” she said softly, clutching her teacup. “I’ve been following your life, Masada.” A flicker of sadness crossed her face. “I know your position. I know you can’t acknowledge me. And I know about your marriage to Makoto.”

Masada could only stare at the table. Makoto had found her way into the gaps of his broken life and helped him rebuild a semblance of strength. The love he felt for her was different from what he carried for Chiyo—but it was love all the same, two flames burning with equal intensity.

Chiyo’s hand crossed the table, her fingers closing around his. “I will always love you. You will always be Shirasu’s father.” Her voice cracked. “I only ask one thing—for our son. The elders gave Ikaru temporary command. You know his cruelty. This isn’t training; it’s daily torture. He forces Shirasu to heal himself just to suffer again. I can’t get to him. He’s utterly alone.”

Her grip tightened desperately. “Please, Masada. You lead a clan now. You have the power to strike the Mitsurugi. Save him. Take him away. I don’t care what becomes of me. Just save our son.”

She broke completely, her grief pouring out. Masada held her as her pain carved canyons of sorrow through him. He had failed. Ikaru’s viciousness was no secret. But attacking the Mitsurugi Clan? The Masamune were still wounded from war—such an act would be suicide.

He had entrusted Seishiro to watch over Shirasu, but deep down he knew no one could truly shield his son from Ikaru. All he could do was cling to the hope that Shirasu would endure until he could engineer the Mitsurugi Clan’s destruction.

He had no real solution for Chiyo, only comfort. He gave her a moment of safety, a sliver of hope. That night, he lost himself in his wife. By morning, her panic had subsided. They parted ways, and Masada gave her a secure line of communication.

He never expected the message that followed. Chiyo was pregnant. Her plea was desperate: Masada must take their second child and raise it.

He understood her terror. She couldn’t bear another child suffering Shirasu’s fate. Nor could he stomach the thought of Chiyo—pregnant and shunned—enduring more agony. But what could he do? Bringing the child home to Makoto would shatter his marriage and his authority as clan leader.

Entrust the baby to a business partner? Too great a risk. Abandon it at an orphanage? The very idea sickened him.

Then, a cold, pragmatic idea took shape. A servant. A trusted, high-ranking servant dedicated to the clan leader could raise a child within the household. It wasn’t unheard of. It would allow Masada to provide, to watch over the child from a distance. It was a vastly better fate than the streets.

It was a bitter compromise, but it was a workable plan. Masada agreed to Chiyo’s request. But as it turned out, the final, unforeseen surprise was yet to arrive.
