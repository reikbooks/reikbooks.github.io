Arrival

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

T adashi’s arrival came at the worst possible moment for Masada.

He had already arranged for a discreet handmaiden to care for Chiyo’s child, intending to finalize the plan only once the baby was safely delivered. But fate intervened, dropping the baby into the middle of preparations for Makoto’s own imminent birth. Masada was entirely unprepared for the timing.

Chiyo had entrusted their infant son to a midwife, who delivered the baby to Masada. Moments later, news arrived: Makoto had been rushed to the hospital with life-threatening complications from her home birth. With no time to process Chiyo’s son, Masada instructed the midwife to wait.

Then came the devastating blow: Makoto had miscarried.

Masada stood frozen, a moment of physical shock. Makoto, who had waited desperately for this child, had lost their baby? His mind, wired for strategy, raced. A catastrophe, yes, but also a split-second opportunity. A desperate, decisive plan formed.

He immediately summoned the midwife and handed the infant to Makoto’s doctor. Makoto, still half-conscious after fighting for her life, was told the baby was hers. Her own stillborn child was secretly buried by the midwife. Chiyo’s baby, a tiny two-week-old, instinctively latched on and nursed from Makoto. In her grief and exhaustion, she never suspected the deception.

Masada’s audacious plan worked seamlessly. To guarantee its secrecy, he sealed the memories of everyone involved—the midwife, the doctor, the assistant. No one would ever know that Makoto had miscarried or that the child she welcomed home was not her own.

A wave of intense relief washed over Masada. He had resolved one crisis. He could now raise this boy as his son. Chiyo’s request, Makoto’s need for healing, and his own duty were all fulfilled in one cruel stroke.

He gave the child the name Chiyo had requested: Tadashi Masamune.

Officially, the boy was a Masamune, not a Mitsurugi. He was the only child with Mitsurugi blood born free from Ikaru’s seal of obedience. Masada felt a surging sense of gratitude.

That night, while Makoto slept in exhaustion, Masada took his turn holding his son. He carefully activated Tadashi’s innate seal paths and reinforced his mental defenses, a precaution born of a father’s fear that his son’s true heritage might someday become a devastating liability. Tadashi’s mind readily accepted the seals. When he finished, Masada smiled, a wave of affection washing over him as he kissed his son’s forehead.

Welcome home, son. I’m sorry you had to be separated from your mother. From now on, I will do my best to take care of you.

When news of Chiyo’s death reached him, a piece of Masada’s soul was buried with her. After mourning and finally making peace with his grief, Makoto became his anchor. But when she, too, was killed, Masada’s world shattered into dust scattered by a storm.

He knew the culprit. Ikaru. He orchestrated a subtle, deniable assassination through one of his men. It was revenge for Masada stealing Chiyo, a cruel taunt that proved Masada could never truly escape. Ikaru had discovered Masada’s secret identity and killed Makoto to drive the point home.

Masada seethed with a cold, helpless fury. He was furious at himself for failing to protect his family. Angry that he had not yet succeeded in forging Tadashi into a strong enough shield. And angry, in a dark, irrational corner of his heart, at Tadashi himself for failing to protect his mother, for not being vigilant.

He stopped the thought instantly, disgusted with himself. Tadashi was only a child, and Masada loved him. He could not crush the boy. Tadashi was already pale and trembling with guilt after Masada questioned him. Anko had warned him that Shiro blamed Tadashi for Makoto’s death; if Masada joined in, the boy would break entirely. He reminded himself this over and over, but the fresh wound of loss demanded an outlet. He craved violence.

One morning, after Shiro and Raito had left into hiding with Makoto’s mother, Masada’s gaze landed on Tadashi, who was quietly trying to make breakfast. The boy flinched under the weight of his father’s sheer rage but held his ground, as if bracing for a strike. Masada had to physically turn away, fiercely suppressing the primal urge to hit. He recognized the impulse—it was the malignant ghost of Daze’s irrational beatings.

No. I cannot become Daze. I will not hit my son. Chiyo entrusted him to me. I am a father who protects his son.

The knot of rage tightened further in his chest. On pure reflex, Masada bolted to his feet and fled the house, his mind a wasteland. He couldn’t look at Tadashi, who looked so much like Chiyo and reminded him of Makoto’s death. He had to escape before he committed a sin he could never forgive.

He went into the forest to hunt, not for sport, but to unleash his destructive instincts. The violence did not calm him; it only widened the hole in his heart. He realized this was not the answer. This emotional reaction was not how he solved problems.

He returned to the main hall, washed his face, meditated, and then immersed himself in the most challenging administrative puzzle he could find—a months-old intelligence leak in a conflict zone. The work was endless, and that suited him perfectly. At his core, Masada was a workaholic.

No one was waiting at home. Shiro and Raito were safe. Tadashi was safe. A little lonely, perhaps, but that was acceptable. The boy needed to learn resilience, just as Masada had been forced to learn. Daze’s neglect had, after all, forged Masada’s relentless self-reliance. Masada wanted the same for Tadashi—the ability to stand on his own feet.

He will be fine. He decided to monitor him from a distance, giving him just enough attention to keep the thread of hope alive. A tiny voice of protest reminded him that this was a cruel, manipulative method to use on his own son, but it was a method that had never failed him. Besides, Tadashi was the heir. He needed to be forged from steel. Daze’s heavy-handed approach with Ken had worked. It was the only frame of reference Masada possessed.

So he worked, and worked, and kept working, until everything went numb and he couldn’t feel a thing.

A blinding white light slammed into Tadashi’s mind. He let out a choked cry, dropping to his knees and clutching his head as if he’d been thrown headfirst into a stone wall.

The world dissolved. He was violently ripped from Masada’s past and thrown back into the dark fortress, the dead power stone still clenched in his hand. A rough hand instantly yanked him backward by the shoulder.

Tadashi spun around, shock freezing his muscles. The monstrous caveman loomed over him, grinning wide. Tadashi was too slow. The creature’s massive fist connected with his face, sending him sprawling. Blood streamed from his temple. The monster raised a massive, spiked club.

Tadashi scrambled away just as the club smashed into the floor, cracking the stone. On pure instinct, Tadashi snatched the monster’s wrist, yanked it forward, and drove his fist into its stomach. It was like hitting concrete. The monster didn’t even flinch. It grabbed Tadashi’s hand and swatted him away like a fly. Tadashi slammed into the far wall, pain exploding through his back.

Gasping for breath, blinking away the dizziness and blood, Tadashi crawled onto his feet and put distance between them. The creature had black eyes, a demonic grin, and a brutish face that twisted Tadashi’s memory of Daze.

Tadashi pressed against the wall, searching for an escape. There was none. The only exit led back through the door he’d entered.

The monster charged. Tadashi forced his weakened body to move. Left. The club smashed into the wall beside him. He scurried away, his instincts screaming that he was no match for this brute. He had no weapon, no energy for seals.

At the first chance, he ducked under a swing and darted past the monster’s side, sprinting for the door. A whip cracked through the air, striking his back. Tadashi cried out, feeling the sharp tip tear his skin.

What are you doing? Get up! Masada’s voice from the bandit fight echoed in his head.

The monster retracted the whip. Tadashi rolled as the weapon slammed into the floor, leaving a deep gash. He staggered up, dodging another lash, and raced into the labyrinthine corridor beyond. The black stone walls groaned around him. He ran, breath ragged, chest tight.

He reached a junction. To the left, a dark wooden door. To the right, a bright, open doorway with the sound of trickling water. But on the left door, a smear of fresh blood stained the wood.

Father?

Tadashi hesitated. He wanted to escape, but he had come here to find his father. That blood meant Masada was wounded. Ignoring his own agony, Tadashi chose the left path.

Hold on, he pleaded with himself. It’s not time to rest. I have to find Father.

The wooden door required another blood seal, slicing his palm like a razor. He hissed, biting his lip, and shoved his way in. A small corridor stretched before him, its walls covered in carved statues of tormented, screaming figures. The terrifying sight only strengthened his resolve. The monster’s guttural grunts grew closer. Tadashi ran faster.

Strange moans echoed, making his head throb. The corridor darkened; the air grew thin. Tadashi’s chest constricted painfully. Finally, he reached a pitch-black chamber with a glowing white portal frame at its center, currently inactive. Suddenly, the whip lashed his back again. He collapsed, a strangled cry caught in his raw throat. His back felt flayed. He tried to crawl away, but the whip struck once more.

“Aah!”

He fell flat, coughing violently, blood spraying from his mouth. He curled into a ball, tears finally breaking free. His ragged breaths were loud in the silence.

Stop, please…

A sob choked him. All he wanted was to save his father. Why couldn’t he do anything right?

I’m sorry, Father….

His vision blurred. The monster stood over him. Tadashi whimpered, trying futilely to crawl away, his body shaking uncontrollably. The creature wouldn’t let him escape. A massive hand seized his hair and yanked his head back violently.
