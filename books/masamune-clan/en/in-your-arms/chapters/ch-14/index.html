Doubles

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

M asada stepped outside, the main hall’s heavy door closing behind him. He took a deep, steadying breath of the cool air, but the sharp edge of anger still pulsed in his temples. He had suspended the clan’s administrative duties, assigning Shiro to oversee the wounded and the search for the two fugitives. Shirasu’s actions had been a direct assault, and Tadashi’s complicity was a glaring failure.

His mind replayed the scene. Tadashi’s shock had been genuine; he clearly hadn’t expected Shirasu. But then Shirasu had whispered something, and Tadashi had followed without a moment of hesitation. If Shirasu merely wanted to talk, why the public spectacle? It had to be urgent. Something important enough to risk everything.

Following the trail of broken twigs and disturbed undergrowth, Masada made his way toward the river behind the main hall. The path led him to the edge of the Black Forest. He paused, scanning the dense trees, when a sharp SNAP echoed from the shadows.

Masada spun, his shortsword flashing into his hand. Silence. Then, a figure emerged from the trees.

Tadashi.

His clothes were disheveled, his hair a mess. A trail of fresh blood trickled from his temple. His face was ghostly pale, his eyes wide and unfocused. He stared at Masada, his breath coming in ragged gasps.

“F-Father?” he rasped.

Masada’s grip tightened on his sword. He slowly lowered it, taking a cautious step forward. “What happened? What were you doing with Shirasu?”

Tadashi blinked, struggling to focus. “I… Shirasu… he had important information,” he mumbled, shaking his head as if to clear it. “He didn’t mean to attack the clan! We were hiding… the men were chasing us…”

Masada’s frown deepened. “And?”

“A new enemy… they’re targeting the King. Setting a trap. Shirasu didn’t get to finish. We were attacked.”

“By whom?”

“I don’t know.” Tadashi panted, shaking his head again. “Men in black, masks. They hit me. When I woke up, Shirasu was gone. We have to find him.”

Masada studied his son, a cold dread seeping into his chest. Tadashi’s condition was alarming, but his carelessness was infuriating. How could he be so easily ambushed? And Shirasu… after all this time, why now? Why a warning?

The threat to the King, however vague, could not be ignored. He had to follow the lead. “Very well,” Masada said, his voice clipped. “Show me where you were attacked. There might be clues.”

A slow, unsettling smile spread across Tadashi’s face. “This way, Father.”

Masada followed, his senses on high alert. The forest grew unnaturally silent, the air thick and heavy. He watched Tadashi’s back. The boy’s steps were suddenly sure, his earlier disorientation completely gone. A cold knot tightened in Masada’s stomach.

Something was deeply wrong.

What was Tadashi hiding?

“This is so annoying,” Shirasu grumbled, kicking a loose stone.

When Seishiro had warned him he’d lose most of his powers inside Dorimu’s world, Shirasu had just shrugged it off. What choice did he have? But experiencing it was a different kind of torture. Without his healing and teleportation, their progress was agonizingly slow.

“So, how do we break out of this?” Tadashi asked, stepping over a thick, gnarled root.

“We find the version of Dorimu that’s running this show and kill it,” Shirasu said, brushing a low-hanging branch aside. “That should open a portal. But we have to be quick—it’ll just respawn as someone else if we’re too slow.”

“How many times can it do that?”

“Indefinitely, as long as its real body is still alive in the cave. Seishiro’s waiting to finish it off the moment we’re clear.”

Tadashi frowned. “Why not just kill it in the real world now?”

“Because if the dream world collapses while you’re still in it, you die,” Shirasu explained patiently. “So no heroic suicide attempts, got it? No ‘accidentally’ falling off cliffs. If you die in here, you die for real.”

“What about you?”

“I can get out anytime using this,” Shirasu said, tapping the ancient medallion around his neck. “But it’s a one-way ticket. Once I’m out, I can’t get back in.”

Tadashi fell silent for a moment, processing. “So Dorimu can imitate anyone it’s captured?”

“Yeah, living or dead. We found some of Seishiro’s missing men in the cave, along with a bunch of others.”

“So people like Shiro and Raito here… they’re just constructs? Part of the dream?”

“Basically. They’re just props, acting based on your memories. They’re not real.”

Tadashi’s questions were relentless. Could Dorimu hear them? Control the environment? Shirasu finally threw his hands up in mock exasperation. “Look, next time I’ll get a Ph.D. in dream-world entomology before I come to rescue you, okay? I’m winging it based on what Seishiro told me.”

A small smile touched Tadashi’s lips. “It’s a good theory. You pass.”

Shirasu laughed and punched his brother’s shoulder playfully.

“How have you been?” Tadashi asked quietly, his voice softening. “Since I left?”

Shirasu shrugged. “Drank the taverns dry, moped for a day. Seishiro’s keeping me busy. I was planning on being a permanent nuisance until he kicked me out.” He glanced at Tadashi. “What about you? Any panic attacks? Has… has the memory seal been a problem?”

Tadashi’s smile vanished. He looked away, unable to answer. Shirasu immediately regretted asking.

“Well, if you need—”

“Well, well… Look what we have here.”

Shirasu spun around, sword already in hand. His blood ran cold. That voice. It couldn’t be.

Tadashi froze beside him, face pale. Shirasu instinctively moved, putting himself between his brother and the figure emerging from the trees.

“Strolling about without my permission, Shirasu? How bold.” The man stepped into a shaft of light, a black sword scraping the ground. Long white hair, a piercing gaze that could strip flesh from bone.

Ikaru.

A cold smirk twisted his lips as his eyes landed on Tadashi. “Hello, Tadashi. Kneel over there and wait your turn.”

“Bastard!” Shirasu snarled. “Go, Tadashi! Find Masada! I’ll catch up!” Without waiting, he lunged at Ikaru.

Their blades met with a deafening clash. Ikaru parried with infuriating ease. “Still weak, Shirasu. Have you forgotten all my lessons?”

“Shut up! You’re not him! You’re a pathetic imitation!” Shirasu hissed, twisting his sword for a counterattack.

Ikaru laughed, a hollow, cruel sound. “You should never steal from a hungry tiger. I’m going to tear your soul apart.”

Tadashi stumbled, his breath catching in his throat. He leaned against a tree trunk, his heart hammering. The primal fear sent a cold shock through his body. His body remembered the terror, even if Masada’s seal kept the specifics locked away.

He had to find his father. But where? The main hall was on lockdown. Masada would be searching for him. It was a needle in a haystack.

An idea, desperate and dangerous, sparked. The memory seal itself. He knew its design from the Mitsurugi texts. He could use it as a crude tracking beacon, but activating it would break the seal. The memories would come flooding back.

Do you really want to remember? a voice whispered. Maybe it’s better not to know.

But he had no choice. This was for his father.

Tadashi sank to the ground, crossing his legs. He closed his eyes, focusing inward, finding the complex knot of energy that was Masada’s seal. Taking a deep, shuddering breath, he pulled on the seal and reversed its flow.

Agony exploded in his skull. He cried out, crumpling to the forest floor, clutching his head as if it might split open. It felt like a hot iron driving into his brain. He curled into a ball, tears streaming down his face as he fought the urge to scream.

Somehow, he forced himself to his feet. Staggering, leaning on trees for support, he followed the painful, pulsing signal. By the time he reached a small wooden house on the forest’s edge, he was gasping, his vision blurry. Blood dripped from his nose. He wiped it away with a trembling hand.

Suddenly, a hand grabbed his shoulder and yanked him back. A fist slammed into his jaw. Stars exploded in his vision. As he stumbled, he saw his attacker’s face.

His own face.

Dorimu!

Tadashi scrambled backward, but the doppelgänger pressed the attack. They traded blows, but the pain in Tadashi’s head was debilitating. He was being driven back, defenseless.

Just then, the door to the wooden house creaked open. Masada stepped out, his eyes widening in shock at the sight of the two identical Tadashis.

In that instant, the memory seal in Tadashi’s mind shattered completely.

The flood of locked memories—the pain, the fear, the betrayal—hit him with the force of a physical blow. Dorimu seized the opening, landing a vicious kick to his chest that sent him flying into a tree trunk. Darkness swallowed Tadashi’s consciousness before he could even cry out.
