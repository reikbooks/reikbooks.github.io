Underworld

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

S eishiro’s missing men were still a day’s ride away. Seishiro was eager to press on and finish the job, but Shirasu insisted they stop for lunch.

“I’m not hungry,” Seishiro grumbled, wrinkling his nose.

“Well, I am,” Shirasu retorted, pulling his horse alongside his friend’s.

Seishiro snorted. “You’re not hungry. You just want to show off that ridiculously elaborate bento you made.”

“Hey, I put serious effort into this! The least you could do is say, ‘Wow, Shirasu, what a great new hobby! You’re so talented!’”

Seishiro rolled his eyes but reined in his horse by the shore of a vast, clear lake. Shirasu dismounted and retrieved their provisions. He handed a lunchbox to Seishiro, who lifted the lid and stared at the contents.

“Well?” Shirasu prompted, grinning.

“It looks like a kindergartner made it,” Seishiro deadpanned.

“Damn you!” Shirasu laughed, punching his arm lightly.

They were halfway through the meal when a group of bandits emerged from the treeline, surrounding them. Shirasu’s mood plummeted. Couldn’t they have waited until I finished eating?

Seishiro merely raised an eyebrow, staying put as he casually finished a mouthful of rice. Shirasu deflected a senbon needle aimed at his friend and shot him a glare.

Seriously? Are you trying to get killed?

Seishiro just grinned and gave him a thumbs-up. Enjoy the fight!

Ugh! Shirasu tossed his lunchbox aside and sprang to his feet, drawing his shortsword. He met the bandits’ attack head-on. Maybe this wasn’t so bad—he’d been itching for a fight anyway. The bandits fell swiftly, the surviving few scrambling to retreat.

Shirasu pursued them to where they’d tethered their mounts. There, he found something that first chilled his blood, then made it boil: Masada’s horse was tied among the bandits’ own, its saddlebags still attached. Masada would never abandon his horse willingly. Something terrible had happened.

That bastard! Masada was utterly unreliable. Shirasu slammed his fist into a nearby tree trunk. Tadashi… is he all right?

He exhaled sharply. His resolve to stop caring about Tadashi and Masada was completely dependent on Tadashi being safe. If Tadashi was hurt, there was no way he could stand by.

Shirasu cornered one of the fleeing bandits. After a brief, forceful interrogation about what happened, he immediately activated the tracking seal he had secretly placed on Tadashi back when his brother was still in a coma. He and Seishiro followed the rapidly strengthening trail deep into an untraveled part of the forest.

“Sei! Look!” The seal’s signal abruptly terminated at the mouth of a remote, dark cave. At its entrance, two figures lay motionless.

Masada awoke at the edge of a dense forest. Behind him, the cave’s mouth was barely visible through a thick, unnatural fog. He sat up with a jolt, frowning. What had happened? He’d been fighting snakes in the cave…

Tadashi!

He scrambled to his feet, ignoring the aches protesting through his body. A sharp throb came from his leg. He looked down to see a red, swollen snakebite. He didn’t remember the bite, but a smaller snake must have struck during the fight with the larger one. His vision was slightly blurred, his head spinning. Was the venom still active? If it was lethal, why was he conscious? Why was he outside the cave? Where was Tadashi?

Tadashi would never have left him lying there. He would have stayed or tried to move him, though Masada knew his son was in no condition for that. Had Tadashi gone for help? Or was he still inside? If so, who had brought Masada out here?

He took a deep breath and made his choice. He couldn’t leave Tadashi alone in that snake-infested cave. He staggered toward its entrance. The distance should have been short, but the cave never seemed to get any closer, receding with every step. The trees changed, yet a powerful déjà vu gripped him, as if he were walking in place, seeing the same scenery on a repeating loop.

Something mystical was preventing his return.

Masada closed his eyes, reaching out with his senses. Darkness. Void. No aura, no life. It was like standing in a void. He was trapped, and he recognized the faint, chilling signature of Mitsurugi dark arts within the fog.

Was he in the Dimension of Darkness? He had only seen it from afar—a portal opened by Ikaru—a place of absolute black. This place, with its hazy, overcast sky and the distant rumble of thunder, felt disturbingly real, yet profoundly wrong.

He clenched his fists and followed a narrow path. The forest thickened; the air grew heavy and difficult to breathe. He reached a fork. The path to the right led into darker, tangled woods, where roots and leaves seemed to writhe and groan. A thick root shot out and wrapped around his leg.

He cried out as a dual sensation overwhelmed him: a suffocating pressure, yet also a blissful, weightless release from all burdens. A cloud enveloped his mind, setting him adrift. A faint voice called out.

Father…

Masada gasped, a sharp pain blooming in his chest. Panic surged. He slashed at the root with his shortsword, drawing a dark sap like blood. He fell to his knees, panting. Scrambling up, he hurried down the left path. A glow in the distance, followed by a rumbling, promised an escape.

The path opened onto a barren, desert-like plain. Spears and arrows lay scattered everywhere, some half-buried. Tattered armor clung to the ground. In the center, a sword was thrust into the earth, a cloth strip fluttering from its hilt. A fierce sandstorm raged. Behind it, a path led onward.

Masada noted the wind direction, tore a strip from his sleeve to cover his head, and pushed forward. The storm immediately threw him back. Grit stung his eyes. Wincing, he rose and hunched low, fighting for every step. As he neared the sword, he recognized it—the clan’s ancient heirloom, the blade lost and retrieved, the one entrusted to Tadashi as successor. It had been sealed in the vault ever since. Tadashi certainly hadn’t brought it.

Masada tried to pull it free. It didn’t budge, cemented in place. The storm howled. His vision darkened. He pushed on, step by agonizing step, until he finally emerged on the other side. His body ached all over, gritty with sand. The storm raged behind him, but here, the air was still. He took a relieved breath and continued.

The path led down into a valley dominated by a vast, dark swamp. The surrounding trees were withered and dead. A narrow trail cut through the mire. Masada stepped onto it and immediately sank, the deep bog swallowing him. He gasped, swallowing brackish, salty water, coughing and choking. The memory of Tadashi drowning in the river surged back, a wave of sorrow seizing his chest.

He swam frantically to the surface, breaking through the black water around the gnarled roots. Shaking, he followed the path through the dead forest. Something was fundamentally wrong here.

The path narrowed further, eventually opening to a clear, deep river. Masada swam across. Strangely, there was no sound—no lapping water, no splashing, not even the sound of his own breathing. He tried to clear his throat; silence. The soundless river washed the swamp’s filth from his body.

On the far bank, a line of stone warrior statues stood guard. Their mouths were gagged, their expressions tormented. Masada reached out and touched one. Water streamed down its stone cheeks like tears. Suddenly, he remembered Tadashi’s face from long ago, just before he placed the speech seal. Tadashi had been crying, looking at him with pleading eyes.

Please… Help me. Father…

Masada drew a shuddering breath, shaking his head to dispel the memory. He hurried on. A field of dry grass stretched to the foot of two steep, towering mountains. An urge to avoid them, to go around, seized him.

Something moved behind the trees. Masada froze. A small child, translucent and glowing with a faint white light, stood at the mountain’s base, looking up at the climbing path. The boy turned his head slightly, watching Masada from the corner of his eye.

Masada’s breath caught. That look… It was Tadashi . He didn’t know how he knew, but he was certain. The boy—a ghost of Tadashi as a child.

Was it a trick? A deception?

The apparition turned and walked away, vanishing into the shadows. Masada’s voice stuck in his throat. He wanted to call out, but the ghost was gone. Something squeezed his heart. He had to follow. He was looking for Tadashi, wasn’t he? But this felt cold, alien. A world of spirits? Did it mean… they were both already dead?

A rumble echoed in the sky. The fog thickened. The air turned frigid. He had no other choice.

Masada studied the mountain. A light flickered at the summit, beckoning. He began the arduous climb. The path was winding and treacherous. His breath grew ragged, his leg throbbing, his energy nearly spent. Just a little more. He could see the glow.

He arrived at the ruins of an ancient temple hidden behind a dense canopy. The interior was destroyed, as if by cannonfire or an earthquake. Halls stood empty, doors cracked and torn from their hinges. Masada traced the carvings on the broken stone gate. The archway was shattered. On the left, inscribed was Mitsurugi . On the right, Masamune .

A realization, vast and terrifying, clicked into place. Something familiar, something he had touched and manipulated time and time again. He stood motionless, the final pieces connecting.

The entangling forest. The storm-battered desert with its immovable sword. The suffocating swamp with its dead trees. The silent river with its weeping statues. The two mountains, a heavy burden on a pair of shoulders.

He had never seen this world before, but he recognized its core essence. He had felt it every time he opened Tadashi’s energy paths, every time he reinforced the seals within his son’s mind.

He knew where he was.

This world… was Tadashi’s subconscious.
