Love

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

T he next time Tadashi shifted through time, he arrived at a bustling market. It was winter. Fifteen-year-old Masada was arranging his wares at a stall. Outwardly, his expression gave nothing away, but Tadashi saw the chill that gripped him.

Tadashi immediately took his father’s hand, and Masada’s thoughts and feelings flooded his mind.

Masada had traveled to neighboring kingdoms to source goods for his business. It had started out profitable—many people were eager for imports but couldn’t travel themselves. But recently, he’d been cheated in the Fumo kingdom. He’d lost nearly all his capital.

He’d returned home to sell off his remaining inventory, only to walk straight into a clan crisis. Daze was facing political opposition. His rivals were circling, and the family had been placed under scrutiny. Daze, who had no idea where Masada had gone, was predictably furious. Masada had only informed his mother.

Masada sat on the stool behind his stall and winced. His entire back ached. It wasn’t the first time Daze had whipped him, but he’d become adept at avoiding his father’s wrath.

He’d graduated from the academy’s accelerated class with top marks, followed orders meticulously, spoke only when spoken to, stayed out of clan politics, and was polite to everyone—especially those officials whose favor he needed to cultivate. He avoided Daze’s opponents and kept his head down.

It wasn’t that difficult. Daze paid little attention to Masada anyway, rarely assigning him important missions. Unlike Ken, Masada wasn’t micromanaged. He’d grown comfortable with that freedom—perhaps too comfortable, failing to notice the shifting political climate.

It had been years since his last severe punishment, which made this one cut deeper.

Masada sighed, rubbing his face. The blizzard had kept customers away. He hadn’t sold anything since yesterday, and now he couldn’t even go home. Daze had thrown him out.

Daze wouldn’t publicly disown him—that would be the scandal his enemies were waiting for—but he’d quietly revoked all of Masada’s privileges as the clan leader’s son. Not that Masada had ever used them.

He shouldn’t have been surprised. Daze’s emotions often exploded like this. Masada hated every insult, every time his father called him a stupid, useless child who couldn’t do anything right.

Still, he hadn’t expected Daze to make such a drastic decision just because Masada had been gone a few days without permission—even though he’d completed all his missions and filed for leave.

No point dwelling on it. What was done was done. He had to move forward.

Besides, he should be happy, shouldn’t he? No more daily confrontations with Daze. Hadn’t he once dreamed of leaving home? Traveling the world, opening an international trading business—free from his father and the clan’s demands. Maybe this was his chance.

He just had to survive the winter. If his goods didn’t sell by next week, he’d have to request extra missions or find side jobs on the black market. After that, he’d leave the clan’s forces and forge his own path.

The sound of someone clearing their throat startled both Tadashi and Masada. Tadashi turned and froze. The figure before him was Akito—Sadi’s father—but much younger, still a teenager.

“Still no buyers, Masada?” Akito asked with a smile.

Masada’s instincts warned him to be careful. Akito had joined their clan a month ago. He was sharp, with a good head for business; they’d met on the black market. But Akito was also close to Daze’s enemies, so Masada had kept his distance.

Masada straightened and smiled. “Is there something you’re looking for, Akito?”

“I heard you’re not allowed in the main hall anymore. Planning on a career change to full-time merchant?”

Masada’s gaze cooled. “Our clan needs merchants to develop its economy—”

“Pfft. Cut the diplomatic talk. I’m not saying being a merchant is a bad thing. In fact, I came to ask you to be my partner.”

Masada was silent for a moment. “What kind of partnership?”

Akito shrugged. “I’ve studied this clan. There’s one business I think will be profitable because we don’t have a proper distribution center. It’s a small clan now, sure, but if your father leads it well, it will grow. And when that happens, I’ll be ready. I’ve watched how you work, Masada. I want to invite you to open a logistics and warehousing business. Are you interested?”

Masada studied him. “I’d like to hear more.”

For the next half hour, Tadashi listened as Masada and Akito discussed business. He’d often heard them argue or negotiate as adults, but he’d never seen them get along like this. They were both sharp, their minds clicking instantly. Akito had a plan, a little capital, and a pitch for investors. He needed a partner with trading skills—someone others had dismissed for being too young.

Masada absorbed it all. He was impressed by Akito’s vision, but several things gave him pause.

First, Akito’s ethics were… gray. He had no qualms about using methods that skirted the law.

Second, Akito planned to get funding from Daze’s enemies. Masada wanted no part of that political game.

Third, Akito’s plan centered on internal clan business. Masada wanted to travel, not be tied down.

So he politely declined. Akito’s face flushed; he hadn’t expected rejection.

“Fine, if you’re so sure. I guess I misjudged you. I hope your little trinkets sell and you don’t end up a frozen sardine by the end of the year,” Akito snapped, standing and stalking off like a pouting child.

Masada bit his lip, and Tadashi smiled, seeing him hold back a laugh. For the first time, Tadashi was seeing another side of his father—not the untouchable fortress, but the young man beneath.

Masada had always been distant, a powerful and mysterious figure. He advised Tadashi as a clan leader corrects a successor, not as a father guides his son. It wasn’t the same.

Tadashi wanted to know this private side of him—to understand him, to be close to him. But was that even possible?

He stared at his father, swallowing hard. A silent longing welled up, sharp and bittersweet.

Father…

A cold jolt shot down Tadashi’s spine. Masada’s face and the world around him began to fade. Time was shifting again. Tadashi blinked, trying to steady himself. What was happening? When he’d grabbed the power stone, he’d hoped to find his injured father. Instead, he was being swept through Masada’s past as a mere observer. Was his body still in the fortress? Could he control this? What if he was trapped forever?

He pushed the dark thoughts aside.

The current of time carried him to another market, one he didn’t recognize. Masada was arranging his stall. Tadashi reached for his hand.

Don’t get caught. Just observe the situation in the Mitsurugi Clan and report back. Do you understand, Masada? Daze’s voice echoed in Masada’s mind.

Masada took a deep breath. After a year off the radar, the clan had summoned him for an infiltration mission—using his merchant cover to gather intelligence for the war.

“Excuse me, do you sell kitchen utensils?”

Masada turned. Standing there was a Mitsurugi woman, her smile gentle.

She had long silver hair and wore a pure white kimono. Her face was one Tadashi knew all too well, and the sight of it now made his chest ache. His breath hitched.

This was it—the moment his father first laid eyes on his mother, Chiyo.

And with the vision came the memory of Shirasu’s voice when he first met him, a ghost that had haunted him ever since: Masada only married Chiyo for the mission. He never truly loved her. You were an unwanted child. He regretted your birth.

Tadashi had taken the blow back then without a word, letting it fester in silence.

His entire life, he’d trained himself not to expect anything from his father. He accepted the blatant favoritism shown to Shiro and Raito—he was their big brother; he loved them too. He endured his father’s absence, his harsh demands, giving his loyalty freely and burying his own needs.

But a part of him, a stupid, hopeful part, still clung to the stubborn hope that Masada did want him, did love him, even if he never showed it.

Now, seeing the beginning of their story, the pain surged back, fresh and raw. Soon, he was about to witness the truth for himself.
