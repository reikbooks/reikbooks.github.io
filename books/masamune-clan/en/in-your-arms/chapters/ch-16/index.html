Regrets

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

M asada opened his eyes to a familiar ceiling. He was in a small, clean room at the village inn, the morning sun casting a warm glow through the window. A figure sat in a chair beside the bed, engrossed in a book.

Seishiro.

“Is everyone all right?” Masada’s voice rasped from a dry throat. “Tadashi and Shirasu?”

Seishiro glanced up briefly before returning to his book. “They’re fine. We’re at the inn. Shirasu healed you both. He’s with Tadashi next door. Claims he’s allergic to being in the same room as you.” Seishiro’s tone was flat, matter-of-fact.

“And the creature in the cave? The Dorimu?”

“Killed it. Burned the nest. You three were the only ones who made it out. The others were too far gone—their souls were destroyed when the dream world collapsed.”

Masada absorbed the grim news, then pushed himself up to sit. The pain was gone, but a deep fatigue lingered. He reached for a glass of water on the bedside table, his eyes catching the date on the wall calendar. What felt like a month in the dream world had been only two days. If he didn’t return to the clan by tomorrow, he would be declared missing, and Shiro would be installed as acting leader.

“Shirasu won’t let Tadashi leave until he’s fully recovered,” Seishiro added, noting Masada’s gaze.

“I understand the necessity, but I must return immediately,” Masada replied, his mind already racing through the implications of his absence. “Urgent clan matters cannot wait.”

“Why is your clan so fragile that it falls apart the moment you step away?”

Masada turned. Shirasu stood in the doorway, his expression unreadable. He entered the room and gestured for Masada to stay seated. Picking up a stethoscope from the bedside, he conducted a quick, perfunctory examination.

“Any pain? Dizziness? Shortness of breath? Nausea? Flashbacks?” he asked, his voice clipped.

“None.”

“Lucky you.” Shirasu snorted, removing the stethoscope. “I don’t care what you do, but at least eat something before you go running off.”

Masada nodded his thanks. Seishiro stood, grabbed his cigarettes, and headed for the door. “Don’t smoke near Tadashi,” Shirasu snapped at his retreating back.

“Nag.” Seishiro slammed the door behind him.

Left alone, Masada observed his eldest son. Shirasu slumped into the vacant chair, crossing his arms and legs as he fixed Masada with a steady stare. His hair was unkempt, dark circles shadowed his eyes, and exhaustion was etched into his features.

“Are you all right?” Masada asked. “The Dorimu stabbed you. The wound was fatal.”

“I got out fast and healed myself,” Shirasu shrugged, the gesture failing to mask his weariness.

Masada gave a curt nod. “I’m glad you survived.”

A tense silence stretched between them before Shirasu broke it, his voice low and serious. “Did you know Tadashi saw your past? With Chiyo?”

Masada froze, his mind reeling. “What do you mean?”

“Seishiro confirmed it. You and Tadashi were connected when you entered the Dorimu’s realm. He woke up inside your subconscious and found a chamber of memories. I assume you experienced the same?”

Masada’s mind flashed to the terrible room where he’d witnessed Tadashi’s suffering at Ikaru’s hands. He had once told Shirasu he knew of their ordeal, but seeing the visceral reality of it had been a brutal shock.

“They are our children, Masada. Please, save them from that place…” The echo of Chiyo’s desperate plea tightened his throat.

He was a failure of a father. He had not protected them. But they were still alive. He still had a chance to mend what was broken. He had to be strong enough to try.

“Yes,” Masada finally admitted, his voice thick. “That is correct.”

Shirasu was quiet for a long moment, studying him. When he spoke again, his voice was barely a whisper. “Did you really marry my mother because you loved her?”

A painful loss constricted Masada’s chest. “Of course. I never stopped loving her.”

Shirasu closed his eyes, exhaling slowly as if releasing a burden he’d carried for years. “Good,” he murmured, his voice thick with emotion. “I’m glad she had some happiness.”

Masada swallowed hard, the weight of his past choices pressing down on him. “Shirasu… I want to apologize for abandoning you and your mother. I caused you immense suffering. I understand if you cannot forgive me, or if you no longer wish to acknowledge me as your father. But you are still my son. If there is anything I can ever do to ease your burden, now or in the future, you need only ask.”

For a long moment, Shirasu just stared, his gaze sharp as a blade.

“Masada, you need to understand something. The only reason I survived Ikaru was because of Tadashi’s kindness. Yes, my plan to kill Ikaru failed, and Shiro finished the job, but none of it would have happened if Tadashi hadn’t agreed to help me.

“He did that to save you and your clan. He did it all for you. He would do anything for you, Masada, because he loves you. Don’t you see? The one thing he wants, the one thing he’d never dare ask for, is to know that you love him too.

Shirasu leaned forward, his voice dropping to a harsh, accusing whisper. “Perhaps you tell yourself that of course you love him, you’re his father. But you almost never show it. I watched you, Masada. I saw how you ‘disciplined’ him. It was the same way Ikaru ‘disciplined’ me. You overload him with work, you punish his mistakes severely, you manipulate him. You torture him every single day—don’t look so surprised.

“You don’t hit him, Masada. You don’t use physical torture. You just neglect him. You ignore his needs. You break him mentally and emotionally. You’re worse than Ikaru—you’re destroying the son of the woman you claim to love.”

He stood abruptly, cutting off any reply. “It’s too late for us, Masada. But if you’re not a complete fool, you still have a chance with Tadashi. He still loves you, against all reason. My one request is that you stop torturing him and show him you love him.

“Sometimes I think Mother left her love for me with Tadashi. I always see her in his smile. Don’t you want to see Chiyo smile at you again, Masada?”

Shirasu turned to leave, pausing at the door for one last, searing question. “You were a father before you were a clan leader. Why did you think you had to abandon one to be the other? You can treat Shiro and Raito with kindness. Why not Tadashi? Do you even see him as your son?”

The door slammed shut, leaving Masada alone in the ringing silence. He took a long, shaky breath, running a hand over his face. He had resolved to change, but Shirasu’s words laid bare the depth of his failure. Small adjustments were not enough. If he wanted to be a better father, he had to take real, decisive action before he lost his son for good.

He sat for a long time, watching the dust motes dance in the sunlight, his mind grappling with the truth.

Shirasu jolted awake in the chair by Tadashi’s bed, the phantom sound of Ikaru’s voice still echoing in his ears. His head pounded, his vision swam, and a wave of nausea washed over him.

Trembling, he clutched the ridged surface of the power-stone bracer on his wrist, its warmth a tangible anchor to reality. Breathe. It’s over. You’re safe. Tadashi is safe. He took a deep, shuddering breath and turned to check on his brother.

A soft groan from the bed made him start. Tadashi was thrashing, his face pale and slick with sweat, his body trembling. A choked cry escaped his lips as he woke with a violent start, eyes wide with terror.

Shirasu’s heart clenched. He knew that suffocating panic all too well. He had trained himself to wake silently, a habit born from the fear of alerting Ikaru. “Hey,” he said softly, his voice low and calm. “You’re awake?”

Tadashi flinched, squeezing his eyes shut. Shirasu reached out and firmly took his hand. “Shh… it’s me, Shirasu. Ikaru is dead. We’re safe. You’re in my room. Squeeze my hand if you understand.”

After a moment’s hesitation, Tadashi’s hand tightened around his. He slowly opened his eyes, the fear in them gradually receding as he focused on Shirasu’s face.

“You’re thirsty,” Shirasu stated, offering a small, reassuring smile. He helped Tadashi sit up and held a glass of water to his lips, steadying his trembling hands. “Slowly.”

“Th-thank you,” Tadashi whispered, his voice raw.

Shirasu set the glass aside and gently rubbed his brother’s shoulder, offering silent comfort. A knock at the door made Tadashi jump, his breathing quickening, eyes darting fearfully around the room.

“It’s okay,” Shirasu soothed, squeezing his hand. “It’s just Seishiro. I’ll get it. You just stay here.” He waited for Tadashi’s nod before opening the door.

“Room service for Master Shirasu,” Seishiro droned, holding a tray of food. He entered, placed the tray on the table, and lit a cigarette. “Figured you hadn’t eaten.”

“Hey, I told you not to smoke in here—” Shirasu began, but was cut off.

“Father is looking for me?” Tadashi’s voice came from the bathroom doorway. He emerged, looking slightly more composed, his hair damp.

“Your blood pressure’s high,” Shirasu said, steering him toward the food. “Eat first. You’re not going anywhere until you’re not a zombie.”

Tadashi grimaced but reluctantly began to eat under Shirasu’s watchful eye.

“I’m heading out,” Seishiro announced, blowing smoke toward the ceiling. “Meet me at the East warehouse when you’re done playing nursemaid. And bring money. You still owe me.”

Once Seishiro had left, Shirasu turned his attention to Tadashi, making sure he ate his breakfast. He sat close, pushing food around his own plate.

“You cook better than this,” Tadashi said softly when they were finally alone.

Shirasu huffed a faint, exhausted laugh. “Obviously.” He kept his gaze on Tadashi until the bowl was empty.

“How is Father?” Tadashi asked quietly, setting down his glass.

“Annoying as ever,” Shirasu snorted. “Perfectly healthy.”

For a moment, Tadashi was silent. He drew his legs onto the sofa, hugging his knees to his chest.

“Are you still angry? I’m sorry I went home with Father,” he whispered.

Shirasu released a sharp breath. “I understand your position. I was just shocked—it all happened so fast. That bastard showed up out of nowhere and forced you to leave.” He shook his head slightly. “After you were gone, I had time to think.”

“While you were drunk?”

Shirasu laughed softly.

“While I was drinking, yes. But I have a high tolerance; I was still sober, I swear. I decided to stop worrying about you and Masada. It wasn’t like you remembered what happened. You couldn’t even look at me without it being awkward.

“But when I found out Masada wasn’t taking proper care of you, especially when I saw how the Dorimu had ensnared you… I couldn’t just stand by.”

Shirasu held out his left hand, palm open and facing up. A silent question.

You understand what I mean, don’t you?

When they were trapped in the Mitsurugi clan’s headquarters, spoken words were often too dangerous. This was their only way to communicate. A language of touch to share strength, to say everything words could not.

I’m hurting. I’m sorry. I need help. Thank you. Are you okay? Just hold on. I’m with you.

Tadashi’s hand trembled slightly as it rose to meet Shirasu’s. Their fingers laced together, a familiar, grounding pressure.

I understand, brother.

With Tadashi’s memories restored, Shirasu allowed his eyes to close, a wave of relief and sorrow washing over him. “You are all I have left,” he murmured. “Let me protect you now, as you once protected me.”

Tadashi’s hand squeezed his, a desperate, grateful clutch.

“Please,” Tadashi begged, his voice cracking. “Don’t tell Father my memory seal is broken.”

Shirasu studied him. Tadashi’s face was pale, a thin mask over his fear. With the seal broken, the flashbacks would come more frequently, the panic attacks more intense. His performance on Masada’s missions would suffer. He was terrified of failure, of disappointing his father.

“Of course not. You remember my advice, right?” Shirasu had given him techniques to manage the trauma. He was sure Tadashi remembered them, but such things were mastered through practice, not just memory.

He unfastened the bracer from his own wrist and secured it around Tadashi’s. “Use this as an anchor. If you feel a panic attack coming, touch it. Count the stones, focus on the energy flowing through it.”

Then, he retrieved a book from the bracer’s storage seal and pressed it into Tadashi’s hands. A Comprehensive Guide to Overcoming Post-Traumatic Stress.

Tadashi stared at the bracer and the book, a genuine, grateful smile finally breaking through. “Thank you,” he breathed.

“I’ll be traveling with Seishiro for a while,” Shirasu said, answering the question he saw in his brother’s eyes. “Just until I find work at a hospital or can open my own practice. The moment I’m settled, I’ll send you my address.”

A shy hope ignited in Tadashi’s gaze. “Once my duties are done… could I visit you?”

“Of course,” Shirasu said, his smile widening as he took his brother’s hand once more, his grip firm and reassuring. “My door will always be open for you, Tadashi. You will always have a home with me.”
