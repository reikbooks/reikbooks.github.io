The Past

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

A spike of cold fury seized Tadashi’s chest. Who did this? That creature? The urge to turn back and face it flared within him, hot and immediate. But cold logic doused the impulse. He was unarmed, drained. If that thing had bested his father, he stood no chance as he was.

CLANG!

The fortress gate slammed shut, its echo ringing through the halls. The creature was inside.

Stay calm. Don’t be reckless. Observe the enemy first, then find a weapon. The labyrinth might offer something. He stepped into the maze—and froze. A trail of blood stained the floor, dark and wet, as if a body had been dragged through the passage.

Father!

Tadashi raced down the winding corridors, following the crimson trail. His boots hammered against the stone, a reckless noise he couldn’t silence. He couldn’t afford stealth, not with his father bleeding somewhere ahead. The labyrinth grew darker, the air thicker, until he stood before a sealed door etched with glowing carvings.

A blood seal.

Without a second thought, he pressed his palm against the cold, circular sigil at its center.

Ah!

A sharp prick stung his finger. His blood activated the seal, and the door swung open. Tadashi darted inside, shoving it closed until the lock clicked shut. He allowed himself one unsteady breath before scanning the room.

Dark symbols covered the walls, their shapes naggingly familiar—he’d seen similar ones in the Mitsurugi Clan’s Book of Dark Arts, but couldn’t decipher them. In the center, a stone pillar held a glowing power stone. The blood trail ended there.

A teleportation device? With hesitant steps, he steeled himself and reached out. His palm met the stone’s surface. A searing light erupted, and the world dissolved into nothing.

Tadashi’s senses reeled as he materialized in a large dining hall. The Masamune Clan banner was draped on the wall. The smell of food and the clatter of pots came from an adjacent kitchen. He scanned the room, a trespasser’s guilt knotting his stomach.

Where am I?

A door opened across the hall. A man walked in, a boy of about ten trailing behind him. The man collapsed into the chair at the head of the table with a tired grunt, poured water, and gulped it down.

“Mother, we’re home,” the boy announced, sitting next to his father. They both looked content, like they’d just finished a workout.

Tadashi stood awkwardly, his apology poised on his lips, but neither of them so much as glanced in his direction.

A woman entered from the kitchen with a bowl of steamed vegetables. “You’re back? Breakfast is ready,” she said, setting the table before joining them.

“Where’s Masada?” the boy asked.

“Haven’t seen him since last night. I’ll call him.”

“Don’t!” The man’s snapped command made Tadashi flinch. “He’s being punished for disrupting Ken’s training yesterday.”

Tadashi went rigid. The names echoed in his mind, a brutal realization dawning. Masada. Ken .

This man was Daze Masamune, his grandfather.

His heart hammered against his ribs. Daze had died before Tadashi was born. This house should be a ruin, burned to the ground during the war. So why was it intact? Why was Daze alive ?

A cold dread washed over him.

Have I traveled back in time?

He clenched his fists. His mother, Chiyo, could manipulate time. Had he inherited a shred of that power? He’d been flung into the past once before, but last time, he was solid. Real. Now, his hand passed straight through a wall hanging as if he were made of mist.

He had to know more. Leaving the dining room, he stopped at the first door down the hall. A faint sound came from within. Without hesitation, Tadashi phased through the shoji screen.

A small boy sat curled on a futon, leaning against the wall with a thick book open in his lap. He was crying silently, his small shoulders shaking with stifled sobs.

When Tadashi appeared, the boy looked up. Tadashi’s breath hitched. The boy was his father as a child. Masada’s eyes, glistening with tears, held a silent, desperate plea.

Father… can you see me?

“Father?” Tadashi whispered, his own voice raw.

But Masada’s gaze dropped back to his book. He couldn’t hear him. Heart aching, Tadashi sat beside him, taking in the heartbreakingly young features—a mere child, no older than five.

Masada bit his lip, a grimace of pure hurt twisting his face. Compelled by a force he didn’t understand, Tadashi reached out and gently covered the boy’s small hand with his own.

The moment they touched, a powerful surge of energy, like lightning, coursed through him.

Tadashi gasped, his vision whiting out as a flood of images and sounds—a tangled, reversing storm of memories—invaded his consciousness.

I just wanted to join the training. How was that disrupting anything? I already mastered the technique. Father isn’t fair. Why does he always pay attention to Ken?

Tadashi recoiled. That was Masada’s thought, clear as day in his head. He tried to block it out, but the mental presence remained, a faint whisper he couldn’t silence.

Along with the thoughts came a wave of Masada’s raw emotions: hunger, exhaustion, anger, sadness, disappointment, and a distinct, throbbing pain in his arm.

Is he hurt?

The door slid open. Masada shot to his feet, heart racing. Why is Father here already? I’m not ready—

Daze stepped in and shut the door. “Have you finished the book?” His tone was ice.

Masada shook his head slightly. The book was huge. He’d only learned to read two weeks ago—by sneaking into classes. Understanding the long texts was a struggle, let alone memorizing them.

“What have you been doing since yesterday? Sleeping?” Daze’s accusation cut through the room.

“No, Father… I’ve been trying—”

Daze ripped the book from his grasp. “What’s on page fifteen?”

Masada blanched. He had no idea.

“Your hands,” Daze ordered, tossing the book aside. He fetched a long wooden ruler from a cabinet.

Masada’s stomach twisted into a knot. He fought back a sob. His hands already ached so much—

“Masada.” Daze’s voice was a low threat.

Shaking, the boy rolled up his sleeve and laid his arm across the table.

Tadashi drew a sharp breath. He knew what was coming, but the reality was a gut punch. Masada’s arm was a mess of blue-black bruises, swollen and inflamed.

CRACK!

A mirror of the pain exploded in Tadashi’s own arm. Masada let out a choked whimper. Daze struck again. When it was over, Masada’s knees gave way.

“You will not eat until you’ve mastered that book,” Daze said, wiping a smear of blood from the ruler. “Do not embarrass me. I won’t tolerate a stupid son. Study harder.”

“Yes, Father,” Masada whispered, his voice thick with tears.

The moment Daze left, Masada collapsed to the floor and wept. Tadashi sank down beside him, eyes squeezed shut, face buried in his hands.

His father had never spoken of his childhood. Tadashi hadn’t known his grandfather was this cruel—or that Masada had been jealous of Ken.

I will be better than Ken in every way, Masada vowed silently.

Before Tadashi could even react, the room fractured, and he was thrown into a whirlwind of time.

Tadashi blinked. The scene had shifted. He now stood outside the main hall on a bright, festive morning. New Year’s decorations adorned the buildings. Following the royal ceremony, the clan would hold a grand feast.

On the terrace, Daze and Ken circulated among the elders, the mood joyous. A solitary figure observed from behind a cluster of trees. Masada. He looked older, maybe seven or eight. Tadashi reached out and took his hand.

Instantly, Masada’s thoughts flooded into his. He learned that Masada had not only mastered that first punishing book—he had memorized countless others. He shadowed Ken’s studies, mastering every subject, his understanding and scores now exceeding his brother’s.

In combat, they were neck and neck. Ken retained a slight lead in a few areas, but Masada was confident he had left “average” far behind. Daze should have seen it. He should have acknowledged him. But he remained blind.

Why does Father always favor Ken? Just because he’s the firstborn? Why not choose me? I’ve worked so hard. If Father just gave me a chance, I know I could be better.

Masada’s hands balled into fists, jealousy burning in his chest as he watched Daze, arm around Ken, boast to the officials. When the feast began, the favored son was ushered inside. Masada turned his back. He was not welcome.

Tadashi followed, his mind reeling. Masada was undeniably gifted and self-assured, truly believing he was Ken’s superior. But as the younger son by five years, his path was blocked. The clan would never depose a competent heir.

Masada felt thrown away. He desired the title for himself.

So that’s the reason, Tadashi understood. Is that why Father pushed for Shiro to be heir?

Shiro, just a year his junior, was intelligent and driven, always competing with Tadashi. The jealousy was palpable. She’d even won the selection exam, only stepping aside for their mother’s sake.

After Makoto died, Shiro took on the family’s responsibilities. She sacrificed her own goals. And eventually, after Tadashi’s failure, she did inherit the role.

Shirasu said she defeated Ikaru. Tadashi didn’t doubt it. Masada must have been so proud of her—a stark contrast to the disappointment Tadashi represented.

Tadashi drew an unsteady breath. It’s fine. I am truly inadequate. I simply need to strive harder to earn Father’s approval. He’s offered me this chance for redemption. If I can succeed in this mission… clear the clan’s debt… perhaps he will forgive me.

He followed Masada across the courtyard.

“Hey, you!” a voice called.

Masada turned. A clan officer waved him over. Masada approached and bowed.

“Watch this balloon stall. Don’t leave until they’re all gone. Understood?”

Masada knew he was being used, but saying no meant trouble. The officer could report him, giving Daze’s enemies an excuse to cause problems. Another beating was certain.

“Yes, sir.”

The officer left for the main hall. Masada glared at the balloons, then got an idea. He gathered them and headed for the crowds at the royal shrine. He would sell them there.

Tadashi watched, impressed by his father’s quick wit. It was a glimpse of the man he knew—the one who always found a solution.

That day, Masada vowed to become a rich merchant, build his own fortune, and never rely on his family again. Tadashi felt the iron will radiating from him. He finally understood: his father’s entire future—the clan leader, the diplomat, the man of power—was forged in this single moment.

This was the man who was horrified when Tadashi broke the King’s mirror. The man desperately saving their clan. The man who asked for his help.

Tadashi knelt before young Masada. He closed his eyes, pressing his forehead to his father’s, his heart aching with regret.

Father… I get it now. I’m sorry, I’ll do my part. I’ll be better this time, I promise.
