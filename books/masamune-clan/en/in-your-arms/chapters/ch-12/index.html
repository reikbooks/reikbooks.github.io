Answers

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

T adashi gasped, freezing as he stared at his father. Masada’s form was translucent, glowing with a faint, ethereal white light like a ghost.

What in the world was happening?

Masada slowly turned from him, approaching the writhing sculptures carved into the wall. A low, tortured sound emanated from them—the sound of a soul torn between two loyalties, trapped in unceasing agony. Tadashi stood rooted, watching the figures twist. He slowly reached out to touch one, but snatched his hand back with a jolt.

You have to get out, Tadashi. Masada’s thought echoed in his mind, clear and urgent.

He flinched, turning toward his father. Masada was walking toward the portal in the center of the room—a frame, empty and inactive.

Where does he want me to go? Tadashi took a hesitant step forward, head lowered, every muscle coiled tight. The phantom sting of the whip marks on his back throbbed in time with his heartbeat.

“Father?” Tadashi’s voice was a strained whisper. “Where are we? Why… why are you see-through?” The knot of fear in his stomach tightened as he forced himself to stand at his father’s side, fighting the instinct to flee.

Masada didn’t answer, only stared at the portal with a vacant gaze. Tadashi shifted his attention to the carvings on the frame, which connected to the twisting sculptures on the walls.

With a trembling hand, Tadashi touched the frame. Instantly, a sharp jolt of static electricity shot through him, followed by a torrent of information flooding his mind.

Oh … He got it now.

This fortress… was his father’s subconscious.

The pieces clicked into place. No wonder everything felt so strange. This silent, sturdy fortress was a labyrinth built from his father’s soul, its walls imprinted with the history of the Masamune and Mitsurugi. Masada’s past was a locked room, and only his descendants could enter.

It was guarded by a monster wearing Daze’s face, a manifestation of his deepest conflict. Masada hated his own father. He was determined not to become Daze, yet duty forced him to adopt a similar harshness to protect the clan. That burden was drowning him in guilt.

Tadashi’s eyes drifted back to the writhing carvings. They were the physical form of Masada’s internal war: the man who wanted to be a good father, forever at odds with the clan head who had to sacrifice his own desires. Decisions that filled him with guilt… toward Tadashi. That was why the portal remained inactive. Tadashi could not leave until he helped his father forgive himself.

He lifted his gaze to Masada, who stood with glistening eyes that betrayed him. Having walked through his father’s memories, Tadashi now faced the bitter truth he had always feared.

You were not the child they wanted. They regretted your birth.

It was true. Masada had never anticipated Tadashi’s existence. He hadn’t wanted him. He had felt regret. But Masada was a man of action. As soon as he learned of the pregnancy, he accepted the situation and searched for a solution. He resolved the crisis and, from the bottom of his heart, had wanted to raise Tadashi well.

Masada loved Tadashi. Or, he had loved him once.

Tadashi hadn’t seen what happened after his father retreated into his work. He didn’t know why his father grew cold. Was it his own mistakes? Did circumstances force Masada to become a monster? Or did his father still love him, in a way Tadashi couldn’t see?

Whatever the reason, one truth remained: Tadashi loved his father. He always had. So he would keep striving to meet his expectations, to be the son his father could be proud of.

Tadashi’s throat was tight. He reached out, his hand trembling as he took his father’s and held on.

“I understand, Father,” he managed, the words rough. “It’s okay. I’m okay.”

He looked at Masada, eyes burning. His body was a map of aches, but he clung to the memory of his father’s first embrace—the warmth of it surrounding him now.

Thank you for accepting me. For loving me, even for a little while. I won’t ask for more. I won’t be a burden. You don’t have to change.

It didn’t matter if Masada never showed his love again. Tadashi understood him completely now. He would do anything for his father.

“I forgive you,” Tadashi whispered. He offered a small smile and then embraced him, knowing this might be the last time. Once he left this place, he might never hold his father like this again.

He held on tightly.

The clan came first. An heir could not be selfish. Tadashi didn’t have many personal wishes. He just wanted his family to be happy.

He wanted his father to be happy.

“I love you, Father.”

A soft, white light enveloped them. It spread to the portal’s frame, illuminating all the carvings. The thick darkness that had shrouded the portal vanished. The portal blazed with light, a new world opening wide before him.

When Tadashi released his embrace and looked up, Masada was gazing down at him, his face wet with tears, a grateful smile on his lips.

Go, Tadashi. Our journey is still long.

Masada pressed a kiss to Tadashi’s forehead, just as he had on that first night he held his son. A single tear fell from Tadashi’s eye. Masada gently wiped it away.

Goodbye, Father. I will always love you.

Tadashi stepped into the portal. And his entire world went white.

A blinding light.

Tadashi woke with a blink. His head throbbed, and his body felt weak and shivery. For a few moments, he lay on the hard stone floor, blinking and catching his breath.

The sound of drizzling rain filled the morning chill. He was still in the cave. Someone was with him.

Father?

Tadashi tried to call out, but his throat was raw and sore. He swallowed, the taste of blood still lingering. Masada appeared at his side and lit a power stone. Warmth and a dim glow filled the cave. Masada appeared unharmed.

Relief washed over Tadashi. His father was safe. He must have managed to give him the antidote before passing out.

Masada watched him. “Can you get up?” he asked, his voice low and measured.

Tadashi was silent for a moment, then nodded, though he wasn’t sure he could. As he tried to rise, Masada helped him—not pulling him roughly, but lifting him carefully. Tadashi’s head spun as he finally managed to sit up.

Masada retrieved a coconut from behind a rock and instructed Tadashi to drink. The fresh, sweet coconut water soothed his throat. Tadashi closed his eyes, grateful. Masada waited for him to finish the meal.

“This cave is shrouded in a dark power,” Masada said slowly, glancing toward the back where the snakes had appeared. “But we’re safe now.”

Tadashi just nodded. Masada watched the rain dripping at the cave’s mouth. For a while, neither spoke. Tadashi looked at his father, unsure of what to say. He had just seen his father’s past and emerged from his subconscious. Was the entire journey a hallucination from the snake venom? Tadashi clenched his fists. It had felt too real. His clothes weren’t torn and the whip marks were gone, but the memory of the pain lingered.

Tadashi glanced at Masada, who seemed unaware. Should he tell him? He looked down, having no idea how to start the conversation or what he hoped to gain.

Let it go. It’s better not to say anything. It won’t make a difference. Stop hoping. You just need to be the dutiful son he needs.

Masada moved the power stone closer. He sat down beside Tadashi, his position causing Tadashi to lean slightly against him. A gentle warmth slowly enveloped Tadashi. His father’s presence at his side made him feel stronger.

“Rest. We’ll leave after the rain stops,” Masada said calmly.

Tadashi nodded, clinging to the warmth left behind by the fragments of his childhood memories.

Tadashi woke slowly the next morning, watching sunlight filter through the gaps in Raito’s window. His younger brother was still snoring, tangled messily in his blanket. For a moment, Tadashi just stared at Raito’s face, squeezing his hand as an anchor to reality. His body didn’t ache as much; he felt refreshed.

The journey home had been uneventful. They had arrived at midnight. A pang of longing pinched Tadashi’s heart at the sight of home. He had survived.

He didn’t know if his perspective had shifted since leaving Masada’s subconscious, but during the entire journey back, he no longer sensed the sharp edge of anger from his father. Masada still looked serious and focused, lost in thought, but he didn’t look at Tadashi with disappointment or impatience. He didn’t say much, but his actions spoke volumes.

Father had found him food and water. He had given him a walking stick and helped him walk. He had stopped several times for Tadashi to rest. He had found a healer in the nearest village and ensured Tadashi ate and rested before they continued. He had even managed a smile when they finally reached the clan.

Tadashi bit his lip, trying to suppress the seeds of hope blooming in his chest. Maybe, all this time, he hadn’t been paying enough attention to his father’s gestures. Maybe Father had always been looking out for him, without Tadashi realizing it. Maybe, if Tadashi continued to be a good son, someday, Father would tell him that he still loved him.

When Tadashi arrived at the main hall, Shiro threw her arms around him, weeping. Tadashi stiffened, closing his eyes as he patted his sister’s back. Sadi walked up behind Shiro and greeted Tadashi with a hard punch to the arm. They didn’t have much time to talk, because Masada had already summoned Tadashi to the meeting room.

The New Year’s mission evaluation with the elders was grueling. They questioned his every decision. Tadashi did not mention his cooperation with Shirasu. He only emphasized that he had meant no harm when he destroyed the King’s heirloom mirror; he had acted quickly to save everyone from the mass hypnosis as the dark realm border opened and creatures began to emerge.

The elders finally accepted his explanation. All that remained was repaying the debt. Masada outlined his plan, and Tadashi agreed to undertake the secret mission his father assigned.

Two days later, Tadashi departed. The mission was complicated, involving several parties antagonistic to the King, but after three weeks, he completed it. The clan received a huge payment. Masada immediately handled the administration with the royal treasurer. Their clan was declared free of debt.

Masada and Shiro returned home, and they all had dinner together. For the first time in a long, long time, Tadashi felt his life was perfect.

The next morning, Tadashi was attending a clan meeting in the main hall when, suddenly, Shirasu appeared in the middle of the room, directly in front of him.

Tadashi stared, shocked. The other clan members were also stunned. Shiro was the first to react. She attacked Shirasu with her chain sickle.

Shirasu should have been able to dodge easily with teleportation, but he didn’t. The sickle blade struck his upper arm. Shirasu yanked Shiro’s chain and stabbed her with a short sword. Shiro dodged and launched a spinning kick. They seemed evenly matched in close quarters.

“What are you waiting for? Seize the traitor!” someone shouted from the back.

Tadashi jolted into action just as several clan guards surrounded Shirasu. Shirasu was cornered, but then he threw two grenades.

BOOM! The west side of the hall shattered, followed by the back wall. People screamed. Chaos erupted. In the confusion, Shirasu’s nunchaku chain shot out, wrapping around Tadashi and dragging him into a firm hold. The cold kiss of steel pressed against his throat, but the angle was wrong for a killing cut—it was a performance for the crowd.

“We need to talk,” Shirasu whispered, the words meant for Tadashi alone.
