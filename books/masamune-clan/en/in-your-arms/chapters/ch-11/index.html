The Cage

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

M asada rested his palm against the cold stone of the temple gate. A faint vibration thrummed beneath his touch, and a network of white light erupted in response, pulsing with a slow, deliberate rhythm. The shockwave washed over the wall, knitting together the fine cracks like a rapid, visible healing wound.

He was stunned. This was the first time he had seen a physical manifestation of the mental defense seal he had woven into Tadashi and Shirasu. He had designed it not to make them invulnerable, but to grant them resilience—a capacity to absorb and recover from trauma.

He stepped inside the temple. A frigid wind sighed through the desolate corridors, stirring the dust of the ruins. Crumbling walls stretched before him, some sections blasted away as if struck by heavy artillery. When Masada’s fingers brushed against the stone, a flood of memories erupted—fragmented images of pain and humiliation. He stood frozen, watching the ghost of his own actions replay in the scars etched on the walls.

He was the source of most of the damage. He pressed his palm against a deep fissure, one that connected to a larger fracture in a nearby pillar. The crack was ancient, its edges softened by moss.

“You should have been more careful with your siblings, Tadashi. As the eldest, you must always watch over them. Do you understand?”

“Yes, Father. I’m sorry.”

“You are forbidden from playing outside for the next week. I will assign you additional duties.”

Masada blinked, the memory a distant, hazy thing. He couldn’t recall the specifics of the mistake, only the stern reprimand. Yet the deep, scarring mark it had left on his son was etched into the stone, clear as day.

A tightness constricted his chest, radiating from the crack in the wall. He gritted his teeth and moved on, following the winding corridor until a heavy pull from the left drew him into a dark, stifling passage. The air grew thick and hot, stinging his eyes. He emerged into a vast, gaping chasm in the temple’s heart—a crater where the foundation had been punched through. The walls were a tapestry of claw marks and deep gouges, stained with dull, dark red, like dried blood. The entire area was scorched and blackened, littered with rubble and dust. A filthy puddle of water seeped from the cracked floor, and thick moss choked the edges, isolating the wound.

Masada recoiled. He knew this place. This was the psychological scar from Tadashi’s punishment mission.

He remembered the day Tadashi had failed to appear for the clan heir’s evaluation. Masada had sat in the meeting hall, his face burning with shame as the elders and his rivals assailed him. Akito had seized the opportunity to demand Tadashi’s removal. Masada had endured it all, swallowing the humiliation, his mind reeling. How could Tadashi, who had been so meticulously instructed, have missed such a critical event?

When Tadashi finally appeared that night, pale and trembling, confessing he had pawned the clan’s heirloom sword, Masada had nearly shattered. He had wanted to give up entirely. If he had committed such a failure in front of Daze, his father would have beaten him within an inch of his life.

But you are not Daze, a voice within him whispered. You hate that man. You swore you would never become like him. You promised Chiyo you would protect your son.

He had called Tadashi in privately. He hadn’t struck him. He hadn’t cursed him. With a voice vibrating with suppressed rage, he had advised his son to reflect on his mistake and accept the clan’s severe punishment. The heir must place the clan above all else—family, friends, even himself. Obedience was paramount. The punishment mission was designed to instill that absolute obedience, training him in the art of disguise as a slave.

At the time, Masada had approved it without a second thought. Now, seeing the devastation it had wrought—the crater it had left in his son’s soul—he questioned everything. He should have been able to discipline Tadashi without causing this level of damage. Tadashi had prioritized his family because Masada himself had failed, forcing the boy to bear the burden of his younger siblings.

He sighed, his gaze returning to the blackened wall. He tentatively touched its edge, and a shard of Tadashi’s raw memory pierced him. This was not the sanitized clan report; it was the full, unvarnished agony. Masada had never known how close Tadashi had come to breaking, how the brutal training under the kingdom’s best slave trader had pushed him to the brink. For a year and a half, Masada had refused to speak with him privately, cutting off his allowance, offering no reprieve. He had been absent when his son needed him most.

What kind of father are you?

Masada gasped and snatched his hand back, staring at the ruin in despair. He retreated down the corridor to another sealed room, this one barred by an iron door. The walls around it were viciously carved with knife marks and dark symbols. Masada read the signature.

Ikaru Mitsurugi.

Suddenly, the walls trembled violently. A gale roared down the hall, slamming into him with the stench of blood. He stumbled, his hand instinctively grabbing the stone wall to steady himself. A torrent of Tadashi’s memories under Ikaru’s control flooded his mind—a relentless, electric current of pain. Masada fought the urge to scream. He wrenched his hand free, his vision blurring, nausea overwhelming him.

“Your father will see you soon, Tadashi,” Ikaru’s voice whispered from the stones. “What will he think? You are the son he never wanted. You have always disappointed him. Are you still worthy of his name?”

The walls cracked under the psychic strain. When Masada had repaired Tadashi’s mental seals after the coma, he had worked quickly, refusing to delve into the memories. Now he understood. Ikaru had systematically destroyed his son, body and soul. A rage hotter than any he had ever known boiled in his blood. He wanted to tear Ikaru apart, but the man was long dead.

Masada slammed his fist against the iron door. A white light exploded. The entire temple shook violently, new cracks spiderwebbing across the walls. He flinched, horrified at causing more damage. He took a stabilizing breath and gently stroked the vibrating stone, his palm glowing with a soft light.

I’m sorry, son. Don’t be afraid. I’m here. I know everything now. It’s okay. Calm down.

The energy from his seal rippled across the wall, sealing the new cracks. The tremor subsided. Guided by instinct, he pressed on through the labyrinth until he reached the innermost chamber.

The ghost of the child Tadashi was waiting, standing before an inactive portal. The boy, who looked about six years old, wore the formal kimono from his succession ceremony. Bruises marred his jaw and cheeks, and the red marks of the Ember Seal were visible on his neck. The child bowed respectfully, his face a mask of pain.

Masada found it difficult to breathe. He was no longer the man who had welcomed his son into the world. The demands of the clan had eroded his empathy. He had tried to balance his duties, but his heart had hardened. He had pushed Tadashi to watch over his siblings, all the while burying his guilt, letting his son’s sacrifices become routine and expected.

When Tadashi reached out a small hand, his expression worried, a lump formed in Masada’s throat. He took the hand, and Tadashi gripped it tightly.

“This way, Father,” the child whispered. He climbed onto an altar and touched a symbol. The portal shimmered to life, revealing an exit.

Tadashi bowed his head, his eyes glistening, but he said nothing. Masada felt a tremor of pure affection from his son.

“I will not leave you, Tadashi,” Masada vowed, squeezing the small hand.

Tadashi merely nodded, still looking down. He slowly released Masada’s grip. A single tear traced a path down each cheek, which he quickly wiped away. Masada gently touched his chin, lifting his face. The boy’s sad smile was a mirror of Chiyo’s on the day they had parted. Masada’s heart shattered.

“Goodbye, Father. I will always love you.”

Masada’s breath hitched. He looked at his son, vowing to be a better father. With one last glance, he stepped through the portal.

In the dark room, the monster that had seized Tadashi’s hair forced him to his knees. Dazed and unsteady, Tadashi gripped the creature’s wrist. The blood from his sliced palm made contact with its skin, and the monster roared in agony, flinging him away as if burned by acid.

Tadashi landed hard, gasping, tears streaming down his face. The whip marks on his back felt like hot iron. Every movement was agony. He struggled to his feet and stared in shock as the monster spasmed, its form shrinking and writhing. Smoke rose from its skin. It transformed into a familiar figure.

Masada stood before him, his gaze filled with sorrow.

“F-Father?!” Tadashi whispered, his body trembling violently.
