Chapter 4

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

T adashi stared at Chiyo, momentarily speechless. She knew he was from the future? The questions caught in his throat as he waited for her to explain.

“When you arrived, I had just begun my maternity leave,” Chiyo said gently. “Perhaps because you never saw me at work, you didn’t know my true role. I am the guardian of the Celestial Pearl at Jikan Temple. I know its purpose, its power—and the ancient ritual that allows one to traverse time.”

She paused, her eyes soft with understanding. “When you collapsed in my garden with the pearl in your grip, I knew exactly what you were: a traveler from another time.”

“You had the pearl all along?” The words came out rougher than he intended, betrayal stinging despite himself. But hadn’t he kept his own secrets? They’d both been guarding their truths, circling each other in this dance of half-spoken stories.

Chiyo unclasped her necklace, its iron pendant swinging gently. With practiced fingers, she opened the seal to reveal a hidden compartment. Two crimson pearls appeared, gleaming against the chain.

“You wouldn’t let go of yours,” she said, a slight smile playing on her lips. “As you can see, I guard this era’s pearl. When used, it transports its wielder to its counterpart in the desired time—which explains your arrival in my house. I couldn’t know if you were friend or foe, so I chose to watch and wait.”

Tadashi understood her caution and was even grateful for the time to heal. But the weight of his mission pressed down on him. If he’d gotten the pearl when he first arrived, if he’d returned immediately… His clan wouldn’t have to face the royal family’s judgment. The shame of his failure wouldn’t have spread to those he sought to protect.

Could he still plead with the King, offer himself as the sole target of punishment? But after nearly a month of silence… By the third day of a delayed mission, his clan would already have faced consequences. His father, as clan head, would have been judged. The thought of his entire clan bearing the weight of his absence made his chest tighten with dread.

Tadashi bowed deeply, his stomach churning. “Lady Chiyo, I have nothing to prove that I didn’t wish you any harm. But please, have mercy—the royal family will punish my clan for my delay in returning the pearl.”

The silence that followed felt endless.

“Rise,” Chiyo commanded, her voice carrying both authority and compassion. “Look at me, and listen carefully.”

When he met her gaze, he found it heavy with an ancient sorrow.

“I can return you to any moment you choose. Your clan need not suffer.” She paused, her expression hardening slightly. “But there can be no more secrets between us. You will answer all my questions, confess everything that has transpired, truthfully. Do you understand?”

Tadashi nodded, shame rising for his earlier suspicions.

“I knew you were from the future because my family has guarded the Celestial Pearl for generations,” she continued. “Never in our records has it been lost and returned to the King. Now tell me—who are you truly? What year claims you? Who is your master, and how did he manage to obtain the Celestial Pearl?”

Tadashi drew a sharp breath. Could he actually tell her? He was bound by his father’s speech seal, a curse that prevented him from revealing certain truths to anyone. Yet, since arriving in the past, the usual headaches were absent, and he hadn’t felt the seal’s presence.

“I am Tadashi of the Masamune Clan,” he murmured, half expecting the searing agony that usually accompanied breaking the seal. He released his breath softly when nothing happened. Perhaps here, years before the seal’s creation, its power held no sway. “My father is Masada, our Clan Head. My mother was Makoto.” The words felt strange on his tongue, forbidden yet freeing. Chiyo’s eyes widen at his family name. The Masamune weren’t a prominent clan, but they had once played an important part in the war.

“I come from seventeen years hence, where the royal family tasked me with retrieving the pearl from the Mitsurugi Clan.”

Chiyo’s face paled. “You’re enslaved by the royal family?” Her eyes flickered to the Fuun mark on his skin.

“Ah… No,” Tadashi murmured, touching the brand self-consciously. He received the slave mark when he was captured and interrogated by the enemy during a mission. The magical mark couldn’t be erased, and his clan… exploited it ever since, sending him on difficult infiltration missions as a servant. He wasn’t a slave, but he considered his clan his master because it was his duty to serve them.

“This… this is from another time. Another failure.” Tadashi swallowed. The admission tasted bitter, but at least it was honest.

As Tadashi recounted his mission to retrieve the Celestial Pearl, Chiyo’s expression grew increasingly grave.

“The man you described—the one with long hair—that was Ikaru, Head of the Mitsurugi Clan.” Her fingers twisted in her lap. “You’re fortunate to have survived an encounter with him. Though I’m troubled that you couldn’t identify the bodyguard who offered his blood for the ritual…” She trailed off, lost in thought.

“Few families possess the ability to manipulate time,” she continued. “The guardianship passes through bloodlines. But Ikaru’s interest in this particular moment puzzles me. He’s already Clan Head in this time—what could he hope to change?”

Tadashi shook his head. The Mitsurugi Clan was notably absent from his historical studies, their internal workings shrouded in mystery.

“Think carefully,” Chiyo pressed. “What significant events occurred this year?”

He searched his memory: the war had ended two years prior, the clans were rebuilding, and the Mitsurugi had retreated from public view. Nothing stood out as remarkable.

Chiyo’s expression softened. “Tell me about yourself, then. Your family. This may be your only chance to speak freely. Whatever you share will remain between us. I wish to know and remember the real you.”

Something in her gentle tone broke through his reserve. Words spilled out: his stern father and his failure to uphold his position as clan heir, his siblings, and finally, his mother—her sacrifice to save his life, the weight of her absence.

Chiyo pulled him into an embrace, her touch maternal and comforting. When she drew back, her eyes studied his face intently. “How old are you, Tadashi?”

“Seventeen.” He managed a wan smile. “Actually, I won’t even be born for another few days.”

A strange look crossed Chiyo’s features. “Have you had any previous encounters with the Mitsurugi Clan?”

The question seemed oddly specific, but Tadashi began recounting his past conflicts with their rival clan. As he spoke, Chiyo’s shoulders stiffened.

“Heavens! You uncovered Seishiro’s drug operation?” Chiyo’s eyes widened with dawning horror. “Now I understand Ikaru’s desperation. He’s trying to eliminate future threats before they manifest—by preventing your birth.”

Ice flooded Tadashi’s veins. “My mother—”

“Is safe,” Chiyo assured him firmly. “Ikaru failed to return to the past, remember? But we must move quickly. There are preparations needed before I can send you home.”

They rested briefly that night, departing before dawn. Chiyo cradled her infant son, a small bundle of belongings strapped to her back. Behind her study’s bookshelf, she revealed a hidden passage that wound its way beneath the village, emerging among the weathered stones of Jikan Temple’s graveyard.

“Where are we going?” Tadashi whispered, watching Chiyo brush away tears for the third time since they’d set out.

Her voice wavered but didn’t break. “To a trusted friend. She’s a midwife in a far village. She’ll take my son to his father.”

“What?” Tadashi stopped short. “But why would you—”

“Because I won’t let him share his brother’s fate.” Chiyo’s words carried steel beneath their sorrow. “The Mitsurugi Clan took my firstborn. The villagers would make this child’s life hell. His father… I’ve told him about my pregnancy. He has agreed to raise him.”

“You could run,” Tadashi argued, though his chest ached with understanding. “Use the pearl—”

“The greater the temporal distance, the more power required,” Chiyo cut in. “As guardian, I’m bound by ancient laws. I’m forbidden to cross and alter the timeline. The consequences of breaking them…” She shuddered.

“I can’t run now, Tadashi. I’m bound to my duties at the temple and with the Mitsurugi. You don’t comprehend how this clan operates. Ikaru would hunt us relentlessly. Death would be a mercy compared to what he’d do if he caught us.”

She drew a steadying breath. “Better they believe I have suffered a miscarriage. With his father, my son will be safe. Loved.” Her voice cracked on the last word.

“But what about you, Chiyo?” Tadashi asked softly, though he dreaded the answer.

Chiyo’s smile was like moonlight on broken glass. “I’ll be alright. This is what it means to be a mother. Sometimes love means letting go.”

They traveled to the village where Chiyo’s midwife acquaintance resided. As it turned out, it was the very village where Makoto’s maternal family lived.

The village streets felt hauntingly familiar. Each corner whispered of his mother’s childhood, of the life she’d lived before becoming Makoto of the Masamune Clan. But she wasn’t here. She had already relocated to the city after marrying his father. Tomorrow was Tadashi’s birthday. Perhaps his grandmother was also by his mother’s side, as she prepared to give birth at their clan’s compound.

Tadashi was overcome with a sudden desire to see his mother again… but he hastily pushed the thoughts away. Some paths were better left unexplored.

Outside the birthing clinic, autumn leaves scattered across the garden. Chiyo emerged after what felt like hours, her face raw with grief. Without a word, Tadashi draped his haori over her trembling shoulders.

They sat together on a weathered stone bench, Chiyo’s tears soaking into his sleeve as she clung to him. Each shuddering breath seemed to hollow her out further, until she was nothing but bone-deep exhaustion and loss.

“We should rest here tonight,” Tadashi murmured, supporting her weight as she swayed. “You’re in no condition to travel.”

Chiyo managed a weak nod.

That night, he kept vigil in the corner of their borrowed room, watching as she tossed in fitful sleep. Each time she woke, fresh tears tracking down her cheeks, he felt the echo of her pain. The empty cradle of her arms seemed to mock them both—her child gone to safety, while his own birth loomed mere hours away.

Morning brought a heavy silence over their meager breakfast.

“The pearl,” Chiyo said finally, her voice rough. “Where exactly did the fisherman find it?”

Tadashi described the abandoned, half-submerged coastal temple near the royal palace.

“Then that’s where we’ll end this.” She met his gaze steadily. “The pearl ties you to that moment of discovery, so I will send you there. Hide there until the time comes to report to the King. Do not present the pearl to them whole. You must destroy the pearl from your era, rendering it unusable for future use. Once you’re gone, I’ll ensure the pearl of this era finds its way to that temple, completing the circle.”

When they reached the location, the full moon illuminated the temple. Chiyo gave him her iron pendant. Inside nestled a sealed letter. “Read it only after you arrived safely,” she whispered, her fingers lingering on the chain.

Tadashi nodded as he wore it. The pendant felt impossibly heavy against his chest—a mother’s last gift.

“Tadashi.” Chiyo’s voice trembled as she cupped his face between her palms. “Listen carefully. You do not have to achieve anything to be deserving of love. Your mother—” Her breath caught. “She loved you unconditionally. Her last wish wasn’t meant to be a burden. Above all else, she wanted your happiness.”

The words pierced him like arrows, striking tender places he’d forgotten existed. Before he could respond, Chiyo pulled him into a fierce embrace.

When she drew back, she pressed the Celestial Pearl into his palm. Her hands were steady now as she unsheathed a small blade. Ancient words fell from her lips like autumn leaves as she drew the edge across her palm.

The moment her blood touched the pearl, the coastal sky darkened. Wind howled around them, a tempest gathering strength. Chiyo’s eyes held his, brimming with something he couldn’t name—grief? Pride? Love?

“Be well, my dear one,” she whispered.

The blade kissed his arm. As his blood met the pearl, reality fractured. A shockwave propelled him into the dark sky. The last thing he saw was Chiyo’s smiling face, tears streaming freely now, before the storm swallowed him whole.

Time tore through him like countless blades, his body crushed and scattered before the wind hurled him downward. He struck the ocean’s surface with brutal force. Gasping for breath, Tadashi swam ashore and set to complete his task. He unearthed a chest from beneath the temple ruins, confirming that Chiyo’s Celestial Pearl was inside. He then placed the chest upon the wall, in plain sight for the fisherman to discover. He also destroyed the Celestial Pearl he had stolen from Seishiro, preserving the fragments to hand to the King, five days from now.

Afterwards, he walked along the beach, stopped beside a large rock, and lay down on the wet sand. Tadashi opened the seal in the iron pendant. Chiyo’s letter fell onto his chest. He tilted it up towards the faint light from the sliver of moon, his heart pounding as he began to read.

My dearest Tadashi,

By the time you read this, I will likely be gone. My strength will have been completely depleted from sending you home, or the Mitsurugi Clan may have executed me for taking the Celestial Pearl.

This was my choice, so do not blame yourself. Just as you would give your life for those you love, so would I. Forgive me and your mother for making these decisions without your consent. We only wanted you to live a full life.

I am so very happy that destiny brought us together. I have come to consider you as my own son. Spending time with you brought me such joy. These past weeks have been among the most wonderful of my life.

You do not have to achieve anything to earn my love. I love you just as you are, Tadashi. Whatever paths you’ve walked, whatever choices lie ahead, know that I will always love you.

From your loving mother,

Chiyo Mitsurugi.

Tadashi reread the letter, folded it with trembling hands, and tucked it back within the pendant. He drew a shaky breath and looked up to the sky. His heart pounded with pain, which did not subside, even as he pressed the pendant tightly against his chest.

His mind replayed images of Chiyo and his mother smiling, looking at him for the last time. Two women who had given everything for him.

We only want you to live.

The ocean lashed the shore, its salt spray mingling with tears he no longer tried to hide. Time blurred like watercolors in rain. The tide rose and fell, sometimes burying him under. The wind listened to his heartbeat, and the night answered his silent vows.

He would live to honor the sacrifice of both his mothers.

He would be stronger to protect his family.

He would give his all to serve the clan.

Tadashi did not wipe the seawater from his face. He merely bit his salty lip and closed his aching eyes. He lay there beneath the brilliant sky and softly glowing moon, clutching the pendant necklace like an anchor against the storm in his heart.

Silent and still until morning came.
