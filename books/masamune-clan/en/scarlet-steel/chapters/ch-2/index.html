Chapter 2

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

K enji’s words hung in the air, heavy and suffocating. Elder Mei… was attacked? And I was the suspect?

“What?” I managed, shaking my head. “Where did this happen?”

Kenji drummed his fingers on the desk, a nervous habit I recognized all too well. “This morning, around ten. In the main hall. She and Tadashi were preparing for an inter-clan meeting. They were in the back courtyard when an arrow—aimed at her heart—was fired.”

My stomach lurched. The main hall was restricted . How…?

“Is she… alive?”

“Yes. She reacted quickly, so it hit her upper chest, near her right arm. Tadashi administered an antidote and first aid immediately. Sadi, who was on patrol, took her to the medical hall while Tadashi searched for the attacker.”

“And?” I pressed, my voice tight. “Did he find them?”

Kenji shook his head.

“No trace? No weapon? Surely you can track the arrow’s trajectory.”

“The shot came from a tree in the back courtyard, bordering the forest. The attacker left a maze of false trails. They knew the terrain, escaped fast, and took the weapon. Clearly planned.”

“Tadashi didn’t see anything? A description?”

Kenji leaned back, his expression that of a school principal about to discipline a truant student. “He said he only caught a glimpse; he was focused on Elder Mei. He’s with the elders now. They asked me to… gather the usual suspects.”

My gaze narrowed. “You’re accusing me ?”

“Several witnesses saw your argument with Elder Mei yesterday. You two often disagree. Motive.”

“Are you insane? That’s the most ridiculous thing I’ve ever heard! What would I possibly gain? If I wanted her dead, I’d hardly used such an obvious method. My arrow wouldn’t miss, and I wouldn’t be walking around waiting to be caught.”

Oh, shit. Did I just imply to Kenji that I could kill Elder Mei and get away with it? Smooth, Anko. Real smooth.

Kenji held up a hand. “Relax… I don’t believe you did it. This is standard procedure. If you’re innocent, you have nothing to fear. Where were you all day?”

“The usual,” I said, trying to sound casual. “Slept in—late, because of yesterday’s mission. Left for the academy around 10:30, had lunch with Katsu, then taught until the afternoon.”

“So, no alibi for this morning,” Kenji pointed out.

My mouth opened, then closed. He had a point. No one could vouch for me.

Damn it.

“So, you’re arresting every clan member who slept in? Because that’s your logic?” I countered, my tone laced with sarcasm. This was absurd.

Kenji sighed. “Let’s go to the meeting room. Perhaps you can offer some insight after hearing Tadashi’s statement.”

I got up and practically stormed out. Akito’s booming voice was the first thing I heard when I entered the small meeting room.

“—utterly incompetent! You were supposed to improve security! First, the Ishikawa clan infiltrates, you claim it’s resolved, and now this ? You were the first to see the attacker, the first to give chase, you know the hall better than anyone, and you found nothing ? No perpetrator, no weapon. Are you incapable of doing anything correctly?”

Akito was practically foaming at the mouth, towering over Tadashi, who knelt, head bowed, in the room’s center. All the elders and several clan officials were present. Uncle Masada presided. Shiro sat beside him, arms crossed. She gave me a sharp look as I entered, then turned her attention back to Akito, her gaze critical.

Akito looked ready to launch into another tirade, so I cut in.

“Have you questioned the security guards on duty this morning, Master Akito?” I asked, my tone deliberately bored and accusatory. “Shouldn’t they have been more alert? How did they miss this? Did they find anything unusual? I heard Sadi was leading the hall patrol at the time?”

Heh. Akito’s face flushed crimson the moment I mentioned his son’s stellar performance.

“Welcome, Anko,” he sneered. “So, Kenji caught you. Do you even have an alibi? Sadi’s team followed protocol, patrolled as scheduled, and found nothing suspicious. Do you know what that implies?”

He stepped closer, invading my personal space. “It implies the culprit is someone with access to the hall, familiar with the terrain, the patrol schedules, and Elder Mei’s routine – someone with a motive . Didn’t you have a rather… public … disagreement with her yesterday, Anko? A significant one?”

The room buzzed with murmurs.

“Watch yourself,” I hissed, my face hot. “Perhaps Kenji should question everyone in this room. We all know the security details, don’t we? Including you . Do you have a problem with Elder Mei, Master Akito? I heard one of her decisions negatively affected your business interests.”

Kenji interrupted, addressing Tadashi. “Have you secured the back courtyard? We need to overhaul the patrol techniques, schedules, and routes. Too many vulnerabilities there.”

“Yes, I’ll implement it immediately,” Tadashi said.

“And erect a shield around the entire hall compound,” Akito ordered. “If anyone even brushes against the fence, the guards need to be alerted.”

“Assuming the patrol leader is more capable than Sadi was today,” Hoseki, one of the clan officials – in charge of logistics and transportation – remarked.

“What’s that supposed to mean?” Akito rounded on him. Then, pointing at Tadashi, “If Kenji and this boy did their jobs, we wouldn’t have intruders. Why not implement a single-entry system, with escorts? The compound security checks are inadequate. You only check for weapons and prohibited items. Clearly, that’s insufficient.”

The situation quickly deteriorated. Akito and Hoseki, both businessmen supposedly supporting clan operations, were constantly at odds, vying for market share. The room was devolving into a petty squabble. Uncle Masada finally intervened.

“Enough!” he roared. The room fell silent. Hoseki shot Akito a smug look. Akito merely snorted, but he quieted. They were like children fighting over a toy.

“Tadashi, Shiro, wait in my office. The rest of you, dismissed. This meeting is adjourned.”

Everyone dispersed. I decided to check on Elder Mei at the medical hall. We clashed, certainly, but I didn’t hate her. She was simply doing her job, in her own… infuriating … manner.

Elder Mei was asleep when I arrived. Doctor Wu had just left, leaving Katsu on duty. He was adjusting the IV drip, his face brightening when he saw me. The smile vanished when I gave him only a curt nod.

“What’s wrong?” he asked softly.

I shook my head, unwilling to relive the day’s events. “How is she?”

“The arrow wound wasn’t too deep; Doctor Wu treated it. She’ll require some physical therapy, limited movement for a few weeks.”

“The poison? Did you identify it?”

Katsu sighed. “Just a type of snake venom. Fortunately, Tadashi administered the antidote quickly, as it’s lethal if untreated. But her condition is… unstable. We’re monitoring her closely, hoping for the best.”

I frowned. Snake venom… that sounded like Yamato Yamaguchi, the city’s underworld boss. But why would he target Elder Mei? What was his angle?

Katsu left me alone with her. A few minutes later, Elder Mei started trembling.

“Elder Mei!” I jumped up.

She was seizing, eyes wide and staring, struggling to breathe.

“Katsu!” I yelled, but he didn’t hear. I ran to the door and screamed, “HELP!”

Two nurses rushed in, Katsu close behind.

“What happened?” he asked, his voice edged with panic.

“She woke up, started seizing!”

Katsu issued orders to the nurses, and they moved with practiced efficiency. I could only stand there, frozen in the corner, as Elder Mei’s body convulsed again. She suddenly sat up, her face pallid, and vomited a thick, black, bloody mass. Katsu ushered me out while they worked on her.

I found a nurse and told her to get Sadi.

“I need a favor,” I said as soon as he arrived, about an hour later. I’d been keeping watch on Elder Mei. She was awake, but still weak. “We need someone guarding her around the clock. Create a shift schedule. I’m worried she’ll need help again.”

Sadi shrugged. “No problem. I’ll set it up. I can take the first watch tonight, but I’m supposed to help Tadashi set up the shield around the hall tomorrow morning. Could you stay here for a bit? I’ll brief the patrol team.”

“Yeah, thanks.”

I returned to the bedside and asked if she needed anything, perhaps water. She shook her head, gesturing for me to sit.

“Tell me what’s happening in the clan,” she said. I updated her on the meeting. She listened, her expression thoughtful.

“Akito’s right about tightening security at the hall. We can’t allow this to happen again,” she said. “His suggestions about the shield and single-entry access are valid. Ideally, we’d have individual escorts, but we likely lack the manpower.”

I stared at her. Even half-dead, Elder Mei was prioritizing the clan. She managed a weak smile.

“Thank you for watching over me. You’re a good person, Anko. I know I can rely on you.”

“You’re welcome, Elder Mei,” I mumbled, feeling a blush rise on my neck. “Just doing what I can. I don’t want to see you, or anyone, get hurt.”

Elder Mei nodded. “If you have time tomorrow, assist Tadashi with the shield. It requires significant energy to install; it’s not easy. You’re his sealing mentor. You can check his work, ensure it’s complete.”

I agreed and kept her company for a while, then said goodbye when Sadi returned to take watch. The next morning, I encountered Akito and Doctor Wu talking near the main hall gate.

“Ah, our prime suspect,” Akito greeted, a nasty smirk on his face. “Failed to kill Elder Mei with an arrow, so you tried again at the medical hall last night. Too bad you screwed that up as well, eh?”

“What are you talking about?” I hissed. This man seriously needed a good slap.

“Don’t make baseless accusations, Akito,” Doctor Wu said calmly. “I don’t believe Anko is guilty. If she wanted Elder Mei dead, she wouldn’t have called for the nurses when Elder Mei had a seizure. Without immediate treatment, that allergen would have been fatal.”

I stared at him, stunned. “Allergen? You mean someone tried to poison her again last night?”

Doctor Wu shook his head. “No, the allergen is a substance that triggers allergic reactions. In sensitive individuals, it can cause severe reactions, even death. It appears this allergen was mixed with the snake venom on the arrow. Because the allergic reaction went untreated initially, it nearly proved fatal last night.”

I just nodded, trying to process this. I’d been wondering why the attacker used poison – typically used for stealth assassinations – in such an overt arrow attack.

It was a triple threat. If the arrow failed, the poison would activate. If the poison was neutralized, the often-overlooked allergic reaction would finish the job.

My mind raced. Who knew about Elder Mei’s allergies? Medical personnel? A chilling thought struck me.

Katsu…he had access. He knew about allergies.

But he’d helped Elder Mei during the seizure. If he wanted her dead, he could have simply faked the treatment, declared it too late. He could have injected her with something lethal and walked away, no problem.

“Does someone need medical expertise to use this allergen, Doctor?” I asked.

Doctor Wu shook his head. “This allergen is actually a common substance. People are allergic to all sorts of things – dust, nuts, milk, insect stings, pollen… Anyone could do it, as long as they knew Elder Mei’s specific allergy. It’s highly likely the culprit is someone who’s known her for a long time.”

After thanking Doctor Wu, I hurried to the back courtyard of the hall. Tadashi and Sadi were already there.

Tadashi stood in the center of the courtyard, scribbling in a notebook. His brow was furrowed; he was muttering to himself. Sadi walked from the courtyard’s edge, reporting the distance he’d covered.

Tadashi nodded, jotting down the information. Then he instructed Sadi to walk in another direction, calculating the distance.

“Shield design finished yet?” I asked. “Elder Mei sent me to oversee.”

“This is the final calculation,” he said, handing me the notebook. It contained the shield’s specifications.

An energy shield is created using inner power, visual reconstruction, and a bit of math.

Sounds simple, but in reality, very few can actually erect one. The energy required depends on the area, thickness, type of protection, mobility, and duration.

For instance, a sun shield only blocks energy attacks. A star shield is solid, deflecting physical attacks, and can even be designed to repel rain. It’s highly adaptable but drains energy like crazy.

If we used that to protect the hall, yesterday’s arrow wouldn’t have gotten through. But the person powering the shield would probably pass out from exhaustion in fifteen minutes.

You can adjust the energy source, distributing the burden among those inside the shield. But then everyone would be under constant, intense pressure. No one could work in the hall for hours like that. So, Tadashi wasn’t going with that option.

He’d chosen a detector shield, commonly used as the outermost layer of residential protection. Lightweight, low energy consumption, thin, invisible, doesn’t block attacks, but incredibly sensitive. The range and sensitivity type can be quickly adjusted.

I examined the design in the notebook.

The shield’s energy source would be the guards. It would alert them when someone approached within five meters of the shield wall. The guards would instantly know the breach’s exact location and the identity of the trespasser, allowing for a swift response. They could also add or remove authorized personnel from the access list, making it perfect for a single-entry security system.

The shield specifications would be copied onto paper and planted at the central point where Tadashi stood. It would be activated with his power until the transfer was complete, then sealed.

“You should add location tracking for everyone inside the shield, so we know their whereabouts at all times. That would help compensate for the lack of individual escorts,” I suggested.

More complex specifications meant more energy, but I figured it was still manageable.

Tadashi nodded, making a note. “I want to test each specification before we plant it. Can you try to breach the shield from various points around the perimeter? Sadi will check the response time.”

I nodded. Tadashi closed his eyes, concentrating. Slowly, a white glow appeared, forming a dome-shaped shield wall. Currently, it was entirely sustained by Tadashi’s energy.

“Raise the perimeter to match the fence height, about two meters,” I said. “I’ll test four points. And we’ll time how long it takes Sadi to reach each breach.”

I headed for the western edge of the shield, near the swamp behind the hall. A faint tingling sensation tingled as I crossed the barrier. Thirty-five seconds later, Sadi arrived, running. I told him to relay the time to Tadashi.

Then I walked toward the northern end, near the medical hall. Sadi arrived in forty-eight seconds.

As I approached the eastern end, near the elders’ offices, I spotted Hoseki approaching Akito near the restrooms.

Unwilling to deal with either of them, I took a detour. As I passed the southern end, near Uncle Masada’s office, the shield suddenly flickered and died .

I frowned. Was Tadashi already exhausted? It hadn’t even been an hour.

What had happened?

I hesitated, waiting for Sadi. When he didn’t appear, I headed back to the starting point. They seemed to have moved under the tree.

From a distance, I saw Tadashi kneeling, one knee on the ground. Beside him, a figure lay sprawled.

Wait… that’s…

My breath caught.

Sadi .

Someone had attacked him.

I broke into a run.
