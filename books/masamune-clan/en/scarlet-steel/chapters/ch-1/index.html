Chapter 1

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

W hen I received the mission briefing, I knew there’d be some bullshit involved. But belly dancing and doing backbends on some sleazy informant’s yacht? Elder Mei, in all her infinite wisdom, had simply said, “Create a distraction while Tadashi retrieves the documents.”

A distraction. Right .

Tadashi had provided me a costume, but I was thinking, you know, normal dinner party. Mingle, maybe spill a drink or two. Naturally, it was never that simple. Tadashi got caught—and while those goons chased him with lassos that materialized out of thin air, I ended up raiding the enemy boss’s room, fighting off a small army, and feeling my back threaten to quit its day job.

Crawling out of that viper’s nest, clutching the clan’s precious documents, I hurled my heels at the tree where Tadashi was hiding. Missed his head, damn it . But that didn’t stop me from launching the rest of my “accessories” his way:

The tiny, beaded clutch that couldn’t even hold a damn onigiri; the dangly gold earrings that snagged in my hair every five seconds; the four-layered pearl necklace choking the life out of me; the ring I’d had to constantly adjust to keep the diamond from popping out mid-punch; the itchy silver bracelet that dug painfully into my wrist with every block; the gold chain bracelet on my upper arm that some thug nearly ripped off; the ankle bracelet with bells —because stealth is clearly overrated. Seriously! Is this the Masamune clan or a goddamn traveling jewelry show?

I ignored Tadashi, who scrambled to collect the clan’s precious inventory, and grabbed a change of clothes from his bag. I ripped that silk dress off like it was on fire—too much skin, too many slits. They might as well have sent me in buck naked!

“This is it. My last mission. I swear on my brother’s grave,” I growled, pulling on a tank top, jacket, and pants. “I’m leaving the clan. Moving continents. Changing my name. And anyone they send after me? Dead. Absolutely dead .” I shoved the documents and that god-awful dress into the bag. Tadashi seemed to have gathered all my projectiles, and I tossed him the bag.

The only reason I didn’t vanish into the night, after being paraded around like some cheap prize, was Tadashi. Elder Mei would have his head on a platter. She’d even told him to wear makeup and this flimsy, see-through… thing that was about as practical as a paper umbrella in a hurricane. Her brilliant plan? If I refused to “charm” our greasy informant, Tadashi would have to give it a shot. Are you kidding me?!

Tadashi’s definitely old enough to make his own choices, but I’d been looking out for him since he was a snot-nosed kid. How could I let him get pawed by that tattooed, armpit-reeking slimeball? Not a freaking chance in hell.

I vented the whole way back. The stale-ass shrimp I’d nearly choked on, the waves that threatened to send my stomach contents overboard, the pervert who’d grinned every time a gust of wind gave him a free show… I wanted to gut that informant with my blade fan and feed his remains to the sharks and those freakish octopus monsters.

Tadashi just listened, the good little soldier he is. Eventually, I sounded like some deranged street vendor hawking rotten fish.

“So,” I said, my voice tight with barely suppressed rage, “what do you think?”

He looked at me like I’d just asked him to solve the world’s most complicated riddle. Classic Tadashi.

“Uh… thanks for… not making me do it alone,” he mumbled, wincing.

Asdlkjfgh! Seriously? That’s all he’s got?

“Don’t you want to, I don’t know, protest ? Have the elders ever ordered you to play lapdog to a scumbag like that before?”

“I’ve, uh, had to work undercover as a waiter before—”

Pfft!

“Don’t play dumb!” I snapped. I knew damn well that since he got that Fuun tattoo, he’d been playing the role of servant or slave, especially after they stripped him of his heir status. But that wasn’t the point. “This was a red mission! You haven’t even gone through Yuwaku’s class, have you?”

Yuwaku. Fifty years old and still the reigning champion of the class I despise the most. The seduction class. The one where they teach you how to use your body as a weapon. The last time they tried to stick me with one of those missions, I broke the target’s teeth and left him with a very… uncomfortable groin. Made it crystal clear I wasn’t playing that game. They were forced to scratch my name off the red mission list.

Tadashi fell silent. My stomach twisted. I stopped dead, crossing my arms.

“Don’t tell me you’re on board with the elders and their idea of ‘appropriate’ missions? That you’re okay with them treating clan members, like me , like this?” I hissed.

Tadashi frowned. “I would never disrespect you, Anko.”

“Do you even know what disrespect is? If your boss tells you to strip every time you serve him a drink, that’s not okay! Even if he doesn’t lay a hand on you. If you can’t see that, you’re no better than the elders. Good thing you didn’t become clan leader.”

…Okay, low blow. I didn’t actually agree with their decision to demote him and put Shiro in his place. And who knows if Shiro would even be any different, but I was so furious with the whole clan I could spit. Before I completely lost it, I stomped ahead, refusing to speak another word to Tadashi.

Back at the compound, I headed straight for the Clan Hall. Elder Mei was just wrapping up another mission briefing—probably assigning someone else to be groped for the clan’s benefit. I barged into the meeting room and slammed the documents down.

Elder Mei barely flinched. She simply raised a perfectly sculpted eyebrow. “Almost late, Anko. Something the matter?”

Something the matter?!

My face burned like a raging sunset.

“Your precious informant introduced me as a ‘guest attendant’ at his club.”

And after, what, thirty minutes of forced small talk, that maggot started pawing at me, while the informant sat there, enjoying the show!

Elder Mei took her sweet time opening the packet, reading the documents, then rolling them back up like they were the most fascinating thing in the world. Finally, she looked at me like I was a toddler who’d just thrown a tantrum.

“And? It’s perfectly normal for a club owner to introduce a new attendant to his regulars. It should have expedited your work,” she said, her tone dripping with condescension.

I ground my teeth. “I’ve told you before, I’m not selling my body for any mission. And why was Tadashi even put in charge of something like this?”

Elder Mei crossed her arms, a little shrug that screamed indifference. “You weren’t asked to sleep with him, Anko. It’s not a brothel.”

“Oh, so that makes it okay for them to touch me?” I planted my hands on my hips, my voice rising. “I didn’t think our clan would sink this low. If anyone with enough cash can pay to have me manhandled, what’s the difference between us and some back-alley pimp? Might as well hang a sign out front: ‘Masamune Clan: Male and female entertainers, ninja skills included. Satisfaction guaranteed, or your money back.’”

A flash of red stained Elder Mei’s cheeks. Good.

“Don’t be dramatic. No one is above serving the clan, Anko. Not Masada, not Tadashi, and certainly not you. This clan isn’t your personal babysitter, catering to your every whim. Everyone serves. Even the kids participate in clan activities. Why should you, a full-fledged member, be any different? I’m sure your late father raised you with a sense of duty, didn’t he?”

Oh, she did NOT just bring my father into this.

“Don’t try to deflect!” I snapped. “There’s something seriously messed up with how this clan operates, and you’re too blind to see it. Just wait. Others will feel the same. I refuse to be groped. And if I ever have kids, they sure as hell won’t be subjected to this. I’m not asking for the moon, just a little respect for my boundaries. If you can’t handle that, then I’d rather—”

“Afternoon. Sorry I’m late,” Tadashi said, breathless, from the doorway. He shot me an annoyed look, but I pointedly looked away, my face still hot.

Yeah, because I’d ditched him and taken the horse, hoping he wouldn’t catch up.

“Anko, submit a written report to Kenji. I need to speak with Tadashi alone. Our business is concluded. Please close the door on your way out,” Elder Mei said, already rising.

I curled my lip. “My pleasure,” I replied, the sarcasm thick enough to choke on.

Elder Mei gave me a sharp look, but I just rolled my eyes and stalked out. If they think a grown-ass member of the force is going to just roll over and play nice, they’ve got another thing coming.

The slam of the door echoed behind me. Small victories. They treat me like dirt; I don’t owe them any fake respect.

After furiously messing up my already disastrous hair and kicking rocks all the way, I detoured to the clan’s dojo. I took out my frustrations on some practice dummies, slicing them to ribbons with my blade fan, then pummeled a punching bag that had the audacity to just hang there.

The impromptu training session helped, taking the edge off my urge to torch the hall. I headed over to the long bench where I’d stashed my water bottle. My plan: go home, scrub myself raw, soak in a hot bath, chug some chocolate milk, and pass out until next Tuesday.

Sounded perfect—except for the fact that I apparently didn’t pay attention when a dark shadow tried to sneak up on me.

Instinct took over. Blade met flesh.

“AAARGH!”

“Katsu?!” I hissed, yanking out the blade fan I’d reflexively jammed behind me. What the hell was he doing sneaking up on me?

The tall, white-coated idiot crumpled to his knees, groaning, his right hand clutching his lower ribs, which were now bleeding. I bit my lip, wincing. Crap. Deep or not, that had to hurt like hell. I’d put my whole body into that.

Katsu glanced at the dojo entrance, checking for witnesses. Just us. He let out a muffled yelp, then his healing ability kicked in, closing the wound. Thank god.

I cleared my throat, fighting the urge to facepalm. Greeting my boyfriend with sticky hair, a sweat-soaked tank top, and a fresh stab wound? Not exactly on my bucket list.

“You okay? Need a hand up?” I asked, guilt twisting my stomach.

Katsu shook his head, giving me a shaky thumbs-up. He’s the clan’s resident miracle worker, patching up everything from paper cuts to severed limbs like they’re brand new, but he doesn’t use his powers for everyday stuff. It drains him, and the patient feels the pain all over again—sometimes bad enough to trigger a heart attack. And, let’s be real, the clan would exploit the hell out of him if they could.

He wobbled to his feet and sank onto the bench, his forehead beaded with cold sweat, his face still a little green around the gills. Healed, yeah, but definitely not pain-free.

“Sorry. After that mission… I’m a little jumpy. Thought you were some creep,” I said, offered him my water bottle.

He managed a weak smile. “My fault. Should’ve known you’d react… explosively.”

I watched him drink. He brushed a lock of hair—the color of corn silk and softer than it seemed—out of his eyes. It was a little long for a usually meticulous doctor, but it fit his laid-back vibe. He raised an eyebrow, looking up at me. His forehead creased, then his lips curved into a smile. His Adam’s apple bobbed as he swallowed.

“Anko,” Katsu said, his brown eyes twinkling. “You’re looking at me like you want to devour me.”

Huh. Not a bad idea.

“Is that so?” I asked, my breath catching. I tore my gaze away from his neck and grinned. “Funny, I am hungry. Starving, actually. But also exhausted.”

“Ah, yes… you must be worn out. How about a massage? I give a very good massage.”

I laughed. “Tempting, but I’ll pass. Hot bath, and then straight to bed for me.”

“Are you sure? I’m off shift. I could whip up your favorite udon. Dinner under the stars?”

Heh. Persistent little bastard. I finally caved. We made a quick stop at the grocery store. After I tweaked the protective seal so he could get into my place, he started cooking while I hit the bath.

We carried our udon bowls to the backyard and settled onto the wooden deck, side by side.

“You’re a damn good cook, you know that?” I said, slurping noodles. Okay, he’s stable, kind, and can cook. What more could a girl ask for?

Katsu grinned, those dimples of his practically begging to be poked.

“Well, duh. I’ve been learning since I was a kid. Even did a stint as a chef’s assistant during med school at that fancy restaurant in Arihara.”

“That’s the city in the next kingdom over, right?”

I stared at the small pond in the backyard, my mind drifting to Arihara. Maybe it was too late, but I’d always wanted to study there. Something completely unrelated to ninja bullshit. Maybe even teach elementary school. Anything would be better than stagnating in the clan.

It’s not the missions themselves, it’s the… everything else. The twisted values, the politics, the backstabbing. Eight years mentoring Tadashi at the academy, only to be constantly hauled before the elders to answer for him? It’s enough to drive anyone insane.

Honestly… if the clan and I are so incompatible, leaving should be the easy solution, right? But there’s my father’s reputation to consider—he was a respected member of the clan. And, I guess, a sense of… obligation. They raised me, after all.

“Hey,” Katsu whispered, nudging me. “You went somewhere else. Clan troubles?”

Crap. Caught daydreaming. I forced a grin.

“Just… I don’t know. Maybe I need a change of pace. What’s your take on the clan, now that you’re here?”

Katsu inhaled, and I felt the vibration of it against my arm.

“Well, I’m lucky. I found an angel like you… Haha—ow! No pinching…”

I snorted. “Seriously!”

His laughter faded. He draped his arm around my shoulders, his fingers gently stroking my upper arm.

“I am serious. I can’t really judge the clan as a whole…I’ve only been here six months. Honestly, I was surprised you even offered me the position. Doesn’t Doctor Wu have any apprentices here?”

I shrugged. Doctor Wu’s the senior clan physician, mostly working out of the main hall now. He retired from the city hospital, but he’s still active here. Apparently, not many clan members go into medicine, let alone apprentice with him. The few who did were too junior, didn’t have the qualifications. He wanted someone with a medical degree and practical experience.

“Anyway, I can’t really give you an objective opinion, other than my own experience. My dad was from the clan; my mom was the daughter of a tribal leader in some village. They met, got married, while he was on a long-term mission. As soon as his duty was done, he left her. Never looked back. Apparently, it was amicable—she didn’t chase after him—but it still made me resent him. So, naturally, I don’t have a high opinion of the clan.”

“Is your father still alive?”

“Yeah,” Katsu whispered. “But I’m not ready to forgive him. I don’t want to see him.”

“So why take the job?”

“Money? Haha… Seriously, though, once I got here, the people seemed… normal. Typical, I guess. Some are decent, some are assholes. The elders are strict, but I haven’t had any issues with them. I’m rarely called to the main hall; only when Doctor Wu’s unavailable.”

Huh. So Katsu doesn’t feel the pressure, the… wrongness… of this place. Maybe I’m the weird one.

Katsu gave my arm a gentle squeeze.

“If you’re that unhappy, why not leave? The world’s a big place. Find somewhere new, somewhere that actually excites you. Or is that forbidden? Will they hunt you down and kill you?”

“Not exactly. If I keep the Masamune name, I’m still technically on call for assignments. If I drop the name, no assignments, no real repercussions… They’d just note my last known location.”

Oh, and they’d seize all my assets within the compound, and anything registered under the family name outside of it. I can’t imagine leaving the house, all those memories of my family… but maybe I do need to turn the page, close that chapter, and start a new one.

Honestly… I just need to find the guts to do it.

I glanced at Katsu, and he gave me that warm, supportive, seductive smile. Maybe, someday, I could draw strength from him, and finally walk away.

“It’s getting late. I should go,” Katsu said, kissing my forehead. “Sleep tight. I’ll pick you up at the academy for lunch tomorrow.”

After he left, I slept like the dead. The next day, I felt a thousand times better. But after I finished teaching, one of the clan members tracked me down. Apparently, Kenji, the head of security, wanted to see me in the Clan Hall.

My stomach dropped. When I got there, I went straight to his office.

“Is this about yesterday’s report?”

“No.”

I crossed my arms. “Then what?”

Kenji gestured for me to sit. He watched me with that intense gaze, like a hawk sizing up its prey. Or a cop about to interrogate a suspect.

“Someone attacked Elder Mei with a poisoned arrow,” he said slowly. “Tell me everything you did today, Anko.”
