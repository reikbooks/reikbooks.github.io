It was a triple threat. If the arrow failed, the poison would activate. If the poison was neutralized, the often-overlooked allergic reaction would finish the job.

Katsu…he had access. He knew about allergies.

But he’d helped Elder Mei during the seizure. If he wanted her dead, he could have simply faked the treatment, declared it too late. He could have injected her with something lethal and walked away, no problem.

People are allergic to all sorts of things – dust, nuts, milk, insect stings, pollen… Anyone could do it, as long as they knew Elder Mei’s specific allergy. It’s highly likely the culprit is someone who’s known her for a long time.”

Tadashi nodded, jotting down the information. Then he instructed Sadi to walk in another direction, calculating the distance.

“Shield design finished yet?” I asked. “Elder Mei sent me to oversee.”

“This is the final calculation,” he said, handing me the notebook. It contained the shield’s specifications.

An energy shield is created using inner power, visual reconstruction, and a bit of math.

Sounds simple, but in reality, very few can actually erect one. The energy required depends on the area, thickness, type of protection, mobility, and duration.

For instance, a sun shield only blocks energy attacks. A star shield is solid, deflecting physical attacks, and can even be designed to repel rain. It’s highly adaptable but drains energy like crazy.

If we used that to protect the hall, yesterday’s arrow wouldn’t have gotten through. But the person powering the shield would probably pass out from exhaustion in fifteen minutes.

You can adjust the energy source, distributing the burden among those inside the shield. But then everyone would be under constant, intense pressure. No one could work in the hall for hours like that. So, Tadashi wasn’t going with that option.

He’d chosen a detector shield, commonly used as the outermost layer of residential protection. Lightweight, low energy consumption, thin, invisible, doesn’t block attacks, but incredibly sensitive. The range and sensitivity type can be quickly adjusted.

I examined the design in the notebook.

The shield’s energy source would be the guards. It would alert them when someone approached within five meters of the shield wall. The guards would instantly know the breach’s exact location and the identity of the trespasser, allowing for a swift response. They could also add or remove authorized personnel from the access list, making it perfect for a single-entry security system.

The shield specifications would be copied onto paper and planted at the central point where Tadashi stood. It would be activated with his power until the transfer was complete, then sealed.

There. In the corner. Surrounded by chaos. Crates smashed, boxes ripped open. Some were burning. The overhead sprinklers had activated, drenching everything in a futile attempt to control the flames. Tadashi struggled to his feet, leaning heavily against the wall. Hoseki… he was down, unmoving.

I bit my lip. I’d stabbed Hoseki’s shoulder…

Before I could move to check on them, the whir of the nunchaku struck the back of my head.

The world went black. My shattered ribs ground together—a searing agony—as I fell.

Vaguely, I heard a scream. Mine? I blinked, trying to get up, but my vision was a blur. I couldn’t move, like I was trapped in quicksand.

Someone was holding my shoulders. Someone familiar—someone I should know—but their name kept slipping away. They whispered something, but the words were lost.

An explosion overhead.

The ground shook.

The person holding me pressed a hand to my broken ribs. Then, a shockwave—blinding pain, PAIN, and everything turned white.

I woke to Hoseki’s grim face. My head felt stuffed with cotton wool, my thoughts sluggish. What happened—Katsu?!

“Where is he?” I rasped.

Hoseki frowned, still clutching his injured shoulder.

“Tadashi? Trying to dig us out.”

He gestured. We were still in the warehouse. Most of the walls had collapsed, sealing the exit. But the area around me was clear, as if protected by a shield. I turned. Tadashi was at the blocked entrance, hacking at the rubble with a pair of kunai.

I shook my head, trying to clear it.

“Not Tadashi. Katsu! The damn doctor!”

“Oh… your traitor boyfriend? He blew the cave roof and ran.”

“He’s not my boyfriend!” I snarled.

Hoseki just shook his head, a pitying look on his face. “His real name isn’t Katsu. It’s Shirasu. Shirasu Mitsurugi. He’s been spying on us since the Ishikawa incident.”

He told me about Shirasu’s illicit dealings with Yamato, who, surprise, was also a Mitsurugi. When the attacks started, Hoseki had increased his surveillance. He’d followed Yamato and seen him reporting to Shirasu.

“I thought you were working with Shirasu,” Hoseki said.

I scoffed. “And I thought you were working with Yamato, committing those murders.”

Our argument was cut short by the sound of falling rocks. Tadashi coughed but kept digging, clearing a path.

When the exit was finally clear, Tadashi stumbled toward us. His hair was matted with dust and grime. His clothes were soaked and ripped, his face and body crisscrossed with cuts from the creatures’ blades.

“We need to report to the main hall,” Tadashi said. “Before Shirasu can do more damage. The shield in the main hall isn’t up yet; he can come and go as he pleases.”

Hoseki nodded grimly and headed out.

“How did you find me?” I asked Tadashi.

“The Sagara clan paper I gave you. It had a tracking seal.”

“Oh gods. I forgot to read it. What did it say?”

“Information on the dark creature. It’s called a Doboro. Weak to water.”

I glanced at him and the sprinkler. Of course.

“Do you… know anything about Shirasu?” I asked as we walked.

He shook his head. “I rarely went to the main hall for treatment. I usually asked Raito to tend to my injuries, so I hardly ever saw Shirasu. Maybe he was avoiding me and Shiro, afraid we’d recognize him.”

I frowned. “Recognize him? Why?”

Tadashi hesitated, then swallowed. “Shirasu… he… he’s the one who killed my mother.”

I stared at him, speechless. Then I took a deep breath and walked beside him in silence.

That night, I gathered everything Shirasu had given me, everything that reminded me of him. I dumped it all in a box and left it by the trash bin outside. But the next morning, a necklace lay on my bedside table. A flower pendant. A small, white tulip—a symbol of apology.

I froze. Shirasu? He’d been here ?

Oh, gods. I’d forgotten to remove his access from the house’s detector shield. What did he want? I’d slept soundly, unaware he’d been in my room… A shiver ran down my spine.

He could have hurt me, killed me, while I slept. But he hadn’t. And he hadn’t taken anything valuable. In fact… there was one thing that had bothered me since the warehouse.

Before he fled, he’d healed my broken ribs. And when the cave roof collapsed, he’d shielded me from the falling debris.

I… I didn’t want to think about it. Didn’t want to consider that maybe, just maybe, he hadn’t meant for any of this to happen. That there was an explanation. I couldn’t afford to hope. Not again.

He was the enemy. He’d lied, betrayed me, framed me for murder. I looked at the tulip. A tiny slip of paper was tied to the chain.

Forgive me, Anko. I’m in your debt.

I crumpled the paper, then dropped the necklace into a jewelry box, slamming the lid shut. I left the room, refusing to look back.

Epilogue: Tadashi Masamune

Epilogue: Tadashi Masamune

S hirasu was a ghost. His personal files at the clan, blood test results, lineage records – everything that proved his existence had been erased. Not here, not in Arihara. His supposed graduation from the medical university there, his forged assignment letter… all gone.

Nobody knew who Katsu Masamune, or Shirasu Mitsurugi, truly was . His origins, his parents… a complete mystery. Anko had mentioned he’d claimed his mother was dead, his father still alive within the clan. If that was true, no one stepped forward. Perhaps he came from an unregistered marriage, outside the official clan lineage. That would explain how he’d slipped past the main hall’s staff screening.

Hoseki severed his ties with Yamato, who’d fled, abandoning his criminal empire. From the few of his men we captured and interrogated, Yamato appeared to be the sole link between that group and the Mitsurugi Clan.

Still, we couldn’t relax. The attacks had been strategic, multi-pronged. Elder Mei, Father’s right hand, oversaw clan operations. Akito had been a pillar of our finances. And Kenji… head of security.

The Mitsurugi had crippled us. Another blow was unthinkable.

Security tightened to a stifling degree. All key figures, including Father and Shiro, now had personal bodyguards. Father had forbidden Shiro from returning home; they were both living in the isolated confines of the main hall. I couldn’t come and go without written authorization. Even seeing or speaking with Father or Shiro required an appointment through the clan secretary.

Anko had taken a week-long suspension from the clan, along with a leave of absence from the academy. When she returned, she was… subdued. Shiro mentioned her performance had declined.

“You need to help her,” Shiro had told me during a briefing. “She’s struggling. Leaving or staying… she can’t decide. Duty, responsibility, the need for validation, the need to be useful… she’s clinging to everything that binds her here. This clan is her identity. If she’s not a Masamune, who is she? That’s the question she needs to answer. Help her find it.”

It seemed Shiro understood I’d faced a similar crisis. After she’d been named heir, I’d been through hell. Stripped of my rights, dismissed, ridiculed… The way everyone treated me changed, forcing me to confront the fact that I was, apparently, nothing . If I wasn’t the heir, who was I? What was my purpose?

The question wasn’t easily answered. Finding one took time.

But what could I do for Anko? How could I help when she’d slammed the door in my face the last time I’d tried to visit?

A month after… everything… the elders handed Anko and me a new assignment. It was almost a cruel joke, a mirror image of our last mission, but with our roles reversed.

Anko was in charge, tasked with retrieving the documents. I was the distraction, working in the field.

I hadn’t even been in position with our target for thirty minutes when Anko signaled the extraction. Fast . I was just about to wrap up my task as a masseur in disguise and make my excuses when Anko burst in and practically hauled me out.

The client, at the hot springs resort, was not pleased. He chased us, clad only in a pathetically small towel. His men, seeing their boss in pursuit, joined in.

“I was almost finished,” I protested, once we’d finally escaped. I leaned over, hands on my knees, trying to catch my breath. Anko stood beside me, hands on her hips, equally winded.

“Seriously? You wanted to spend another minute with that Mr. Walrus Mustache?”

“No, but we didn’t have to run like that .”

Anko laughed, a genuine, carefree sound. “That was fun, though. Did you see his face when his towel slipped? Ahahaha…”

I couldn’t help but smile. I grabbed my bag and changed while Anko found a seat on a large rock at the cliff’s edge.

Below us, a pine forest stretched out, bisected by a path leading to the town. The distant lights of oil lamps flickered, like fallen stars against the velvet backdrop of the night. The moon was full, the sky a clear, starry canvas, a gentle breeze rustling the trees.

I sat beside Anko, offering her a drink.

“Can I ask you something?” she said after a moment. “You really don’t mind these kinds of missions?”

Ah. She was still wrestling with that. Anko refused red missions, believed the clan was wrong to even offer them.

The designation didn’t automatically mean prostitution. It simply meant we might need to use any means necessary, including seduction, to achieve our objective.

Elder Mei had marked this mission with two labels: black for Anko, red for me. Even though I was only assigned as a masseur, and it was unlikely to escalate, Anko had been furious when she’d read the plan, glaring at me when I’d said I was willing to accept it.

I looked out at the shimmering cityscape below, then shrugged. “What if I want to do them?” I asked quietly.

Anko stared at me, her expression a mixture of disbelief and something else.

“My body is mine ,” she said, her voice firmer now. “It’s my business, not the clan’s. I won’t tolerate them assigning me missions that demand I use it like this . Why can’t you see the clan gives you no real choice ?”

I took a deep breath. Since childhood, the elders had demanded everything from me—for the good of the clan. Obedience wasn’t a choice; it was an expectation. At first, it was suffocating. But over time, I began to see… possibilities.

Orders could be interpreted. Tweaked . As long as the main objective was achieved, and I was careful not to get caught, I had options. There were always choices. And always consequences.

How many times had I walked a path the elders disapproved of? They’d stripped me of my position as heir because my choices didn’t align with their expectations. That much was clear.

And yet, on quiet nights, when Father’s indifference felt like a crushing weight… I wondered. His deliberate distance—it almost seemed like a test . Perhaps he wanted me to learn to choose . Not to blindly follow the clan. Not even him. But to weigh my own decisions. To act not out of duty, but out of conviction .

He wanted me to question. To think. To decide. To take responsibility, even when my choices disappointed him.

Perhaps.

I turned to Anko. “Why do you have to judge my choice? I never thought I was better than you, Anko, or that you were lesser for refusing missions. We all have our boundaries. We serve the clan in our own ways,” I said. “Elder Mei respected your decision by adjusting the mission labels. If you have concerns about the missions, you can always talk to her.”

Anko was silent for a moment, her gaze thoughtful. Then, she let out a long breath. “If you don’t mind my asking… why are you like this?” Her voice was quiet but firm. “Why do you keep serving the clan, even though they mistreat you? No—scratch that. They downright abuse you. All the time. At least, that’s what it looks like to me.”

She shook her head, frustration etched on her face. “You were my student, Tadashi. I always went the extra mile to protect you. But lately… you don’t look like you need my protection anymore. I just don’t understand why you’d keep chaining yourself to the clan. Don’t tell me you actually enjoy the endless missions and punishments when you don’t meet their expectations. They’ve practically enslaved you! So, why?”

I looked down, swallowing hard. That question had been a constant companion since my demotion.

When they’d named me heir, they’d said it was because they believed in me. I hadn’t wanted to disappoint them. I’d felt obligated to serve. But when the clan discarded me—after everything I’d given up for them—I’d told myself I still wanted to serve.

Yet a part of me had wondered. If my work meant so little to them… if I was so easily replaced… then shouldn’t I just leave ?

Could I?

It had taken me a long time to find the answer. But in the end, I had.

“I’m always grateful for everything you did for me, Anko. But I can’t leave the clan,” I whispered.

Anko’s brows furrowed.

“Because the people I love are here,” I said, thinking of Father, Shiro, Raito. The faces of those I’d served during my atonement mission five years ago flashed through my mind. Some were awful people. But the rest… the rest were just people . I remembered their names, their voices. I’d seen their daily lives, learned their worries, their hopes.

Each person in this clan was a piece of something larger. And my work—what I did —mattered to them .

That made my struggles worthwhile.

“I’m willing to do the missions because I believe this clan is worth serving.”

A quiet moment stretched between us. Then, slowly, Anko smiled, a genuine, understanding smile. She nudged my shoulder. “You’re too idealistic. Too forgiving. But I’d rather have that than fear and blind obedience.” Her voice softened. “Thank you, Tadashi. I think… I think I know what I need to do.”

It was nearly midnight by the time I finished my report at the main hall.

“I’m home,” I murmured, closing the back door. The faint glow of the oil lamp on the porch filtered through the window, casting long shadows down the dark hallway. The kitchen was empty, silent. Odd. Raito wasn’t home yet.

A crumpled note on the kitchen table caught my eye. Raito’s sprawling handwriting.

“Out of town for 3 days with Master Toya – urgent project. Borrowed your piggy bank money – allowance GONE T_T Will pay you back, promise!”

Tch . That kid. I tossed the note in the trash. My gaze fell on the pile of dirty dishes in the sink, the overflowing laundry basket. A week’s worth of Raito’s mess, no doubt. Gods . I’d told him a thousand times…

How did Shiro manage him? When Shiro gave an order, Raito jumped. But when I said anything, Raito just grinned. Once, I’d let his dirty clothes pile up until he had nothing clean left to wear. The next day, he’d worn my clothes!

Ugh .

I sighed and tackled the dishes. Laundry tomorrow, then a full house cleaning. My recent mission schedule had been brutal; I hadn’t had time to keep up.

After a quick wash in the bathroom, I headed for my room. But as I stepped into the living room, a prickling unease settled over me. Something wasn’t right.

I paused, silently reviewing the status of my detector shield. Intact. Only family members could pass. It should be fine…

…but something was wrong .

A faint sound, from inside the house.

Barefoot, wearing only the inner layer of my kimono, I was unarmed. I glanced at my bag on the dining table, then slowly backed away, grabbing a tanto . I crept along the wall, following the sound.

Father’s study. The door was ajar.

I bit my lip.

The study door was never left open. And I’d locked it after Father and Shiro moved to the main hall.

So, what was that sound…?

I swallowed.

It sounded like… singing. A fragment of a lullaby, the one Mother used to sing to me. But… how could anyone get inside ?

My grip tightened on the tanto . I approached the door. The sound grew clearer. The humming stopped the instant I reached the doorway.

“I’ve been waiting, Tadashi,” a soft voice said.

I froze. A dark silhouette stood by the window, outlined by the dim lamplight.

“Who’s there?” I choked out. “How did you get in?”

A chuckle.

“The usual. Picked the lock,” he said, stepping forward, out of the shadows.

Before the intruder could fully reveal himself, I lunged, slashing at his chest.

But before my tanto connected, my detector shield flared .

A dark shape flashed from the side, slamming me against the wall, a web of crackling energy wrapping around me, searing my nerves. Shirasu’s Doboro . The creature was infused with lightning.

“Aaargh!”

I struggled, trying to cut the tentacles. The angle was impossible. The creature slashed my right arm, ripping the tanto from my grasp. It choked me, squeezing the air from my lungs, its electrified tentacles sending jolts of pain through my body.

The creature’s master – Shirasu – picked up my fallen blade, a thin smile playing on his lips. The Doboro’s grip loosened slightly. I formed a seal.

Burn.

The creature shrieked, a sound that pierced my eardrums. But instead of releasing me, instead of burning, it tightened its hold, shocking me again. The electricity surged through me, making my muscles spasm.

“Is that how you greet your guests, Tadashi?” Shirasu tutted.

Without warning, he drove my own tanto into my stomach, again and again, twisting the blade with sickening deliberation before ripping it out. The monster holding me, supporting me, forced me to my knees and made me prostrate myself in front of him.

I fought to stay conscious, but Shirasu slammed his boot down on my head, blurring my vision. I stared, numb, at the blood pooling on the tatami mats of Father’s study. My head throbbed, my thoughts scattered.

“Wh-what do you want?” I whispered, my throat raw. I didn’t know how long I could stay conscious with the gaping wounds in my stomach. Could I feign compliance, then attack when he was vulnerable?

AARGH!

I don’t know what Shirasu did, but searing pain exploded through my abdomen.

“Don’t worry, Tadashi. You don’t get to die this time. I can keep hurting and healing you. So behave,” Shirasu said. “I just want to talk .”
