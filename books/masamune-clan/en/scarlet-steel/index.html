Scarlet Steel

Book 9 of Masamune Clan Series

Haunted by the past. Trapped in the present. Torn between duty and desire.

Anko yearned for a peaceful life: good food, loyal friends, and the joy of teaching. But mentoring Tadashi has forced her into endless battles with the clan elders. Ready to break free, she finds herself entangled in a deadly case that threatens to consume her. Can she escape the shadows of the clan, or will she be dragged down into the abyss?

Elder Mei nodded. “If you have time tomorrow, assist Tadashi with the shield. It requires significant energy to install; it’s not easy. You’re his sealing mentor. You can check his work, ensure it’s complete.”

I agreed and kept her company for a while, then said goodbye when Sadi returned to take watch. The next morning, I encountered Akito and Doctor Wu talking near the main hall gate.

“Ah, our prime suspect,” Akito greeted, a nasty smirk on his face. “Failed to kill Elder Mei with an arrow, so you tried again at the medical hall last night. Too bad you screwed that up as well, eh?”

It appears this allergen was mixed with the snake venom on the arrow. Because the allergic reaction went untreated initially, it nearly proved fatal last night.”

It was a triple threat. If the arrow failed, the poison would activate. If the poison was neutralized, the often-overlooked allergic reaction would finish the job.

My mind raced. Who knew about Elder Mei’s allergies? Medical personnel? A chilling thought struck me.

Katsu…he had access. He knew about allergies.

But he’d helped Elder Mei during the seizure. If he wanted her dead, he could have simply faked the treatment, declared it too late. He could have injected her with something lethal and walked away, no problem.

Doctor Wu shook his head. “This allergen is actually a common substance. People are allergic to all sorts of things – dust, nuts, milk, insect stings, pollen… Anyone could do it, as long as they knew Elder Mei’s specific allergy. It’s highly likely the culprit is someone who’s known her for a long time.”

Tadashi nodded, jotting down the information. Then he instructed Sadi to walk in another direction, calculating the distance.

“Shield design finished yet?” I asked. “Elder Mei sent me to oversee.”

An energy shield is created using inner power, visual reconstruction, and a bit of math.

Sounds simple, but in reality, very few can actually erect one. The energy required depends on the area, thickness, type of protection, mobility, and duration.

For instance, a sun shield only blocks energy attacks. A star shield is solid, deflecting physical attacks, and can even be designed to repel rain. It’s highly adaptable but drains energy like crazy.

If we used that to protect the hall, yesterday’s arrow wouldn’t have gotten through. But the person powering the shield would probably pass out from exhaustion in fifteen minutes.

You can adjust the energy source, distributing the burden among those inside the shield. But then everyone would be under constant, intense pressure. No one could work in the hall for hours like that. So, Tadashi wasn’t going with that option.

He’d chosen a detector shield, commonly used as the outermost layer of residential protection. Lightweight, low energy consumption, thin, invisible, doesn’t block attacks, but incredibly sensitive. The range and sensitivity type can be quickly adjusted.

I examined the design in the notebook.

The shield’s energy source would be the guards. It would alert them when someone approached within five meters of the shield wall. The guards would instantly know the breach’s exact location and the identity of the trespasser, allowing for a swift response. They could also add or remove authorized personnel from the access list, making it perfect for a single-entry security system.

The shield specifications would be copied onto paper and planted at the central point where Tadashi stood. It would be activated with his power until the transfer was complete, then sealed.

“You should add location tracking for everyone inside the shield, so we know their whereabouts at all times. That would help compensate for the lack of individual escorts,” I suggested.

More complex specifications meant more energy, but I figured it was still manageable.

Tadashi nodded, making a note. “I want to test each specification before we plant it. Can you try to breach the shield from various points around the perimeter? Sadi will check the response time.”

I nodded. Tadashi closed his eyes, concentrating. Slowly, a white glow appeared, forming a dome-shaped shield wall. Currently, it was entirely sustained by Tadashi’s energy.

“Raise the perimeter to match the fence height, about two meters,” I said. “I’ll test four points. And we’ll time how long it takes Sadi to reach each breach.”

I headed for the western edge of the shield, near the swamp behind the hall. A faint tingling sensation tingled as I crossed the barrier. Thirty-five seconds later, Sadi arrived, running. I told him to relay the time to Tadashi.

Then I walked toward the northern end, near the medical hall. Sadi arrived in forty-eight seconds.

As I approached the eastern end, near the elders’ offices, I spotted Hoseki approaching Akito near the restrooms.

Unwilling to deal with either of them, I took a detour. As I passed the southern end, near Uncle Masada’s office, the shield suddenly flickered and died .

I frowned. Was Tadashi already exhausted? It hadn’t even been an hour.

What had happened?

I hesitated, waiting for Sadi. When he didn’t appear, I headed back to the starting point. They seemed to have moved under the tree.

From a distance, I saw Tadashi kneeling, one knee on the ground. Beside him, a figure lay sprawled.

Wait… that’s…

My breath caught.

Sadi .

Someone had attacked him.

I broke into a run.

W hat the hell happened?

Sadi lay sprawled on the ground, no visible wounds, but his face was an alarming shade of grey-blue, like he was being strangled. Beside him, a discarded medicine pouch lay open, its contents spilled. Tadashi knelt over him, his hands a blur, forming intricate seals with frantic energy.

Open. Weave. Energy Transfer. Protection.

The seals layered themselves over ones he’d already created, activating in rapid succession. A choked sound escaped Tadashi, his jaw clenched, sweat plastering his dark hair to his forehead.

“What’s going on?” I demanded, dropping to my knees beside them. Tadashi was trying to clear Sadi’s energy pathways, but they were a tangled mess, like snarled threads.

Something was blocking Sadi’s energy flow, suffocating him. I traced his pathways, searching for the blockage, but found only a dead end. My blood ran cold. Sadi’s energy pathways weren’t just blocked; they were shredded , destroyed by a broken seal deep within his body. This was no ordinary silencing seal – it was far more complex.

This seal responded to an external energy source – its creator. It controlled the body’s vital functions. Like a gatekeeper manipulating a portal, the creator could control the very energy that sustained Sadi’s life. They could ignite his pain receptors, induce internal bleeding, paralyze him, blind him… anything they desired.

As long as the creator powered the seal, Sadi would function normally. But when that energy was cut off, the seal shattered, severing all his vital pathways. Tadashi had managed to patch most of the severed pathways, binding them with his own energy. It was messy, but he was reconnecting entire systems.

However, the broken seal itself continued to wreak havoc, a frenzied octopus flailing razor-sharp blades.

It was a slave seal.

An ancient, banned seal, its use so dangerous it put the wearer’s life in constant jeopardy. Today, a Fuun tattoo was sufficient to mark someone as unfree, but in the past… individuals literally gambled their lives to their masters.

I gritted my teeth, immediately forming seals to support Tadashi’s efforts. “Who did this to him?”

“His father,” Tadashi replied, his voice strained, a thin line of blood trickling from the corner of his mouth.

Akito… He had enslaved his own son? My stomach twisted with disbelief.

“You need to deactivate and remove the seal before reconnecting the pathways,” I said, my voice sharp. “Otherwise, it’s futile – like digging a hole and filling it back in.”

“I can’t,” Tadashi gasped. “Look closer… the second layer.”

I focused, my eyes tracing the intricate design. Beneath the surface, a hidden pattern lurked, invisible to a casual glance. This… it was a permanent seal. It couldn’t be removed. Once broken, ownership wasn’t erased; it could only be transferred . If it wasn’t, Sadi would die instantly…

Tadashi and I locked eyes. He swallowed, his face ashen. “I’ll… take over,” he whispered, his voice raw. “I’ll reactivate the seal so it stops cutting off his energy.”

My breath caught. Tadashi… was going to become Sadi’s master? My immediate instinct was to object, to insist the elders handle this. But… would they be any better? Tadashi, at least, wouldn’t misuse the seal. And time was running out. Tadashi was on the verge of collapse, and Sadi’s body couldn’t withstand much more. There was no other option.

“Alright,” I said, my voice firm. “I’m with you.”

With painstaking care, Tadashi drew out the seal’s main energy pathway. Like a skilled seamstress carefully unpicking a delicate seam, I caught the fragile ‘threads’ of energy as they separated.

The instant the pathway connected to Tadashi’s energy, the slave seal reactivated. It ceased its destructive thrashing, slowly smoothing itself out, like a crumpled can regaining its original form.

I exhaled, wiping a bead of sweat from my brow. Tadashi bowed his head, his eyes squeezed shut, his hands clenched so tightly his knuckles were white. His entire body trembled. All that remained was to release the temporary bindings and restore Sadi’s own pathways, but Tadashi was clearly at his limit. This must have felt like performing surgery without anesthetic.

“Just a little longer,” I murmured, my voice soothing.

I focused on straightening and repairing the energy pathways, one by one. It was a delicate, precise task, like tuning the strings of a fragile instrument. Each time I untangled a knot, Tadashi jolted, as if shocked by a live wire. When I was finally finished, his body swayed. I reached out, grabbing his arm to steady him.

“There’s one pathway… I can’t repair it,” I said, my voice low. “It’s stuck; it seems like it’s short-circuited. You’ll still be linked to Sadi through it. Do you want me to keep trying, or…?”

Tadashi drew a shuddering breath. “Which pathway?” he asked, his voice barely a whisper.

“Brain function.”

Critical. And incredibly delicate. As long as Tadashi lived, leaving it connected shouldn’t be a problem… but nobody could be certain. There were too many unknowns. “The short circuit might be temporary,” I said. “Maybe it’ll resolve itself when your energy recovers. You can try to release it later, since you’ll have complete control over the seal. You might even find a way to modify or completely remove the slave seal.”

Tadashi nodded weakly. “Thank you, Anko. Let’s… stop here. Don’t… tell anyone about this seal,” he whispered.

I agreed, severing the flow of our combined energy. The slave seal mark at the base of Sadi’s spine briefly flared black, then vanished. Tadashi immediately collapsed, curling up on the grass. I sat back, exhausted and breathless. My head throbbed, my body felt like it had been put through a meat grinder. I could barely think straight. All I wanted was to lie down and sleep for a week.

“Anko…” Tadashi gasped, his voice a ragged whisper. “Please… help… Master Akito…”

Akito? What about— Realization slammed into me.

The slave seal… it shattered when the master died .

I scrambled to my feet and sprinted towards Akito’s office in the main hall.

Was I too late? My lungs burned, my heart hammered, a desperate prayer that I was wrong forming on my lips. But there, at the edge of the gazebo, a body lay sprawled.

Akito.

I knelt, my hand trembling as I reached out.

Still warm… but no pulse.

His face was tinged with blue, the telltale sign of suffocation. The image of Subaru, my brother, flashed through my mind – his death… the same chilling signs…

My heart pounded. I rolled Akito over, searching the base of his neck.

There. Two tiny puncture marks, faint but unmistakable. You’d miss them if you weren’t looking. They resembled a snakebite, but were far smaller, and in a location impossible for a snake to reach. A mark never found in natural deaths.

I stared at Sadi’s father’s lifeless face.

Someone had murdered Akito Masamune. Inside the main hall. Using the same method used to kill my brother.

A flicker of movement caught my eye. Instinct took over. I whipped out my tanto and slashed. A shriek, high-pitched and grating, ripped through the air. I spun, rising, but the figure blurred. Then, it reappeared, far across the courtyard.

Teleportation?

It was tall and black, like a skeletal octopus. Six black tentacles writhed, a swimming motion that propelled it across the ground. Then, in a blink, it vanished.

I stood there, frozen, just as Kenji and Hoseki arrived.

“Master Akito, are you alright? Master Hoseki is waiting—” Kenji’s voice trailed off as he saw the body.

“What happened?!” Hoseki demanded, his voice sharp.

Kenji knelt, checking Akito’s vital signs.

“We’re too late,” I said, my voice hollow, sheathing my tanto . “He was already dead when I found him.”

Kenji examined the body, his expression grim.

“He was fine just a short while ago. This is… unnatural. Master Hoseki, call Doctor Wu. Anko, help me move him inside.”

Hoseki hesitated, his eyes narrowed with suspicion. “What were you doing here, Anko?”

I glared back, my voice tight. “I was helping Tadashi test the shield in the back courtyard. Then I saw Master Akito lying here. Didn’t you see the shield earlier?”

Kenji nodded. “Is the testing complete?”

“Yes, we just tested the detection capabilities today. We can probably install it tomorrow.”

Hoseki looked unconvinced, but he hurried off. After I helped move Akito’s body to the inner corridor of the hall, Doctor Wu confirmed the time of death: less than fifteen minutes ago.

Kenji glanced at me, then ordered his men to secure the doors of the main hall. He summoned Hoseki and me to the meeting room. Elder Low and Shiro were already present.

They’d been in a meeting. Akito had stepped out to retrieve some data from his office, and Hoseki had gone to the restroom. When Akito hadn’t returned, Hoseki had asked Kenji to check on him. And they’d found me… standing over Akito’s body.

Everyone stared. I growled, my fists clenching, feeling the weight of their silent accusations.

A moment later, Tadashi knocked and entered, looking like death warmed over – the seal transfer had clearly taken a heavy toll. I cleared my throat and gave my statement. Tadashi confirmed and added his own details.

After everyone had given their statements, Shiro’s gaze locked onto Tadashi, who pointedly avoided her eyes. He was desperate to conceal the truth about Sadi’s slave seal. I understood – it meant exposing the ugly, fractured relationship between Sadi and his father.

Shiro’s eyes shifted to me, sharp and predatory, like a shark circling its prey. She requested that Doctor Wu be summoned. We waited in tense silence while Kenji called him – the doctor was still performing a more thorough examination at the medical hall.

“Doctor?” Shiro asked, her voice cutting through the quiet as soon as Doctor Wu entered. “Cause of death?”

“Suffocation. No signs of strangulation, no external wounds, his blood was clean. Preliminary results indicate his respiratory system was healthy, but something caused it to shut down completely. Similar to a heart attack.”

“Pressure points? Energy blockages?” Shiro pressed. “A seal that could induce suffocation?”

Doctor Wu frowned. “That’s difficult to say. As far as I know, all seals dissipate upon death, leaving no trace. For now, the official cause is natural causes.”

I hesitated. I wanted to mention the puncture marks at the base of Akito’s neck – almost certainly from that thing , that octopus creature… but I couldn’t be certain. Subaru had died fighting clans skilled in the dark arts. There had been… strange deaths back then, attributed to dark creatures.

Was the creature just a hallucination? Kenji and Hoseki hadn’t reacted to its loud shriek when I’d attacked it. If I claimed Akito was murdered by a flying, teleporting octopus… they’d undoubtedly lock me up for insanity.

Shiro nodded, then her gaze drilled into me.

“There were no witnesses to Anko’s whereabouts during the shield test. Her alibi is weak. Assuming this was murder, even with the limited timeframe, it was technically possible for her to have carried it out, correct?” Her voice was ice, daring me to contradict her. “And, Anko, you do frequently utilize the Suffocation seal in combat, don’t you?”

I stared back, my face burning, fists clenched, unable to deny it. Shiro was suspicious; she knew Tadashi and I were hiding something.

But how could I defend myself without revealing that I’d been with Tadashi, saving Sadi’s life? Shiro would demand answers about Sadi’s slave seal, which Tadashi wished me to keep secret.

My head spun. I scoffed, my voice laced with defiance.

“I didn’t do it. I was in the wrong place at the wrong time. If I had killed him, why would I just stand there? You have no evidence, no witnesses.”

Shiro held my gaze, searching for any flicker of deception. Then, a thin, chilling smile touched her lips.

“Very well. We’ll await Kenji’s investigation. I’ll instruct him to increase surveillance.”

Translation: I was going to be followed, watched, every move scrutinized. Wonderful .

After we were dismissed, Tadashi and I went to check on Sadi at the medical hall.

“How is he? Have you told him about the seal transfer?” I asked.

Tadashi shook his head.

“No. He was still unconscious when I brought him in. Kenji’s men appeared almost immediately, summoning me to the meeting.”

When we arrived, Sadi was awake, sitting beside his father’s cot, lost in thought, staring at the corpse. He turned to Tadashi, frowning.

“I thought I was dying. You… you saved me?”

Tadashi shrugged. “I’ll explain later. Do you need anything?”

Sadi shook his head. “I’m going home to prepare for Father’s funeral. I’ll call if I require assistance. Did you finish planting the shield?”

“No, we’ll continue tomorrow. Thank you for your help earlier.”

Sadi nodded. I touched his shoulder, offering my condolences.

“I had a question, actually. But if it’s not a good time…”

Sadi raised an eyebrow. “Ask away.”

“It’s about Hoseki. I saw him approach your father near the restrooms earlier… I know they were business rivals. Were there any recent… issues?”

Sadi hesitated, thinking.

“Father suspected Hoseki was collaborating with Yamato, the mafia boss, to gain an advantage. I don’t know the specifics, but Hoseki’s warehousing business has been booming. Elder Mei temporarily reassigned my father’s clan contract. Father’s warehouse couldn’t meet the demand at the time, so Elder Mei accepted a bid from Hoseki. In retaliation, Father expanded into logistics and managed to win back the clan’s business. Hoseki accused him of bribery.”

Yamato Yamaguchi… that name again. I pictured him: tall, imposing, black hair with white tips, covered in tattoos.

I sighed. “Was Hoseki still in contact with Yamato?”

“Yes. Father had me follow him once. They meet frequently at Hoseki’s warehouse in Arihara.”

I frowned. Was Hoseki’s connection to Yamato relevant, or just a coincidence? If Hoseki was a traitor, he could have obtained the poison from Yamato.

Yamato was deeply involved in the underworld. It wouldn’t surprise me if he dealt with the Mitsurugi clan, with their expertise in the dark arts. If Yamato could control that octopus-like creature, Hoseki might be working with him.

But several things didn’t add up…

First, why target Elder Mei? If it was purely a business matter, eliminating Akito would have been sufficient, wouldn’t it?

Second, if that creature was controlled by Yamato, how did it enter and exit the main hall undetected?

Security had been tightened after the Ishikawa spy incident. All hall staff had to undergo blood tests, confirming their clan lineage. Did that mean Yamato was an illegitimate son of a clan member?

Ugh. That was a huge leap. Too little information, too many assumptions. Something was missing .

“Do you know anything about Yamato and Elder Mei’s relationship?” I asked Sadi. “Any conflicts?”

Sadi glanced at Tadashi, who remained silent. “I don’t. Perhaps one of his businesses was shut down, or his family members were harmed during one of our missions in his territory…”

Right… our clan had disrupted Yamato’s operations several times. I’d even been on one of those missions, raiding their casino.

“Why do you ask?” Sadi frowned.

I told him about the flying octopus, the puncture marks on Akito’s neck – identical to Subaru’s. Something had been injected, something that paralyzed the respiratory system or nerves. An undetectable poison.

“You mean… the octopus’s ‘bite’ was poisonous, like that wolf monster attack?” Sadi mused.

I shrugged. “It’s possible if the creature is from the dark realms. Nobody knows the types of poisons or their effects. I wouldn’t be surprised if those Mitsurugi bastards have been experimenting.”

“So, Hoseki betrayed us, collaborating with Yamato, who’s a pawn of the Mitsurugi clan. But what’s the Mitsurugi clan’s ultimate objective?” Sadi asked.

We were silent for a moment, then Tadashi spoke, his voice low.

“They’re retaliating,” he said slowly. “We’ve been hitting them hard, and now they’re striking back.”

Sadi looked at him. “Because we disrupted their drug trafficking ring?”

“That’s one reason,” Tadashi nodded. “Raito killed the controller of the living corpses in the Koga clan case – probably someone important to them. I killed the Skull King and captured one of their members. They wouldn’t just let that go.”

I agreed. Tadashi stood briskly. “I’ll research that octopus creature you saw, Anko. The Sagara clan specializes in purification rituals. They might recognize it.”

I thanked him, and then I headed home.

That night, sleep eluded me. Every tiny noise jolted me awake. After midnight, a sound made me sit bolt upright, tanto in hand.

Knock, knock, knock.

I frowned. Why hadn’t the detector shield alerted me? Was it someone I’d authorized—

“Anko,” a familiar voice whispered urgently from outside the window. I leaped down and yanked the curtain back.

“Tadashi? What are you doing here?” I asked, pushing the window open.

Tadashi shook his head, pressing a folded piece of paper into my hand. “The Sagara clan interview notes, like I promised. But you need to go . Now.”

“Why?”

“The elders ordered Shiro to lead a squad to arrest you.”

“What? Why ?”

Tadashi looked worried.

“Nobody’s seen Kenji since he left the meeting. They just found him. Dead. In the back garden of his office, in the main hall. And the murder weapon… is your blade fan .”
