I used to look up to my older brother, but now, every time I see him, I feel like I’m suffocating in the shadow of someone I can no longer trust.

That morning, I went downstairs for breakfast with Father. But as soon as I saw Tadashi in the dining room, I immediately turned around and headed back to my room. Unfortunately, Shiro had already seen me.

“Raito,” Shiro called out. “Come eat first.”

I grumbled under my breath and reluctantly returned to the dining room. I thought they were having breakfast with Father, but he had already left for work.

Well, then there was no need to be so formal. I grabbed a pear from the table and gave Shiro a quick wave.

“I’m heading out, Shiro,” I replied casually, intending to leave immediately.

“Raito, could you please sit down for a moment?” Tadashi asked.

Huh! Does he think I miss talking to him?

“What is it?” I snapped rudely, ignoring his request. “I’m running late.”

I’d only taken three steps when something whizzed past me. I caught it reflexively, and then my eyes widened.

It was one of Shiro’s throwing knives, the blade gleaming. Shiro looked at me with a cold stare. She never made idle threats.

“Sit,” Shiro commanded.

I had no choice but to sit in front of her. Shiro prepared the tea with meticulous movements, then poured it for Tadashi and me.

I glared at Tadashi, who was staring down at his tea. He wore simple farmer’s attire: an earth-toned kimono and hakama. I didn’t see his jacket or katana, but his kusarigama lay beside his right foot, the chain secured to his obi sash.

I snorted in annoyance. He was the one going on a mission, so why drag me into this tedious tea ceremony?

Tadashi and Shiro took respectful sips of their tea and placed their cups down.

Our clan has a strong tradition: a tea ceremony before battle. It’s a farewell ritual, a time for apologies from the departing and prayers for their safe return from those who remain.

I folded my arms across my chest and didn’t touch my cup at all.

Tadashi put down his cup and bowed respectfully.

“Thank you for the hospitality,” he said. I was about to tell him to skip the formalities, but a sharp look from Shiro stopped me.

“There’s something I want to tell you, Shiro, Raito,” Tadashi said, looking at us. “Currently, a spy has infiltrated our clan. The elders don’t know who they are or what their agenda is.”

“What’s clear is that the Mitsurugi clan is targeting our family. They want revenge because I captured a member of their leader’s family.”

I groaned. Ever since our late mother was attacked by another clan, Father erased Shiro’s and my identity documents. If anyone asked about us, Father would tell them that we had moved with Mother’s family.

Shiro and I changed our names. Only the clan elders and my cousin, Anko, knew our true identities. None of our neighbors, friends, or teachers knew we were the clan leader’s children. We had to watch our words.

We also moved from our original home. We used two houses for camouflage, each with secret entrances. We always made sure no one followed us home, entering as stealthily as possible.

Even with all these precautions, we still had to suffer because Tadashi was antagonizing other clans?

“I’ve added more protective seals to our home, but I ask both of you to be more cautious. If possible, don’t leave the house until I return from the mission,” Tadashi said. “At least for the next week.”

You’ve got to be kidding me!

“I object,” I said loudly. “I can’t possibly stay at home. I have to finish Master Toya’s project assignment next week.”

Tadashi frowned. “I’ll speak to Master Toya—”

“You can’t do that!” I exclaimed.

Tadashi usually disguised himself as my guardian to handle academy matters and such, but I’d be damned if he interfered with my project by contacting Master Toya. “This project with Master Toya is important to me. Don’t you know how much time I’ve spent preparing for it? You’re never home, and you’re not Father. You have no right to tell me what to do!”

“Raito!” Shiro scolded me.

I fell silent. Tadashi didn’t say anything. Shiro then turned to Tadashi.

“Then you can’t go out either,” Shiro declared. “You’re being targeted too.”

Tadashi shook his head. “I was the one who helped Father set up the complex’s security. Those intruders shouldn’t be able to breach the clan compound gates, but somehow they did. The elders are holding me accountable—”

“Blah blah blah… whatever,” I said angrily, getting up from my seat. “I don’t care if you’re on a mission or not. I’m leaving.”

I slammed the door and kicked a loose pebble down the road.

Damn it, he ruined my mood.

After a few days, on a sunny Saturday morning, Saki and I were riding horses. We were both apprentices under Master Toya, the kingdom’s top war doctor. This time, we were tasked with preparing some potions.

We were to search for rare medicinal herbs in the Black Forest, in the mountains between the Tsuki and Ikki kingdoms.

We arrived at a meadow on the western edge of the forest, a good place to tie our horses. Someone else was already there.

“Hey, Saki!” a young man with curly hair and a tall, slender build called out.

“Hey, Oda!” Saki greeted him. “Are you here for Master Toya’s project too?”

“Where else am I supposed to find the potion ingredients? Seriously, my group assignment is ridiculously difficult.”

“Oh, right, let me introduce my partner, Yukito,” Saki said. “Yukito, this is Oda, a senior apprentice. He once read a love letter aloud in front of my house!”

“Oh my god, how many times do I have to tell you it was because I lost a bet!” Oda protested, his face turning red.

I laughed. “How did you survive Saki’s older brothers?”

Saki has three fierce older brothers. They once interrogated me for two hours after I walked her home.

“Haha… I nearly died, you know? Saki’s eldest brother, Jiro, kept an eye on me and followed me everywhere for a month!”

I grinned and extended my hand, giving the name I used now. “Yukito Masamune. Nice to meet you.”

“Oda Ishikawa, pleased to meet you too. Just call me Oda.”

We talked as we walked into the forest. Oda was 20 years old, four years older than Saki. We were all the youngest in our families. For some reason, I hit it off with Oda immediately. He was mature, but we just clicked. He also had a good sense of humor.

So different from Tadashi. I wished Oda was my older brother.

When we were resting near a small pond, Oda suddenly stood up. His face was tense. He didn’t say anything, just took out four senbon needles in his right hand and a short sword in his left.

A chill went down my spine. Enemies!

I ducked as shuriken thudded into the tree behind me. Wrong weapon! I nocked a poisoned arrow and aimed.

Thwack! Thwack! Two figures fell.

Oda flicked his senbon, hitting a target behind Saki. Two more attacked from her side, but she was ready and met them with her fists.

I loosed arrow after arrow, then frowned. Something was wrong. I was certain my poisoned arrows had found their marks, but why did they keep moving?

“Yukito! Oda—” Saki gasped, her voice strained. “They’re not human!”

At that moment, an enemy appeared before me, swinging an axe. I rolled to avoid the blow, using the momentum to slip past him and slam his head into a nearby rock. A foul stench filled the air. The man groaned. Even with his head caved in, he still got up.

What the hell? Are they zombies?!

They were too close now.

Void Strike!

I charged, slamming my palm into his back with bone-crushing force. He fell, thrashing and still trying to rise. I pinned his hand down with my foot and snatched his axe.

Oda muttered “Agni” beside me, invoking his clan’s fire technique. His short sword burst into flame. He plunged the flaming sword into the enemy’s heart. The creature groaned and ignited, but still lurched forward. I swung the axe, severing its head. Finally, it collapsed.

We exchanged a quick glance. A horde of undead burst from the bushes.

“Saki, behead them!” Oda shouted, intercepting the newly arrived enemies. I joined the attack with the axe, helping Saki. But the enemies were endless, as if constantly being summoned from the grave.

I heard movement in the trees above, but I was too busy fighting off the corpses. One of them grabbed Saki’s leg. She fell, surrounded by the undead.

Saki!

Suddenly, the figure in the trees invoked a Masamune clan technique.

Sword of Light!

A wave of blinding light struck the corpses from above. The undead disintegrated into ash. Startled, I looked up. Who’s there?! The dense foliage obscured my view.

In the corner of my eye, I caught a flash of light from the bushes behind Saki. Another enemy! He drew his sword, crackling with white static.

Saki!

CLANG!

Before I could warn her, a scorching chain sickle shot out from the person in the trees, wrapping around the enemy’s sword. The Solar Blade! But the enemy jerked his sword forcefully, unleashing a massive electric blast that struck the chain wielder directly. The figure above cried out. He issued a command as he fell.

“Raito, kill him!”

That voice… Tadashi?!

I took over the fight, loosing three arrows at the enemy from different angles.

THWACK! THWACK! THWACK!!!

The enemy screamed and collapsed just as Tadashi crashed down, a foot away from me.

“Saki! Yukito! Are you alright?” Oda shouted.

“Oda,” I said sharply. “Check the enemy!”

I knelt and helped Saki up.

“Tch!” Saki spat, sitting up. “Damn, those things were disgusting.”

Oda dragged the body from the bushes. “He’s dead.” He tossed it towards the pond. “Anyone recognize him?”

Three arrows protruded from the enemy’s chest. His face was bruised and blue from the poison. His hair and eyes were a dull grey-white.

“Mitsurugi Clan!” Saki hissed. She approached the corpse, fury blazing in her eyes. “They dared to attack me?!” Saki angrily searched the corpse.

I frowned. Saki was the heir to the Fuma Clan leader. It was possible this person was targeting her, but any clan would think twice before recklessly messing with the powerful Fuma Clan.

Wait a minute… the zombie controller was from Mitsurugi Clan?!

I froze, remembering Tadashi’s warning about the Mitsurugi Clan. Was this person actually targeting me? If so, that means someone knows my true identity as Raito, the clan leader’s son. Because as Yukito, I’m nobody in the Masamune Clan. Is this the work of the infiltrator spy?

Tadashi groaned behind me. I turned and picked up his fallen kusarigama. What the hell is Tadashi doing here?!

I grabbed Tadashi’s arm and hauled him up. He winced as he sat up, his hand instinctively gripping my wrist. I leaned down and muttered in his ear.

“Get a grip. Don’t disgrace our clan.”

Tadashi blinked and obeyed. Once he’d regained his composure, I released him and bowed respectfully.

“Tadashi,” I greeted him, forcing a smile onto my face. “Are you alright?”

Tadashi looked at me, swallowing hard, surprised by my sudden shift in demeanor. He took my outstretched hand to stand up. I returned his weapon.

“I’m fine,” he rasped, clearing his throat. “Thank you, Yukito.”

Saki bowed to Tadashi. “Masamune. Thank you for saving us.”

Tadashi nodded respectfully. “Fuma, I apologize for the trouble. Did you capture the one controlling the undead?”

Saki shrugged. “Yukito finished him off. Why did a Mitsurugi Clan ninja suddenly appear here?”

Tadashi flushed slightly. “I’m sorry, he was chasing me.”

“You’re on a mission?” Saki asked, frowning. “Are there more Mitsurugi ninjas around?”

“Yes,” Tadashi said curtly, his eyes narrowing. “What are you doing here? This forest is dangerous.”

“We’re collecting plants to make potions,” Oda explained.

Tadashi turned to him. “Are you Fuma’s bodyguard?”

“No,” I interjected. “He’s Oda from the Ishikawa Clan, a fellow apprentice.”

Tadashi looked at me, then Oda, and finally turned back to Saki.

“Excuse me, may I speak with Yukito for a moment?” he requested politely.

What now? I thought, clenching my jaw. Tadashi led me away from Saki and Oda. I had no choice but to follow.

“Why didn’t you listen to me, Raito?” Tadashi asked once we were out of earshot.

“Strolling around town would be one thing, but a remote place like this? Who would help you if you got into trouble? And you brought Fuma with you. Do you realize what would happen to our clan if something happened to her?”

“Don’t be a hypocrite!” I snapped. “Saki’s the same age as you. If you can go anywhere on missions, why can’t she? You’re both clan heirs. She can take care of herself.”

“That’s not what I meant… I didn’t expect anything to happen to you. But this is a bad time to be in this forest. Do you know how many dangerous people are out here?”

I exploded. “You’re the one who led them here! Don’t deny it! If you were doing your job properly, they wouldn’t be able to track you. So if anything happens, it’s on you! You’re the clan heir! Your subordinates shouldn’t have to pay for your mistakes!”

Tadashi fell silent, rubbing his face.

“Raito, my mission isn’t finished,” Tadashi said quietly. “You and your friend need to leave. I’m being chased. I can’t protect all of you—”

“No one asked for your shitty protection! Argh! Why are you so arrogant?! Just because you graduated from the accelerated class, you think you’re better than everyone?! You can’t even beat Shiro, and she never even went to school! You’re pathetic! I’m not surprised Mother was disappointed—”

“Raito!” Tadashi’s face flushed crimson, his shoulders trembling. His hands clenched as if he might punch me. I half hoped he would, just so I’d have a reason to hit him back.

“Don’t say that….” His voice was hoarse. He closed his eyes briefly, taking a deep breath. “I’ll escort you to the edge of the forest,” he said quietly, his tone firm. “Then I’ll create a diversion so you don’t get caught in the crossfire. Please escort Fuma back to the city. Do you understand, Raito?”

“I don’t care about your stupid mission! I’ll take Saki wherever she wants to go. And don’t call me Raito,” I hissed. “Raito died when you left three years ago. Remember? You’re not my brother anymore!”

Tadashi paled, looking as though he’d been slapped. He turned away without another word. Good. I hated the sound of his voice.

Suddenly, Saki’s distant scream cut through the air. I spun around, ready to run towards her, but several smoke bombs exploded around me.

BANG!

I stumbled back, colliding with Tadashi. The ground rumbled beneath us. An earthquake?! What’s—

The ground surged and cracked violently. The next thing I knew, it collapsed beneath us and we plunged into the darkness.

I cried out as my feet slipped out from under me.

I scrabbled at the crumbling earth, but it offered no purchase, sending me tumbling deeper. I tried to run, but the chasm walls shifted around me.

Crap! They’re trying to crush me!

Tadashi was ahead of me. Red earth cascaded down as the chasm narrowed. A sliver of light beckoned at the end of the passage, but a boulder slammed into me, sending me sprawling. My right foot wedged between rocks.

“Tadashi!” The name escaped my lips before I could stop it.

Damn it! Shame burned my cheeks. Why did I call out to him?!

Tadashi turned and rushed towards me. His eyes widened at the sight of my predicament. Without a word, he lifted my arm to help me up. I tried to wrench my foot free, but it was stuck fast.

The rocks creaked in omnious rumble, the pressure increasing on my ankle. I groaned in pain.

The chasm walls were closing in, pressing us together. The opening ahead shrunk rapidly. A wave of cold dread washed over me.

I was going to die.

“You should go,” I croaked. “Save yourself.”

“I won’t leave you, Raito,” Tadashi said firmly.

I almost laughed hysterically. If he had dared to say that at any other time, I would have lashed out at him. Tadashi knelt and struck the rock with the handle of his sickle, freeing my trapped foot.

He then invoked the Star Shield . Powerful bluish-white energy spiraled outwards towards the exit, halting the collapsing walls.

I scrambled through the narrow opening, then froze when I reached a dead end. A deep ravine stretched out in front of me. I had no time to warn Tadashi. The earth rumbled shut behind me as the Star Shield dissipated. Tadashi slammed into me, and together we fell into the gaping abyss.

AAH!

Tadashi held me close and threw his kusarigama. The chain wrapped around a protruding rock, halting our fall with a jarring jolt. He cried out as the chain, wrapped around his hand, tightened, wrenching his shoulder.

The bottom of the ravine was lost in the darkness below. Tadashi’s grip loosened. I was flung away, crashing through branches and rocks before sliding down the cliff face.

Pain.

Then darkness.

I gasped awake.

Aah… I squeezed my eyes shut as pain shot through my body. Every muscle screamed in protest. A throbbing ache pulsed in my foot. I winced and clenched my fists, then forced my eyes open.

I blinked slowly and breathed.

Each breath sent lances of pain through me. A gloomy fog pressed down, obscuring the sky. The air was heavy, my breathing was ragged. I tried to sit up, fighting through waves of agony. Damp moss surrounded me.

I looked at my throbbing foot and swallowed. My ankle was definitely sprained—red, swollen, and hot. A long gash ran across my calf, but thankfully, no bones were broken. I could still move my knee, though the pain was excruciating.

I clenched my jaw.

With trembling hands, I ripped open my tattered clothes and unzipped my armor. My medicine pouch was intact, but my water bottle was missing. I took two painkillers and swallowed them dry. Then, I poured disinfectant onto a long bandage and wrapped it around the wound on my calf.

The disinfectant stung.

I looked around. Withered trees towered above me. The ground was a tangle of vines, bushes, and moss. I hadn’t fallen far from the cliff wall. The distant rush of a river reached my ears.

I applied disinfectant and Master Toya’s ointment to my ankle. He could heal such injuries with ease, but I wasn’t that skilled yet.

Damn it! All I wanted was to collect potion ingredients. Now, thanks to Tadashi, I was stuck in this ravine. Anger simmered in my chest. Why did that guy always cause problems?!

I took a deep breath and tried to stand, but my knee buckled, sending me crashing back down. Agony shot through my foot, white hot and blinding. I gasped for breath.

Damn it! Come on, Raito. Get up.

I slowly rose again, my knee trembling. Just a little pain. Don’t be a wimp . Sweat dripped down my face as I found my feet. Finally! I paused, maintaining my balance.

What should I do now? Tadashi was nowhere in sight. Had he left me? A pang of something like betrayal tightened my chest.

“I won’t leave you, Raito.”

Heh. So much for his promise.

I had to find Saki and Oda, but climbing with this sprained ankle was impossible. Was there another way? I forced myself to walk, each step a spear of pain through my foot. After a few minutes, the pain eased slightly. The medicine was starting to work.

Wait, the river. There was a village at the foot of the mountain. I didn’t know if this river led there, but it was better than trying to climb.

I limped towards the sound of rushing water. Hopefully, it wasn’t far.

As I walked, I tried to piece together what had happened. The earth-splitting ninja was Koga. But why did they attack us? The Mitsurugi wanting revenge made sense, but the Koga were just a small clan, usually hired for treasure hunts.

Why were both clans after Tadashi?

Last week, when Tadashi was leaving… what was his mission about? I’d stormed off before he’d finished. I only remembered the warning about the clan infiltrator.

Was his mission to find the spy? But wasn’t the infiltrator in the compound? Why leave the city? Had the infiltrator escaped, and Tadashi was in pursuit? But Tadashi was being chased. Perhaps the infiltrator was Koga or Mitsurugi, and Tadashi had discovered their identity. They wouldn’t want him talking. They were probably trying to silence him.

I picked my way carefully across a gravelly patch, nearing the cliff face for support. Then I saw him.

Tadashi lay slumped behind a boulder at the cliff’s edge.

I froze.

Was he dead?

My head throbbed. Tadashi lay face down, his clothes and hakama torn and bloodstained. Scratches and cuts covered his arms and legs. I reached for his wrist. He had a pulse, but it was weak.

Kneeling, I eased him down from the boulder and turned him over. His shirt was ripped open, his armor shattered. Blood trickled from his temple. His lip was split and his face was deathly pale.

He was barely breathing.

“Tadashi,” I whispered, feeling unease. No response. I gripped his arm and shook him again, my voice rising. “Tadashi!”

Tadashi gasped, then hissed, his body stiffening. Trembling all over, he clutched his ribs.

“Tadashi, wake up!” I shouted, hauling him up against the rock. He choked, his face turning pale. Tears streaming down his cheeks. His breath came in ragged gasps.

“R-Raito?” he whispered, frowning. “A-Are you… alright?”

Idiot. Who falls into a ravine and walks away unscathed?

I snorted, getting to my feet. “I’m following the river to find a way out. Are you coming?”

I didn’t want to be around him, but my bow, arrows, and equipment bag were lost in the fall. All I had left were kunai and shuriken. I hadn’t seen Tadashi’s bag or weapon pouch, but it seemed safer to stick together—if he could even walk. I started walking without waiting for his answer.

“Raito—” Tadashi’s weak voice called out. “W-Wait….”

I was already far ahead when Tadashi caught up. He winced repeatedly, his face pale and strained, his brow furrowed. He was clearly in pain. His ribs were probably bruised, though I was surprised they weren’t broken after that fall. He kept his head down, watching his steps carefully.

I hadn’t noticed before, but he wore shackles on his ankles. The chains were cut, the broken remnants dragging and clinking on the gravel with each step.

Where had he been? Six days. Had he been captured? But why hadn’t they killed him? His behavior was strange. If he was being chased, why not report back to the clan immediately? He’d said his mission wasn’t finished. What else was he doing out here?

Exasperated, I finally asked, “Why are the Mitsurugi and Koga chasing you? What’s your mission?”

As usual, he didn’t answer. He never told me anything about his missions. Silence or a change of subject was his standard response. I used to be patient. But three years ago, he’d left without a word. Father said it was a mission.

A year and a half of silence, no news from him at all. Didn’t he know how worried I’d been?

When he returned, I found out that he’d met with Father several times at the compound, giving mission reports. But he hadn’t visited me, not even when I broke my leg. It seemed like Shiro and I meant nothing to him.

I was done being patient. If the clan meant more than his family, fine. He could look after the damn clan forever!

Tadashi looked away. I growled. His refusal to answer didn’t surprise me, but his avoidance infuriated me.

“I’d rather be doused in boiling water than walk with you. But thanks to you, we’re stuck,” I said angrily. “Why did I ever think you’d changed. How can I fight if I don’t know what we’re facing? Why did you even save me?”

Tadashi remained silent, his gaze averted. My anger intensified.

“You’re making me sick. We can’t work together if you won’t be honest. We might as well split up. I don’t need your help to find Saki and Oda.”

Tadashi turned to me sharply. “You still want to find them? I thought you wanted to go home.”

I stopped, incredulous.

Unbelievable!

“Why can’t you care about anyone else?! I’m not a coward like you!” I snapped. “Saki screamed before we fell. Something happened to her. If all you care about is the clan, then think about this: What will you tell the Fuma Clan leader if his daughter was attacked because you brought the enemy to her?”

Tadashi’s lips tightened. “Don’t worry about that. I’ll look for Fuma. You need to get home and stay away from that Ishikawa boy.”

How dare he! Who did he think he was?!

“You have no right to control my life! You’re a failure as a brother! Oda’s a hundred times better than you! I hope you die on your stupid mission!” I shouted, shoving him hard.

Tadashi wasn’t prepared. He stumbled backward, groaning in pain. Good. My foot throbbed as I stalked away, but I didn’t care. I had to get out of there. His attitude was infuriating!

I followed the river, ignoring everything else. I faintly heard Tadashi calling, but I pressed on, putting distance between us. I didn’t want to hear another word.

The fog deepened and I didn’t know how long I’d been walking. My foot ached, so I stopped to take more medicine. Suddenly, several objects whizzed towards me. On reflex, I ducked and took cover behind a large boulder. Smoke bombs exploded around me.

I grabbed a kunai just as someone attacked from the right.

Clang!

My wrist ached from the impact. My kunai flew from my hand. I squinted through the smoke, leaping back as the enemy attacked again. I dodged, grabbing his elbow, and twisted.

Void Strike!

Crack!

“Aargh!”

The scream alerted his companions. I swallowed. Like the Star Shield , using the bone-crushing technique drained me. Damn it! I snatched up the fallen sword. Sword fighting wasn’t my forte, but I had no choice. I dodged left, then parried the next blow, forced onto the defensive.

“Where’s the bracelet?” The Koga ninja pressed his attack.

“What bracelet?” I grunted, struggling to maintain my guard.

“Don’t play dumb!” he snarled, forcing me back. “The bracelet that damn slave stole! He was talking to you before you fell!”

I gasped. So that was it. Tadashi’s mission was to steal a bracelet from the Koga Clan!

“I don’t know anything, I swear!” I retorted. “He was just yelling at me for being in the wrong place at the wrong time.”

The man snarled. “Then you’d better die!”

Their attack intensified. My arm trembled in pain. The ground started to shake violently, making my stomach churn. Were they going to bury me alive again?

The river, my only escape—but there was no opening!

More smoke bombs exploded. The earth rumbled. A flash of steel deflected an attack aimed at my back.

Clang!

Tadashi?!

The ground cracked.

“Run!” Tadashi hissed, his sword slashed at the opponents in a blur. I dodged and ran for the river, Tadashi close behind. We were almost there when something whizzed towards me. My throbbing foot slowed my reaction.

An arrow shot! No time—

Tadashi shoved me down, taking the arrow in his shoulder.

“ A AAH!” Tadashi screamed in pain.

His sword clattered to the ground. He jolted, falling against me. I staggered under his weight as he slumped, his face pressed against my chest, his hand gripping my sleeve. If we fell here, we would get caught.

I growled and grabbed his arm, pulling him up forcefully. Tadashi’s cry was muffled against my shirt, his whole body trembling. I slung his arm over my shoulder and half-carried him towards the riverbank.

Three more steps.

We leaped into the river. Tadashi’s grip loosened and he went under. I hauled him back up, gasping for air as we surfaced. His breathing was ragged, his face ashen.

“Hurry, before their reinforcements arrive,” I hissed, ignoring the arrow protruding from his shoulder. I swam as hard as I could against the strong current.

The river carried us out of the Black Forest. My back burned with exhaustion, my swimming slowing. Tadashi was quiet, the rasps of his breath sounded painful. Several times, his grip weakened, and I snarled at him to stay conscious.

I was on the verge of passing out when I saw torchlight flickering on the riverbank—temple ruins. Finally! I dragged us ashore, half-throwing Tadashi onto the grass. I collapsed beside him, gasping for air on the damp ground. My head pounded, every part of my body ached.

I lay still, staring up at the stars.

I didn’t remember anything about Mother. She’d died when I was four. Since then, Shiro and Tadashi raised me.

Shiro had been like a mother—feeding me, teaching me, training me in the dojo.

Tadashi… he read me bedtime stories. He held me when I had nightmares. He carried me everywhere. He walked me to school and played with me. He picked me up when I fell and treated my wounds when I got hurt. He let me mess up his room while he studied. He listened to my chatter during dinner.

He checked and praised my training progress, then corrected my mistakes and weaknesses. With him, I felt special. He never forced me to master any particular technique. I never felt like I had to be the best to make him proud.

I felt accepted for who I was. His presence calmed me.

He was a father, a brother, and a friend to me. That’s why when he left, he left a gaping hole in my heart. Like a wound that wouldn’t heal.

He’d hurt me, and I’d wanted him to hurt in return.

It seemed my wish had come true.

I looked at Tadashi. He was still lying face down on the ground, unmoving. The arrow was still embedded in his back. His body trembled. His face was pale and contorted in pain. His eyes were closed. Had he fainted?

“Tadashi?” I murmured, my gaze tracing the arrow’s path. Could it have hit something vital? Depending on the type of arrow, its depth, and its location, an arrow wound could be serious.

Somehow, seeing him suffer like this didn’t make me feel any better. He’d saved me, multiple times. He wouldn’t have done that if I meant nothing to him.

He didn’t want me to die.

And I… I realized I didn’t want him to die either.

Tadashi’s eyelids fluttered open, and he answered me in a weak whisper.

“Yes?”

A silent plea echoed in my mind, unable to voice it to Tadashi. My voice caught in my throat, refusing to come out.

Don’t die. Don’t die. Don’t die.

Don’t leave me alone.

I must have been silent too long. Tadashi’s cold fingers brushed my arm, his expression worried.

I won’t leave you, Raito. His words echoed in my mind, a warmth spreading through my chest, tightening my throat and making it hard to breathe.

I clenched my fists and looked away. Gingerly, I sat up and grabbed my medicine pouch, then swallowed two more painkillers. I also injected an ibuprofen ampoule into Tadashi’s arm.

“I’m going to check the arrow,” I said, my voice steadier now. Tadashi closed his eyes briefly, then gave a slight nod.

I tore open Tadashi’s kimono to examine the wound. The arrow was lodged near his left shoulder blade. Only half the arrowhead had penetrated his armor—it must have been a long shot. It was a plain iron arrow, about 3cm long, without barbs. It didn’t seem to have hit any bones.

I handed Tadashi a rolled-up piece of kimono to bite down on. “It’s not too deep. I’m pulling it out.”

Tadashi cried out as I pulled the arrow free, then fainted. I let out my breath. Better this than him enduring the pain while I treated the wound.

I cut his inner kimono into bandages, then removed his armor and undershirt. Tadashi looked thinner than I remembered. His ribs were bruised, his back crisscrossed with long, whip-like welts, red and inflamed.

The pieces clicked into place. Tadashi had left disguised as a poor farmer. He must have removed and hidden his armor and kusarigama somewhere, then infiltrated the Koga Clan as a slave. He received those whip marks there. Tadashi managed to steal the bracelet, they discovered him, and he’d been running ever since.

If the bracelet was valuable, the Mitsurugi being after it too made sense. I nodded, the theory fitting. But the sight of his back… it made my stomach churn.

I quickly packed the arrow wound, bandaging his shoulder tightly with strips of kimono. His armor was ruined, but his undershirt, despite the bloodstains, was still usable. I took off my haori and draped it over him. After I gathered my things, I woke him.

Tadashi sat half-conscious for several minutes, his gaze fixed on my haori. I waited until he seemed more alert, then stood.

“Can you walk?” I asked, my voice gentler than I expected.

Tadashi looked up, his eyes glistening. The ibuprofen was a low dose, just enough to take the edge off. He still couldn’t use his left arm. I offered my hand. He reached for it slowly, and I helped him up.

“I’ll help you find Saki,” I said firmly. “Then you’re coming home, mission or not.”

Tadashi didn’t answer, but he could barely stand, so I assumed he agreed. I didn’t know what he needed to do to finish his mission, but my mind was made up. If he didn’t ask for my help, then I wouldn’t push him to talk about his missions either.

We walked slowly. I noticed the temple ruins and headed towards them, hoping for shelter. I needed to make sure it was safe, but the sword was back by the river. All I had were a few shuriken. We were poorly armed, but I wasn’t backing down.

Finding no sign of enemies around the perimeter, we cautiously entered the temple ruins.

The temple floor sloped downwards, with stairs flanking on either side. In the center, Saki was tied to a pillar, gagged and bleeding. A circle of red flames blazed around her.

“Saki!” I shouted.

“No, don’t—” Tadashi hissed, grabbing my arm. I yanked free and ran towards Saki.

Everything happened at once. Ninjas emerged from behind the pillars, surrounding us. A fireball struck Tadashi from behind, sending him crashing to the ground.

“Two clan heirs in one trap. Pathetic,” a voice sneered from above. A figure dropped from a beam in the ceiling, landing on Tadashi’s back.

“Hello, Tadashi. I hear you killed my brother.”

“Oda?!” Shock pulsed through me.

“Wait your turn, Yukito,” Oda said coldly. “I have business with this scum.”

He stomped on Tadashi’s back, his heel grinding into the bandaged shoulder. Tadashi screamed, then another kick to his ribs silenced him. Rage burned through me. I clenched my fists, wanting to attack, but I was surrounded.

“Search him,” Oda ordered to his men, his arms folded. He turned to me, a cold smile twisting his lips.

“A week ago, your damn clan sent my brother to retrieve the royal bracelet from Koga headquarters. I don’t know how the Koga found it, but the King is offering a reward for its return,” Oda explained. “Wealth and power. So, naturally, we wanted it.”

Wait… How could Oda’s brother be on a Masamune mission? Unless…

“Your brother infiltrated my clan?”

Oda shrugged. “Nothing personal, Yukito. You have no idea how many of your clan are spies in other clans. This idiot infiltrated the Koga easily enough. My brother was in his team, acting as a slave trader. Two days ago, my scouts reported his death. This idiot killed him.”

“He doesn’t have the bracelet,” one of Oda’s men reported.

Oda growled. “Take him to the basement. I’ll interrogate him myself.”

They tied me up next to Saki, leaving us within the circle of fire. Time crawled. I didn’t know how long we’d been there when I felt Saki stir.

“Saki, help me save Tadashi!” I whispered. “They’re interrogating him in the basement.”

Saki only nodded. Damn, I forgot they gagged her.

“Hey, are you still wearing your kakute ring?” I asked in low voice.

Saki had a ring inherited from her father, a symbol of the clan heir. The silver ring had three sharp points hidden beneath a cover. When closed, it looked like an ordinary ring, but when opened, it could be used as a weapon.

Saki’s eyes widened in understanding. She quickly opened the ring and sawed at my ropes. I kept watch on the guards around us. Three guards below the platform, six above. Once my hands were free, I untied Saki.

With a flick of my wrist, I opened the weapon seal on my wrist sheath. Kunai and a few shuriken—it wasn’t much, but it was something. Tadashi had made it for me, a hidden compartment for weapons.

Suddenly, a piercing scream echoed from the basement. My heart clenched.

Tadashi! He sounded like an animal being slaughtered.

I stood, throwing my poisoned shuriken.

Thwack! Thwack! Thwack!

The three guards below collapsed. Shouts of surprise from above. Four more fell, but two escaped, racing down the stairs. I started to jump the circle of fire, but Saki grabbed my arm.

“Don’t! It’s not normal fire!”

I hissed as my hand brushed the red flames, a searing pain burning to the bone. Hah! Good thing I hadn’t jumped.

The two guards reached us, extinguishing the flames. Saki moved, a blur of motion, disarming them with deadly precision. She snatched up a naginata. I grabbed a sai and a discarded bow and quiver.

We ducked behind a pillar. More guards approached. Then the earth rumbled, the floor cracking beneath the steps, swallowing several guards. Screams of terror filled the air.

The Koga had found us!

The Ishikawa guards turned to face the new threat.

“Yukito, hurry!”

The ground shook violently, cracks spreading across the floor. Pillars swayed, stones crumbled. We ran for the basement, scrambling over rubble. I pushed ahead, reaching the torchlit end of the collapsing corridor.

The door was locked. Saki kicked it open, splintering the rotten wood. Red torches flickered on the walls.

The room was empty.

My haori lay discarded on the floor. I snatched it up, my heart pounding.

Beneath it, Tadashi’s undershirt was stained crimson with blood.
