Chapter 1

← Back to Masamune Clan Series [EN]

I used to look up to my older brother, but now, every time I see him, I feel like I’m suffocating in the shadow of someone I can no longer trust.

That morning, I went downstairs for breakfast with Father. But as soon as I saw Tadashi in the dining room, I immediately turned around and headed back to my room. Unfortunately, Shiro had already seen me.

“Raito,” Shiro called out. “Come eat first.”

I grumbled under my breath and reluctantly returned to the dining room. I thought they were having breakfast with Father, but he had already left for work.

Well, then there was no need to be so formal. I grabbed a pear from the table and gave Shiro a quick wave.

“I’m heading out, Shiro,” I replied casually, intending to leave immediately.

“Raito, could you please sit down for a moment?” Tadashi asked.

Huh! Does he think I miss talking to him?

“What is it?” I snapped rudely, ignoring his request. “I’m running late.”

I’d only taken three steps when something whizzed past me. I caught it reflexively, and then my eyes widened.

It was one of Shiro’s throwing knives, the blade gleaming. Shiro looked at me with a cold stare. She never made idle threats.

“Sit,” Shiro commanded.

I had no choice but to sit in front of her. Shiro prepared the tea with meticulous movements, then poured it for Tadashi and me.

I glared at Tadashi, who was staring down at his tea. He wore simple farmer’s attire: an earth-toned kimono and hakama. I didn’t see his jacket or katana, but his kusarigama lay beside his right foot, the chain secured to his obi sash.

I snorted in annoyance. He was the one going on a mission, so why drag me into this tedious tea ceremony?

Tadashi and Shiro took respectful sips of their tea and placed their cups down.

Our clan has a strong tradition: a tea ceremony before battle. It’s a farewell ritual, a time for apologies from the departing and prayers for their safe return from those who remain.

I folded my arms across my chest and didn’t touch my cup at all.

Tadashi put down his cup and bowed respectfully.

“Thank you for the hospitality,” he said. I was about to tell him to skip the formalities, but a sharp look from Shiro stopped me.

“There’s something I want to tell you, Shiro, Raito,” Tadashi said, looking at us. “Currently, a spy has infiltrated our clan. The elders don’t know who they are or what their agenda is.”

“What’s clear is that the Mitsurugi clan is targeting our family. They want revenge because I captured a member of their leader’s family.”

I groaned. Ever since our late mother was attacked by another clan, Father erased Shiro’s and my identity documents. If anyone asked about us, Father would tell them that we had moved with Mother’s family.

Shiro and I changed our names. Only the clan elders and my cousin, Anko, knew our true identities. None of our neighbors, friends, or teachers knew we were the clan leader’s children. We had to watch our words.

We also moved from our original home. We used two houses for camouflage, each with secret entrances. We always made sure no one followed us home, entering as stealthily as possible.

Even with all these precautions, we still had to suffer because Tadashi was antagonizing other clans?

“I’ve added more protective seals to our home, but I ask both of you to be more cautious. If possible, don’t leave the house until I return from the mission,” Tadashi said. “At least for the next week.”

You’ve got to be kidding me!

“I object,” I said loudly. “I can’t possibly stay at home. I have to finish Master Toya’s project assignment next week.”

Tadashi frowned. “I’ll speak to Master Toya—”

“You can’t do that!” I exclaimed.

Tadashi usually disguised himself as my guardian to handle academy matters and such, but I’d be damned if he interfered with my project by contacting Master Toya. “This project with Master Toya is important to me. Don’t you know how much time I’ve spent preparing for it? You’re never home, and you’re not Father. You have no right to tell me what to do!”

“Raito!” Shiro scolded me.

I fell silent. Tadashi didn’t say anything. Shiro then turned to Tadashi.

“Then you can’t go out either,” Shiro declared. “You’re being targeted too.”

Tadashi shook his head. “I was the one who helped Father set up the complex’s security. Those intruders shouldn’t be able to breach the clan compound gates, but somehow they did. The elders are holding me accountable—”

“Blah blah blah… whatever,” I said angrily, getting up from my seat. “I don’t care if you’re on a mission or not. I’m leaving.”

I slammed the door and kicked a loose pebble down the road.

Damn it, he ruined my mood.

After a few days, on a sunny Saturday morning, Saki and I were riding horses. We were both apprentices under Master Toya, the kingdom’s top war doctor. This time, we were tasked with preparing some potions.

We were to search for rare medicinal herbs in the Black Forest, in the mountains between the Tsuki and Ikki kingdoms.

We arrived at a meadow on the western edge of the forest, a good place to tie our horses. Someone else was already there.

“Hey, Saki!” a young man with curly hair and a tall, slender build called out.

“Hey, Oda!” Saki greeted him. “Are you here for Master Toya’s project too?”

“Where else am I supposed to find the potion ingredients? Seriously, my group assignment is ridiculously difficult.”

“Oh, right, let me introduce my partner, Yukito,” Saki said. “Yukito, this is Oda, a senior apprentice. He once read a love letter aloud in front of my house!”

“Oh my god, how many times do I have to tell you it was because I lost a bet!” Oda protested, his face turning red.

I laughed. “How did you survive Saki’s older brothers?”

Saki has three fierce older brothers. They once interrogated me for two hours after I walked her home.

“Haha… I nearly died, you know? Saki’s eldest brother, Jiro, kept an eye on me and followed me everywhere for a month!”

I grinned and extended my hand, giving the name I used now. “Yukito Masamune. Nice to meet you.”

“Oda Ishikawa, pleased to meet you too. Just call me Oda.”

We talked as we walked into the forest. Oda was 20 years old, four years older than Saki. We were all the youngest in our families. For some reason, I hit it off with Oda immediately. He was mature, but we just clicked. He also had a good sense of humor.

So different from Tadashi. I wished Oda was my older brother.

When we were resting near a small pond, Oda suddenly stood up. His face was tense. He didn’t say anything, just took out four senbon needles in his right hand and a short sword in his left.

A chill went down my spine. Enemies!

I ducked as shuriken thudded into the tree behind me. Wrong weapon! I nocked a poisoned arrow and aimed.

Thwack! Thwack! Two figures fell.

Oda flicked his senbon, hitting a target behind Saki. Two more attacked from her side, but she was ready and met them with her fists.

I loosed arrow after arrow, then frowned. Something was wrong. I was certain my poisoned arrows had found their marks, but why did they keep moving?

“Yukito! Oda—” Saki gasped, her voice strained. “They’re not human!”

At that moment, an enemy appeared before me, swinging an axe. I rolled to avoid the blow, using the momentum to slip past him and slam his head into a nearby rock. A foul stench filled the air. The man groaned. Even with his head caved in, he still got up.

What the hell? Are they zombies?!

They were too close now.

Void Strike!

I charged, slamming my palm into his back with bone-crushing force. He fell, thrashing and still trying to rise. I pinned his hand down with my foot and snatched his axe.

Oda muttered “Agni” beside me, invoking his clan’s fire technique. His short sword burst into flame. He plunged the flaming sword into the enemy’s heart. The creature groaned and ignited, but still lurched forward. I swung the axe, severing its head. Finally, it collapsed.

We exchanged a quick glance. A horde of undead burst from the bushes.

“Saki, behead them!” Oda shouted, intercepting the newly arrived enemies. I joined the attack with the axe, helping Saki. But the enemies were endless, as if constantly being summoned from the grave.

I heard movement in the trees above, but I was too busy fighting off the corpses. One of them grabbed Saki’s leg. She fell, surrounded by the undead.

Saki!

Suddenly, the figure in the trees invoked a Masamune clan technique.

Sword of Light!

A wave of blinding light struck the corpses from above. The undead disintegrated into ash. Startled, I looked up. Who’s there?! The dense foliage obscured my view.

In the corner of my eye, I caught a flash of light from the bushes behind Saki. Another enemy! He drew his sword, crackling with white static.

Saki!

CLANG!

Before I could warn her, a scorching chain sickle shot out from the person in the trees, wrapping around the enemy’s sword. The Solar Blade! But the enemy jerked his sword forcefully, unleashing a massive electric blast that struck the chain wielder directly. The figure above cried out. He issued a command as he fell.

“Raito, kill him!”

That voice… Tadashi?!

I took over the fight, loosing three arrows at the enemy from different angles.

THWACK! THWACK! THWACK!!!

The enemy screamed and collapsed just as Tadashi crashed down, a foot away from me.

“Saki! Yukito! Are you alright?” Oda shouted.

“Oda,” I said sharply. “Check the enemy!”

I knelt and helped Saki up.

“Tch!” Saki spat, sitting up. “Damn, those things were disgusting.”

Oda dragged the body from the bushes. “He’s dead.” He tossed it towards the pond. “Anyone recognize him?”

Three arrows protruded from the enemy’s chest. His face was bruised and blue from the poison. His hair and eyes were a dull grey-white.

“Mitsurugi Clan!” Saki hissed. She approached the corpse, fury blazing in her eyes. “They dared to attack me?!” Saki angrily searched the corpse.

I frowned. Saki was the heir to the Fuma Clan leader. It was possible this person was targeting her, but any clan would think twice before recklessly messing with the powerful Fuma Clan.

Wait a minute… the zombie controller was from Mitsurugi Clan?!

I froze, remembering Tadashi’s warning about the Mitsurugi Clan. Was this person actually targeting me? If so, that means someone knows my true identity as Raito, the clan leader’s son. Because as Yukito, I’m nobody in the Masamune Clan. Is this the work of the infiltrator spy?

Tadashi groaned behind me. I turned and picked up his fallen kusarigama. What the hell is Tadashi doing here?!

I grabbed Tadashi’s arm and hauled him up. He winced as he sat up, his hand instinctively gripping my wrist. I leaned down and muttered in his ear.

“Get a grip. Don’t disgrace our clan.”

Tadashi blinked and obeyed. Once he’d regained his composure, I released him and bowed respectfully.

“Tadashi,” I greeted him, forcing a smile onto my face. “Are you alright?”

Tadashi looked at me, swallowing hard, surprised by my sudden shift in demeanor. He took my outstretched hand to stand up. I returned his weapon.

“I’m fine,” he rasped, clearing his throat. “Thank you, Yukito.”

Saki bowed to Tadashi. “Masamune. Thank you for saving us.”

Tadashi nodded respectfully. “Fuma, I apologize for the trouble. Did you capture the one controlling the undead?”

Saki shrugged. “Yukito finished him off. Why did a Mitsurugi Clan ninja suddenly appear here?”

Tadashi flushed slightly. “I’m sorry, he was chasing me.”

“You’re on a mission?” Saki asked, frowning. “Are there more Mitsurugi ninjas around?”

“Yes,” Tadashi said curtly, his eyes narrowing. “What are you doing here? This forest is dangerous.”

“We’re collecting plants to make potions,” Oda explained.

Tadashi turned to him. “Are you Fuma’s bodyguard?”

“No,” I interjected. “He’s Oda from the Ishikawa Clan, a fellow apprentice.”

Tadashi looked at me, then Oda, and finally turned back to Saki.

“Excuse me, may I speak with Yukito for a moment?” he requested politely.

What now? I thought, clenching my jaw. Tadashi led me away from Saki and Oda. I had no choice but to follow.

“Why didn’t you listen to me, Raito?” Tadashi asked once we were out of earshot.

“Strolling around town would be one thing, but a remote place like this? Who would help you if you got into trouble? And you brought Fuma with you. Do you realize what would happen to our clan if something happened to her?”

“Don’t be a hypocrite!” I snapped. “Saki’s the same age as you. If you can go anywhere on missions, why can’t she? You’re both clan heirs. She can take care of herself.”

“That’s not what I meant… I didn’t expect anything to happen to you. But this is a bad time to be in this forest. Do you know how many dangerous people are out here?”

I exploded. “You’re the one who led them here! Don’t deny it! If you were doing your job properly, they wouldn’t be able to track you. So if anything happens, it’s on you! You’re the clan heir! Your subordinates shouldn’t have to pay for your mistakes!”

Tadashi fell silent, rubbing his face.

“Raito, my mission isn’t finished,” Tadashi said quietly. “You and your friend need to leave. I’m being chased. I can’t protect all of you—”

“No one asked for your shitty protection! Argh! Why are you so arrogant?! Just because you graduated from the accelerated class, you think you’re better than everyone?! You can’t even beat Shiro, and she never even went to school! You’re pathetic! I’m not surprised Mother was disappointed—”

“Raito!” Tadashi’s face flushed crimson, his shoulders trembling. His hands clenched as if he might punch me. I half hoped he would, just so I’d have a reason to hit him back.

“Don’t say that….” His voice was hoarse. He closed his eyes briefly, taking a deep breath. “I’ll escort you to the edge of the forest,” he said quietly, his tone firm. “Then I’ll create a diversion so you don’t get caught in the crossfire. Please escort Fuma back to the city. Do you understand, Raito?”

“I don’t care about your stupid mission! I’ll take Saki wherever she wants to go. And don’t call me Raito,” I hissed. “Raito died when you left three years ago. Remember? You’re not my brother anymore!”

Tadashi paled, looking as though he’d been slapped. He turned away without another word. Good. I hated the sound of his voice.

Suddenly, Saki’s distant scream cut through the air. I spun around, ready to run towards her, but several smoke bombs exploded around me.

BANG!

I stumbled back, colliding with Tadashi. The ground rumbled beneath us. An earthquake?! What’s—

The ground surged and cracked violently. The next thing I knew, it collapsed beneath us and we plunged into the darkness.
