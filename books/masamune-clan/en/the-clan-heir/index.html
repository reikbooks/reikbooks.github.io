T he morning sun, warm on my back, felt like a brand as I slipped through a gap in the bamboo fence. The bushes lining the path offered little cover, but anything to avoid the judging eyes near the Masamune clan complex’s main gate.

Elder Fei would have a fit if he knew I was sneaking out. Two hours of kneeling in seiza position, stuck with both legs tucked under me, listening to lectures about a clan leader’s courage.

I knew that.

But right now, facing anyone felt impossible. No one said it to my face, but the whispers followed me like shadows. This past month, I’d become the hottest gossip in town.

“Hey, did you hear? That Tadashi, the Masamune heir in the accelerated class… he totally bombed the academy exams!”

“What? Seriously?”

“Yeah, my little sister’s in his class. Everyone else took it last month. Results are out, he’s the only one left. And the graduation ceremony’s, like, next week!”

“Wasn’t he supposed to have skipped, what, three grades?”

“Well, maybe they let him, but he’s just not ready. I heard his cousin’s been working the teacher’s council nonstop.”

“Oh, right… Anko, right? The royal commander’s kid? The genius one? Isn’t she his mentor too?”

“See? Total advantage! Having your own family as a teacher… Anko must have been boosting his grades all along.”

“Maybe Tadashi’s just clueless? Like, completely blanked on the final?”

“How embarrassing. Heard his dad even went to the school… probably trying to bribe the principal! Haha…”

I don’t know how these rumors spread outside the clan. The Masamune’s even had a dedicated gossipmonger, paid to control these things. She was supposed to shut down anything negative about us.

But now, everyone knew. Outside the complex, I got stares of pity. Inside, it was pure anger.

“Hey… why’s Tadashi just taking it? Our clan’s reputation is going down the drain!”

“I don’t get it… he’s not even clan leader yet, and he’s already messing up this bad. What happens when he actually takes over? We’ll be crushed!”

“Why aren’t the elders doing anything? Replace him! They say he wasn’t even the best candidate. How’d he even get chosen in the first place?”

“Well, you know… the leader’s pet… who knows what kind of favors he’s been pulling with the elders. Spoiled rotten, that one!”

I wiped the cold sweat from my brow. Spoiled? Since I was five, Father had me training day and night, on top of all my clan duties. When I entered the academy, he’d requested Anko as my mentor - but she never went easy on me. If anything, her training was harder than the other teachers’. More tasks, more pressure, all while keeping up with my clan obligations.

Every Saturday night, it was the same: clan meetings. The elders hated seeing me idle. Always another errand to run, another task to complete. Barely time for family, let alone friends. Sleep was a luxury, meals something to be squeezed in. I worked ten times harder than anyone, yet it was never enough. Criticism and lectures were my only rewards.

A shiver ran down my spine. What if Elder Han caught wind of the rumors? He was obsessed with the clan’s reputation - my humiliation would be his entertainment for the next year.

Honestly, I was at my wit’s end. I’d asked Anko countless times when I could retake the exams. But each time, her answer was the same, curt and dismissive. No news from the teacher’s council. Be patient. But my patience had run out weeks ago.

The mere thought of the exam sent a spike of pain through my skull. Lately, even finding Anko, or Father, or any of the elders had become a challenge. They were always conveniently absent when I needed them. And ever since I’d reported the exam delay, the clan meetings were off-limits. Guards at the main hall turned me away without a second glance.

It was all so suspicious. Were they planning something? What was going on?

“Hey, watch out!”

My hand shot out, pulling a pedestrian back just as a horse-drawn carriage thundered past. The girl, about my age, wore a kimono emblazoned with the Masamune clan crest. Relief flickered in her eyes, then vanished as she recognized me.

“Don’t touch me, you idiot!” She ripped her arm free, her face burning with shame. Without another word, she fled.

Have I become that despicable?

No one else had heard her words, but I still felt my cheeks burn. It wasn’t the first public humiliation, and each one stung worse than the last. I resisted the urge to hide my face - weakness was not an option, not for a Masamune.

I blinked, looking around. The market. Just a few alleys from the academy. The bystanders who had witnessed my humiliation were now openly pointing and whispering.

Head down, I plunged into the crowd, desperate to escape their judgment. Anko. She was my only hope. Today, I would get some answers.

Luckily, for once, she was in her office. When I knocked, she looked up from a mountain of scrolls, her face brightening.

“Tadashi!”

Maybe, just maybe, she had good news.

“Hi, Anko,” I said, trying to match her cheer.

“Your graduation exam mission has finally come through,” Anko said, waving a scroll in the air.

My stomach dropped. That didn’t sound good. She unrolled the parchment, revealing a message written in stark, official script. I broke the seal, my eyes skimming the words. Three times I read it, hoping each time the words would magically change.

They didn’t.

“Escorting Princess Kaguya to the Fumō Kingdom?” My voice was barely a whisper. My fingers traced the official academy seal, as if that could somehow make this all a bad dream.

Why was I given a mission like this? My classmates were out there building irrigation channels, fixing bridges, things with minimal risk of being murdered. I’d have gladly traded places with any of them.

But this… this was a suicide mission in disguise. Sure, it wasn’t explicitly stated, but with my current skills? What were the chances I’d actually survive this?

Dread coiled in my gut. Was this some elaborate scheme to get rid of me?

“It’s a bit unusual, yes,” Anko admitted. “Normally, this kind of task would go to a more experienced ninja. But your father insisted.”

Of course, he did.

So it wasn’t a plot from the academy. This was an elder-approved scheme. There were a few who openly despised me, who saw me as a threat to their own ambitions. But an elder’s order meant this had been discussed, debated, and ultimately agreed upon.

Father sometimes went against their wishes, but when it came to my future, my “training,” they were usually on the same page. Which meant…

My eyes darted to Anko, my last hope. She’d always been the voice of reason, shielding me from my father’s and the elders’ more outlandish ideas. Surely, she’d objected to this, right?

“The paperwork took ages,” Anko continued, oblivious to my internal panic. “Your father had to pull a lot of strings, get the palace on board with the timeline…”

The palace?

So Anko had agreed to this. Why else would Father go to such lengths?

I sank back onto my heels, carefully rolling up the mission scroll. Father… he’d always dreamed of elevating our clan’s status. And this… this was it. Successfully escorting a member of the royal family? That would erase any whisper of my supposed incompetence.

This was the highest honor a clan could receive, a mission usually reserved for the most elite warriors. But for me, a fifteen-year-old kid with barely any real-world experience, to pull this off? The Masamune name would be etched in legend.

Problem was, I was nowhere near ready. Sure, the elders had sent me on missions with the elite teams, but as an observer, not an active participant. I’d learned a lot, but I hadn’t even mastered our clan’s ultimate technique yet.

I gripped the scroll tighter, my knuckles turning white. This mission was too much. I couldn’t do this.

Anko was watching me intently. I knew better than to complain, to refuse an elder’s direct order. But after eight years, she knew me better than anyone.

“Don’t worry,” she said, her lips curving into a reassuring smile. “You won’t be alone. I’ll be with you every step of the way.”

Relief washed over me, so potent I almost sagged with it. Right. Anko was coming. This wasn’t so bad after all. With her by my side, maybe… maybe we could pull this off.

“Be ready tomorrow, bright and early. We leave at dawn.”

I nodded, scrambling to my feet. “Yes, Anko.”

There was so much to do. Weapons and supplies to gather, a route to plan, contingencies upon contingencies to account for. The princess’s life, and the Masamune clan’s honor, rested on my shoulders. No pressure.

Leaving the academy, I walked taller, my steps lighter despite the weight of the mission. I had no choice but to face this head-on. The sun felt warm on my face, and I took a deep breath, steeling my resolve.

Father, I won’t let you down. I will make you proud.

***

A nko and I had been ready and waiting in the palace hall since sunrise. My stomach was already doing nervous flips. Escorting Princess Kaguya was a huge responsibility, even with Anko at my side.

I’d gone over the planned route with the royal guard captain several times, just to be sure. Then I triple-checked the carriage for Princess Kaguya, making sure it was roadworthy and secure. Not that I could spot sabotage if it was staring me in the face—that was Anko’s expertise.

The plan was for the Princess to ride inside the carriage, naturally, with a palace soldier as the coachman. I would ride ahead on horseback, scouting the path and keeping an eye out for trouble, while Anko guarded from behind.

A royal steward, looking incredibly important, asked us to wait in the hall until Princess Kaguya finished breakfast. I was hoping we could leave before eight o’clock. That way, we could make it to Sakuragi Village by nightfall.

Fortunately, Princess Kaguya was spotted approaching the hall at half-past seven. Relief washed over me, even if it was quickly replaced by a fresh wave of nerves.

“Princess Kaguya of the Tsuki Kingdom,” the royal steward announced as the Princess arrived.

I dropped to one knee and bowed deeply, trying to remember everything I’d learned about courtly etiquette. Anko followed suit behind me, giving a respectful greeting to Princess Kaguya.

“Good morning, Your Highness,” I said, trying to keep my voice from cracking. I bowed, acutely aware of my own awkwardness. “My name is Tadashi from the Masamune clan. It’s an honor to accompany you to Fumo. I am tasked with escorting you to the Fumō Kingdom. My mentor, Anko Masamune, is a ninja academy instructor. She will accompany us in this journey. We will do our best to ensure your safety.”

Princess Kaguya, at twenty-one, was five years younger than Anko. She wore a royal kimono with cherry blossom motifs, her long hair flowing gracefully. I knew from the whispers that Princess Kaguya was famous not only for her beauty, but also for her sharp mind and diplomatic skills.

Princess Kaguya’s voice, when she spoke, was surprisingly soft, yet it commanded attention. “Rise, Tadashi of the Masamune clan.” Her gaze, sharp and assessing, lingered on me for a moment, sending a chill down my spine. Was I being too informal? She turned to Anko, a hint of impatience flickering in her eyes. “We should depart. Time is of the essence.”

After ensuring Princess Kaguya was comfortably seated in the cabin, we finally set off.

Though I rode ahead, I tried to stay within calling distance of Princess Kaguya’s coachman. I had made sure to tell the Princess to inform us if she needed anything, but she remained silent for most of the day.

By lunchtime, my stomach was rumbling. I stopped our group at a small park by a lake, figuring everyone could use a break. Approaching Princess Kaguya’s carriage, I saw her through the cabin window. She was hunched over a stack of papers spread out on a small table beside her bed.

The royal steward had informed me earlier that Princess Kaguya would be signing a trade agreement with the Fumō Kingdom. No wonder she looked so serious.

When I knocked on the cabin door, the Princess looked up, a flicker of annoyance crossing her face.

“Pardon the interruption, Your Highness,” I said. “Would you like to stop for lunch?”

Princess Kaguya glanced at the window.

“Oh… I haven’t finished. There’s still work to be done,” she said. “Please go ahead if you wish to eat. There’s no need to wait for me. I’ll take lunch and dinner in the cabin. Please don’t disturb me until then.”

“As you wish, Your Highness.”

I relayed Princess Kaguya’s message to the coachman and Anko, trying not to let my relief show. Spending another hour in this tense silence wasn’t exactly appealing.

As the afternoon wore on, the sky rumbled, mirroring my stomach, and gray clouds rolled in, obscuring the sun. A chilly wind blew, sending leaves scattering across the path. Rain was coming.

We were passing through the ruins of an old city when the first drops began to fall. It was a somber, almost eerie place, with crumbling stone walls and empty buildings that seemed to watch our every move. I gripped the hilt of my sword, my senses on high alert.

A flash of movement on the hillside opposite us caught my eye. Before I could react, an arrow came whistling out of the shadows, aimed straight at the carriage.

“Princess, watch out!” Anko shouted, her voice tight with urgency.

I spun around, throwing three shuriken towards the source of the arrow. My heart hammered in my chest. There was no time to think, only react.

The shuriken hit their mark with a sickening crunch of bone. Through the gloom, I saw a skeletal figure crumble to the ground, its skull split in two.

Skeleton monsters. What else could go wrong?

I didn’t have time to ponder the question. The carriage door flew open, and Princess Kaguya peeked out, her eyes wide with alarm.

“What’s going—”

Another arrow zipped past, narrowly missing the Princess. Anko shoved her back into the carriage, throwing herself on top as more arrows clattered against the open door. One shrieked past my ear, embedding itself in the wooden frame inches from my head.

“Anko!” I cried, my blood turning to ice.

“I’m fine,” she grunted, scrambling back to her feet. “Just a scratch. The Princess is safe. That’s what matters!”

But from the wince that flashed across her face, I knew it was more than a scratch.

And we weren’t out of danger yet. Not even close.

From the shadows of the ruined buildings and the undergrowth on the hillside, more figures emerged. Dozens of them, their skeletal forms rattling as they lumbered towards us, swords and axes clutched in their bony hands. We were trapped, the carriage wedged between the steep hillside and the crumbling wall of what was once a temple.

They were going to overrun us. I didn’t hesitate.

“Protect the Princess!” I yelled, leaping from my horse and drawing my sword.

Two skeletons rushed me. I met them head-on, my blade flashing. The sound of bone shattering was sickeningly loud. I ducked under a rusty axe, pivoted, and slammed a spinning kick into the chest of another attacker, sending it reeling.

These creatures might be slow, but there were so many of them! I fought like a man possessed, my movements almost instinctive. Slash, parry, thrust. Every strike had to count.

A blur of black shot out from the trees above, moving with a speed and agility the other skeletons lacked. This one was different. This one was dangerous.

I shoved two more attackers aside, racing towards the carriage just in time to see the black-boned figure land on the roof. It was taller than the others, its skull crowned with a twisted circlet of gold. The Skeleton King.

“Stay back!” I roared, putting myself between the creature and the carriage.

The Skeleton King chuckled, a dry, rattling sound that sent chills down my spine. It leaped down, drawing a sword that gleamed with an unnatural light.

We clashed, the air ringing with the sound of steel against steel. The force of its blow nearly knocked me off my feet. This creature was strong. Stronger than I’d anticipated.

I gritted my teeth, pushing back against its assault. Every parry, every thrust, sent tremors of pain shooting up my arms. I landed a few blows, but the creature was relentless.

As if sensing my growing desperation, the Skeleton King let out a piercing shriek, and the other skeletons surged forward, drawn back into the fray. I was surrounded once more, my attention split between the relentless attacks and the looming threat of the King.

Then I heard it. A scream. Princess Kaguya.

“Anko!”

Kicking a skeleton aside, I backflipped just in time to see the Skeleton King emerge from the overturned carriage. My heart lurched. Where were they?

The Skeleton King turned, its empty sockets fixed on me. Then, from the wreckage of the carriage, I heard Princess Kaguya’s voice.

“Anko, please… wake up!”

Relief flooded through me. They were alive. But Anko was hurt. I had to end this. Now.

The Skeleton King roared a challenge, and I met it head-on. Desperation surged through me. I wasn’t going to last much longer against this beast. There had to be another way.

My hand went to the hilt of my sword. The clan’s ultimate technique. The Sword of Light. It could destroy creatures like this, I knew it. But I hadn’t mastered it. Not yet.

What choice did I have?

I broke away from the Skeleton King, taking a deep breath. The sequence of movements for the technique flooded my mind, ingrained through countless hours of practice.

Tadashi… our clan’s ultimate technique isn’t just about mastering the form. Anko’s words whispered in my memory.

I closed my eyes, focusing my will, drawing on every ounce of strength I had left. This had to work. It had to.

“Sword of Light,” I whispered.

Hot energy crackled along my blade, but instead of bursting into the blinding light I’d hoped for, it flickered erratically, then died.

I opened my eyes, a cold dread gripping me.

The Skeleton King stood before me, a grotesque mockery of life. And it was smiling.

It happened so fast. One minute it was standing there, the next it was a blur of motion. I didn’t even see the blow that caught me squarely on the jaw.

A wave of dizziness washed over me. My vision swam. No… Not again!

The agony that followed was blinding. I staggered, the taste of blood filling my mouth. But I clung to my sword, even as my knees buckled and the world tilted sickeningly beneath me.

The Skeleton King wasn’t finished. With a brutal kick to my stomach, it sent me flying backwards. I crashed into the stone wall, the air driven from my lungs.

I lay there, gasping, my vision fading. My body throbbed with a pain so intense I thought I might pass out.

I had failed. Again.

My ears were ringing, the ground still trembling beneath my feet as I struggled to stand. The Skeleton King was gone. I swiped the rain from my face, vision slowly returning. In the distance, I could make out a few skeletal figures scrambling through the hills, fleeing from Princess Kaguya’s carriage.

Wait…why were they running? Could it be…?

My heart hammered against my ribs as I sprinted towards the carriage, the downpour a curtain of cold needles against my skin. The coachman slumped lifelessly on his seat, two arrows protruding from his back. His skin was already cold and clammy to the touch, tinged an unnatural blue. Poisoned, then.

Panic seized me. Anko! She was hit by an arrow too! I fumbled with the carriage door, only to find it locked from the inside.

“Your Highness? The monsters are gone! Please, open the door!”

Silence. Then, a click, and the door swung open, revealing a pale, shaken Princess Kaguya.

“Are you alright, Your Highness?” My gaze swept over her, searching for any sign of injury.

She offered a small nod, stepping aside to let me enter. Anko was slumped against the wall next to the bed, head bowed, eyes closed. Relief washed over me, momentarily eclipsing the fear.

I dropped to my knees beside her. Her pulse was faint but steady. Thank the ancestors, she was alive. A bruise was already forming on her temple, no doubt a parting gift from the Skeleton King. A bandage, clumsily applied, covered the wound on her arm where the arrow had struck.

“It was a graze, but…” Princess Kaguya’s voice trembled. “She bandaged it herself. Before facing that…that monster, she swallowed something. A black marble. An antidote, she called it.”

I nodded, relief giving way to a grim understanding. Every Masamune carried those. A potent concoction, brewed by the clan’s most skilled healers.

“She’ll be fine,” I reassured, more for my benefit than the Princess’s. “She just needs time to recover.”

Princess Kaguya released a shaky breath.

I glanced around the carriage. Papers from the table were scattered across the floor, some torn, some trampled underfoot. I winced, acutely aware of the puddles my soaked clothes were creating.

“You said they fled?” The Princess’ voice was sharp now, laced with tension.

I straightened, my gaze snapping up to meet hers. Princess Kaguya paced the small space, her agitation growing with each passing second. Her eyes darted repeatedly to the now-empty table.

What was it? What was she…? Then it hit me. Why would the Skeleton King enter the carriage if not to harm the Princess?

“Then why weren’t you chasing after them?!” The Princess’s voice cracked like a whip.

I flinched, the phantom pain in my gut flaring as if I’d been struck.

“They have the royal seal!” Her face flushed crimson, hands clenched into fists.

“You know,” she continued, her voice tight with barely suppressed fury, “when your father and the academy head proposed you for this mission…I disagreed.”

I bowed my head, shame washing over me. Every word was a lash against my skin.

“But your father…he’s the most loyal soul King Hanzo has. My father only agreed because of the trust he places in your clan.”

The air grew thick and heavy in my lungs. Father…I’ve failed you. I’ve failed us both.

“I didn’t expect much,” the Princess spat, “but to think you would let them escape like that… Is this all the heir of the Masamune clan is capable of?”

Silence descended, thick and heavy. The steady drumming of rain against the carriage roof was the only sound that dared fill the space between us. Finally, she drew a shuddering breath.

“There are protective seals on it. They cannot use it without me.” Her voice was calmer now, but the underlying current of disappointment cut deeper than any blade. “However, this delays everything. I cannot sign the treaty without it. While I can send word to my father and request a spare be sent, who knows how long King Fumo is willing to wait. It makes us appear incompetent, unable to even safeguard a simple seal! Do you understand?”

Each word was a stone added to the growing weight in my stomach. “I understand, Your Highness,” I murmured, my throat tight. “I’m sorry. I will find them.”

The urge to stay, to protect, warred with the desperate need to right this wrong. The monsters were gone. They had what they came for. It was unlikely they would return.

Taking a deep breath, I reinforced the seal on the carriage door for good measure, then turned to leave. “I’ll begin my search immediately.”

Hours later, I returned empty-handed, the setting sun painting the sky in hues of bruised purple and angry red. The rain still fell, relentless and unforgiving, mirroring the storm raging inside me.

“I haven’t found any trace of them, Your Highness,” I reported, my voice devoid of hope.

“Enough.” Princess Kaguya’s voice was flat, devoid of emotion. “We will continue to the Fumō Kingdom. I will request assistance there. They will have more…experienced guards at their disposal.”

I nodded numbly, bowing low. “As you wish, Your Highness.”

Thunder cracked overhead as I closed the carriage door, the sound echoing my own shattered confidence. The night stretched before me, vast and empty like the pit in my stomach.

I had failed. And not just in protecting Princess Kaguya. I had failed to consider the importance of the seal, of the political machinations that swirled unseen beneath the surface. There were those who would benefit from this treaty’s failure. I, blinded by my naive sense of duty, had played right into their hands.

The elders wouldn’t accept excuses. I had made a grave mistake, one that had angered the princess and tarnished the Masamune name.

I drew a shaky breath, my chest aching with a weight of despair. Was it already too late? Would searching for the seal even matter now? Even if I found it, could I ever erase the disappointment in Princess Kaguya’s eyes?

Doubt gnawed at me. She wouldn’t forget this, not easily. My clan, once trusted, was now branded unreliable in her eyes. Her curt words, her cold gaze… it was as if a chasm had opened between us.

What could I do?

The relentless rain offered no solace, only a chilling echo to the turmoil within me. I bit back a groan, my shoulders slumping under the immense burden of my failure.

Forgive me, Father. I have brought shame upon us all.

***

I continued the journey, taking over the reins from the coachman. It was already late, and Sakuragi village was still far away. I was afraid we wouldn’t make it to the village by nightfall. There were no other villages to stop at along our route. I wanted to keep going through the night, but the heavy rain had flooded the road.

Twice already, the carriage had nearly gotten stuck in the mud. Without Anko and the coachman’s help, I would have never been able to get it back on the road. The lack of moonlight didn’t help; it was hard to see where I was going.

I didn’t want to stop in the open, though. That would only invite trouble if bandits passed by.

In the end, I decided to stop near Moon Ravine Cave. The entrance was easily accessible from the road. When I explained my plan to Princess Kaguya, she simply nodded.

Supporting the half-conscious Anko, I guided Princess Kaguya into the cave.

It was warm and dry inside. A type of luminescent moss grew along the sloping entrance tunnel. We walked carefully. Not far from the entrance, the tunnel widened. A streak of light illuminated the end of the long corridor. When we reached it, it was like entering another world.

The ceiling soared high above. Stalactites and stalagmites were scattered everywhere. The path before us split in two, with high rock cliffs on either side. In the middle was a gaping chasm, interspersed with cascading waterfalls and lava flows from within the mountain. Light from the hot lava filled the corridors and crevices along the cliff sides.

I took the path on the left. From yesterday’s survey, I knew this side was wider and easier to navigate. A few hundred meters ahead was a cavity in the cliff side with a flat, rocky base, suitable for resting.

I pulled out two thick blankets from my waterproof backpack and laid them out for Princess Kaguya and Anko to sleep on. After they were comfortably settled, I did a quick patrol to ensure our surroundings were safe. Then I made a campfire near Anko, in front of the cave cavity.

Princess Kaguya had used her large towel to shield herself from the rain. I had also wrapped Anko in a towel, but I was soaked through. I couldn’t change; I’d left my spare clothes in the carriage so my backpack could fit the blankets and some supplies.

The cold was bone-chilling, numbing. I couldn’t keep wearing wet clothes all night. I sighed, taking off my jacket and trousers and hanging them near the campfire to dry.

My thick leather armor was also wet, but I didn’t want to sit in just my underwear, even though Princess Kaguya and Anko were asleep. Hopefully, the armor would dry quickly if I sat near the fire.

I took out a small kettle and water bottle. My hands were wrinkled and shaking from the cold as I boiled water to make herbal tea. Then I remembered the powder for the brew was in the pocket of my wet clothes.

Oh no… I hoped it wasn’t ruined. Anxiously, I retrieved the medicine pouch from my clothes and examined it.

Fortunately, it was waterproof, so the contents were still dry. I sorted through it and opened the parchment containing the energy-restoring powder. Carefully, I poured it into the tea thermos and stirred. Then I sat cross-legged in front of the campfire, slowly sipping the hot tea.

Ah… warmth spread through me, chasing away the chill in my bones. The wounds on my body hadn’t healed and still stung, but my muscles, which had been locked in pain, slowly began to relax. I took a deep breath and tried to analyze the day’s events.

Dark creatures like the Skeleton King and his minions couldn’t have just appeared on their own, let alone attack us in such an organized manner. Dark creature attacks were usually based on their natural instincts—brutal and without calculation. They would typically harm anyone who stood in their way.

But now, someone had summoned them from the dark dimension and managed to control them to steal the royal seal.

Who could control such monsters?

I closed my eyes.

On my eighth birthday, Elder Yun took me to his hidden library. He locked me in there, refusing to let me out until I had memorized the contents of The History of Ninja Clan Conflicts. One chapter explained the dark dimension and the creatures that had crossed over from there.

It also contained a list of clans that used black magic to control monsters, but all those clans had been defeated during the War of Light. Their members were executed, their books burned to ashes. No one knew about the darkness-summoning techniques they had once used.

Our clan members who had infiltrated the mafia clans’ lairs hadn’t mentioned anything about such books. Our spies had once heard rumors that the Mitsurugi clan had found one of the forbidden books, but upon further investigation, the book in question turned out to be a fake.

If that was the case, could a member of a forbidden clan have escaped? They could have infiltrated and hidden, then used their black magic to summon the skeleton monsters.

But why act now?

I shook my head, dizzy with speculation about who could be the Skeleton King’s boss. Clearly, if they only wanted to thwart this trade agreement, they could have simply killed the Princess. But they only took the royal seal. That meant they wanted to use it—for what else but to forge documents? What kind of document forgery, I had no idea.

But… Princess Kaguya had mentioned earlier that there was a protective seal on the royal seal. If they couldn’t open that seal, then they would come again, to kidnap someone who could break it.

A flicker of anxiety pressed on my chest.

Princess Kaguya was still in danger!

My muscles tensed again. I forced myself to take deep breaths.

My body felt battered. I wanted nothing more than to curl up and relieve the fatigue from working all day, but I couldn’t sleep now. I had to keep protecting the Princess. There was no one else to take turns guarding her while Anko was still sick.

I looked over at Anko, who was sleeping restlessly. I poured some tea from the kettle and woke her briefly so she could drink.

She had a high fever, her body burning up. The poison on that arrow must have been incredibly strong. She was still sick even after taking the antidote.

I gritted my teeth. Could Anko hold out until we reached the kingdom of Fumō?

I prepared a warm compress for Anko. Soon, the herbal tea seemed to take effect. Her fever gradually subsided until, at last, her breathing eased into a peaceful rhythm.

I gazed at the firelight licking the darkness of the cave. Anko… If only you were awake. She would know what to do. She always did.

What would happen if we made it safely to Fumō? Even if the Princess survived, the royal seal had been stolen. With a failure this grave, the royal court would surely blame Father and the academy head. Anko would be stripped of her rank, her career in ruins. My exam? A disaster. The relationship between the ninja academy and the Masamune clan, the years of work Father poured into it, all gone.

This could be the end of his leadership. He’d be so disappointed.

If the elders replaced him, I would lose his protection too. Anko and I, judged, punished by the clan. Ostracized. Exiled. Cast out. Images swirled in my mind, dark and suffocating.

A hand on my shoulder – Anko’s hand. I could almost feel it. “Focus, Tadashi. You’re on a mission. For a ninja, losing focus can mean losing your life.”

Her voice, a whisper in the darkness, brought me back. Right. I can’t afford to panic. I still had to protect Anko and Princess Kaguya.

I cleaned up the drinking equipment, then put on my now-dry jacket and pants. Princess Kaguya was sleeping soundly. But Anko… her fever had spiked again. I replaced the compress, worry twisting in my gut. Towards dawn, she finally stirred, but her face was pale, her breath shallow.

Suddenly, a flicker of movement outside the cave. Left flank.

THUNK! THUNK!!

I jumped, deflecting three arrows with my sword. Two more whizzed past, thudding into Anko’s blanket.

They were back!

But before I could react, four shuriken flew from the cave mouth in a deadly arc, each one finding its mark with a sickening CRACK . Anko! She was on her feet, sword gripped tight, though she swayed with every movement. Behind us, Princess Kaguya’s startled gasp echoed in the cave.

Anko didn’t hesitate. “Tadashi, protect the Princess!” she commanded. “I’ll distract the Skeleton King.”

“Understood!”

Princess Kaguya scrambled back, her face ashen. I guided her towards a corner, minimizing our exposure. Three skull monsters lurched forward, but I cut them down before they could reach us. But even as I fought, Princess Kaguya’s cry pierced the chaos.

“Anko, watch out!”

Anko was hemmed in by a horde of skull monsters, their bony claws reaching for her. And behind them… the Skeleton King, his sword raised high, ready to strike her down.

Without thinking, I let three shuriken fly. They found their mark, deflecting the King’s blow at the last moment. He roared in frustration, buying Anko precious seconds.

She spun, a whirlwind of blades, narrowly avoiding his next attack. For a moment, she teetered on the edge of collapse, then used a nearby skull to regain her balance, slashing out again.

My breath caught in my throat. Anko wasn’t fully recovered… yet here she was, pushing herself to the limit for this mission.

I had to help her.

I ripped my sword free from the grip of two skeletons, then struck with all my might, a flurry of punches and elbows fueled by desperation.

CRACK! CRACK! Both skulls shattered, sent flying into the darkness.

But it wasn’t enough. As long as the Skeleton King lived, he would just summon more of his minions. I can’t protect the Princess and fight them all.

“Your Highness, stay back!” I commanded, already forming the hand seals. The Star Shield. A dangerous technique, draining, painful, but I had no other choice. I slammed my fist to the ground, summoning the power within.

WHOOSH!

A torrent of bluish-white energy erupted around Princess Kaguya, forming a shimmering dome of protection. But the center of that light, it was like a hand, gripping me, squeezing the very life out of me. My vision swam, chest constricted, yet I gritted my teeth, forcing the energy to flow until the shield was complete.

By the time it settled, I could barely breathe, let alone stand. But I had to keep going.

Time to deal with these skeletons.

Dodging, weaving, I cut a path through the horde, each swing of my sword a desperate prayer. They swarmed the shield, unable to penetrate its defenses, giving me the advantage.

But then I saw him, the Skeleton King, summoning reinforcements. I fought my way towards Anko, but more of them surged between us, forcing us apart.

I tried to keep her in sight, but it was like trying to track a single leaf in a storm. This mission, it was my responsibility. Mine . Anko was injured, and I couldn’t be there to protect her. Every blow she took was my fault.

She was in trouble. The Skeleton King was toying with her now, her movements slower, each parry more labored. I should have seen it. His strategy. His focus on her. But I was too distracted, too focused on my own fight.

Should have. Should have. Should have.

By the time I realized what was happening, it was too late.

The Skeleton King had her backed against the cliff edge. As she deflected one of his attacks, he slammed a brutal kick into her back. She staggered, her foot slipping on the loose shale.

I lunged forward, reaching for her, but she was already falling, tumbling over the precipice.

“ANKO!”

My heart seized, a cold fist squeezing the air from my lungs.

It wasn’t like the stories said, where time slowed down. It all happened so fast, yet I saw it all. The shock on her face as she fell, then the small, sad smile she gave me. A silent farewell.

And then, she was gone.
